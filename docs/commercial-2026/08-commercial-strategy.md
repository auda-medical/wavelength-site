# Phase H: commercial strategy, revenue streams and professional-body opportunities

Prepared 10 October 2026. **Private.** Figures marked *assumption* are not forecasts: replace them with actual quotes
and invoices as they arrive (the business dashboard's Finance tab holds the actuals). Nothing here has been sent to
any organisation. No price, payment model or paid feature has been implemented.

## 1. Principles that constrain every recommendation

1. The core logbook stays genuinely useful without payment: logging, supervision workflows, sign-off, progress and the
   currently advertised exports. Never charge for mandatory sign-off or for access to existing records, and never
   promise that every future feature will be free.
2. Never sell accreditation, competency sign-off or supervisor approval. No sponsor or customer influences verification
   or assessment.
3. Earn revenue from additional value: hands-on education, tailored training, institutional tools and optional extras.
4. Decide with evidence: each opportunity has a demand test before significant build.
5. Keep NHS and private roles separate (section 13).

Break-even: about 7 paid places with two paid instructors (fixed costs about £2,225; each extra delegate contributes
about £354 after fees and catering). Above 10 delegates a further instructor is needed, which steps the fixed cost.

Levers, in order of effect: fill rate (marketing and the free logbook audience), faculty cost per delegate, venue cost,
equipment support, and a second date when one sells out. A price rise is the weakest lever while the market sits at
£375 to £395.

## 5. Tailored training quotation framework

```
Direct costs      = faculty days × day rate + venue (if we arrange it) + equipment hire/transport
                    + travel + accommodation + catering + materials + card or bank fees
Owner time        = preparation + delivery + travel + administration hours × an owner rate (management figure)
Base              = direct costs + owner time + administration allowance (10%)
Contingency       = 10% (UK) or 15 to 20% (overseas: currency, travel disruption, customs for equipment)
Price             = (base + contingency) ÷ (1 − target margin)       target margin 35 to 45%
```

Overseas checklist before quoting: professional indemnity cover in that country, local regulator or licensing rules
for teaching on patients or volunteers, equipment import (ATA carnet or local hire), visas, tax and VAT treatment of
the service, and payment terms (deposit in advance). Decline politely where the arrangements would not be viable:
the website already says each enquiry is assessed on its merits.

Illustrative only: a one-day focused session for 10 clinicians at their site in the UK, two faculty days at an
assumed £400, travel £150, materials £50, owner 14 hours at an assumed £80 management rate: base £2,082 including 10%
administration; with 10% contingency and a 40% target margin the quote is about £3,800. Record every real quote in the
pipeline so the framework can be calibrated.

## 6. Institutional services (validate before building)

What organisations might pay for, built on what exists: department dashboards (aggregate progress by pathway),
supervisor coordination (who supervises whom, outstanding reviews, turnaround), programme administration across sites,
course attendance and Academy completion records, and organisation-level reporting.

Governance needed before any of it: a data processing agreement, a DPIA covering organisation access to trainee
progress, explicit learner consent or a clear lawful basis for sharing individual progress with an employer, role-based
access, and NHS information governance review (DSPT for NHS customers).

| Model | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Per organisation per year | Simple to buy and budget | Hard to scale fairly across small and large sites |
| Per programme (for example a deanery's EM trainees) | Matches how training is funded | Needs a programme sponsor |
| Per active learner per year | Fair, scales with value | Unpredictable for the buyer |
| Annual site licence with tiers by active learners | Predictable and fair | More to explain |

Recommendation: interview three training leads (EM, acute medicine, ICU) about their supervision problem; offer one
free pilot in exchange for feedback and a letter of intent; set prices only after the pilot. No institutional pricing
is published or implemented.

## 7. Optional premium features (product ideas, not decisions)

Possible extras that add value without weakening the free core: an advanced personal analytics view (patterns in image
quality ratings, turnaround, gaps against curriculum), structured revision programmes linked to Pearls and Academy,
case-based learning libraries, encrypted cloud backup of images (needs a DPIA first, per CLAUDE.md), and certificates
of completion for structured programmes. Each needs a demand signal first (for example 50 users asking, or a pre-order
list). Payment would need the owner's explicit approval and store-policy review.

## 8. Recurring education

Options: monthly online case review (small cohort), refresher days for previous delegates, faculty development for
supervisors. Test with one free session and a waiting list; recommend only where the content and teaching workload is
repeatable (recorded material plus a short live element).

## 9. Sponsored education policy (outline)

Disclosure on every sponsored event page; no sponsor influence on content, faculty choice, assessments, sign-off or
supervisor verification; equipment sponsorship accepted only with a written agreement; no advertising inside the
logbook that interferes with use; NHS conflicts-of-interest declarations kept by the owner.

## 11. FAMUS course-approval feasibility

- Route (SAM website, read 10 October 2026): contact the FAMUS administrator with the programme, proposed dates and
  confirmation that the minimum standards are met; £200 per approved run; the first run includes a FAMUS committee
  member on the faculty, after which the same programme runs with local faculty.
- Still to read: SAM's "Minimum standards for FAMUS-approved courses" (PDF, updated 10 May 2021). It could not be read
  from the build environment. Its faculty, ratio, model and machine requirements decide the cost.
- Programme outline to test against those standards: theory (e-learning before the day), thoracic, abdominal-renal and
  DVT stations, supervised scanning on models at one instructor per four or five, an introduction to the FAMUS
  logbook, supervision and ACT, and how to find a SAM-registered supervisor.
- Faculty: FAMUS-registered supervisors for the modules taught (Wavelength's faculty already includes FAMUS-accredited
  instructors; registration status per module must be confirmed in writing).
- Expected cost: as the core course model (section 4) plus £200 approval and the committee member's expenses on the
  first run (*assumption*).
- Revenue: similar band (£290 to £395 per place locally); demand overlaps with acute medicine trainees, a new audience.
- Feasibility: **good**, subject to the minimum standards and faculty registration. Decision for the owner; no claim of
  approval until SAM confirms in writing.

## 12. FUSIC and RCEM collaboration opportunities

- FUSIC: Wavelength already tracks FUSIC modules and now represents Heart mentors and supervisors, module trainers and

- RCEM: the curriculum leaves logbook platforms to local choice provided the minimum dataset is captured; the RCEM
  e-portfolio remains the record. The opportunity is regional teaching and evidence quality, not a College contract.

## 13. NHS and private roles (governance)

The owner is an NHS consultant. Before any institutional sale or partnership: declare the interest under the employing
trust's conflicts-of-interest policy (NHS England "Managing conflicts of interest in the NHS"); do not use NHS time,
equipment, email or confidential information for Wavelength; do not sell to a department where the owner has a
decision-making role without independent procurement; keep a register of declarations and of any gifts or sponsorship;
and record any NHS venue use under a commercial agreement.

## 15. Decision criteria for future approval

- A course date runs if forecast fill is at least 7 paid places four weeks before (break-even).
- A new course type needs a waiting list or enquiries from at least 20 clinicians and a contribution of at least 40% at
  60% fill.
- Tailored quotes target 35 to 45% margin after contingency.
- Recurring or digital products need a demand test (for example 50 sign-ups or 10 pre-orders) before build.
- Institutional products need three discovery interviews and one pilot with a letter of intent.
- Prefer opportunities with indicative profit per owner hour above £75 that do not grow linearly with teaching days.
