A red, hot, swollen area raises one question: cellulitis or abscess? Cellulitis needs antibiotics. An abscess needs drainage. Examination often cannot tell them apart. Ultrasound can, in a minute or two.

![Wavelength Pearl: abscess. Gentle compression makes the contents swirl. Check colour Doppler before drainage.](/learn/images/abscess-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use the linear probe with plenty of gel, and light pressure on a tender area.
2. Start over normal skin, then move over the swelling.
3. Scan in two planes, and measure the collection in three dimensions.
4. Press gently and release, and watch the contents.
5. Switch on colour Doppler over and around the collection.
6. If unsure, compare with the same area on the other side.

## Reading the view

- **Cellulitis.** Thickened, bright subcutaneous fat broken up by dark strands of fluid: a cobblestone pattern. No discrete collection.
- **Abscess.** A dark or mixed collection, often irregular, with brighter debris inside and a brighter rim of tissue. Posterior acoustic enhancement often shows behind it.
- **Swirl.** Gentle pressure makes the contents move. Movement shows liquid, even when the collection looks grey.
- **Colour Doppler.** No flow inside an abscess. Flow is often seen around it, in the inflamed rim.

> **The aha** If it looks like fluid, make it move before you call it an abscess.

> **Pitfall** A vessel, a pseudoaneurysm or an inflamed lymph node mimics a collection. Use colour Doppler before any needle or incision. A lymph node has a bright centre (the fatty hilum) and flow entering it.

## Before drainage

- Mark the deepest point and the best approach.
- Measure the depth from skin to collection.
- Note any vessel nearby, and plan to avoid it.
- In the neck, groin and near joints, think about specialist referral.

?? A swollen, red forearm shows thickened subcutaneous fat with a cobblestone pattern and no discrete collection. What is the most likely diagnosis?
+ Cellulitis
- Abscess
- Lymph node
- Haematoma
: Cobblestoning is oedema between fat lobules in inflamed tissue, the typical pattern of cellulitis. An abscess shows a discrete collection. A lymph node is oval with a bright fatty centre. A haematoma is a collection, often after trauma or anticoagulation.

?? A grey, solid-looking lump shows swirling contents when you press gently with the probe. What does this show?
+ The lump contains fluid, supporting an abscess
- The lump is solid tissue
- The lump is a lymph node
- The lump is a lipoma
: Movement under gentle pressure shows liquid contents, even when the echoes make the collection look grey. Solid tissue, lymph nodes and lipomas do not swirl.

?? Before draining a suspected abscess in the groin, what must you check?
+ Colour Doppler for flow in and around the collection
- Posterior acoustic enhancement
- The temperature of the overlying skin
- The size of the opposite groin node
: A femoral pseudoaneurysm or a vessel mimics an abscess in the groin. Colour Doppler shows flow inside a vascular lesion. Needling one causes major bleeding. In the groin, many departments ask for specialist input before drainage.

?? An oval lump in the neck has a bright centre with colour flow entering it. What is it?
+ A lymph node
- An abscess
- A pseudoaneurysm
- A sebaceous cyst
: An oval structure with a bright fatty hilum and vessels entering through it is a lymph node. An abscess has no flow inside. A pseudoaneurysm shows swirling flow in a sac connected to an artery. A sebaceous cyst sits in the skin and shows no internal flow.

?? Which probe do you use for a suspected skin abscess?
+ Linear
- Curvilinear
- Phased array
- Endocavity
: The high-frequency linear probe gives the best detail of skin and subcutaneous tissue. Switch to a curvilinear probe only for deep collections, such as in the buttock or a large thigh.
