Ovarian torsion threatens the ovary within hours. Many clinicians look for absent Doppler flow, but the ovary has two blood supplies and torsion is often partial or intermittent, so flow is frequently present. The twisted pedicle and the shape of the ovary tell you more.

![Wavelength Pearl: ovarian torsion. Compare both ovaries, look for an enlarged oedematous ovary and follow the pedicle for a whirlpool sign.](/learn/images/ovarian-torsion-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Confirm the pregnancy test result.
2. Scan transabdominally with the curvilinear probe through a full bladder if possible. Transvaginal scanning by a trained operator gives a clearer view.
3. Find the uterus, then each ovary beside it. Find both ovaries before you call one abnormal.
4. Compare their size and appearance.
5. Look for the pedicle, the vessels running from the ovary towards the uterus and pelvic side wall. Follow it in short axis and watch for a swirl.
6. Add colour Doppler, but read it last.

## Reading the view

- **Enlarged ovary:** clearly larger than the other side, often with oedematous central tissue and follicles pushed to the edge.
- **Whirlpool sign:** the pedicle twists into a spiral or target. This is the most specific sign.
- **Associated mass:** a cyst or dermoid often acts as the lead point.
- **Free fluid:** a small amount around the ovary is common.
- **Doppler:** reduced or absent venous flow supports torsion. Arterial flow is often still present.

> **The aha** If you suspect torsion, follow the pedicle, not just the colour box.

> **Pitfall** Normal arterial Doppler flow does not exclude torsion, especially early or intermittent torsion. A normal POCUS scan does not exclude it either.

## What to do next

Sudden, severe, one-sided pelvic pain with nausea or vomiting needs urgent gynaecology review, whatever the scan shows. The diagnosis is confirmed at surgery, and delay risks the ovary. In children and adolescents, involve the paediatric surgical or gynaecology team early.

?? Which ultrasound sign is most specific for ovarian torsion?
+ A twisted vascular pedicle, the whirlpool sign
- Free fluid in the pouch of Douglas
- A simple ovarian cyst
- Increased flow in the ovary
: The whirlpool sign shows the twist itself and is highly specific. Free fluid and cysts are common in many conditions.

?? A patient has sudden severe right pelvic pain and vomiting. The right ovary is enlarged with peripheral follicles, and arterial flow is present. What do you conclude?
+ Torsion remains likely, so refer urgently to gynaecology
- Torsion is excluded by the arterial flow
- The scan is normal
- This is a haemorrhagic cyst, so discharge
: The ovary has a dual blood supply, and partial or intermittent torsion keeps arterial flow. The clinical picture and the enlarged oedematous ovary drive urgent review.

?? Why find both ovaries before calling one abnormal?
+ Comparing sides shows which ovary is enlarged or oedematous
- One ovary is always absent
- The left ovary is always larger
- Doppler only works when both are visible
: Ovarian size varies between people. Asymmetry is easier to trust than a single measurement.
