Rib fractures near the spine sit outside the reach of a serratus anterior plane block. The erector spinae plane block, described in 2016, places a larger volume under erector spinae, on the transverse process. The fluid spreads over several levels and reaches the dorsal rami and, to a variable extent, the ventral rami of the spinal nerves. The target is bone, a long way from the pleura, which makes it a simple block to learn. Evidence in rib fractures is growing but mixed. Use it within your trust's governance.

## Before you block

1. Check for a pneumothorax on ultrasound or X-ray.
2. Record consent, calculate the maximum safe dose (20 to 30 mL is typical), attach monitoring and know where the lipid emulsion is.
3. Sit the patient up or lie them on the uninjured side.

## The scan

1. Place the probe in a sagittal plane at the level of the fracture, over the midline, to see the spinous processes.
2. Slide laterally, about 3 cm from the midline, until the bony line changes from the rounded laminae to the square, flat-topped transverse processes.
3. Slide further lateral briefly to see the rounded ribs with pleura between them, then slide back to the transverse processes. This confirms you are on the transverse process, not a rib.
4. Insert the needle in plane from the top or bottom of the probe until it touches the transverse process, deep to erector spinae.
5. Inject 2 to 3 mL and watch the fluid lift the muscle off the bone and spread along the plane. Then inject the rest.

## Reading the view

- **Transverse process.** A square, flat-topped bright line with a dense shadow below.
- **Rib.** A rounder bright line, further lateral, with the pleura visible between ribs.
- **Erector spinae.** The thick muscle above the bone.
- **Correct spread.** A long strip of fluid lifting the muscle off the bone and travelling up and down.

> **The aha** The flat top tells you it is a transverse process. Rounded bone with pleura between means you have drifted onto the ribs.

> **Pitfall** A round swelling in the muscle means the tip is too shallow. Touch the bone first, then inject. The spread and the extent of the block vary between patients, so reassess pain and plan other analgesia.

?? Where does the needle tip sit in an erector spinae plane block?
+ On the transverse process, deep to erector spinae
- Between two ribs, through the pleura
- Inside erector spinae muscle
- On the spinous process in the midline
: The target is the plane between erector spinae and the transverse process. The bone stops the needle.

?? How do you tell a transverse process from a rib on ultrasound?
+ The transverse process is flat-topped and square, while ribs are rounded with pleura between them
- Ribs are always deeper
- The transverse process moves with breathing
- Ribs have no acoustic shadow
: The square transverse process sits about 3 cm from the midline. Further lateral, the rounded ribs appear with sliding pleura in the gaps.

?? Why is the erector spinae plane block considered lower risk for pneumothorax?
+ The needle targets bone well away from the pleura
- It needs no needle
- It is performed in front of the chest
- The pleura is absent at that level
: The transverse process acts as a backstop and lies a long way from the pleura.

?? What does correct spread look like?
+ A long strip of fluid lifting erector spinae off the bone and spreading up and down
- A round swelling inside the muscle
- Fluid between the ribs below the pleura
- Fluid in the skin only
: Fluid in the plane spreads over several levels. A round swelling in the muscle means the tip is too shallow.

?? Which statement about the evidence for this block in rib fractures is accurate?
+ It is growing but mixed, and the extent of the block varies between patients
- It is proven to remove the need for any other analgesia
- It has no evidence of any benefit
- It always blocks the whole chest wall on both sides
: Studies show benefit in many patients, but the spread varies. Reassess pain and plan other analgesia.
