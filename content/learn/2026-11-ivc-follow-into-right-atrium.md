The inferior vena cava (IVC), the large vein returning blood from the lower body, is easy to find and easy to mistake. The aorta runs close by, and in long axis the two look alike. The way to be sure is to see the vessel enter the heart.

![Wavelength Pearl: IVC. The subcostal view with the right atrium in the centre, the aorta with a branch running forwards, and the thin-walled IVC entering the right atrium with a hepatic vein joining it.](/learn/images/ivc-atrium-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## Before

Placing the probe in the epigastrium in long axis and looking for a tube often finds the aorta. Its walls are thick and bright, it pulses, it gives off branches running forwards (the coeliac trunk and the superior mesenteric artery), and it never reaches the heart. Measuring this vessel gives a confident answer to the wrong question.

## The move

1. Start in the subcostal four-chamber view.
2. Move the probe until the right atrium sits in the centre of the screen.
3. Rotate the probe 90° so the marker points to the patient's head.
4. Tilt slightly towards the patient's right until a thin-walled vessel runs through the liver into the right atrium.
5. Keep the right atrium on screen the whole time. If it disappears, go back to step 2.

## After

Now the vessel runs through the liver and opens into the right atrium. Its walls are thin, it changes size with breathing, and a hepatic vein drains into it. Measure the diameter about 2 cm from the right atrium, just beyond where the hepatic vein joins, at a right angle to the vessel.

> **The aha** Do not look for the IVC. Look for the right atrium, then rotate. The vessel entering the atrium is the IVC.

> **Pitfall** IVC size and collapse depend on breathing effort, ventilation and pressure in the abdomen and chest. Use them as one part of the picture, alongside the patient, the heart and the lungs, not as a single test for fluid.

?? In long axis in the epigastrium you see a tube with thick, bright walls that pulses and gives off a branch running forwards. What is it?
+ The aorta
- The IVC
- A hepatic vein
- The portal vein
: The coeliac trunk and the superior mesenteric artery run forwards from the aorta. The IVC has thin walls and enters the right atrium.

?? What tells you for certain that the vessel is the IVC?
+ You see it run through the liver into the right atrium
- It is the largest vessel on the screen
- It sits just in front of the spine
- It pulses with each heartbeat
: The vessel entering the right atrium is the IVC.

?? Where do you start to find the IVC?
+ In the subcostal four-chamber view, with the right atrium in the centre, then rotate 90° so the marker points to the head
- Low in the epigastrium in long axis, looking for any tube
- In the right flank, between the liver and the kidney
- Over the umbilicus in transverse
: Keep the right atrium on screen while you rotate and tilt slightly to the patient's right.

?? Where do you measure the IVC diameter?
+ About 2 cm from the right atrium, just beyond where the hepatic vein joins, at a right angle to the vessel
- Where it enters the right atrium
- At the level of the umbilicus
- At its narrowest point, wherever that is
: Measure in the same place each time, so that repeated scans can be compared.

?? A ventilated patient has a large IVC that changes little with breathing. How do you use this?
+ As one clue, alongside the patient, the heart and the lungs
- As proof that the patient needs no fluid
- As proof that the patient needs fluid
- As a reason to stop the scan
: Size and collapse depend on breathing effort, ventilation and pressure in the abdomen and chest.
