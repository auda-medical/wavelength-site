Most anorectal abscesses begin in an infected anal gland and spread through the planes around the sphincters. They may stay close to the skin, or track into the ischiorectal fossa, between the sphincters or above the levator muscle. The size of the lump on the skin tells you little about what lies beneath it, so a scan can help to plan the next step, if the patient can tolerate it.

![Wavelength Pearl: perianal abscess. A shallow dark pocket under the skin, a deep collection tracking towards the sphincter, and bright gas in the tissue.](/learn/images/perianal-abscess-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Explain the scan and give analgesia. Lay the patient on their side, with a chaperone and drapes.
2. Use a linear probe in a cover, on the perineal skin beside the swelling. Use a light touch.
3. Measure the pocket and its depth from the skin. Press gently, and look for [swirl](/learn/abscess-ultrasound/).
4. Follow the pocket deeper, and note where it lies in relation to the ring of the sphincter.
5. Add colour Doppler, and look for bright gas in the tissue.

## Reading the view

- **Superficial abscess:** a shallow dark pocket under the skin, away from the sphincter.
- **Deep track:** a collection running deep or towards the sphincter suggests a deeper abscess or a fistula.
- **Gas:** bright echoes with dirty shadowing in the soft tissue.

> **The aha** A small lump can have a deep root.

> **Pitfall** Ultrasound can miss deep and supralevator collections, so a reassuring scan does not settle the question. Bright gas with dirty shadowing raises concern for Fournier gangrene, a form of [necrotising infection](/learn/necrotising-fasciitis-ultrasound/). A tender perineum with little to see on the skin needs a senior review.

## Action

Refer for surgical review and drainage, following local pathways. Request MRI or CT if disease looks deep or the scan and the examination do not match. Escalate sepsis, diabetes or immunosuppression early. Gas, spreading skin changes, severe pain or systemic upset is an emergency that needs surgeons immediately.

?? What does the size of a perianal lump on the skin tell you?
+ Very little about what lies beneath it
- Exactly how deep the abscess goes
- That no fistula is present
- That it can always be drained in the emergency department
: A small lump can be the top of a deep collection.

?? How should the scan be done?
+ With analgesia, a chaperone, a covered linear probe and a light touch
- With the curvilinear probe pressed firmly
- Only after the abscess has burst
- Without explaining the scan
: The area is very tender. A gentle scan from the skin is often enough to start with.

?? What does a collection tracking towards the sphincter suggest?
+ A deeper abscess or a fistula, which needs imaging and surgery
- A simple superficial abscess
- A normal anal canal
- A lipoma
: Deep tracks need a surgical plan, and often MRI or CT.

?? A patient with severe perineal pain has bright echoes with dirty shadowing in the soft tissue. What is the concern?
+ Fournier gangrene, which is a surgical emergency
- A normal bowel gas pattern
- Simple cellulitis
- A haemorrhoid
: Gas in the soft tissue with severe pain or sepsis needs immediate surgical review.

?? The external scan shows no pocket, but the patient has deep rectal pain and a fever. What next?
+ Further imaging and surgical review, because ultrasound can miss deep collections
- Discharge, because the scan was normal
- Repeat the scan next month
- Treat with laxatives alone
: Deep and supralevator collections can hide from a probe on the skin.
