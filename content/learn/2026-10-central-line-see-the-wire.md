The worst central line complications start with a wire in the wrong place. A wire in the carotid artery, followed by a large dilator and a catheter, can cause a major arterial injury. Flashback, blood colour and a smooth wire pass can all mislead, and a wire can pass through the vein wall into the tissues. Ultrasound shows the wire directly, and it takes a few seconds.

## Before you start

This pearl covers one safety check. It does not teach central venous catheter insertion. Central lines need consent, full sterile precautions, a trained operator or direct supervision, and your trust's policy.

## The scan

1. Before you prepare the skin, scan the neck. Identify the internal jugular vein and the carotid artery in short axis. The vein is usually lateral to the artery and compresses with gentle pressure.
2. Puncture under real-time ultrasound, with the needle tip in view.
3. After you pass the wire, put the probe back over the vein in short axis. The wire should sit clearly inside the black lumen of the vein, as a bright dot or short line.
4. Turn to long axis and follow the wire along the vein. It should run within the lumen, not in the tissues beside it.
5. If you cannot see the wire clearly inside the vein, stop and recheck before you dilate.
6. Dilate only once the wire has been seen in the target vein.

## Reading the view

- **Wire in the vein.** Bright echo inside a black, compressible lumen, in two views.
- **Wire in the artery.** Bright echo inside the pulsating, non-compressible carotid.
- **Extravascular wire.** A bright line running outside the lumen, in the tissue beside the vein.

> **The aha** Hope is not wire confirmation. See it, in short axis and long axis, then dilate.

> **Pitfall** Flashback or easy passage does not exclude arterial or extraluminal placement.

## What the wire check does not show

A wire inside the internal jugular vein does not tell you where the catheter tip ends up. After insertion, confirm the tip position and look for complications such as pneumothorax, in line with your trust's policy.

## The recommendations

The 2019 Society of Hospital Medicine position statement recommends real-time ultrasound for internal jugular vein cannulation, and recommends that you visualise the needle tip and the guidewire in the target vein before dilation. It accepts either a short-axis or a long-axis approach.

?? Why is flashback not enough to show the wire is in the vein?
+ Blood can return from the artery or after a wire has passed outside the vein, and easy passage proves nothing
- Flashback only occurs in the vein
- Flashback is always dark in colour in the vein
- A smooth wire pass always shows the vein
: Arterial blood can return without a pulsatile jet, and a wire can pass through the vein wall. Only the image shows the wire's position.

?? In how many views do you confirm the wire, and when?
+ Short axis and long axis, before dilation
- Short axis only, after dilation
- Long axis only, after the catheter is in
- No view, if flashback was good
: Two views reduce the chance of missing a wire that lies in the artery or outside the vein. Check before you dilate, because dilating the wrong vessel is the harmful step.

?? The wire is not clearly inside the vein lumen. What do you do?
+ Stop, recheck with the probe, and do not dilate until the wire is confirmed
- Dilate gently to see
- Advance the wire further
- Place the catheter and check with a chest X-ray
: Dilation of an artery or the tissues is the step that causes harm. Resolve the doubt first.

?? What does a visible wire in the internal jugular vein not tell you?
+ Where the catheter tip will end up
- Whether the wire is in the neck
- Whether the vein is compressible
- Which side you are on
: Wire position at the neck is not tip position. Confirm the tip and exclude complications after insertion, according to local policy.
