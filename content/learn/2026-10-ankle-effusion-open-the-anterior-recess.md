A hot, swollen ankle might be a septic arthritis, gout, a sprain or cellulitis. Finding fluid in the joint, rather than around it, changes what you do next. The anterior recess of the ankle is the place to look.

![Wavelength Pearl: ankle effusion. The probe over the front of the ankle, a thin normal anterior recess, and fluid distending the recess between tibia and talus.](/learn/images/ankle-effusion-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Lie the patient supine with the knee bent and the foot flat on the bed, or slightly plantarflexed.
2. Place a high-frequency linear probe in long axis over the front of the ankle, in the midline.
3. Find the bright curve of the tibia, then the dome of the talus with its cartilage.
4. Look deep to the extensor tendons, in the recess between the tibia and the talus.
5. Plantarflex the foot gently. Fluid moves forward into the recess and becomes easier to see.
6. Compare with the other ankle.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** a thin anterior recess, with at most a trace of fluid.
- **Effusion:** dark fluid distending the recess, deep to the tendons, between tibia and talus.
- **Not the joint:** fluid around a tendon is tenosynovitis, and swollen fat is oedema or cellulitis.

> **The aha** Find the talus, then look deep. The joint is under the tendons.

> **Pitfall** Do not mistake fluid in a tendon sheath, or swelling under the skin, for a joint effusion. The joint fluid sits between the bones.

## Action

A hot ankle with an effusion, especially with fever, needs aspiration for possible septic arthritis. Use the same view to guide the needle, avoiding the dorsalis pedis artery and the extensor tendons.

?? Where does ankle joint fluid collect in the anterior view?
+ In the anterior recess, deep to the extensor tendons, between tibia and talus
- Inside the Achilles tendon
- Under the skin over the lateral malleolus
- Around the peroneal tendons only
: The anterior recess is the most accessible part of the ankle joint.

?? What makes a small ankle effusion easier to see?
+ Gentle plantarflexion of the foot
- Firm probe pressure
- Full dorsiflexion against resistance
- Colour Doppler at maximum gain
: Plantarflexion moves fluid forward into the anterior recess.

?? Fluid surrounds an extensor tendon but the recess between the bones is thin. What is it?
+ Tenosynovitis, not a joint effusion
- A septic arthritis
- A normal joint
- A fracture
: Fluid around a tendon is in the tendon sheath, not the joint.

?? A febrile patient has a hot ankle and a distended anterior recess. What next?
+ Aspiration for possible septic arthritis
- Reassurance
- Strapping and discharge
- Repeat the scan next week
: Ultrasound finds the fluid but cannot tell whether it is infected.

?? Which structure should you avoid when aspirating from the front of the ankle?
+ The dorsalis pedis artery
- The Achilles tendon
- The plantar fascia
- The popliteal vein
: The artery runs over the front of the ankle. Colour Doppler helps you find it before the needle goes in.
