The machine assumes every echo travelled straight out and straight back. At a strong curved reflector such as the diaphragm, with air-filled lung behind it, some sound takes a longer path. The machine places those late echoes deeper than they belong, and paints a copy of the liver or spleen above the diaphragm.

![Wavelength Pearl: mirror artefact. The liver copied above a bright diaphragm, the reflected sound path, and the copy changing with the angle.](/learn/images/mirror-artefact-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are fabricated educational illustrations, not patient scans.*

## Recognise it

1. Find the bright curved diaphragm from the right or left upper quadrant.
2. Compare the tissue on each side of it.
3. A mirror image repeats the organ below, with the same texture and often the same vessels.
4. Change the probe angle or the window. The copy changes with the sound path, because it is not a structure.

## What it tells you

- **Mirror image present:** the lung above the diaphragm is full of air. In the eFAST and lung scan this is the normal finding.
- **Mirror image lost, with the spine visible above the diaphragm:** the "spine sign". Fluid in the chest lets sound through, so you see the vertebrae continue upwards. Think pleural effusion or haemothorax.
- **Tissue-like lung above the diaphragm, with moving bright air bronchograms:** consolidation, not a mirror. Real lung keeps chest anatomy; a mirror copies the abdomen.

> **The aha** The mirror is normal. Losing it is the clue.

> **Pitfall** Do not call a mirrored liver a mass or a consolidation. Change the angle first, and look for air bronchograms before you call consolidation.

## Action

Move, angle and compare. Use a second view before you record an unexpected finding above the diaphragm, and read the result with the pleural effusion pearl's spine sign.

?? What causes the mirror image of the liver above the diaphragm?
+ A longer reflected sound path which the machine places too deep
- Liver tissue growing in the chest
- Colour Doppler aliasing
- Pressing too hard with the probe
: The machine assumes the sound travelled in a straight line, so it misplaces the late echoes from the reflected path.

?? In the upper quadrant view, what does a mirror image above the diaphragm usually mean?
+ Air-filled lung above the diaphragm, the normal finding
- A pleural effusion
- A haemothorax
- A liver mass
: Air behind the diaphragm makes it a strong reflector. Fluid removes the reflector and the mirror disappears.

?? The mirror image is gone and the vertebrae can be followed above the diaphragm. What does this suggest?
+ Fluid in the chest
- A normal lung
- A pneumothorax
- A broken probe
: Fluid carries sound, so the spine shows above the diaphragm. This is the spine sign of a pleural effusion or haemothorax.

?? How can you test whether tissue above the diaphragm is a mirror copy?
+ Change the angle and use another window
- Turn the colour gain to maximum
- Freeze the first image and stop
- Press harder until it disappears
: A mirror artefact depends on the sound path, so it changes when you change the angle.

?? What helps tell consolidated lung from a mirror copy of the liver?
+ Moving bright air bronchograms and chest anatomy in the tissue
- A completely black appearance
- Hepatic veins continuing into it
- An exact copy of the liver's vessels
: Consolidated lung looks like liver but contains air bronchograms and belongs to the chest. A mirror simply repeats the organ below.
