At a glance, a round black circle on the screen is a round black circle. A vein and the artery beside it look alike in short axis, especially in a shocked patient with a weak pulse. Cannulating the artery by mistake leads to bleeding, a painful haematoma and, if a drug goes in, possible harm to the hand. The bedside test takes two seconds: press.

![Wavelength Pearl: vein or artery. A true vein collapses with gentle pressure. Use compression before colour.](/learn/images/vein-or-artery-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Scan in short axis with a high-frequency linear probe. Find the target and the vessel beside it before you puncture.
2. Look at both at rest. Shape alone does not separate them.
3. Apply gentle, even pressure with the probe. The vein flattens and disappears. The artery stays round and pulsates.
4. Release the pressure and let the vein refill before you puncture, using a light hand.
5. Add colour Doppler second, if you need more. It supports the answer, but compression is the key bedside test.

## Reading the view

- **Vein.** Collapses with gentle pressure. Thin wall, no pulsation.
- **Artery.** Stays open under the same pressure. Thicker wall, pulsates.
- **Colour.** Red and blue show the direction of flow relative to the probe, not the type of vessel. Change the angle or the settings and the colours change. Spectral Doppler gives more: arterial flow is pulsatile, venous flow is phasic or steady.

> **The aha** The best colour test is pressure. If it does not collapse, do not call it a vein.

> **Pitfall** Colour depends on angle and settings. Do not trust colour alone.

## When the vein does not compress

If a vein does not compress, think of:

- **Thrombus.** Echogenic material in the lumen. Choose another vein.
- **Scarring** from past cannulation, with a thick, stiff wall.
- **Too little pressure** on a deep vein. Press a little more, then check again.
- **An artery.** Look for pulsation and check with Doppler.

Do not cannulate a vessel you cannot prove is a vein.

?? How do you tell a vein from an artery at the bedside?
+ Apply gentle pressure: the vein collapses and the artery stays open
- Use colour: red is always the artery
- Choose the larger vessel, as veins are always larger
- Choose the deeper vessel, as arteries are always deeper
: Compression is the key bedside test. A vein collapses with gentle pressure and the artery does not.

?? Why do you not rely on colour Doppler alone?
+ Colour shows flow direction relative to the probe and depends on angle and settings
- Colour Doppler never shows venous flow
- Arteries never show colour
- Colour always shows veins in red
: Red and blue show direction of flow relative to the probe. Tilt the probe or change the settings and the colours change.

?? A vein does not compress with gentle pressure. Which is a likely cause?
+ Thrombus in the lumen
- Heavy probe pressure
- A tourniquet on the arm
- Plenty of gel on the skin
: Heavy pressure collapses a vein. A vein which does not compress has thrombus or scarring, needs more pressure because it is deep, or is an artery.

?? What does spectral Doppler show in an artery?
+ Pulsatile flow
- No flow
- Flow that varies only with breathing
- Flow that stops with gentle pressure
: Arterial flow is pulsatile. Venous flow is phasic with breathing or steady.
