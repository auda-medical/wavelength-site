Mastitis is common in breastfeeding women and usually settles with continued feeding or expressing, and antibiotics if needed. A minority go on to form an abscess, which often does not settle on antibiotics alone. Non-lactational abscesses happen too, particularly in smokers, and tend to recur. Ultrasound shows whether there is a pocket to treat, and repeated needle aspiration has replaced surgical incision as the usual first step for many of them.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Explain the scan, keep the patient covered, and offer a chaperone.
2. Use a high-frequency linear probe with plenty of gel and a light touch.
3. Sweep over the tender area in a radial pattern, spreading out from the centre.
4. Compare it with normal tissue nearby or with the other breast.
5. Add colour Doppler, press gently, and measure any pocket.

## Reading the view

- **Mastitis:** bright, swollen tissue with blurred layers and no pocket.
- **Abscess:** an irregular dark or complex pocket with debris, no flow inside it, and often flow around it.
- **Solid area:** a mass that does not behave like fluid needs the breast clinic.

> **The aha** Find the pocket. Needle first, blade later.

> **Pitfall** A red breast that does not settle, or a solid area, needs the breast clinic. Inflammatory cancer can look like infection. Dark does not always mean fluid, so check for swirl and flow rather than relying on the colour.

## Action

Give antibiotics according to local guidance, and encourage the patient to keep feeding or expressing. Refer to the breast team, who usually offer [needle aspiration under ultrasound](/learn/abscess-drainage-ultrasound/), repeated if needed. Incision is usually kept for large collections, thin or failing skin, or when aspiration has failed. Sepsis needs admission. If the problem does not settle, arrange assessment to exclude cancer.

?? What does ultrasound add in a hot, tender breast?
+ It shows whether there is a collection or only mastitis
- It replaces any need for follow-up
- It proves the organism causing the infection
- It shows the antibiotic to use
: The finding of a pocket changes the treatment.

?? Which appearance fits mastitis?
+ Bright, swollen tissue with blurred layers and no pocket
- A discrete dark pocket with debris
- A round black cyst with a thin wall and no symptoms
- A bright line with a clean shadow behind it
: Oedema without a pocket is treated as mastitis.

?? What is the usual first procedure for a breast abscess?
+ Needle aspiration under ultrasound, repeated if needed
- Immediate incision in the emergency department
- Massage until it bursts
- No procedure at any stage
: Aspiration often avoids a surgical scar and helps the patient keep feeding.

?? A patient with an abscess is breastfeeding. What advice do you give?
+ Keep feeding or expressing
- Stop feeding at once and for good
- Avoid all fluids
- Bind the breasts tightly
: Milk stasis makes things worse. The breast team can advise on feeding from the affected side.

?? A red, thickened breast has not settled with antibiotics, and the scan shows no pocket. What next?
+ Refer to the breast clinic to exclude inflammatory cancer
- A further course of antibiotics, with no review
- Reassure and discharge
- Incise the reddest area
: Inflammatory cancer can look like infection.
