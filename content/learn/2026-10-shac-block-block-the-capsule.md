The shoulder anterior capsular block, or SHAC block, targets the small nerve branches which supply the front of the shoulder joint. It works far from the neck, so it avoids the brachial plexus roots and the phrenic nerve. The team who described it in 2021 reported good pain relief without motor block in patients with painful shoulders. Evidence in emergency care, for dislocations and fractures of the upper humerus, is still limited to small reports. Treat it as an advanced option, under local governance.

## Before you block

1. Confirm the diagnosis on X-ray and record the neurovascular examination.
2. Record consent, calculate a safe dose, attach monitoring and know where the lipid emulsion is.

## The scan

1. Lie the patient supine with the arm by the side, in neutral or slight external rotation if comfortable.
2. Place the linear probe transversely on the front of the shoulder, just below and lateral to the coracoid.
3. Identify the coracoid, the round humeral head, the subscapularis tendon lying over it, and the deltoid above.
4. Insert the needle in plane, keeping the shaft and tip in view, and aim deep to subscapularis onto the anterior capsule over the humeral head, lateral to the coracoid.
5. Inject a small test dose and watch the fluid spread along the capsule, under the tendon.

## Reading the view

- **Coracoid.** A bright bony curve with a shadow, on the medial side of the image.
- **Humeral head.** A larger bright curve with the capsule lying on it.
- **Subscapularis.** A fibrillar band running across the front of the humeral head.
- **Good spread.** A thin dark layer of fluid between the tendon and the capsule.

> **The aha** Block the capsule, not the whole neck. Stay lateral to the coracoid and spread along the bone.

> **Pitfall** The axillary artery, axillary vein and brachial plexus run medial to the coracoid. Keep the needle lateral to it. If the tendon swells or injection pressure is high, you are inside the tendon: stop and reposition.

?? Why is the SHAC block considered phrenic-sparing?
+ It is placed on the front of the shoulder joint, far from the brachial plexus roots and the phrenic nerve
- It uses a local anaesthetic which does not affect nerves
- It is always given with a nerve stimulator
- It blocks the phrenic nerve and the shoulder together
: The injection targets the small nerves supplying the anterior capsule. It works at the shoulder itself, far from the neck where the phrenic nerve runs beside the C5 root.

?? Which bony landmark must you keep medial to your needle during a SHAC block?
+ The coracoid process
- The acromion
- The greater tuberosity
- The spine of the scapula
: The axillary vessels and the brachial plexus run medial to the coracoid. Keeping the needle lateral to it protects these structures.

?? Where should the local anaesthetic spread in a SHAC block?
+ Deep to subscapularis, along the anterior capsule over the humeral head
- Around the C5 and C6 roots
- Inside the subscapularis tendon
- Under the deltoid on the back of the shoulder
: The target is the plane between subscapularis and the anterior capsule, where the small capsular nerves run. Injection into the tendon is painful and ineffective.

?? During a SHAC block, the tendon swells and the injection needs high pressure. What does this mean?
+ The needle tip is inside the tendon, so stop and reposition
- The block is working well
- You have entered the joint
- You have reached the brachial plexus
: Swelling within the tendon and high pressure suggest an intratendinous injection. Stop, withdraw slightly and look for spread along the capsule instead.

?? How strong is the evidence for the SHAC block in emergency shoulder dislocation?
+ Limited to small reports, so use it as an advanced option under local governance
- Several large emergency department trials prove it superior to sedation
- It is the recommended first-line analgesia in national guidance
- It has been abandoned because it does not work
: The block was described in 2021 and studied mainly in planned shoulder pain management. Emergency department evidence is still small, so it suits experienced operators working within local governance.
