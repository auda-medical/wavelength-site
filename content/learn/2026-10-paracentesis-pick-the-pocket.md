A diagnostic tap is part of the assessment of anyone admitted with ascites, and a large-volume drain relieves pain and breathlessness. Most complications come from the needle path: bowel caught against the wall, or a bleed from an abdominal wall vessel. Ultrasound shows both before you start. In large observational studies, ultrasound guidance was associated with fewer bleeding complications.

![Wavelength Pearl: paracentesis. Choose the widest fluid pocket with a clear path, no bowel and no vessels in the route.](/learn/images/paracentesis-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Lay the patient supine, tilted slightly towards the side you plan to drain.
2. Use the curvilinear probe in both lower quadrants, lateral to the rectus muscle. The left lower quadrant is often deeper and has thinner fat.
3. Find the largest pocket and sweep through it in two planes. Look for bowel loops floating up into the path.
4. Switch to a linear probe and colour Doppler over the planned entry point to find the inferior epigastric vessels and any wall varices.
5. Measure the depth from skin to fluid, and the depth of fluid under that point.
6. Mark the site and drain without moving the patient, or drain under real-time guidance.

## Reading the view

- **Good window:** a deep pocket, several centimetres of fluid, with bowel well away from the path through the breathing cycle.
- **Unsafe window:** bowel loops close to the wall, a shallow pocket, or a vessel on colour Doppler in the path.
- **Fluid character:** clear black fluid, or debris and septations, which change the differential. See the [ascites pearl](/learn/ascites-ultrasound/).

> **The aha** Pick the pocket, not just the first black space.

> **Pitfall** A pocket that looks large in one plane may be narrow in another, and bowel moves with breathing. Sweep in two planes, and do not move the patient between marking and needling.

## Action

Follow your local paracentesis procedure and consent process. Routine correction of clotting is not usually needed before paracentesis in cirrhosis, but follow local guidance. Send fluid for cell count, culture and albumin; a neutrophil count over 250 per cubic millimetre suggests spontaneous bacterial peritonitis.

?? Where is the inferior epigastric artery, and why does it matter?
+ In the abdominal wall near the rectus sheath, so enter lateral to the rectus and check with colour Doppler
- Behind the bladder, so it does not matter
- Inside the liver
- In the midline only
: A wall vessel in the needle path causes the serious bleeding complications of paracentesis.

?? A pocket looks deep in transverse but narrow in sagittal. What next?
+ Sweep further or choose another pocket, because the target must be adequate in both planes
- Drain it anyway
- Use a longer needle
- Ask the patient to sit up after marking
: One plane can mislead. Confirm the pocket in two.

?? Why not move the patient between marking and needling?
+ The fluid and bowel shift, so the mark no longer matches the pocket
- It increases the risk of infection
- It changes the colour Doppler settings
- It makes no difference
: Mark and drain in the same position, or use real-time guidance.

?? Which probe is best for looking for abdominal wall vessels?
+ A linear probe with colour Doppler
- A phased-array probe without colour
- An endocavitary probe
- No probe is needed
: Wall vessels are superficial and small, so a high-frequency probe and colour find them best.

?? Ascitic fluid shows more than 250 neutrophils per cubic millimetre. What does this suggest?
+ Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis
- Normal ascites
- Heart failure
- Pancreatitis
: This threshold diagnoses spontaneous bacterial peritonitis in the right setting.
