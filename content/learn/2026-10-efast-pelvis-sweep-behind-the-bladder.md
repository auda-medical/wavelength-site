Free fluid in the pelvis does not promise to sit in the middle of one suprapubic image. The pelvic view of the eFAST has to sweep through the dependent spaces behind and beside the bladder, in both sagittal and transverse.

![Wavelength Pearl: eFAST pelvis. Free fluid behind the bladder in sagittal and transverse sweeps, with a normal pelvis for comparison.](/learn/images/efast-pelvis-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are fabricated educational illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Place the curvilinear probe just above the pubic symphysis and angle down into the pelvis.
2. In sagittal, find the bladder and sweep from one side, through the midline, to the other. Look behind the bladder and, in women, around and behind the uterus.
3. Turn 90 degrees into transverse. Fan up and down through the whole bladder and the space behind it.
4. Set the depth so the space behind the bladder is fully on the screen.
5. Save clips of the sweeps, not one still image.

## Reading the view

- Free fluid is usually black or dark grey, and collects behind the bladder in men (the rectovesical pouch) and behind the uterus in women (the pouch of Douglas). Fresh blood can be grey.
- In women, a small amount of fluid can be normal. Read the amount, the place, the mechanism and the observations together.
- An empty bladder gives a poor window. A poor pelvic view does not make the whole scan negative.

> **The aha** The bladder opens the window. The diagnosis is in the spaces behind and beside it.

> **Pitfall** Bowel loops, seminal vesicles, ovaries and other normal structures can look like a small pocket of fluid. Confirm the shape and the place in the second plane.

## Action

In unstable trauma, read the pelvic view with the rest of the eFAST and your trauma pathway. Repeat it if the observations change. A negative scan must not delay definitive care when suspicion stays high.

?? Which views make up the pelvic window?
+ Sagittal and transverse sweeps
- One transverse still image
- One sagittal still image
- Colour Doppler of the bladder wall
: The two planes cover each other's blind spots.

?? Where do you look for free fluid in the male pelvis?
+ Behind the bladder, in the rectovesical pouch
- Inside the bladder
- Inside the prostate
- Between the skin and the rectus muscle
: The rectovesical pouch is the lowest point of the peritoneal cavity in men.

?? Why can one central still image miss free fluid?
+ Fluid can sit behind and to one side, at the edge of the chosen plane
- Free fluid moves too fast to see
- The bladder blocks all ultrasound
- Only CT shows fluid
: Fanning through the whole pelvis samples all the dependent spaces.

?? Is all pelvic fluid in a woman after trauma blood?
+ No. A small amount can be normal, so read it in context
- Yes, always
- No pelvic fluid is ever caused by trauma
- Only if the bladder is empty
: The mechanism, the observations, the amount and the other eFAST windows guide the reading.

?? What does a negative eFAST mean in an unstable patient you are still worried about?
+ It does not exclude important injury, so continue the trauma pathway
- It excludes bleeding completely
- The patient can go home without reassessment
- It proves the bleeding is retroperitoneal
: The eFAST depends on the amount of fluid and the windows, and it does not look at every injury.
