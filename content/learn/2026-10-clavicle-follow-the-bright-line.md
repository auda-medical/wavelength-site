Clavicle fractures are common after a fall onto the shoulder, especially in children. The bone is superficial along its whole length, so a linear probe shows its cortex clearly. In children, studies have found ultrasound accurate for clavicle fractures, and it can be done without moving a painful shoulder.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Sit the patient up, supported, with the arm resting.
2. Place a high-frequency linear probe along the clavicle, so the cortex is a bright curved line.
3. Sweep from the sternal end to the acromion, keeping the probe on the line of the bone as it curves.
4. Turn to short axis over any abnormality.
5. Compare with the other side if you are unsure.

## Reading the view

- **Normal clavicle:** a smooth, continuous bright line with a shadow beneath.
- **Fracture:** a step, a gap or overlapping ends, most often in the middle third.
- **Supporting signs:** a haematoma over the bone, and tenderness as the probe passes.
- **The ends:** the acromioclavicular joint at the shoulder, and the sternoclavicular joint in the middle. A step here may be a joint injury rather than a fracture.

> **The aha** Follow the cortex until it stops being smooth.

> **Pitfall** The bone curves near both ends, so the probe drifts off the line and the cortex looks broken. Keep the probe aligned with the shaft and confirm in two planes.

## Action

Most midshaft fractures are treated in a sling through your fracture pathway, with an X-ray if your pathway requires one. Skin tented or broken over the fracture, a cold or weak arm, or a posterior sternoclavicular dislocation (pain at the sternal end, difficulty swallowing or breathing) needs urgent orthopaedic review; the last needs CT.

?? Where are most clavicle fractures?
+ The middle third
- The sternal end
- The acromial end
- They are evenly spread
: The midshaft is the commonest site.

?? Why can the clavicle look broken when it is not?
+ The bone curves, so the probe drifts off the line
- The clavicle has no cortex
- Ultrasound cannot see bone
- The skin is too thick
: Keep the probe aligned with the shaft as it curves.

?? A fracture tents the skin over the clavicle. What next?
+ Urgent orthopaedic review
- Sling and discharge
- Ignore it
- Repeat the scan next week
: Threatened skin may need early fixation.

?? A patient has pain at the sternal end of the clavicle and difficulty swallowing after an injury. What do you suspect?
+ A posterior sternoclavicular dislocation, which needs CT and urgent review
- A simple midshaft fracture
- An acromioclavicular sprain
- Normal anatomy
: The displaced clavicle can press on the trachea, oesophagus and great vessels.

?? What else should you check in a clavicle fracture?
+ The skin, the nerves and the circulation of the arm
- The gallbladder
- The optic nerve
- The ureteric jets
: Neurovascular injury is uncommon but important.
