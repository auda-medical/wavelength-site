Fundoscopy is difficult through a small pupil, a cataract or a bleed. The ultrasound probe looks through the closed eyelid. In a multicentre emergency department study, point-of-care ultrasound detected retinal detachment with a sensitivity of 96.9% and a specificity of 88.1%.

![Wavelength Pearl: ocular ultrasound. Make the eye move. A retinal detachment is tethered at the optic disc. A posterior vitreous detachment moves freely.](/learn/images/ocular-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Do not scan if you suspect an open globe. Pressure risks extruding the contents.
2. Select the ophthalmic or ocular preset. It keeps the power within the limits set for the eye.
3. Ask the patient to close the eye. Cover the eyelid with a thick layer of gel, or a clear dressing then gel.
4. Rest your hand on the patient's nose or forehead so the probe floats on the gel with no pressure.
5. Scan in transverse and sagittal planes through the whole globe.
6. Turn up the gain to see faint membranes.
7. Ask the patient to look left and right, then straight ahead, while you watch. These are the after-movements.

## Reading the view

- **Normal.** A black vitreous, the lens at the front, and a thin bright retina lining the back of the globe, with the optic nerve shadow behind it.
- **Retinal detachment.** A thick, bright, often folded membrane, fixed to the optic disc at the back. It moves little with eye movement.
- **Posterior vitreous detachment.** A thin, faint membrane which moves freely and swings with eye movement. It does not attach to the optic disc.
- **Vitreous haemorrhage.** Grey, swirling echoes in the vitreous, best seen with high gain, which move like a snow globe after eye movement.

> **The aha** Don't just see the membrane. Make the eye move. Tethered and stiff means retina. Free and floating means vitreous.

> **Pitfall** A posterior vitreous detachment and a haemorrhage can hide a retinal tear or a detachment. Ultrasound is less sensitive for posterior vitreous detachment. Every patient with new flashes and floaters needs same-day ophthalmology assessment, whatever the scan shows.

?? A thick, bright membrane is fixed to the optic disc and moves little with eye movement. What is it?
+ Retinal detachment
- Posterior vitreous detachment
- Vitreous haemorrhage
- A normal finding
: The retina is anchored at the optic disc, so a detached retina stays tethered there. It is thick and bright and moves little. A posterior vitreous detachment is thin, mobile and not tethered at the disc.

?? When must you not perform ocular ultrasound?
+ When you suspect an open globe injury
- When the patient wears contact lenses
- When the patient has a cataract
- When the vision is normal
: Pressure on an open globe risks extruding the eye's contents. With penetrating trauma or a suspected rupture, protect the eye with a shield and refer urgently.

?? Why do you choose the ophthalmic preset?
+ It limits the acoustic output to the levels set for the eye
- It gives a deeper image
- It switches on colour Doppler
- It improves the frame rate
: The lens and retina are sensitive to heating and mechanical effects. The ophthalmic preset keeps the mechanical index and thermal output within the stricter limits for the eye. Keep the scan short.

?? Grey, swirling echoes fill the vitreous and move like a snow globe after eye movement. What is the most likely diagnosis?
+ Vitreous haemorrhage
- Retinal detachment
- Lens dislocation
- A normal eye
: Blood in the vitreous gives low-level echoes which swirl after eye movement. Turn the gain up to see them. Look carefully for a detachment hidden within the haemorrhage.

?? A patient with new flashes and floaters has a normal ocular ultrasound. What do you do?
+ Arrange same-day ophthalmology assessment
- Discharge with no follow-up
- Repeat the scan in a month
- Prescribe eye drops and review in a week
: Ultrasound does not exclude a small retinal tear, and it is less sensitive for posterior vitreous detachment. New flashes and floaters need a dilated examination by ophthalmology the same day.
