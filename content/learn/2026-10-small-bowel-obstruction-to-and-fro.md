Small bowel obstruction is fluid-filled. Fluid carries ultrasound well, so the dilated loops show up clearly, often before an X-ray shows the classic pattern. The movement inside the bowel helps separate mechanical obstruction from ileus.

![Wavelength Pearl: small bowel obstruction. Dilated loops with to-and-fro movement suggest mechanical obstruction.](/learn/images/sbo-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear probe.
2. Mow the lawn: sweep the whole abdomen in rows, top to bottom, in transverse and then in longitudinal.
3. Measure the widest small bowel loop from outer wall to outer wall.
4. Watch the contents of a dilated loop for 10 to 20 seconds.
5. Follow the dilated loops until they become collapsed.

## What to look for

- **Dilated loops.** Small bowel over 2.5 cm across is dilated. Fluid-filled loops with the valvulae conniventes, thin folds crossing the full width, look like a keyboard or a ladder.
- **To-and-fro movement.** The bowel contents move back and forth without moving on. Strong, ineffective peristalsis against a blockage causes this.
- **Transition point.** Dilated bowel meets collapsed bowel. The cause often sits here.
- **Free fluid between loops.** Fluid between dilated loops, alongside a thick bowel wall or absent peristalsis, raises concern for strangulation.

> **The aha** Dilated, to-and-fro and a transition point: think mechanical obstruction.

> **Pitfall** Late obstruction loses peristalsis, so the bowel looks still, as in ileus. Absent movement does not exclude obstruction. Ultrasound supports the diagnosis. CT defines the cause and complications.

## Ileus or obstruction?

In ileus, the bowel dilates without a blockage. Peristalsis is reduced or absent all along the bowel, with no transition point, and both small and large bowel often dilate.

?? Above what diameter is small bowel considered dilated on ultrasound?
+ 2.5 cm
- 1 cm
- 5 cm
- 8 cm
: Small bowel wider than 2.5 cm, measured from outer wall to outer wall, counts as dilated. A diameter over about 3 cm is commonly used too, so some guides quote 2.5 to 3 cm. Measure the widest loop you find.

?? In a dilated loop, the contents move back and forth without moving on. What does this suggest?
+ Mechanical small bowel obstruction
- Paralytic ileus
- Normal peristalsis
- Free intraperitoneal fluid
: Strong peristalsis against a blockage pushes contents forward, and they fall back, giving to-and-fro movement. In ileus, peristalsis is weak or absent throughout. Normal peristalsis moves contents in one direction.

?? How do you find the transition point?
+ Follow the dilated loops until they become collapsed
- Scan the right iliac fossa only
- Look for the largest loop
- Measure the bowel wall thickness
: The transition point is where dilated bowel above the blockage meets collapsed bowel beyond it. Follow dilated loops in a systematic sweep until they change calibre. The largest loop is not necessarily next to the blockage.

?? A patient with suspected obstruction has still, dilated bowel with no peristalsis. Which statement is correct?
+ Late obstruction loses peristalsis, so this does not exclude a mechanical cause
- This excludes mechanical obstruction
- This is diagnostic of ileus
- Repeat the scan in 24 hours before acting
: As obstruction progresses, the bowel tires and peristalsis fades, so a late mechanical obstruction looks like ileus. Use the history, the examination and CT to define the cause. Delay risks missing strangulation.

?? Which finding in suspected small bowel obstruction raises concern for strangulation?
+ Free fluid between loops with a thickened bowel wall and absent peristalsis
- Valvulae conniventes visible across the loop
- To-and-fro movement of contents
- Loops measuring 2.8 cm
: Free fluid between loops, a thick bowel wall and loss of peristalsis suggest compromised bowel. These patients need urgent surgical review and CT. Valvulae conniventes identify small bowel. To-and-fro movement and modest dilatation are features of obstruction itself.
