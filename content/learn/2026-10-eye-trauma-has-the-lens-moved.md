A blow to the eye can tear the fibres that hold the lens, so the lens tilts (subluxation) or falls free (dislocation). Patients notice blurred or double vision in that eye. Marfan syndrome and homocystinuria make it more likely after minor trauma. Ultrasound through the closed lid finds the lens quickly, and the same scan looks for the other causes of sudden loss of vision in the [ocular Pearl](/learn/ocular-ultrasound/).

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. First ask: could the globe be ruptured? If so, stop here.
2. Use a high-frequency linear probe with a low-output (ocular) preset if your machine has one.
3. Ask the patient to close the eye, cover the lid with plenty of gel and rest your hand on the face so the probe barely touches.
4. Scan in transverse and sagittal planes, with the eye looking straight ahead.
5. Ask the patient to look side to side, and watch what moves.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** the lens at the front of the globe, just behind the iris: an oval with bright front and back edges and a dark centre. The vitreous behind is black.
- **Posterior dislocation:** the lens lies in the vitreous, often on the posterior wall, and is missing from its place behind the iris. It may move as the eye moves.
- **Subluxation:** the lens is tilted or shifted to one side.
- **Anterior dislocation:** the lens lies forward in the anterior chamber. This can raise the eye pressure and needs emergency treatment.

> **The aha** Find the lens before you read the vitreous.

> **Pitfall** Suspected globe rupture is a reason not to scan: an irregular pupil, a penetrating injury, extensive subconjunctival haemorrhage or a soft, misshapen eye. Pressure on a ruptured globe can push its contents out. Shield the eye and call ophthalmology.

## Action

Record visual acuity. Any suspected lens injury, or loss of vision, needs urgent ophthalmology review. Shield the eye, give analgesia and an antiemetic, and avoid anything that raises eye pressure.

?? Where is the normal lens on a closed-eyelid scan?
+ Just behind the iris, at the front of the globe
- Against the posterior wall
- Within the optic nerve sheath
- Outside the globe
: The lens sits in the near field, at the front of the eye.

?? What does the normal lens look like?
+ An oval with bright front and back edges and a dark centre
- A bright line with a shadow
- A round bright ball with a comet tail
- A floating membrane
: The edges of the lens capsule are bright.

?? Where does a traumatically dislocated lens most often go?
+ Back into the vitreous, often lying on the posterior wall
- Into the optic nerve
- Into the orbital fat
- Into the eyelid
: Posterior dislocation is the commoner direction.

?? When should you not perform ocular ultrasound?
+ When globe rupture is suspected
- When the eyelid is closed
- When the patient can look straight ahead
- After any fall
: Shield the eye and call ophthalmology.

?? How should you scan an intact injured eye?
+ Plenty of gel, the hand resting on the face and almost no pressure
- Firm pressure to improve contact
- Without gel, through the open eye
- With an abdominal preset at full output
: Keep the pressure and the output low.
