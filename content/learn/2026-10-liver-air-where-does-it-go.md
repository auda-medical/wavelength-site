Gas in the liver shows as bright echoes, sometimes with ring-down artefact behind them. It sits in one of two places. Portal blood flows into the liver and out to its edges, so gas in the portal veins is carried to the periphery. Bile drains back towards the hilum, so gas in the bile ducts stays central. The two have very different causes.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear probe below the right costal margin and between the lower ribs.
2. Sweep through the whole liver in more than one plane.
3. Find where the bright echoes are: out near the capsule, or close to the hilum along the larger ducts.
4. Watch them. Gas moving with the blood flow in the portal vein is portal venous gas.
5. Add pulsed-wave Doppler to the main portal vein. Gas bubbles cause sharp, bright spikes on the trace.
6. Look at the gallbladder and the bowel for a cause.

## Reading the view

- **Portal venous gas:** small bright foci spread to the periphery of the liver, within a couple of centimetres of the capsule, often moving with portal flow, with spikes on the portal vein Doppler trace.
- **Pneumobilia:** bright linear echoes along the central bile ducts near the hilum, often with ring-down artefact, moving little with blood flow.
- **Causes of portal venous gas:** bowel ischaemia, severe sepsis, necrotising enterocolitis in newborns, and less serious causes after endoscopy or with bowel inflammation.
- **Causes of pneumobilia:** a previous sphincterotomy or ERCP, a biliary stent or surgical join to the bowel, and rarely a gallstone eroding into the bowel.

> **The aha** Peripheral is portal. Central is biliary.

> **Pitfall** Gas in nearby bowel or lung, and reverberation from the ribs, can mimic intrahepatic gas. Confirm that the echoes are inside the liver in two planes, and follow their pattern.

## Action

Pneumobilia after known biliary intervention is usually expected. Unexplained portal venous gas in an unwell patient, especially with abdominal pain, lactate rise or sepsis, needs urgent CT and surgical review for bowel ischaemia.

?? Where does portal venous gas usually appear in the liver?
+ At the periphery, near the capsule
- Only at the hilum
- In the gallbladder wall
- In the hepatic artery
: Portal blood flows into the liver and out to the edge, carrying gas with it.

?? Why does pneumobilia stay central?
+ Bile drains towards the hilum, so gas stays in the central ducts
- Bile flows out to the capsule
- The ducts are only at the periphery
- Gas cannot enter the ducts
: Biliary gas sits along the larger central ducts near the hilum.

?? What does gas in the portal vein do to a pulsed-wave Doppler trace?
+ It causes sharp, bright spikes
- It removes the signal completely
- It reverses the flow
- Nothing
: Moving bubbles reflect strongly and cause spikes on the spectral trace.

?? A patient has pneumobilia two years after an ERCP and sphincterotomy. What does it most likely mean?
+ An expected finding after the sphincterotomy
- Bowel ischaemia
- Portal vein thrombosis
- Liver abscess
: Air enters the bile ducts through an opened sphincter.

?? An unwell patient with abdominal pain and a raised lactate has peripheral gas throughout the liver. What next?
+ Urgent CT and surgical review for bowel ischaemia
- Reassure: it is pneumobilia
- Repeat the scan next week
- Start an ERCP
: Portal venous gas can signal ischaemic bowel.
