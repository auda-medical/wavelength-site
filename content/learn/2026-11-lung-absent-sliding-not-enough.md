You have found the bat, watched the pleural line, and seen no sliding. Many clinicians stop here and call a pneumothorax. Absent sliding tells you something is wrong. It does not tell you what.

![Wavelength Pearl: lung scan. B-lines and lung pulse rule out pneumothorax under the probe. A lung point confirms it.](/learn/images/lung-pearl.webp)

## Where to look

In a supine patient, free air rises to the front of the chest. Start with the linear or curvilinear probe in the second to fourth intercostal spaces in the mid-clavicular line, probe head to foot across two ribs. Scan both sides.

## Four signs, two questions

Each sign answers one question: are the two layers of pleura touching under the probe?

- **Lung sliding.** Shimmer along the pleural line as the lung moves with breathing. The layers touch.
- **B-lines.** Bright vertical lines from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen, moving with breathing. They start at the lung surface, so the lung is against the chest wall. The layers touch.
- **Lung pulse.** A subtle flicker of the pleural line in time with the heartbeat, with no sliding. The heart moves the lung, so the lung is against the chest wall. The layers touch.
- **Lung point.** The place where absent sliding meets sliding, as the lung edge moves in and out of view with breathing. This confirms pneumothorax.

> **The aha** One B-line or a lung pulse at a spot means no pneumothorax under the probe at that spot. Absent sliding alone means keep scanning.

## When sliding is absent without a pneumothorax

- The patient is not breathing, or is holding their breath.
- Right main bronchus intubation. The left lung does not ventilate, so it shows no sliding but a lung pulse.
- Pleural adhesions after infection, surgery or pleurodesis.
- Severe consolidation, contusion or large bullae.

## Use M-mode

Place the M-mode line across the pleural line. A moving lung gives a grainy pattern below the line, the seashore sign. No movement gives flat horizontal lines all the way down, the barcode sign. At the lung point, the trace switches between the two with breathing.

> **Pitfall** A large pneumothorax with a fully collapsed lung has no lung point, because the lung edge never reaches the chest wall. In a patient with signs of tension, treat the patient. Do not delay decompression to look for a lung point.

## Test yourself

?? A ventilated patient has no lung sliding on the left. You see a lung pulse at several points on the left anterior chest. What is the most likely explanation?
- Left pneumothorax
+ Right main bronchus intubation
- Left pleural effusion
- Bilateral pneumothorax
: A lung pulse means the left lung is touching the chest wall, so a pneumothorax is unlikely at those points. The left lung is not ventilating, which fits a tube in the right main bronchus. Check the tube position. A pneumothorax shows no lung pulse. An effusion shows fluid above the diaphragm, not this pattern.

?? Which finding confirms a pneumothorax?
+ A lung point
- Absent lung sliding
- A-lines without B-lines
- A barcode sign on M-mode
: The lung point, where sliding and no sliding meet, is the only sign here which confirms pneumothorax. Absent sliding and a barcode sign show no movement, which has other causes such as apnoea and adhesions. A-lines appear in normal lung and in pneumothorax, so they do not separate the two.

?? You scan a supine trauma patient for a pneumothorax. Where do you start?
+ Anterior chest, second to fourth intercostal space, mid-clavicular line
- Posterior chest at the lung base
- Supraclavicular fossa
- Mid-axillary line at the level of the diaphragm
: Free air rises to the least dependent part of the chest, which in a supine patient is the anterior chest. Start there and move laterally to look for a lung point. The lung bases and the mid-axillary line suit fluid, which collects in dependent areas. The supraclavicular fossa gives a small, awkward window.
