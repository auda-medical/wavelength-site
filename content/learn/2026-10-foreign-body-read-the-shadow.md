A splinter, a shard of glass or a thorn left in a wound causes pain, infection and repeat visits. Glass and metal show on X-ray. Wood, plastic and thorns usually do not. Ultrasound shows all of them, and maps their depth and their relation to tendons and vessels before you cut.

## The scan

1. Use the linear probe with plenty of gel. For fingers, use a thick layer of gel or a stand-off.
2. Start at the wound and scan along the likely track, in two planes.
3. Look for a bright structure. Turn the probe until it lies along its length.
4. Note what lies behind it.
5. Mark its position on the skin in two planes and measure its depth.
6. Note nearby tendons, nerves and vessels. Use colour Doppler for vessels.

## Reading the view

- **Wood, plastic and thorns:** a bright line with a clean dark shadow behind it.
- **Glass and metal:** often bright reverberation lines or a comet tail behind the object, with or without a shadow.
- **A dark halo** around the object suggests inflammation, granulation tissue or a small abscess. It often appears after a day or more.

> **The aha** Find it, map it, then cut.

> **Pitfall** Gas from the wound, scar, calcification and the edges of bone or sesamoids mimic a foreign body. Scan in two planes and compare with the other side.

## Before removal

Mark the skin over the object in two planes, record its depth and the nearest structures, and consider removal under real-time guidance. Deep objects, objects near nerves, vessels or joints, and those in the hand often need specialist removal.

?? Which foreign body is usually invisible on X-ray but visible on ultrasound?
+ A wooden splinter
- A metal needle
- A glass fragment
- Gravel
: Wood, plastic and thorns are usually radiolucent. Ultrasound shows them as bright structures with a shadow. Metal, most glass and gravel show on X-ray.

?? What usually appears behind a metal foreign body?
+ Reverberation lines or a comet tail
- No change at all
- Increased colour flow
- A dark halo only
: Smooth metal reflects the beam back and forth, giving bright parallel reverberation lines or a comet tail. Glass often does the same, sometimes with a shadow.

?? What does a dark halo around a foreign body suggest?
+ Inflammation, granulation tissue or a small abscess
- That the object is glass
- That the object has dissolved
- That there is no foreign body
: Tissue reaction forms a dark rim around a retained object, often after a day or more. It suggests inflammation or infection and makes the object easier to find.

?? A bright line is seen in a wound. Which step helps confirm it is a foreign body and not normal anatomy?
+ Scan it in two planes and compare with the same area on the other side
- Increase the gain
- Switch to the curvilinear probe
- Scan in one plane only
: Gas, scar, calcification and bone edges look bright too. A foreign body is separate from normal anatomy and absent on the other side. Two planes show its true shape.

?? What do you record before removing a foreign body?
+ Its position in two planes, its depth and the nearest tendon, nerve and vessel
- Only its length
- Only whether it shows on X-ray
- The colour of the surrounding skin
: Mapping the object guides the incision and shows when specialist removal is safer, for example near a flexor tendon or a neurovascular bundle.
