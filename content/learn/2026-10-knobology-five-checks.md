Most poor images come from the machine settings, not the patient. Run these five checks before you start looking for pathology.

![Wavelength Pearl: five checks before every scan. Probe, preset, depth, gain and focus.](/learn/images/five-checks-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## 1. Probe

Match the probe to the depth of the target. Use the linear probe for superficial structures such as vessels, lung pleura and nerves. Use the curvilinear probe for the abdomen and aorta. Use the phased array probe to scan the heart between the ribs.

## 2. Preset

Choose the preset for the examination: abdomen, cardiac, vascular or lung. Each preset sets frequency, image processing and screen orientation. A cardiac preset on an abdominal scan flips the orientation marker and confuses the anatomy.

## 3. Depth

Start deep enough to see past your target, then reduce the depth until the structure of interest fills the middle two thirds of the screen. Too deep wastes pixels. Too shallow hides fluid sitting behind the target.

## 4. Gain

Adjust overall gain until fluid looks black and soft tissue shows grey texture. Too much gain fills fluid with false echoes. Too little gain hides soft tissue detail. Use the time gain compensation sliders to even out the near and far field.

## 5. Focus

Set the focal zone at the level of your target. The beam is narrowest there, so lateral resolution is best at that depth.

> **On the course** You practise these checks at every station until they become automatic, before you start each application.

?? You scan the right upper quadrant for free fluid. Which probe do you choose?
+ Curvilinear
- Linear
- Phased array, cardiac preset
- Endocavity
: The curvilinear probe uses a low frequency, so it reaches deep abdominal structures with a wide field of view. The linear probe gives high resolution but does not reach far enough. A phased array reaches deep but with a narrow near field, and the cardiac preset flips the orientation marker. Endocavity probes are for internal use.

?? Your target sits in a small area at the top of the screen with a large dark area below it. What do you change first?
+ Reduce the depth until the target fills the middle two thirds of the screen
- Increase the overall gain
- Move the focal zone to the bottom of the screen
- Switch to the linear probe
: Too much depth shrinks the target and wastes the screen. Reduce the depth so the structure fills the middle two thirds, while keeping enough depth to see fluid behind it. Gain changes brightness, not size. The focal zone belongs at the level of the target.

?? A full bladder looks grey and speckled instead of black. What is the most likely cause?
+ Gain set too high
- Gain set too low
- Depth set too shallow
- Focal zone set too deep
: Simple fluid should look black. Excess gain adds false echoes, so fluid turns grey. Reduce the overall gain, then use the time gain compensation sliders to even out near and far field. Too little gain makes the whole image dark, including soft tissue.

?? Where do you set the focal zone?
+ At the level of the target
- At the top of the screen
- At the bottom of the screen
- It makes no difference
: The ultrasound beam is narrowest at the focal zone, so lateral resolution is best there. Place it at the depth of the structure you are assessing. Most machines show it as a small arrow or marker on the depth scale.

?? You scan the abdomen with the cardiac preset still selected. What problem does this cause?
+ The orientation marker flips to the other side of the screen
- The image becomes black
- The depth is limited to 5 cm
- Colour Doppler stops working
: Cardiac presets place the orientation marker on the right of the screen, while abdominal presets place it on the left. Scanning the abdomen in a cardiac preset reverses the anatomy on screen and leads to errors. Choose the preset for the examination before you start.
