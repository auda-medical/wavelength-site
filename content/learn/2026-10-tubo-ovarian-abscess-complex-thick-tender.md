Pelvic inflammatory disease often looks like little on ultrasound. When it forms an abscess, the scan changes, and a complex mass beside the uterus becomes the clue to pelvic sepsis.

![Wavelength Pearl: tubo-ovarian abscess. A thick-walled complex adnexal mass with septa, debris, hyperaemia and tenderness.](/learn/images/toa-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Confirm the pregnancy test result.
2. Scan transabdominally with the curvilinear probe, in transverse and sagittal, covering the uterus and both adnexa. Transvaginal scanning by a trained operator gives a clearer view.
3. Look beside the uterus for a mass. Note its wall, its contents and whether you can still see a normal ovary.
4. Add colour Doppler around the mass.
5. Press gently over it with the probe and note tenderness.
6. Check the pouch of Douglas for fluid.

## Reading the view

- **Tubo-ovarian abscess:** a thick-walled, multilocular adnexal mass with internal debris, septa or fluid levels, often with the ovary no longer separate from the tube.
- **Inflammation:** hyperaemia in the wall and septa on colour Doppler.
- **Pyosalpinx:** a dilated, fluid-filled tube with thick walls and debris.
- **Free fluid:** complex fluid in the pouch of Douglas.

> **The aha** Complex, thick and tender is the pattern that should worry you.

> **Pitfall** A normal POCUS scan does not exclude pelvic inflammatory disease, and many adnexal masses look complex. Ectopic pregnancy, endometrioma, haemorrhagic cyst, appendix abscess and tumour all mimic an abscess.

## What to do next

Treat as pelvic sepsis: blood cultures, intravenous antibiotics according to local guidance, and urgent gynaecology review. Large abscesses or those not responding to antibiotics need drainage. Arrange formal imaging to confirm the diagnosis.

?? Which appearance suggests a tubo-ovarian abscess?
+ A thick-walled, multilocular adnexal mass with debris and wall hyperaemia
- A thin-walled anechoic cyst
- A small amount of clear fluid in the pouch of Douglas only
- An enlarged ovary with peripheral follicles and a whirlpool sign
: An abscess shows a thick, hyperaemic wall, septa and debris. A whirlpool sign suggests torsion. A simple cyst has none of these features.

?? A febrile patient has pelvic pain, a positive pregnancy test and a complex adnexal mass. What must you consider first?
+ Ectopic pregnancy
- Tubo-ovarian abscess only
- Simple ovarian cyst
- Normal corpus luteum
: A positive pregnancy test with an adnexal mass means ectopic pregnancy until proven otherwise, even with fever.

?? A normal bedside pelvic scan in a febrile patient with pelvic pain means what?
+ Pelvic inflammatory disease is not excluded
- Pelvic inflammatory disease is excluded
- The patient can be discharged without antibiotics
- A tubo-ovarian abscess is confirmed
: Early pelvic inflammatory disease often has a normal scan. Treat on clinical grounds and follow local guidance.
