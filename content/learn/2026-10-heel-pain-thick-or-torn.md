Plantar heel pain is very common, and most of it is plantar fasciitis, worst with the first steps in the morning. A rupture is rarer: a sudden pop and sharp pain under the foot, sometimes after a steroid injection. A quick comparison of both heels with a linear probe separates them.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Lay the patient prone with the feet over the end of the bed, or with the foot resting on its side.
2. Place a high-frequency linear probe in long axis on the sole, over the heel, so the calcaneus is a bright curve.
3. Find the plantar fascia as a bright, fibrillar band arising from the calcaneus and running forwards.
4. Measure its thickness where it leaves the bone.
5. Follow it forwards, then scan in short axis.
6. Compare with the other heel. Gently bend the toes upwards to tension the fascia if you suspect a tear.

## Reading the view

- **Normal fascia:** a thin, bright fibrillar band, usually less than about 4 mm thick at the heel.
- **Plantar fasciitis:** thicker than about 4 mm, darker, with loss of the fibrillar pattern, usually at the heel.
- **Rupture:** a break in the fascia, often a few centimetres forward of the heel, with fluid or haematoma in the gap. The gap opens as the toes are bent upwards.

> **The aha** Thickness suggests fasciitis. A gap suggests rupture.

> **Pitfall** Anisotropy makes the fascia look dark. Keep the beam at right angles to it and compare both sides before calling it abnormal.

## Action

Plantar fasciitis is managed with stretching, footwear advice and time, through your local pathway. A rupture needs offloading in a boot and follow-up with orthopaedics or podiatry. Heel pain with swelling, night pain or systemic symptoms needs other causes considered, such as a calcaneal stress fracture or infection.

?? About how thick is a normal plantar fascia at the heel?
+ Less than about 4 mm
- About 10 mm
- About 20 mm
- It cannot be measured
: Thickening above about 4 mm supports plantar fasciitis.

?? Which finding suggests plantar fascia rupture?
+ A break in the fascia with fluid in the gap
- Diffuse thickening with continuous fibres
- A normal thin fascia
- A bright calcaneus
: Continuity separates fasciitis from rupture.

?? What makes a normal fascia look falsely dark?
+ Anisotropy from an angled beam
- Too much gel
- Colour Doppler
- The calcaneus
: Keep the beam at right angles to the fibres.

?? Which history suggests a rupture rather than fasciitis?
+ A sudden pop under the foot, sometimes after a steroid injection
- Gradual pain worse with the first morning steps
- Pain only at night
- Pain in the calf
: Rupture is usually sudden.

?? How do you make a rupture gap easier to see?
+ Gently bend the toes upwards to tension the fascia
- Press hard with the probe
- Point the foot downwards
- Scan only in short axis
: Tensioning the fascia opens the gap.
