A hot, painful shoulder raises concern for septic arthritis, which is easy to miss in a deep joint. The posterior recess is the most reliable place to find glenohumeral fluid, and the same view guides aspiration.

![Wavelength Pearl: shoulder effusion. Scan the posterior recess, compare both sides, and plan aspiration in plane.](/learn/images/shoulder-effusion-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Sit the patient up and stand behind them, with the forearm resting on the lap.
2. Use the linear probe, or the curvilinear probe in a larger patient.
3. Place the probe just below the scapular spine, parallel to it, and slide laterally to the back of the joint.
4. Find the glenoid, the humeral head lateral to it, and the posterior labrum between them.
5. Look at the capsule over the back of the humeral head. Then rotate the arm outwards, keeping the elbow by the side. External rotation moves fluid into the posterior recess.
6. Scan the other shoulder with the same settings.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** the capsule lies close to the humeral head, with at most a thin dark line, usually 2 mm or less.
- **Effusion:** a dark space lifts the capsule off the humeral head and bulges outwards.
- **Comparison:** a recess clearly wider than the other side supports a true effusion.

> **The aha** If the recess opens, believe the fluid.

> **Pitfall** Ultrasound finds fluid but does not tell inflammatory from septic arthritis. A shoulder that looks infected needs aspiration and orthopaedic review, whatever the scan shows.

## Guided aspiration

From behind, insert the needle in plane from lateral to medial, aiming for the space between the humeral head and the glenoid. Use full aseptic technique and watch the tip enter the fluid. Send fluid for microscopy, culture and crystals.

?? Which view shows a glenohumeral effusion most reliably?
+ The posterior recess, with the arm externally rotated
- The anterior view over the coracoid
- The subacromial bursa
- The axilla
: Studies of injected fluid show the posterior recess detects glenohumeral fluid most reliably, especially with the arm in external rotation.

?? What does a glenohumeral effusion look like in the posterior view?
+ A dark space lifting the capsule off the humeral head
- A bright line over the glenoid
- A step between the humeral head and the glenoid
- Shadowing behind the humeral head
: Fluid fills the posterior recess and pushes the capsule away from the humeral head.

?? How do you confirm a small effusion is real?
+ Compare with the other shoulder using the same settings
- Press firmly with the probe
- Use colour Doppler on the recess
- Measure the humeral head
: Asymmetry between the two sides supports a true effusion, as normal recess width varies between people.

?? A febrile patient has a painful shoulder and a small posterior recess effusion. What do you do?
+ Arrange aspiration and orthopaedic review for possible septic arthritis
- Diagnose frozen shoulder
- Treat as rotator cuff tendinopathy
- Reassure, as a small effusion excludes infection
: The scan finds fluid but cannot exclude infection. Fever and a painful joint need aspiration.
