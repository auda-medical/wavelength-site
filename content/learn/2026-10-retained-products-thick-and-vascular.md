Ongoing bleeding or pain after a pregnancy has ended can come from retained products of conception, clot, infection or, rarely, a vascular malformation or trophoblastic disease. Ultrasound looks inside the uterine cavity. Colour Doppler helps most: retained tissue usually has blood flow, and clot does not.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Explain the scan, offer a chaperone and record the offer.
2. Transabdominal first, with a full bladder if possible: curvilinear probe, the uterus in sagittal and transverse. Transvaginal scanning, where you are trained to do it, gives more detail.
3. Follow the endometrium from the fundus to the cervix in the sagittal plane.
4. Look at its thickness and texture, and for a focal mass in the cavity.
5. Add colour Doppler with a low scale over any mass or thick area, and compare with the wall around it.
6. Check the pouch of Douglas for free fluid.

## Reading the view

- **Empty cavity:** a thin, regular endometrial line with no focal mass.
- **Clot:** echogenic or mixed material in the cavity, usually without internal flow. It may change shape or move.
- **Retained products:** a thick, uneven endometrium or a focal mass, often with blood flow running into it from the wall.
- **Infection:** retained products with fever, tenderness or an offensive discharge suggest an infected miscarriage, a gynaecological emergency.

> **The aha** Thick and vascular raises the concern. Clot is usually still and avascular.

> **Pitfall** Endometrial thickness alone is not reliable, and no single cut-off decides. Some retained products have no visible flow, and strong flow can also come from a vascular malformation. Use colour, the hCG and the clinical picture together.

## Action

Heavy bleeding needs resuscitation and urgent gynaecology review, whatever the scan shows. Otherwise refer to the early pregnancy unit or gynaecology team according to local pathways, with the hCG result. Fever or signs of sepsis need urgent antibiotics and gynaecology review.

?? Which finding favours retained products over clot?
+ Blood flow on colour Doppler within a thick, uneven endometrium
- Complete absence of flow
- A thin endometrial line
- Free fluid alone
: Retained tissue usually has blood flow from the wall. Clot is usually avascular.

?? Is endometrial thickness alone enough to diagnose retained products?
+ No, because no single thickness cut-off is reliable
- Yes, anything over 5 mm is diagnostic
- Yes, anything over 2 mm is diagnostic
- Thickness is never measured
: Thickness, texture, colour flow and the clinical picture are used together.

?? A woman with retained products after a miscarriage has fever and an offensive discharge. What next?
+ Treat as an infected miscarriage: urgent antibiotics and gynaecology review
- Discharge with analgesia
- Repeat the scan in a month
- Reassure: this is normal
: An infected miscarriage can cause sepsis and needs urgent treatment.

?? A patient is bleeding heavily and is hypotensive after a miscarriage. What comes first?
+ Resuscitation and urgent gynaecology review
- A detailed transvaginal scan
- A formal radiology appointment
- An hCG measurement before anything else
: Treat the patient first. The scan must not delay resuscitation.

?? Strong flow in the uterine wall persists and the hCG stays high. What else should you consider?
+ A vascular malformation or trophoblastic disease
- A normal finding which needs no follow-up
- Clot
- A simple ovarian cyst
: Retained products are common, but persistent flow and a high hCG need specialist review.
