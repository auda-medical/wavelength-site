A swollen scrotum is often full of fluid, and fluid is a good window onto the testis. A simple hydrocele is usually harmless. Complex fluid, with debris or septations, suggests infection or blood. The more important finding may be the cause behind the fluid: torsion, infection, a ruptured testis after trauma, or a tumour.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Explain the scan, offer a chaperone and record the offer.
2. Support the scrotum on a rolled towel. Use the linear probe with warm gel.
3. Start on the normal side to set the grey-scale and colour settings.
4. Scan the fluid in two planes. Look for echoes, strands and septations.
5. Look at the testis through the fluid: its outline, its texture and its blood flow, compared with the other side.
6. Follow the spermatic cord, and look into the groin for bowel in a hernia.

## Reading the view

- **Simple hydrocele:** black fluid around the front and sides of the testis, with no echoes.
- **Complex fluid:** debris, fine strands or septations. With fever and tenderness, think of a pyocele; after trauma, think of a haematocele.
- **Testis after trauma:** a broken outline or an uneven, disorganised testis inside a haematocele suggests rupture.
- **Reactive fluid:** a small hydrocele often accompanies epididymo-orchitis, torsion and torsion of an appendage.

> **The aha** Clear fluid is one story. Complex fluid is another. The testis inside tells the rest.

> **Pitfall** Complex fluid is not specific, and a reactive hydrocele can distract from torsion. A new hydrocele in a young man can hide a testicular tumour, so always look at the testis inside it.

## Action

Possible torsion goes to urology at once, without waiting for a scan, as in the [testicular torsion pearl](/learn/testicular-torsion-ultrasound/). A haematocele after trauma, especially with a broken testicular outline, needs urgent urology review because a ruptured testis is repaired early. A pyocele needs urology review and antibiotics. A testicular mass needs an urgent suspected cancer referral.

?? What does a simple hydrocele look like?
+ Black fluid with no echoes around the testis
- A thick-walled mass inside the testis
- Bright echoes with shadowing
- Septated fluid with debris
: A simple hydrocele is anechoic.

?? A man has a tense, swollen scrotum after a kick. The scan shows complex fluid and an irregular testicular outline. What next?
+ Urgent urology review for possible testicular rupture
- Analgesia and discharge
- Aspirate the fluid in the emergency department
- Repeat the scan in six weeks
: A haematocele with a broken outline suggests rupture, which is repaired early.

?? Why look carefully at the testis inside a new hydrocele in a young man?
+ A reactive hydrocele can hide a testicular tumour
- The testis is never visible through fluid
- To measure the fluid volume only
- It is not necessary
: Fluid is a good window. Use it to look at the testis.

?? Complex scrotal fluid with fever and tenderness suggests what?
+ A pyocele
- A simple hydrocele
- A varicocele
- A normal finding
: Infected fluid has debris and septations.

?? A boy with sudden scrotal pain has a small hydrocele. What does the hydrocele tell you?
+ Little on its own, because reactive fluid accompanies torsion and infection
- That torsion is excluded
- That he only needs reassurance
- That the testis is normal
: Look at the testis and follow the cord. The clinical picture decides referral.
