Necrotising fasciitis spreads along the deep fascia faster than the skin changes show. Early on, the leg looks like cellulitis. Pain out of proportion to the redness is often the first clue. Ultrasound looks under the skin at the bedside, while you wait for the surgeon.

## The scan

1. Use the high-frequency linear probe. Switch to the curvilinear probe for a large thigh or buttock.
2. Scan normal skin away from the redness first, to see this patient's normal fat and fascia.
3. Scan the most painful area, then beyond the edge of the redness.
4. Follow the deep fascia: the bright line between the fat and the muscle.
5. Measure any dark fluid lying along the fascia.
6. Look for bright spots in the soft tissue with grey, dirty shadowing behind them.

## STAFF: what to look for

- **S**ubcutaneous **T**hickening: thick, bright fat, often with cobblestoning.
- **A**ir: bright spots with dirty shadowing or ring-down from gas in the soft tissue.
- **F**ascial **F**luid: a dark layer of fluid along the deep fascia.

In an emergency department study, diffuse thickening of the soft tissue with more than 4 mm of fluid along the deep fascia identified necrotising fasciitis with a sensitivity of 88% and a specificity of 93%.

## Reading the view

- **Cellulitis.** Thick, bright fat with dark strands between the fat lobules. The deep fascia stays thin and sharp, with no fluid along it.
- **Worrying.** Fluid tracking along the deep fascia, especially more than 4 mm, beyond the area of redness.
- **Most worrying.** Gas. Bright spots with dirty shadowing in the fat or along the fascia, without a wound or recent injection to explain them.

> **The aha** Cobblestones sit in the fat. The danger sits on the fascia. Look below the cobblestones.

> **Pitfall** A normal scan does not exclude necrotising fasciitis. If you suspect it clinically, call the surgeons, start broad-spectrum antibiotics and do not wait for imaging. Ultrasound, like CT, should speed escalation, never delay it.

?? What does STAFF stand for in the ultrasound assessment for necrotising fasciitis?
+ Subcutaneous thickening, air, fascial fluid
- Swelling, tenderness, abscess, fever, fluid
- Skin thinning, artery flow, fat fluid
- Subcutaneous tissue, abscess, fascial fat
: STAFF describes the three ultrasound findings: thickened subcutaneous tissue, air (gas) in the soft tissue, and fluid along the deep fascia. Thickening alone is common in cellulitis. Air and fascial fluid raise concern.

?? Which finding most separates necrotising fasciitis from simple cellulitis?
+ Fluid along the deep fascia
- Cobblestoning in the subcutaneous fat
- Thickened, bright subcutaneous fat
- Increased colour Doppler flow in the skin
: Cellulitis causes thick, bright fat and cobblestoning, with a clean deep fascia. Fluid tracking along the deep fascia suggests infection spreading in the fascial plane. A fluid layer of more than 4 mm was the threshold used in an emergency department study.

?? Bright spots with dirty shadowing appear in the soft tissue of a painful, red thigh. There is no wound or recent injection. What do they suggest?
+ Gas in the soft tissue, a red flag for necrotising infection
- Calcified lymph nodes
- A normal fascial plane
- Anisotropy from the probe angle
: Gas gives bright echoes with grey, dirty shadowing or ring-down behind them. Without a wound, injection or recent surgery to explain it, gas in the soft tissue of an infected limb needs an immediate surgical review.

?? A patient has severe pain in the calf out of proportion to mild redness, a raised heart rate and a normal ultrasound. What do you do?
+ Arrange urgent surgical review and start broad-spectrum antibiotics
- Discharge with oral antibiotics, as the scan is normal
- Repeat the ultrasound in 48 hours before deciding
- Wait for an MRI before contacting the surgeons
: Ultrasound misses some cases, especially early in the disease. Clinical suspicion drives the decision. Surgical exploration is both the diagnosis and the treatment, and delay increases mortality.

?? Why do you scan normal skin before the red area?
+ To see this patient's normal fat thickness and fascia for comparison
- To check the probe is working
- To warm the gel
- To set the colour Doppler scale
: Fat thickness and brightness vary between patients and body areas. A comparison view tells you whether the fat over the red area is thicker than normal and whether the fascia has changed.
