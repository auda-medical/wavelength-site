Ultrasound cannot see through adult bone, but it shows the surface very well. Instead of looking for a fracture line inside the bone, trace the bright cortex and look for a break in it. The distal radius in children is the best-studied example, but the same method works on any bone surface you can reach.

*Scan images in the graphic are fabricated educational illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use a high-frequency linear probe and plenty of gel. Start away from the most painful spot.
2. Find the bright cortex and slide slowly along it in long axis.
3. At any step, gap, buckle or angle, turn the probe 90 degrees and find the same break in transverse.
4. Look for haematoma beside it, a lifted periosteum and tenderness under the probe.
5. Compare with the other side when the anatomy is complex.

## Reading the surface

- **Direct sign:** a break, step or buckle in the cortex, seen in two planes.
- **Supporting signs:** haematoma, soft tissue swelling, a lifted periosteum and point tenderness.
- **Mimics:** growth plates, ossification centres and nutrient vessels in children, and old injuries in adults, can look like a break. Know the normal anatomy and compare sides.

> **The aha** Follow the cortex, not the shadow. The break is in the bright line at the top.

> **Pitfall** A normal scan of one surface does not exclude a fracture somewhere else, or a fracture into a joint. Follow your local X-ray and fracture pathway.

## Action

Use ultrasound when it answers a focused question at the bedside. Immobilise, and arrange X-ray or follow-up whenever clinical concern remains.

?? What is the main direct ultrasound sign of a fracture?
+ A break or step in the bright cortical line
- Loss of all shadow behind the bone
- Colour flow inside the cortex
- A smooth, continuous surface
: Ultrasound reflects off the bone surface, so a break in the cortex is the sign to find.

?? Why turn the probe 90 degrees?
+ To see the same break in a second plane
- To turn the scan into a CT
- To remove every artefact automatically
- To measure blood pressure
: Seeing it in two planes stops you mistaking an oblique interface or a normal contour for a fracture.

?? Which finding supports a fracture but does not prove one on its own?
+ Haematoma at the point of tenderness
- A continuous cortex
- A normal image of the other side
- A visible vein
: Soft tissue signs add weight but are not specific.

?? What commonly mimics a fracture in children?
+ Growth plates and ossification centres
- Lung sliding
- Ureteric jets
- The IVC
: Knowing the developing anatomy, and comparing with the other side, avoids this trap.

?? Can a normal focused scan exclude every fracture?
+ No
- Yes, after one long-axis sweep
- Yes, if there is no colour flow
- Only in older adults
: Some surfaces cannot be reached and some injuries need X-ray, CT or MRI.
