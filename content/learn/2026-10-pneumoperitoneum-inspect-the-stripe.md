Ultrasound is not the definitive test for a perforation, but it can show free air at the bedside. Seeing gas is not enough, because bowel always contains gas. The clue is gas lying directly against the front of the peritoneum.

![Wavelength Pearl: pneumoperitoneum. A bright peritoneal stripe with reverberation over the liver, compared with bowel gas deeper down.](/learn/images/pneumoperitoneum-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are fabricated educational illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Lie the patient flat. Place a high-frequency linear probe on the right upper abdomen, over the liver, which gives a clean window under the abdominal wall.
2. Find the normal peritoneal stripe: a thin bright line between the abdominal wall and the liver.
3. Look for a stripe which is brighter and thicker than usual, with reverberation lines or a dirty shadow beneath it.
4. Change the angle and, if it is safe, the patient's position. Free air can move; gas inside bowel stays with the bowel.
5. Look for support: free fluid, bright bubbles in fluid, sluggish bowel or local inflammation.

## Reading the view

- **Free air:** the peritoneal stripe is abnormally bright, and repeats beneath itself as reverberation, because gas touches the peritoneum.
- **Bowel gas:** the thin peritoneal line stays visible on top, with the gas artefact deeper, inside a loop.
- A normal scan does not rule out a perforation, especially with a small amount of air or a poor window.

> **The aha** Decide where the gas is. Free air touches and brightens the stripe. Bowel gas sits deeper.

> **Pitfall** After recent surgery or laparoscopy, free air is expected. Read it with the timing and the clinical picture.

## Action

If you suspect free air in an unwell patient, escalate urgently and follow your surgical and CT pathway. Do not let a normal bedside scan overrule strong clinical concern.

?? What is the enhanced peritoneal stripe sign?
+ An unusually bright peritoneal line caused by free air against it
- A thickened gallbladder wall
- A renal stone with twinkle
- Normal lung sliding
: Free gas just under the abdominal wall strengthens the peritoneal echo and causes reverberation beneath it.

?? How does bowel gas differ from free air?
+ A normal thin peritoneal line stays on top, with the gas deeper inside the bowel
- Bowel gas always moves into the chest
- Bowel gas never causes a shadow
- Free air is always completely black
: Where the gas sits in relation to the peritoneal line is the key.

?? Why scan dynamically?
+ Free air and its artefact may change with the angle or the patient's position
- A frozen image shows more movement
- The peritoneum appears only in expiration
- Colour Doppler is required
: Moving the probe and the patient helps separate mobile free gas from gas fixed inside bowel.

?? Does a normal scan exclude a perforation?
+ No
- Yes, if one image over the liver is normal
- Yes, after recent surgery
- Only in children
: Accuracy depends on the amount of air, the window and the scanner. CT or surgical review may still be needed.

?? A septic patient with peritonism has a bright peritoneal stripe with reverberation. What should happen?
+ Urgent escalation, with definitive imaging or surgical management
- Routine outpatient follow-up
- Repeat the same image for an hour
- Reassurance, because ultrasound is non-invasive
: A suspected perforation is time-critical.
