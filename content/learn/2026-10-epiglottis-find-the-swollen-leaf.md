Adult epiglottitis presents as a severe sore throat, pain on swallowing and a muffled voice, often with a normal-looking pharynx. Drooling, stridor or sitting forward are signs of a threatened airway. Direct laryngoscopy by ENT is the usual test. In a stable adult, the epiglottis can also be seen with a linear probe on the front of the neck. Small studies have found it thicker in epiglottitis, but they used different cut-offs, so there is no single agreed number.

## The scan

1. Do not scan a patient with stridor, drooling, distress or any sign of airway compromise. Call for senior, anaesthetic and ENT help at once.
2. Do not lie the patient flat or upset them. Let them sit as they are comfortable. Avoid scanning distressed children.
3. Use a high-frequency linear probe in transverse, in the midline, between the hyoid bone and the thyroid cartilage.
4. Tilt the probe towards the head until a curved, dark band appears deep to the bright pre-epiglottic fat: the epiglottis.
5. Sweep up and down to stay at its level, and confirm it in a longitudinal view.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** a thin, even, curved dark band, like a leaf in cross-section.
- **Epiglottitis:** a thickened, rounded, darker epiglottis, sometimes with brighter, swollen pre-epiglottic fat in front of it.
- **Mimics:** the arytenoid cartilages lie lower, and an oblique probe angle makes a normal epiglottis look thick.

> **The aha** Find the leaf above the thyroid cartilage, then judge its shape.

> **Pitfall** A normal scan does not exclude epiglottitis, and the scan must never delay airway management or ENT review.

## Action

Suspected epiglottitis needs senior review, early ENT assessment and an airway plan, with the patient kept calm and upright. Start intravenous antibiotics and steroids according to local guidance. Ultrasound is an adjunct in stable adults only.

?? Where do you place the probe to find the epiglottis?
+ Transverse in the midline, between the hyoid bone and the thyroid cartilage
- Over the cricothyroid membrane
- Below the cricoid cartilage
- Over the sternocleidomastoid
: The epiglottis is seen through the thyrohyoid membrane, deep to the pre-epiglottic fat.

?? A patient with a sore throat is drooling and has stridor. What do you do?
+ Do not scan: call senior, anaesthetic and ENT help and plan the airway
- Scan the neck first to confirm
- Lie the patient flat for a better view
- Send them for a CT scan alone
: Airway compromise is an emergency, and scanning can make it worse.

?? Where does the epiglottis lie in relation to the pre-epiglottic fat?
+ Deep to it
- In front of it
- Inside the thyroid cartilage
- Below the cricoid
: The pre-epiglottic space lies in front of the epiglottis.

?? A stable adult with severe pain on swallowing has a thin, normal-looking epiglottis on ultrasound. What next?
+ ENT assessment is still needed, because a normal scan does not exclude epiglottitis
- Discharge with reassurance
- Diagnose tonsillitis
- No further assessment is needed
: Ultrasound supports the diagnosis; it does not rule it out.

?? What can make a normal epiglottis look thick?
+ An oblique probe angle
- Too little gel
- Colour Doppler
- A full stomach
: Keep the probe in a true transverse plane and confirm in a second view.
