Each finger has two palmar digital nerves, running beside the flexor tendon sheath with the digital arteries, and two smaller dorsal digital nerves. The palmar nerves supply the palmar surface and, in the index, middle and ring fingers, the back of the finger beyond the middle joint, including the nail bed. Many clinicians perform digital blocks by landmark. Ultrasound shows the nerves and arteries, and confirms the spread with a smaller volume.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## Before you block

1. Record sensation on both sides of the finger and the tendon function before the block. A digital nerve injury must be documented first.
2. Record consent. One to two millilitres on each side is usually enough.
3. Lidocaine with adrenaline is safe in the fingers of healthy patients. A large multicentre study found no finger loss. Follow your trust's guidance, and avoid adrenaline where the circulation to the finger is already poor.

## The scan

1. Rest the hand palm up. Use plenty of gel, or a gel pad, over the base of the finger.
2. Place a high-frequency linear probe transversely over the palmar surface of the proximal phalanx.
3. Identify the bone, the bright flexor tendon in its sheath, and on each side the small digital artery with the nerve beside it. Colour Doppler finds the arteries.
4. Insert the needle from the side of the finger, in plane or out of plane, and place a small deposit beside the nerve on one side.
5. Repeat on the other side.

## Reading the view

- **Proximal phalanx.** A curved bright line with a shadow below.
- **Flexor tendon.** A bright, fibrillar oval on the bone.
- **Digital artery.** A small black circle on each side, with flow on Doppler.
- **Digital nerve.** A tiny bright oval beside each artery.
- **Correct spread.** A small pool of fluid around each nerve.

> **The aha** Two small deposits beat one big bolus. Each nerve needs its own injection.

> **Pitfall** Large volumes around the whole base of the finger raise the pressure in a closed space. Keep the volumes small. If one side stays painful, the nerve on that side was missed. For a wound on the back of the base of the finger, add a small injection over the back of the base for the dorsal nerves.

?? Where do the palmar digital nerves run?
+ On each side of the flexor tendon sheath, beside the digital arteries
- On the back of the finger, over the extensor tendon
- Inside the flexor tendon sheath
- Through the middle of the proximal phalanx
: Each palmar digital nerve runs with its digital artery on one side of the flexor tendon sheath.

?? Which area do the palmar digital nerves supply in the index, middle and ring fingers?
+ The palmar surface and the back of the finger beyond the middle joint, including the nail bed
- Only the back of the finger at the base
- The whole hand
- The skin over the metacarpals
: The palmar digital nerves supply the palmar surface and send branches to the back of the finger beyond the middle joint.

?? What is the current evidence on lidocaine with adrenaline in the fingers?
+ It is safe in the fingers of healthy patients, with no finger loss in a large multicentre study
- It always causes finger necrosis
- It is never used in the hand
- It is safe only in the thumb
: A large multicentre study of over 3,000 hand procedures with adrenaline found no finger loss. Avoid it where the circulation is already poor.

?? After a digital block, the radial side of the finger is numb but the ulnar side is painful. What is the likely reason?
+ The nerve on the ulnar side was missed
- The block was too large
- The radial nerve supplies the ulnar side of the finger
- The patient is allergic to local anaesthetic
: Each side has its own nerve. Pain on one side means that nerve needs its own deposit.

?? Why do you keep the volumes small?
+ Large volumes around the base of the finger raise the pressure in a closed space
- Small volumes last longer
- Large volumes block the median nerve
- Small volumes avoid the need for consent
: High pressure around the base of the finger can compromise the circulation. Small deposits beside each nerve are enough.
