After a fall on the outstretched hand, a raised posterior fat pad on the lateral X-ray is the classic clue to a hidden elbow fracture. Ultrasound shows the same thing directly: fluid in the posterior recess, lifting the fat pad out of the olecranon fossa.

![Wavelength Pearl: elbow effusion. The probe over the olecranon fossa, a normal collapsed posterior recess, and fluid lifting the posterior fat pad.](/learn/images/elbow-effusion-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Bend the elbow to about 90 degrees, with the forearm resting on the patient's lap or a pillow.
2. Place a high-frequency linear probe in long axis over the back of the elbow, above the olecranon, along the line of the humerus.
3. Find the triceps tendon, the posterior fat pad and the dip of the olecranon fossa.
4. Look for dark fluid in the fossa, lifting the fat pad off the bone.
5. Sweep in long axis and transverse, and compare with the other elbow.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** the fat pad sits down in the olecranon fossa, with no visible fluid.
- **Effusion:** dark fluid in the posterior recess lifts the fat pad. Fluid in the joint is abnormal.
- **After trauma:** an effusion strongly suggests an injury inside the joint, but the fracture line itself may not be visible on ultrasound.

> **The aha** If the posterior fat pad lifts, believe the effusion.

> **Pitfall** Ultrasound may find the effusion but miss the fracture. Do not use a scan which shows no fracture line to rule one out. Also, do not mistake the triceps tendon, an olecranon bursitis or anisotropy for joint fluid.

## Action

After trauma, an effusion means treating it as a possible occult fracture: immobilise, X-ray and arrange follow-up through your fracture pathway. Without trauma, a hot elbow with an effusion needs aspiration for possible septic arthritis.

?? Where does elbow joint fluid collect in the posterior view?
+ In the posterior recess, lifting the fat pad out of the olecranon fossa
- In the olecranon bursa
- Inside the triceps tendon
- Under the skin of the forearm
: Joint fluid fills the posterior recess and pushes the fat pad up.

?? A child fell on an outstretched hand. Ultrasound shows a lifted posterior fat pad, but no fracture line is seen. What do you do?
+ Treat it as a possible occult fracture: immobilise and follow the fracture pathway
- Reassure, because no fracture line was seen
- Aspirate the elbow immediately
- Discharge without follow-up
: An effusion after trauma suggests a fracture which ultrasound may not show.

?? Which position helps the posterior view?
+ The elbow bent to about 90 degrees
- The elbow fully straight and locked
- The arm above the head
- The forearm fully pronated and straight
: Flexion opens the olecranon fossa and lets fluid collect in the posterior recess.

?? What can be mistaken for an elbow effusion?
+ An olecranon bursitis or anisotropy of the triceps tendon
- The brachial artery
- Lung sliding
- The ulnar nerve only
: Find the layer: a bursitis is superficial, and an obliquely scanned tendon can look dark.

?? A febrile adult has a hot, swollen elbow with no injury and a posterior effusion. What next?
+ Aspiration for possible septic arthritis
- Reassurance
- An X-ray only
- Ice and rest
: Ultrasound finds the fluid. Only the fluid tells you whether it is infected.
