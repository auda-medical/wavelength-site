A middle-aged player feels a sudden pop or a kick in the back of the calf while pushing off. The calf swells and hurts. This is "tennis leg", a tear where the medial head of gastrocnemius meets soleus. It looks like a DVT on the outside, and ultrasound tells them apart.

![Wavelength Pearl: tennis leg. The normal thin fascial plane between medial gastrocnemius and soleus, torn fibres with fluid in the plane, and the length of the collection.](/learn/images/tennis-leg-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Lie the patient prone, or on their side, with the calf relaxed.
2. Place a high-frequency linear probe in long axis over the medial calf. Use the curvilinear probe for a large calf.
3. Find the medial head of gastrocnemius, soleus beneath it, and the thin bright plane between them.
4. Follow the tapering end of gastrocnemius down the calf, where tears usually happen.
5. Look for torn fibres and dark fluid in the plane, then measure how far the fluid runs.
6. Compare with the other calf.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** a thin, bright fascial plane between the two muscles, with no fluid.
- **Tennis leg:** disrupted fibres at the end of the medial gastrocnemius, with a dark collection tracking along the plane.
- **Large collections** can track a long way down the calf.

> **The aha** The fluid collects in the plane. Look between the muscles, not just inside them.

> **Pitfall** Do not stop at the muscle tear. A DVT can coexist with tennis leg, and a ruptured Baker's cyst or a DVT can mimic it. When the clinical picture suggests a clot, compress the veins.

## Action

Most tennis leg is managed with analgesia, early movement and physiotherapy. Assess for DVT when it is clinically indicated. Very large or expanding haematomas, or a tense calf, need senior review.

?? Where does the fluid of tennis leg usually collect?
+ In the plane between the medial gastrocnemius and soleus
- Inside the popliteal vein
- Behind the knee joint
- In the Achilles tendon
: The tear is at the junction of the medial gastrocnemius and soleus, and fluid tracks along the plane between them.

?? What is the normal appearance of the plane between the two muscles?
+ A thin, bright line with no fluid
- A wide black band
- A round, compressible vessel
- A bright cortex with a shadow
: Fluid in the plane is abnormal.

?? Which condition can coexist with tennis leg and should be assessed when indicated?
+ Deep vein thrombosis
- Pneumothorax
- Renal colic
- Appendicitis
: The two can occur together, and one can be mistaken for the other.

?? What else mimics tennis leg?
+ A ruptured Baker's cyst
- A pleural effusion
- An ovarian cyst
- A pericardial effusion
: Fluid from a ruptured Baker's cyst can track down the calf and cause similar pain and swelling.

?? Why compare with the other calf?
+ The other side shows what is normal for this patient
- It doubles the diagnostic accuracy automatically
- It is needed for Doppler to work
- It replaces the clinical examination
: A side-to-side difference makes a subtle collection easier to trust.
