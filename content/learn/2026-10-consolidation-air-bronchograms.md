Consolidated lung looks like liver on ultrasound: solid, grey and textured. Inside it, bright white dots and lines show air trapped in the airways. These are air bronchograms. One still frame shows they are there. A few seconds of breathing tells you more.

![Wavelength Pearl: consolidation. Dynamic air bronchograms move with inspiration and support pneumonia.](/learn/images/consolidation-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear probe for the bases and deeper consolidation, or the linear probe for small areas near the pleura.
2. Scan the area of concern in both planes, with the marker to the head in long axis.
3. Find the tissue-like lung. Its deep edge is often ragged where it meets aerated lung (the shred sign).
4. Hold still and watch the bright air bronchograms through two or three breaths.

## Dynamic or static

- **Dynamic air bronchograms.** The bright dots move outwards, away from the centre, as the patient breathes in. Air is still flowing through an open airway. This strongly supports pneumonia.
- **Static air bronchograms.** The bright dots stay still. The airway is blocked, so air does not move. This is common in resorptive atelectasis, from a mucus plug or an obstructed bronchus, and also seen in some pneumonias.

> **The aha** Don't just see air bronchograms. Watch them breathe.

> **Pitfall** Static air bronchograms do not exclude pneumonia. Use the clinical picture and the rest of the lung scan.

## Other clues

- **Fluid bronchograms**, black branching tubes inside consolidation, suggest fluid-filled airways behind an obstruction. Colour Doppler shows they carry no flow, unlike vessels.
- A **parapneumonic effusion** next to consolidation supports infection.
- Compare both sides, and look for B-lines in the surrounding lung.

?? Bright air bronchograms move outwards with each breath in. What does this suggest?
+ Pneumonia with open airways
- Resorptive atelectasis
- Pleural effusion
- Pneumothorax
: Movement of air bronchograms with breathing shows air flowing through open airways into consolidated lung. This dynamic sign strongly supports pneumonia. In resorptive atelectasis the airway is blocked, so the bronchograms stay still. Effusion and pneumothorax do not show air bronchograms.

: Static air bronchograms mean air is not moving through the airway. This is common in resorptive atelectasis. Some pneumonias show static bronchograms too, so their presence does not exclude infection. Use the history, examination and the rest of the scan.

?? What does consolidated lung look like on ultrasound?
+ Solid, grey tissue similar to liver, often with a ragged deep edge
- A black, anechoic space above the diaphragm
- Horizontal A-lines with lung sliding
- Bright vertical lines from the pleura to the bottom of the screen
: Airless lung carries the beam like solid organ tissue, so it looks like liver, a pattern called hepatisation. Its deep edge, where it meets aerated lung, is often ragged (the shred sign). A black space above the diaphragm is an effusion. A-lines with sliding are normal aerated lung. Vertical bright lines are B-lines.

?? Black, branching tubes inside consolidated lung show no flow on colour Doppler. What are they?
+ Fluid bronchograms
- Pulmonary vessels
- Air bronchograms
- Pleural fluid
: Fluid-filled airways look like black branching tubes. Colour Doppler separates them from vessels, which fill with colour. Fluid bronchograms suggest an obstructed airway, as in post-obstructive pneumonia. Air bronchograms are bright, not black.

?? Which probe suits a small area of consolidation just under the pleura?
+ Linear
- Phased array
- Curvilinear only
- Endocavity
: The linear probe gives high resolution near the surface, so it shows small subpleural consolidations and the pleural line in detail. Use the curvilinear probe for deeper or larger areas and the lung bases.
