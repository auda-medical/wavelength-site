Ultrasound finds even small volumes of ascites, and it shows what the fluid looks like. Clear, free fluid fits the common causes: cirrhosis, heart failure and low albumin. Fluid with echoes, strands or separate pockets has a different list: infection from a perforation, blood, cancer and tuberculosis. The appearance guides the next test. It does not replace it.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear probe with the patient supine.
2. Look in the dependent places first: around the liver and in Morison's pouch, around the spleen, both flanks and the pelvis.
3. In each pocket, turn up the gain a little and look for echoes, strands and walls.
4. Watch for movement: free fluid shifts with position and bowel floats in it.
5. Look at the liver surface, the spleen and the bowel wall for a cause.

## Reading the view

- **Simple ascites:** black fluid with no echoes, free between bowel loops, which float centrally.
- **Complex ascites:** low-level echoes, debris or fine strands. Think of infection, blood or malignancy.
- **Loculated ascites:** separate pockets divided by septations, which do not move freely. Think of infection, tuberculosis, malignancy or previous surgery.
- **Clues to a cause:** a small, nodular liver suggests cirrhosis; a thick or matted omentum suggests malignancy or tuberculosis.

> **The aha** Black is simple. Debris changes the story.

> **Pitfall** Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis usually looks simple. Floating bowel and omentum can also mimic septations, so sweep through the pocket and watch it move.

## Action

New ascites, and ascites in anyone admitted to hospital, needs a diagnostic tap, whatever it looks like. Complex or loculated fluid in an unwell patient needs senior review, cross-sectional imaging and a search for the source. See the [paracentesis pearl](/learn/paracentesis-ultrasound/) for choosing a safe pocket.

?? What does simple ascites look like?
+ Black fluid with no internal echoes, free between floating bowel loops
- Fluid full of strands and septations
- A thick-walled collection
- Bright fluid with shadowing
: Simple ascites is anechoic and moves freely.

?? A patient with cirrhosis has fever and simple-looking ascites. What next?
+ Diagnostic tap, because spontaneous bacterial peritonitis usually looks simple
- No tap is needed
- Repeat the scan next week
- Discharge
: The appearance cannot exclude infection.

?? Ascites with septations and separate pockets suggests what?
+ Infection, tuberculosis, malignancy or previous surgery
- Uncomplicated cirrhosis only
- Normal fluid
- Dehydration
: Loculation suggests an inflammatory or malignant process.

?? What can mimic septations in ascites?
+ Floating bowel and omentum
- The liver capsule
- The gallbladder
- The aorta
: Sweep through and watch: bowel and omentum move and have their own structure.

?? Where do you look first for a small volume of ascites?
+ The dependent spaces: Morison's pouch, around the spleen and the pelvis
- The epigastrium
- The anterior abdominal wall only
- The chest
: Small volumes collect in the dependent spaces.
