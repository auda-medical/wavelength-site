A painful lump in the neck is a common emergency presentation, in children and adults. The question is whether it is an inflamed lymph node, which settles with treatment, or a collection, which may need drainage. A linear probe answers that quickly for superficial lumps. The same scan can raise the possibility of something more serious.

![Wavelength Pearl: neck lump. An oval node with a bright hilum, and an abscess with complex fluid, an irregular wall and no hilum.](/learn/images/neck-lump-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use a high-frequency linear probe. Sit the patient up with the neck turned slightly away.
2. Find the lump and the landmarks around it: the sternocleidomastoid muscle, the carotid artery and the internal jugular vein.
3. Scan the lump in long and short axis. Measure it in three directions.
4. Look for a central bright hilum, the shape, and the texture inside.
5. Add colour Doppler: a node usually has flow entering through the hilum.
6. Press gently. Pus often swirls.

## Reading the view

- **Reactive node:** oval, smooth, with a dark rim of cortex around a bright central hilum, and flow entering at the hilum.
- **Abscess:** round or irregular, with no hilum, complex fluid with debris, posterior enhancement and no flow in the centre. The contents may swirl with pressure.
- **Suppurative node:** a node starting to liquefy, with a dark centre and a lost hilum. Common in children.
- **Worrying node:** round, with no hilum, or with a dark or cystic centre in an adult. Think of a metastatic or tuberculous node.

> **The aha** Find the hilum before you call it pus.

> **Pitfall** Necrotic nodes from cancer or tuberculosis can look just like an abscess. A cystic neck lump in an adult is a cancer until proven otherwise. Ultrasound sees only superficial lumps: deep neck infections need CT.

## Action

Stridor, trismus, drooling or difficulty swallowing need urgent senior and ENT review, whatever the scan shows. A collection may need drainage by ENT or under ultrasound guidance. A persistent unexplained neck lump, especially in an adult, needs referral on your local urgent suspected cancer pathway. See the [abscess pearl](/learn/abscess-ultrasound/) for the swirl sign.

?? What feature most supports a reactive lymph node?
+ An oval shape with a bright central hilum
- A round shape with no hilum
- Complex fluid with posterior enhancement
- Swirling contents with pressure
: The fatty hilum is the hallmark of a normal or reactive node.

?? A round neck lump has complex fluid, no hilum and posterior enhancement. What is likely?
+ An abscess or a liquefying node
- A normal node
- The carotid artery
- A thyroid nodule
: A collection lacks a hilum and enhances behind.

?? A 55-year-old has a painless cystic neck lump. What should you consider?
+ A metastatic node, so refer on the urgent suspected cancer pathway
- A simple abscess needing antibiotics only
- A normal finding
- A branchial cyst only, with no follow-up
: Cystic nodes in adults can be metastases, for example from throat cancers.

?? A child with a neck swelling has drooling and difficulty swallowing. What next?
+ Urgent senior and ENT review: a deep neck infection is possible
- Discharge with oral antibiotics
- Ultrasound only and review in a week
- Reassure: it is a reactive node
: These symptoms suggest a deep or airway-threatening infection, which ultrasound cannot exclude.

?? What does colour Doppler usually show in a reactive node?
+ Flow entering through the hilum
- No flow anywhere
- Flow only at the edge, never centrally
- Aliasing throughout
: Hilar flow is typical of a benign node.
