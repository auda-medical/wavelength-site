A cannula that gives a flash and flushes easily may still lie outside the vein. A cannula placed in a difficult patient, or in a vein that you found with ultrasound, is the one you most need to trust. Watching a small flush on the screen answers the question in seconds, before you run a drug through it.

![Wavelength Pearl: flush test. A small saline flush should fill the vein lumen, not the soft tissue.](/learn/images/iv-flush-test-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Hold a high-frequency linear probe over the cannulated vein, just proximal to the catheter tip. Find the vein and the cannula tip before you flush.
2. Check that you are looking at a vein: round or tubular, compressible and not pulsatile.
3. Flush a small volume of saline through the cannula while you watch the vein.
4. Look inside the lumen. A swirl of bright echoes, flowing away from the tip, means the fluid is in the vein. The vein may briefly distend.
5. If the lumen stays empty and fluid collects in the soft tissue, the cannula is not in the vein. Stop using it.

## Reading the view

- **Intraluminal flush.** A bright swirl of microbubbles in the black lumen, which stays within the vein.
- **Tissue infiltration.** A dark or mixed pool of fluid in the subcutaneous tissue, with the vein separate and unchanged.

> **The aha** A tiny flush should expand the lumen, not the tissue. The vein should answer, not the fat.

> **Pitfall** If fluid collects in the soft tissue, stop using the cannula.

## What it does not replace

The flush test adds to the usual checks and does not replace them. Look for blood return, ease of flushing, pain and swelling. Before you infuse an irritant or a vesicant drug, follow your trust's policy and confirm placement with extra care. A swirl in an artery can look similar, so confirm that the vessel is a vein first, by compression.

The evidence for ultrasound confirmation of a flush in adults is limited. Studies exist in specific groups, such as children, using other ultrasound methods. Treat the flush test as a practical bedside check.

?? What should a true intraluminal flush look like on the screen?
+ A swirl of bright echoes inside the lumen, which may briefly distend the vein
- Dark fluid collecting in the subcutaneous tissue
- A bright line along the skin surface
- No change at all in the vein
: Saline with tiny bubbles scatters sound, so it looks like a bright swirl. Seeing it stay within the lumen and move along the vein shows the tip is in the vein.

?? You flush and see fluid collect in the soft tissue while the vein stays empty. What do you do?
+ Stop using the cannula
- Flush harder
- Use it for a short infusion only
- Leave it for the next shift to review
: Fluid in the tissue means the cannula is not in the vein. Using it risks infiltration of whatever you infuse.

?? Where do you place the probe for a flush test?
+ Over the cannulated vein, just proximal to the catheter tip
- Over the artery on the other side of the arm
- On the hand beyond the cannula
- Over the tourniquet
: You want the lumen and the catheter tip in view, so the swirl can be seen entering the vein.

?? Why confirm the vessel is a vein before you rely on a flush swirl?
+ A swirl in an artery can look similar, and compression separates the two
- Arteries never show a swirl
- Veins never show a swirl
- Colour Doppler is always unreliable
: A vein collapses with gentle pressure and an artery does not. Confirm the vessel first.
