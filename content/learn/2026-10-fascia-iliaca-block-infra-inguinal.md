A fractured neck of femur hurts with every movement, and older patients tolerate opioids poorly. The fascia iliaca block places a larger volume of local anaesthetic under fascia iliaca, away from the nerves, and relies on spread along the plane to reach the femoral nerve and, less reliably, the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve. UK hip fracture guidance supports a nerve block on arrival, and a Cochrane review found nerve blocks reduce pain on movement and the risk of delirium and chest infection.

## Before you block

1. Record the neurovascular examination and look for other injuries.
2. Record consent. Many patients with a hip fracture have delirium or dementia. Follow your trust's process for a patient who lacks capacity.
3. Calculate the maximum safe dose for the patient's weight. This block uses 30 to 40 mL, so the dose decides the concentration.
4. Attach monitoring and know where the lipid emulsion is.

## The scan

1. Lie the patient supine.
2. Place the linear probe transversely just below the inguinal ligament and find the femoral artery, then the femoral nerve lateral to it.
3. Slide lateral until you see the bright line of fascia iliaca lying on iliacus, about a third of the way from the artery to the anterior superior iliac spine.
4. Insert the needle in plane from lateral to medial, aiming for the plane under fascia iliaca, well away from the nerve.
5. Inject 2 to 3 mL and watch the fluid lift the fascia off iliacus. Then inject the rest, watching the fluid spread medially towards the nerve and laterally.

## Reading the view

- **Fascia iliaca.** A bright line on top of iliacus.
- **Iliacus.** Dark, striated muscle under the fascia.
- **Correct spread.** A dark lens of fluid separating fascia iliaca from iliacus and spreading along it.
- **Wrong layer.** Fluid above the fascia, in the fat or around sartorius, does not reach the nerves.

> **The aha** This is a volume block. You place the fluid in the right plane, not on the nerve, and let the plane carry it.

> **Pitfall** If the fluid gathers only around the femoral nerve, you have performed a femoral nerve block. If it stays above the fascia, you have missed. Reposition before you give the volume.

?? Where does the needle tip sit in an infra-inguinal fascia iliaca block?
+ Under fascia iliaca, on iliacus, lateral to the femoral nerve
- Inside the femoral nerve sheath
- Between the femoral artery and vein
- In the subcutaneous fat over sartorius
: The target is the plane between fascia iliaca and iliacus, lateral to the nerve. The volume then spreads along the plane.

?? Why is the dose the main safety concern in this block?
+ It needs 30 to 40 mL, which brings the total dose close to the maximum for a frail patient
- The needle passes through the femoral artery
- The block is always intravascular
- Local anaesthetic does not spread in this plane
: The block relies on volume. Calculate the maximum safe dose for the patient's weight, and choose a concentration which allows the volume.

?? Which nerve does an infra-inguinal fascia iliaca block reach least reliably?
+ The obturator nerve
- The femoral nerve
- The nerve to the anterior thigh muscles
- The branches to the anterior hip capsule from the femoral nerve
: The femoral nerve is reached reliably and the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve often. Spread to the obturator nerve is inconsistent.

?? How does correct spread look on ultrasound?
+ Fluid separating fascia iliaca from iliacus and spreading along the plane
- A bright swelling within iliacus
- Fluid around sartorius above the fascia
- Fluid inside the femoral artery
: Fluid in the right plane lifts the fascia off the muscle and spreads. Swelling within the muscle or above the fascia means the tip is in the wrong layer.

?? What did a Cochrane review find for nerve blocks in hip fracture?
+ Less pain on movement and a lower risk of delirium and chest infection
- No benefit over paracetamol alone
- A higher rate of falls in the emergency department
- Benefit only when given after surgery
: The review found less pain on movement within 30 minutes, and a lower risk of acute confusion and chest infection.
