Every ultrasound image is a slice through a three-dimensional body. A round structure can be a tube cut across; a dark gap in a tendon can be anisotropy; a fluid pocket can be a fold of bowel. Turning the probe through 90 degrees answers most of these questions in seconds.

![Wavelength Pearl: two planes. A fluid collection in long axis and short axis, and an apparent lesion that is an artefact in one plane.](/learn/images/two-planes-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Find the structure and centre it on the screen.
2. Line the probe up with its long axis and record that view.
3. Keep it centred and rotate the probe 90 degrees for the short axis.
4. Sweep through it in both planes, as in the [sweep, fan, slide pearl](/learn/ultrasound-probe-movements-sweep-fan/).
5. Measure in both planes when size matters.

## Reading the view

- **Vessel:** a tube in long axis, a circle in short axis, continuous as you sweep.
- **Collection:** a defined pocket in both planes, often oval in one and round in the other.
- **Artefact or partial volume:** looks convincing in one plane and disappears or changes shape in the other.
- **Anisotropy:** a dark patch in a tendon that changes with probe angle; see the [anisotropy pearl](/learn/tendon-tear-vs-anisotropy/).

> **The aha** If it matters, prove it in two planes.

> **Pitfall** Anisotropy, partial-volume effects and side-lobe artefacts can create or hide a finding in a single plane. Do not act on a finding you have seen only once.

## Action

Record important findings in both planes, with measurements in both when size matters. Before any needle procedure, check the target and the path in both planes.

?? Why scan in two planes?
+ A real finding makes anatomical sense in both views; an artefact usually does not
- It doubles the image brightness
- It is only needed for the heart
- It replaces colour Doppler
: Rotating 90 degrees tests whether the finding is real.

?? A vessel in short axis looks like what?
+ A circle
- A long tube
- A bright line with a shadow
- A comet tail
: A tube cut across is round.

?? A dark patch in a tendon disappears when you change the probe angle. What is it likely to be?
+ Anisotropy
- A complete tear
- A fracture
- An abscess
: Anisotropy changes with angle; a real tear persists in both planes and all angles.

?? How much do you rotate the probe for the second plane?
+ About 90 degrees
- About 10 degrees
- 180 degrees
- Not at all
: Long and short axis are at right angles.

?? A suspected collection is seen in one plane only. What should you do?
+ Rotate and sweep to confirm it in the second plane before acting
- Drain it straight away
- Report it as definite
- Ignore it
: Do not act on a finding seen in one plane only.
