A swollen, red point of the elbow could be a bursitis, cellulitis, an abscess or a joint effusion. They sit in different layers, and the layer changes what you do next. The olecranon bursa lies just under the skin, on top of the olecranon and the triceps insertion. The elbow joint lies deeper.

![Wavelength Pearl: olecranon bursa. A collapsed normal bursa, a simple bursitis with clear fluid, and a complex bursitis with debris and surrounding hyperaemia.](/learn/images/olecranon-bursitis-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Rest the elbow bent on a pillow. Use a high-frequency linear probe with plenty of gel and almost no pressure, or the bursa empties under the probe.
2. Scan over the point of the elbow in long axis and transverse.
3. Name the layers from the top: skin and fat, the bursa, the triceps tendon and the bright cortex of the olecranon.
4. Decide where any fluid is, then what it looks like.
5. Look deeper, at the posterior recess of the joint, to make sure the joint itself is not full of fluid.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** the bursa is collapsed, with little or no visible fluid.
- **Simple bursitis:** clear, dark fluid in the bursa, above the triceps tendon.
- **Complex bursitis:** debris, septations or a thick wall, often with increased blood flow around it on power Doppler. Infection is more likely, but this appearance is not proof.
- **Cellulitis:** swollen, cobblestoned fat without a collection in the bursa.

> **The aha** First find the layer. Then name the fluid.

> **Pitfall** Ultrasound cannot tell a septic bursitis from a sterile one. A hot, painful bursa in an unwell patient needs aspiration and treatment for infection, whatever the image looks like.

## Action

If infection is possible, aspirate under ultrasound guidance and send the fluid for microscopy, culture and crystals. If the fluid is in the joint, not the bursa, treat it as a possible septic arthritis.

?? Where does olecranon bursitis sit?
+ Just under the skin, on top of the olecranon and the triceps insertion
- Inside the elbow joint
- Deep to the olecranon cortex
- Within the triceps muscle belly
: The bursa is superficial. A joint effusion sits deeper, in the posterior recess.

?? Why use very little probe pressure?
+ Pressure can empty the bursa and hide the fluid
- Pressure creates colour aliasing
- The olecranon breaks easily
- It makes the tendon look torn
: A small superficial collection is easily pushed aside by the probe.

?? What does a complex bursa with debris and surrounding hyperaemia mean?
+ Infection is more likely, but the image cannot prove or exclude it
- It is definitely septic
- It is definitely sterile
- It is a joint effusion
: The appearance raises suspicion. Aspiration and the clinical picture decide.

?? Swollen, cobblestoned fat with no collection suggests what?
+ Cellulitis
- Simple bursitis
- A joint effusion
- A fracture
: Cellulitis swells the fat without a defined fluid collection.

?? Fluid is found deep in the posterior recess of the joint rather than in the bursa. What does this change?
+ It is a joint effusion, so think about septic arthritis and joint aspiration
- Nothing, it is still bursitis
- It can be ignored
- It confirms gout
: The layer decides the diagnosis and the procedure.
