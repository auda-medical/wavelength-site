Infection inside the flexor tendon sheath spreads quickly along the finger and into the palm, and can destroy the tendon. The classic Kanavel signs are a finger held slightly bent, swelling of the whole finger, tenderness along the sheath, and pain on passive straightening. Ultrasound adds a look inside the sheath, and shows whether a collection is in the sheath or above it.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Rest the hand palm up. Use a high-frequency linear probe with plenty of gel and very little pressure.
2. Find the flexor tendon on the front of the finger, a bright fibrillar band over the phalanges.
3. Follow it from the fingertip into the palm in long axis, then in short axis.
4. Look for fluid around the tendon, and its thickness and texture.
5. Add colour Doppler around the sheath.
6. Compare with the same tendon in a neighbouring finger.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** a bright tendon in a thin sheath, with little or no fluid around it.
- **Tenosynovitis:** fluid surrounding the tendon within a distended, often thickened sheath, with increased flow around it and tenderness under the probe.
- **Superficial collection:** fluid above the sheath, under the skin, with the tendon and sheath normal beneath it.
- **Cellulitis:** swollen, cobblestoned tissue without fluid around the tendon.

> **The aha** Follow the tendon. Look for fluid around it.

> **Pitfall** A trace of fluid in the sheath can be normal, and early infection may show very little. The Kanavel signs and the history decide; a normal scan does not exclude flexor tenosynovitis.

## Action

Suspected flexor tenosynovitis needs urgent hand surgery review, intravenous antibiotics, elevation and splinting, whatever the scan shows; many patients need surgical washout. Ask about penetrating injuries and bites, which are high risk.

?? Which finding supports flexor tenosynovitis?
+ Fluid surrounding the flexor tendon within a distended sheath
- A thin tendon with no fluid
- Fluid only under the skin above a normal sheath
- A bright cortex with a shadow
: The fluid is inside the sheath, around the tendon.

?? Which Kanavel sign is pain on passive straightening of the finger?
+ Pain on passive extension
- Fusiform swelling
- A flexed resting position
- Tenderness along the sheath
: Pain on passive extension is often the most useful sign.

?? A patient has all four Kanavel signs and the scan shows little fluid. What next?
+ Urgent hand surgery review: the scan does not exclude infection
- Discharge with oral antibiotics
- Reassure: the scan is normal
- Repeat the scan next week
: Clinical signs decide; early infection may show little fluid.

?? Fluid above a normal sheath, under the skin, suggests what?
+ A superficial collection rather than tenosynovitis
- Flexor tenosynovitis
- A tendon rupture
- A fracture
: Find the layer.

?? Why compare with a neighbouring finger?
+ To judge whether the fluid and sheath are abnormal
- To measure blood pressure
- It is never useful
- To check the nail
: A small amount of sheath fluid can be normal.
