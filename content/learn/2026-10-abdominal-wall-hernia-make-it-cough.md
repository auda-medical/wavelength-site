A tender lump in the abdominal wall, or by a scar, raises the question of a hernia and whether it is stuck. A still image in a supine patient often shows nothing. Movement shows the hernia.

![Wavelength Pearl: abdominal wall hernia. Scan at rest, then during cough or Valsalva, and check reducibility.](/learn/images/hernia-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use the linear probe, or the curvilinear probe in a larger patient.
2. Place the probe over the lump or the most tender point, across the abdominal wall layers.
3. Identify skin, fat, the anterior rectus sheath and the muscle, then follow the fascia looking for a break.
4. Ask the patient to cough or bear down, and watch the defect.
5. If the scan is still negative, stand the patient up and repeat.
6. Press gently with the probe and watch whether the contents go back.

## Reading the view

- **Defect:** a break in the bright fascial line.
- **Contents:** fat looks bright and compressible. Bowel shows a layered wall, fluid or gas, and may move.
- **Reducible:** contents slide back through the defect at rest or with gentle pressure.
- **Incarcerated:** contents stay in the sac and will not reduce.
- **Concern for strangulation:** a non-reducible sac with thick-walled bowel, fluid in the sac, no peristalsis, or no flow in the wall on colour Doppler, together with pain and tenderness.

> **The aha** If the lump hides at rest, make it move.

> **Pitfall** A negative supine scan at rest does not exclude a small or reducible hernia. A lipoma, a lymph node, a haematoma or a collection also cause a lump. Look for a fascial defect and movement before you call a hernia.

## What to do next

A painful, non-reducible hernia, especially with vomiting or signs of obstruction, needs urgent surgical review. A reducible hernia with no red flags can go through the routine surgical pathway.

?? What is the most useful step when a suspected hernia is not visible in a supine patient at rest?
+ Repeat during a cough or strain, and with the patient standing
- Press hard with the probe
- Switch to the phased-array probe
- Report no hernia
: Raised abdominal pressure pushes contents through the defect. Many small hernias only appear with cough, Valsalva or standing.

?? Which finding defines an incarcerated hernia?
+ Contents that stay in the sac and do not reduce
- A fascial defect under 1 cm
- Fat in the sac
- Contents that slide back at rest
: Incarceration means the contents are trapped. Pain, tenderness and bowel wall changes raise concern for strangulation.

?? Which finding in a non-reducible hernia raises concern for strangulation?
+ Thick-walled bowel with no flow in its wall and fluid in the sac
- Bright, compressible fat
- A sac that empties with gentle pressure
- A defect visible only on coughing
: Loss of blood flow, wall thickening, fluid in the sac and absent peristalsis suggest compromised bowel. The patient needs urgent surgical review.
