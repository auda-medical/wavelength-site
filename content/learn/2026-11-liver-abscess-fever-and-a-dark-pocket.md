A liver abscess presents with fever and right upper quadrant pain, often with few other clues, and is easily mistaken for gallbladder disease. Most are pyogenic, spreading from the biliary tree or from an abdominal source such as appendicitis or diverticulitis. An amoebic abscess can appear months or years after travel. A scan aimed only at the [gallbladder](/learn/gallbladder-ultrasound/) can miss it, so sweep the whole liver.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear probe between the lower right ribs and under the costal margin.
2. Sweep the whole liver through each window, including the dome and the left lobe.
3. Look at any lesion in two planes, and note its wall, its contents and the tissue behind it.
4. Add colour Doppler.
5. Look at the gallbladder and the bile ducts for a source, and above the diaphragm for a right pleural effusion.

## Reading the view

- **Normal liver:** an even, fine texture with vessels running through it.
- **Abscess:** a round or irregular dark lesion with debris and a thick, irregular wall, and brighter tissue behind it.
- **Colour Doppler:** no flow inside the pocket, though some flow may lie around it.
- **Gas in the abscess:** bright echoes with dirty shadowing, which is a sign of a more severe infection.

> **The aha** Fever and right upper quadrant pain: sweep the whole liver.

> **Pitfall** An early abscess can look solid and bright, and a normal scan does not exclude it. Tumours, complicated cysts and haematomas can mimic an abscess. Ultrasound cannot tell a pyogenic abscess from an amoebic one, or reliably from a hydatid cyst.

## Action

Take blood cultures and start antibiotics according to local guidance. Request CT and speak to radiology about guided drainage. Ask about travel, because amoebic abscesses are treated differently and hydatid disease changes the plan: send serology and take advice. Never needle a liver lesion in the emergency department.

?? Which presentation should make you sweep the whole liver?
+ Fever and right upper quadrant pain, with a gallbladder that does not explain them
- A painless ankle swelling
- Chest pain on deep inspiration alone
- A week of mild knee pain
: The gallbladder may be normal. The answer may lie elsewhere in the liver.

?? Which appearance suggests a liver abscess?
+ A dark lesion with debris, no internal flow and bright tissue behind it
- An even, fine liver texture
- A thin-walled black cyst with nothing in it and no symptoms
- A bright, curved echo in the gallbladder with a shadow
: The bright tissue behind it shows that sound passes easily through the fluid.

?? The scan looks normal, but the patient remains febrile with right upper quadrant pain. What follows?
+ Further imaging, because a normal scan does not exclude an abscess
- Discharge, because the scan was normal
- Stop the blood cultures
- Scan again next month
: Early abscesses can look solid and bright, and parts of the liver can be hard to see.

?? Why ask about travel?
+ Amoebic abscess and hydatid disease change the management
- It decides which probe you use
- It does not change anything
- Only because of malaria prophylaxis
: Amoebic abscesses are usually treated with medicine, and a hydatid cyst must not be needled without specialist advice.

?? What should the emergency clinician not do with a suspected liver abscess?
+ Put a needle into it in the emergency department
- Take blood cultures
- Speak to radiology
- Request CT
: Drainage is a guided procedure for radiology, after CT and antibiotics.
