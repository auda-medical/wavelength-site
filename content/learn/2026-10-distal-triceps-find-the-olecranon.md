Distal triceps rupture is uncommon and often missed. It follows a fall onto an outstretched hand, a direct blow, or heavy lifting, and is more likely with steroid use or kidney disease. Patients may still extend the elbow with gravity, so the weakness is easy to overlook. Like the [distal biceps](/learn/distal-biceps-rupture-ultrasound/), the tendon is superficial and easy to follow to its insertion.

![Wavelength Pearl: distal triceps. The normal tendon inserting on the olecranon, a rupture with a gap and a retracted tendon, and a small avulsion fragment.](/learn/images/distal-triceps-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Rest the arm across the chest or on a pillow with the elbow bent, so the back of the elbow faces you.
2. Use a high-frequency linear probe in long axis over the back of the elbow, centred on the olecranon.
3. Follow the triceps tendon from the muscle down to its insertion on the olecranon.
4. Turn to short axis and sweep across the tendon.
5. Gently flex and extend the elbow while you watch, and compare with the other side.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** a bright, fibrillar tendon inserting onto the bright olecranon cortex.
- **Complete tear:** a dark gap at the insertion, with the tendon end pulled back up the arm.
- **Avulsion:** a small bright fragment with a shadow, lying above the olecranon within the tendon end. The same flake may show on a lateral X-ray.
- **Partial tear:** some intact fibres, often the deep or lateral part, with a dark defect.

> **The aha** Follow the fibres right to the bone.

> **Pitfall** Swelling, an olecranon bursitis or anisotropy can hide or mimic a tear. A tiny avulsion fragment may be the only bony clue. Scan in two planes and test resisted extension.

## Action

Test extension against gravity and against resistance. Suspected complete rupture needs urgent orthopaedic review, because early repair gives the best results. A lateral elbow X-ray looks for the avulsion flake and other injuries.

?? Where does the triceps tendon insert?
+ The olecranon of the ulna
- The radial head
- The medial epicondyle
- The coronoid process
: Follow the tendon to the olecranon.

?? What is a "flake" on the X-ray in a triceps injury?
+ A small bone fragment pulled off the olecranon by the tendon
- A normal ossification centre in every adult
- A foreign body
- A loose cartilage body
: An avulsion fragment suggests a tendon tear.

?? Why can a triceps rupture be missed?
+ The patient may still extend the elbow with gravity
- It always causes no pain
- Ultrasound cannot see the elbow
- It only affects children
: Test extension against resistance.

?? Which finding suggests a complete tear?
+ A gap at the insertion with the tendon end pulled back
- A continuous fibrillar tendon
- Fluid only in the olecranon bursa
- A smooth olecranon cortex
: The tendon is absent from the bone.

?? What can mimic or hide a triceps tear?
+ Swelling, olecranon bursitis and anisotropy
- The ulnar nerve only
- Colour Doppler
- A normal fat pad
: Scan in two planes and compare with the other side.
