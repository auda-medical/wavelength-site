An ultrasound image is a slice about a millimetre or two thick. A small collection, a stone, a fluid pocket or a foreign body can sit just beside it. A good scan is a survey through the whole target, and the saved image is the record at the end of it, not the scan itself.

![Wavelength Pearl: sweep, fan, slide. Three probe movements, and a collection missed on one frame but found after sweeping and fanning.](/learn/images/sweep-fan-slide-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The movements

1. **Sweep:** move the probe sideways across the target, keeping the same angle, to see neighbouring slices.
2. **Fan:** keep the probe still on the skin and tilt it through an arc, so the beam passes through a volume beneath one point. Useful between ribs or under a narrow window.
3. **Slide:** move the probe along the long axis of a structure to follow it, such as a vessel, a tendon or the bowel.
4. Add **rotation** to turn between long and short axis, as in the [two planes pearl](/learn/ultrasound-two-planes/).

## Using them

- Survey first, from one edge of the target to the other, before freezing anything.
- Move slowly: about a second for each centimetre lets the eye catch small findings.
- When you find something, sweep through it to define its edges, then record it in two planes.

> **The aha** A single saved frame is the end of the scan, not the scan itself.

> **Pitfall** Freezing too early misses small collections, stones, effusions and foreign bodies, and gives false reassurance. A normal frame is not a normal organ.

## Action

Make the survey a habit for every application. When you record a scan, note the views and the movement used, so the next person can repeat it.

?? What does sweeping do?
+ Moves the probe sideways across the target to see neighbouring slices
- Tilts the probe in one spot
- Rotates the probe 90 degrees
- Changes the gain
: Sweeping keeps the angle and moves the slice across the target.

?? What does fanning do?
+ Tilts the probe through an arc from one point on the skin
- Slides the probe along a vessel
- Moves the probe to the other side of the body
- Increases the depth
: Fanning surveys a volume beneath a narrow window.

?? Why is one normal frame not enough?
+ Pathology can sit a few millimetres outside the slice
- The frame is always blurred
- Ultrasound images are three-dimensional
- Saved images are deleted
: Each frame is a thin slice.

?? When should you freeze the image?
+ After surveying the whole target, to record the finding
- At the first view you obtain
- Before placing the probe
- Never
: The saved image is the record at the end of the scan.

?? You find a small collection. What next?
+ Sweep through it to define its edges, then confirm it in two planes
- Freeze immediately and stop
- Turn up the gain until it disappears
- Ignore it
: Define it fully before you act on it.
