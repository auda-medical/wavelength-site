Twinkle is a colour Doppler artefact behind a strongly reflecting, rough surface. It looks like a rapidly flickering red and blue tail, although nothing behind the target is moving. It can make a small stone easier to spot, but on its own it is not specific enough to be the diagnosis: in some studies around half of twinkling spots were not stones on CT.

## The scan

1. Scan the kidney in long axis and transverse on greyscale first.
2. Look for a bright focus with a clean shadow behind it.
3. Put a small colour box over it. Lower the colour scale enough to show twinkle without filling the kidney with noise.
4. Hold the probe still. A real twinkle tail stays anchored to the bright focus.
5. Look for obstruction: a dilated collecting system or ureter, and, where it helps, asymmetry of the ureteric jets in the bladder.

## Reading the clue

- **More convincing:** a bright focus, a clean shadow and an anchored twinkle in a place a stone can sit.
- **Less convincing:** twinkle with no matching focus, no shadow and no obstruction.
- Calcified vessels, calcification in the kidney tissue, strong interfaces and machine noise can twinkle too.

> **The aha** Twinkle should make you look harder on greyscale, not stop looking.

> **Pitfall** The colour settings change the artefact. No twinkle does not exclude a stone, and twinkle alone does not prove one.

## Action

Put the finding together with the symptoms, urine dip, kidney function, infection risk and the need for definitive imaging. Fever with an obstructed kidney is an emergency.

?? What is twinkle artefact?
+ Flickering colour behind a strong, rough reflector, with no real flow
- Blood flow through the stone
- Urine leaking from the kidney
- A spectral Doppler waveform
: It is an artefact made by the reflecting surface, not by moving blood.

?? Which combination is most convincing for a stone?
+ A bright focus, a clean shadow and an anchored twinkle in a likely place
- Twinkle anywhere, with no greyscale target
- Hydronephrosis with no symptoms
- Colour in a normal renal vessel
: Greyscale and colour findings which agree make the call more specific.

?? Why can twinkle alone mislead?
+ Calcification elsewhere and other strong interfaces can twinkle too
- Stones never twinkle
- Colour Doppler cannot show artefacts
- It happens only in the bladder
: CT comparison studies have found many false positives when twinkle is used alone.

?? Does an absent twinkle exclude a stone?
+ No
- Yes, completely
- Yes, if the kidney is a normal size
- Only after analgesia
: Whether a stone twinkles depends on its surface, the machine and the settings.

?? What makes an obstructed kidney time-critical?
+ Signs of infection or sepsis with the obstruction
- Twinkle without pain
- A single ureteric jet
- A simple renal cyst
: An infected, obstructed kidney needs urgent drainage and specialist care.
