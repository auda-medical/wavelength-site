A swollen wrist is common: after a fall, with gout or pseudogout, with inflammatory arthritis, and rarely with infection. The swelling on the surface does not show which layer it is in. Ultrasound does, in a minute, and it guides the needle if fluid needs to come out.

![Wavelength Pearl: wrist effusion. A flat dorsal recess, a distended recess with joint fluid, and a side-to-side comparison.](/learn/images/wrist-effusion-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Rest the hand palm down on a pillow, with the wrist slightly flexed.
2. Place a high-frequency linear probe in long axis over the back of the wrist, in line with the middle finger.
3. Find the distal radius, then the lunate and capitate below it. The dorsal recess lies over these bones, deep to the extensor tendons.
4. Scan in short axis across the wrist.
5. Compare with the other wrist if the finding is small.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** a thin or barely visible recess over the carpal bones.
- **Effusion:** a dark pocket lifting the recess between the bones and the tendons.
- **Tenosynovitis:** fluid around a tendon, which moves with the tendon when the fingers move.
- **Ganglion:** a well-defined dark cyst, often with a neck leading towards the joint.
- **Cellulitis:** swollen, cobblestoned tissue under the skin, with no pocket.

> **The aha** Find the dorsal recess before you chase the swelling.

> **Pitfall** Ultrasound shows fluid, not its cause. It cannot separate infection from gout, pseudogout or inflammatory arthritis, and a small effusion does not exclude infection. After trauma, an effusion may mean a fracture, including the scaphoid.

## Action

A hot wrist that might be infected needs aspiration, under ultrasound guidance if possible, and the fluid sent for microscopy, culture and crystals before antibiotics. After a fall, follow your scaphoid and wrist fracture pathways.

?? Where does a wrist effusion collect?
+ In the dorsal recess over the carpal bones, deep to the extensor tendons
- In the carpal tunnel only
- Superficial to the skin
- Inside the extensor tendons
: The dorsal recess is the most accessible pocket.

?? Fluid around a tendon which moves with the finger is what?
+ Tenosynovitis
- A joint effusion
- A ganglion
- Cellulitis
: Find the layer: tendon sheath or joint.

?? A well-defined dark cyst with a neck leading towards the joint suggests what?
+ A ganglion
- Septic arthritis
- A fracture
- A tendon tear
: Ganglia often connect to the joint or a tendon sheath.

?? A hot, swollen wrist has a small effusion. What next if infection is possible?
+ Aspirate and send the fluid before antibiotics
- Discharge because the effusion is small
- Start antibiotics without aspirating
- Repeat the scan next month
: A small effusion does not exclude infection.

?? An effusion after a fall onto the wrist could mean what?
+ A fracture, including the scaphoid
- Nothing important
- A ganglion
- Gout only
: Follow your wrist and scaphoid pathways.
