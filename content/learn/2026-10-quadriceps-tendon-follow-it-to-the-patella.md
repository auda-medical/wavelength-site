Quadriceps tendon rupture is often missed. Swelling hides the defect, the X-ray looks near normal, and a partial straight-leg raise reassures. Ultrasound shows the tendon directly, and a few seconds of movement settle most doubts.

![Wavelength Pearl: quadriceps tendon. Scan in long axis to the superior patella, look for a gap, and add gentle flexion.](/learn/images/quadriceps-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Lie the patient supine with the knee straight or slightly bent over a pillow.
2. Place the linear probe in long axis above the patella, marker to the head.
3. Find the bright, fibrillar quadriceps tendon and follow it down to where it inserts on the top of the patella.
4. Sweep from medial to lateral across the whole width of the tendon.
5. Gently bend the knee a little, if pain allows, and watch the insertion.
6. Scan the other knee with the same settings.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** parallel bright fibres run in a continuous band onto the superior patella.
- **Complete rupture:** a gap in the tendon, usually within 2 cm of the patella, filled with dark fluid or haematoma. The torn ends look wavy and retracted, and the gap widens as the knee bends.
- **Partial tear:** some fibres are lost, but part of the tendon stays continuous and the gap does not open through the full thickness.

> **The aha** Trust the gap more than the blur.

> **Pitfall** Anisotropy makes a normal tendon look dark when the beam hits it at an angle. Rock the probe heel to toe until the fibres light up, and compare with the other side, before you call a tear.

## What to do next

Suspected complete rupture needs urgent orthopaedic referral, because early repair gives the best results. Splint the knee straight. Check the X-ray for a low-lying patella or a bony avulsion. Also look at the patellar tendon below the patella, which tears in younger patients.

?? Where does a quadriceps tendon rupture usually occur?
+ Within about 2 cm of the superior pole of the patella
- At the tibial tuberosity
- In the middle of the quadriceps muscle
- At the inferior pole of the patella
: Most quadriceps tendon ruptures occur close to the top of the patella. A tear at the inferior pole involves the patellar tendon instead.

?? How do you make a quadriceps tendon tear easier to see?
+ Gently flex the knee and watch the gap widen
- Press hard over the patella
- Switch to colour Doppler
- Fully extend and lock the knee
: Gentle flexion pulls the torn ends apart, so a true full-thickness gap opens. A partial tear or anisotropy does not open.

?? The distal quadriceps tendon looks dark at one angle but fibrillar when you rock the probe. What is it?
+ Anisotropy
- A complete rupture
- A partial tear
- Tendon calcification
: Tendons look dark when the beam is not perpendicular to the fibres. Fibres that light up when you change the angle are intact.

?? An older patient fell, cannot straight-leg raise, and has a gap in the quadriceps tendon above the patella. What do you do?
+ Splint the knee straight and refer urgently to orthopaedics
- Discharge with physiotherapy
- Aspirate the knee
- Book a routine outpatient MRI
: A complete quadriceps rupture needs early surgical repair. Delay makes repair harder and outcomes worse.
