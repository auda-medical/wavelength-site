Hydronephrosis is dilatation of the kidney's collecting system, usually from obstruction further down. In renal colic it supports the diagnosis of an obstructing stone. In a febrile patient it raises concern for an infected, obstructed kidney. The skill is telling it apart from the black spaces which mimic it.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear probe with the patient supine.
2. Right kidney: mid to posterior axillary line, using the liver as a window, marker to the head.
3. Left kidney: sits higher and further back. Start in the posterior axillary line, higher on the flank, and use the spleen as a window. Put your knuckles on the bed.
4. Sweep through each kidney in long axis, then turn 90° and sweep in short axis.
5. Compare the two sides.
6. Scan the bladder. A full bladder causes mild dilatation on both sides. Rescan after the patient has voided.

## Reading the kidney

- **Normal.** A grey cortex around a bright, echogenic centre, the renal sinus. No black spaces inside the sinus.
- **Mild.** The renal pelvis is black and the calyces start to fill.
- **Moderate.** Black, rounded calyces join the pelvis, like a bunch of flowers on their stems.
- **Severe.** Large black spaces with a thin rim of cortex.

> **The aha** Hydro connects. Cysts do not. Follow each black space. If it joins the others and the renal pelvis, it is hydronephrosis.

## Mimics

- **Parapelvic cysts.** Round, separate, and not connected to each other or the pelvis.
- **Prominent renal sinus vessels.** Colour Doppler fills vessels with colour. A dilated collecting system stays black.
- **Extrarenal pelvis.** A renal pelvis lying outside the kidney looks dilated, while the calyces stay normal.

> **Pitfall** No hydronephrosis does not exclude a ureteric stone. Early obstruction, a small stone or dehydration all limit dilatation.

## The older patient with renal colic

A patient over 50 with first-time renal colic needs their aorta scanned. A leaking abdominal aortic aneurysm presents with flank pain and blood in the urine. Thirty seconds on the aorta prevents a fatal miss.

?? Which finding best separates hydronephrosis from parapelvic cysts?
+ The black spaces connect with each other and with the renal pelvis
- The black spaces are larger than 1 cm
- The kidney is bigger than the other side
- The black spaces have bright walls
: In hydronephrosis the dilated calyces drain into the renal pelvis, so the black spaces join up. Parapelvic cysts sit separately, each with its own round outline. Size and kidney length vary in both conditions. Bright walls do not help tell them apart.

?? Where do you start scanning for the left kidney?
+ Higher and further back than the right, in the posterior axillary line
- The same position as the right kidney, on the left side
- The left iliac fossa
- The mid-clavicular line below the costal margin
: The left kidney sits higher and more posterior than the right, and the spleen gives a smaller window than the liver. Start in the posterior axillary line, higher on the flank, with your knuckles on the bed. Mirroring the right side often shows only bowel gas.

?? You see bilateral mild hydronephrosis in a patient with a full bladder. What is the best next step?
+ Rescan both kidneys after the patient has voided
- Refer urgently for bilateral obstruction
- Request a CT urogram
- Diagnose bilateral ureteric stones
: A full bladder raises the pressure in the collecting systems and causes mild dilatation on both sides. Rescan after voiding. If the dilatation resolves, it was physiological. Bilateral obstruction is uncommon and needs a cause such as urinary retention, which a bladder scan shows.

?? A dilated-looking renal sinus fills with colour on colour Doppler. What does this show?
+ Prominent renal sinus vessels, not hydronephrosis
- Severe hydronephrosis
- An infected, obstructed kidney
- A parapelvic cyst
: A dilated collecting system contains urine, which does not flow enough to show colour. Black spaces which fill with colour are vessels. Colour Doppler is the quickest way to separate the two. An infected kidney and a cyst both stay black on colour.

?? A 68-year-old presents with first-time left renal colic. The left kidney shows mild hydronephrosis. What is the next scan?
+ Scan the abdominal aorta
- Scan the gallbladder
- Repeat the renal scan in 24 hours
- Scan the right kidney only
: A leaking abdominal aortic aneurysm mimics renal colic, with flank pain and blood in the urine. Over 50 with first-time renal colic, scan the aorta before accepting the diagnosis. Mild hydronephrosis does not exclude an aneurysm. Comparing both kidneys is good practice, but the aorta is the scan which prevents a fatal miss.
