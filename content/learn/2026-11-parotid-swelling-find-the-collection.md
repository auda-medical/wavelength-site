Acute suppurative parotitis is a bacterial infection of the parotid gland, usually Staphylococcus aureus, in people who are dehydrated, elderly, recovering from surgery or with poor oral hygiene. The gland becomes hot, tender and swollen, and pus may appear at the opening of the parotid duct inside the cheek when the gland is pressed. A few go on to form an abscess, which antibiotics alone may not cure. Mumps and other viral parotitis are usually bilateral and do not form pus.

## The scan

1. Use a high-frequency linear probe over the swelling in front of and below the ear, in transverse and long axis.
2. Find the gland: normally bright and evenly textured, brighter than the nearby muscle.
3. Find the retromandibular vein running through the gland. The facial nerve lies just superficial to it, in the same plane.
4. Sweep the whole gland, press gently, and add colour Doppler.
5. Compare with the other side.

## Reading the view

- **Normal gland:** bright, homogeneous, with the vein inside it.
- **Parotitis:** an enlarged, darker, patchy gland with increased colour flow, sometimes with small dark ducts or reactive nodes, but no discrete pocket.
- **Abscess:** a dark or complex collection with debris and an irregular wall, no flow inside it, flow in its rim, and sometimes swirl on gentle pressure.

> **The aha** Find the pocket. Then assess the depth and extent.

> **Pitfall** The deep lobe lies behind the ramus of the mandible, where ultrasound cannot follow. A necrotic lymph node, an infected cyst, a tumour or a dental or masseteric infection can look similar. Not every parotid swelling is infection, and not every infection is an abscess.

## Action

Treat parotitis with intravenous fluids, antibiotics that cover Staphylococcus aureus, sialogogues, warm massage and mouth care. A collection needs ENT review for ultrasound-guided aspiration or surgical drainage: the facial nerve runs through the gland, so no blind incision. CT shows deep extension or an uncertain anatomy. Trismus, a facial nerve weakness or spreading neck swelling need urgent senior and ENT review.

?? Who typically gets acute suppurative parotitis?
+ An elderly, dehydrated patient after surgery or an illness
- A healthy child with bilateral painless swelling
- A runner with heel pain
- A patient with a kidney stone
: Dehydration and reduced saliva let bacteria ascend the parotid duct.

?? How does a normal parotid gland look on ultrasound?
+ Bright and homogeneous, brighter than the nearby muscle
- Black, like fluid
- Dark with bright striations
- Invisible behind the mandible
: The gland's fat content makes it bright. Infection makes it darker and patchy.

?? Which finding suggests an abscess rather than parotitis?
+ A discrete dark pocket with debris, no internal flow and flow in its rim
- An evenly enlarged gland with increased flow throughout
- A visible retromandibular vein
- A bright, homogeneous gland
: Parotitis is diffuse. An abscess is a focal collection.

?? Why should a parotid abscess not be incised blind in the emergency department?
+ The facial nerve runs through the gland
- The parotid has no blood supply
- Pus in the parotid is always sterile
- Ultrasound cannot show a parotid abscess
: The nerve lies just superficial to the retromandibular vein. ENT drains under guidance or at surgery.

?? The scan shows only diffuse parotitis, but the swelling extends behind the jaw and the patient has trismus. What next?
+ CT and urgent ENT review, because the deep lobe is hidden from ultrasound
- Discharge with oral antibiotics
- Massage the gland firmly and repeat the scan
- Aspirate the superficial gland
: Ultrasound cannot see behind the mandible. Trismus suggests deep spread.
