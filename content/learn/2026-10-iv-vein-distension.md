When the veins look poor, the limb is often the problem, not your needle skill. A cold, raised arm with no tourniquet holds small, flat veins. Fill them first and the same patient may have a good target.

![Wavelength Pearl: distension tricks. Tourniquet, dependent arm, warmth and hand squeezing enlarge a marginal vein before you choose a target.](/learn/images/iv-distension-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## Fill the vein before you scan

1. **Tourniquet.** Apply it above the site, then wait while the veins fill before you rescan. Release it as soon as the cannula is in, or sooner if the patient has pain or the hand becomes dusky.
2. **Dependent position.** Lower the arm below heart level, as far as the patient tolerates, so gravity helps the veins fill.
3. **Warmth.** A warm towel or warm pack over the limb dilates the peripheral veins.
4. **Hand movements.** Ask the patient to squeeze a ball or clench the fist, then relax. A relaxed hand and a filled vein are easier to enter.
5. **Rescan.** Look at the same vein again once the limb is optimised, then choose the target.

## Reading the view

- **Before.** A small, flat vein that looks too thin to use.
- **After.** The same vein rounder and larger, with a clear lumen and more room for the needle.

> **The aha** Do the set-up work first, then choose your target. Compare the vein before and after you optimise the limb, and you will often change your mind.

> **Pitfall** Scanning before you optimise the limb makes good targets look unusable.

## Safety points

- Follow your trust's policy on limbs to avoid, such as an arm with a dialysis fistula or after lymph node clearance, and on any limb with a previous injury.
- If you take blood for a potassium result through the same procedure, prolonged fist pumping can falsely raise the result. Ask the patient to squeeze and relax a few times, not to pump repeatedly.
- Warmth must be comfortable, never hot. Check the skin, especially in patients with reduced sensation.

?? What is the first step when the veins look small and flat?
+ Optimise the limb with a tourniquet, a dependent arm, warmth and hand movements, then rescan
- Choose the largest artery instead
- Use the deepest vein available
- Press harder with the probe
: Simple measures fill the veins. Scan again after they have taken effect, because a vein that looked unusable is often a good target.

?? Why lower the arm below heart level?
+ Gravity helps the veins fill
- It makes the artery collapse
- It lowers the blood pressure
- It removes the need for a tourniquet
: A dependent limb increases venous filling. Patient comfort and safety decide how far you go.

?? What should you do with the tourniquet once the cannula is in?
+ Release it straight away
- Leave it until the first infusion is finished
- Leave it for the first blood sample only
- Tighten it to hold the cannula
: A tourniquet left on for longer than needed is uncomfortable and raises the risk of tissue damage. Release it as soon as the cannula is in, or earlier if the patient has pain.

?? You will use the same cannula for a potassium sample. What do you advise about the hand?
+ Squeeze and relax a few times without repeated pumping
- Pump the fist continuously
- Keep the fist clenched for the whole sample
- Avoid any hand movement at all
: Prolonged muscular activity can falsely raise the potassium result. A few squeezes help the vein fill without distorting the sample.
