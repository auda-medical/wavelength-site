Finding a pleural effusion is the first step, shown in the [pleural effusion pearl](/learn/pleural-effusion-ultrasound/). The next is its character. Ultrasound shows septations and loculations better than CT, and they change the plan: a septated effusion in a febrile patient is pleural infection until proven otherwise.

![Wavelength Pearl: pleural effusion. Simple anechoic fluid, a septated effusion, and debris with loculated pockets.](/learn/images/pleural-effusion-septated-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Sit the patient up if you can, or scan the posterolateral chest with the patient supine.
2. Use the curvilinear probe in the coronal plane above the diaphragm, with the liver or spleen below.
3. Find the diaphragm, the fluid and the lung within it.
4. Turn up the gain a little and look for echoes, strands and walls in the fluid.
5. Sweep through the whole collection: septations may be in only one part.

## Reading the view

- **Anechoic:** black fluid. Can be a transudate or an exudate, so it does not tell you which.
- **Complex non-septated:** echoes or swirling particles within the fluid. An exudate, such as a parapneumonic effusion or blood.
- **Complex septated:** strands or a honeycomb of septations. An exudate, often a complicated parapneumonic effusion or empyema.
- **Homogeneously echogenic:** uniformly grey fluid, which can be pus or blood.
- **Thickened, nodular pleura:** raises the question of cancer.

> **The aha** Anechoic is not the same as harmless. Septations change the story.

> **Pitfall** Compressed or collapsed lung within the fluid can look like septations. Follow the diaphragm, the fluid and the moving lung together, and look for lung tissue with air in it.

## Action

A septated or complex effusion in an unwell patient needs a diagnostic tap under ultrasound guidance and discussion with the respiratory team: a pH below 7.2, pus, or organisms suggest the effusion needs a drain. Use ultrasound to mark or guide every pleural procedure.

?? A black, anechoic pleural effusion is what?
+ Either a transudate or an exudate
- Always a transudate
- Always an empyema
- Always blood
: Anechoic fluid does not tell you the type.

?? What do septations in a pleural effusion suggest?
+ An exudate, often a complicated parapneumonic effusion or empyema
- A transudate from heart failure
- A normal finding
- A pneumothorax
: Septations come from fibrin in an inflammatory effusion.

?? What can mimic septations?
+ Compressed or collapsed lung within the fluid
- The liver
- The heart
- The spine
: Follow the lung and look for its air.

?? A febrile patient has a septated effusion. What next?
+ Ultrasound-guided diagnostic tap and respiratory discussion
- Diuretics
- Discharge with oral antibiotics only
- Repeat the scan in a month
: Pleural infection may need a drain.

?? Which pleural fluid result suggests the effusion needs a drain in pleural infection?
+ A pH below 7.2
- A pH above 7.6
- A clear yellow fluid
- A low protein
: Low pH, pus or organisms point to drainage.
