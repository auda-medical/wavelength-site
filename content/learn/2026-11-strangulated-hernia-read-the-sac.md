An incarcerated hernia is stuck. A strangulated hernia has lost its blood supply, and the bowel inside it is dying. Examination cannot always separate them, and the delay matters. Ultrasound can show what is in the sac and what is happening upstream, but it supports the surgical decision and does not replace it.

![Wavelength Pearl: strangulated hernia. A thin-walled loop passing through a fascial defect, a thick-walled loop with fluid in the sac, and dilated loops upstream.](/learn/images/strangulated-hernia-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use a linear probe over the lump, with a light touch. Use the curvilinear probe in a larger patient.
2. [Find the break in the bright fascial line](/learn/abdominal-wall-hernia-ultrasound/) and what passes through it.
3. Decide whether the contents are fat or bowel.
4. Watch the bowel for peristalsis, measure the wall, and look for fluid in the sac.
5. Add colour Doppler with a low scale, and compare the wall with a normal loop.
6. Change to the curvilinear probe and look in the abdomen for [dilated loops](/learn/small-bowel-obstruction-ultrasound/).

## Reading the view

- **Reducible hernia:** a thin-walled loop with moving contents, which slides back.
- **Incarcerated:** the contents stay in the sac and do not reduce.
- **Bowel at risk:** a thick wall, fluid in the sac, no peristalsis and little or no flow in the wall.
- **Upstream:** dilated, fluid-filled loops with visible folds suggest obstruction.

> **The aha** If it hurts and will not go back, call the surgeons.

> **Pitfall** Preserved flow does not exclude strangulation. A painful, irreducible hernia goes to the surgeons, whatever the scan shows. Never drain fluid in a hernia sac.

## Action

Arrange urgent surgical review. Keep the patient nil by mouth, and give analgesia and intravenous fluids. Send bloods including a lactate, and treat sepsis if it is present. Do not force reduction in a patient who is unwell, or where the scan suggests bowel at risk, because that can return dead bowel to the abdomen.

?? What is the difference between an incarcerated and a strangulated hernia?
+ Incarcerated is stuck. Strangulated has also lost its blood supply
- They mean exactly the same thing
- Incarcerated contains only fat
- Strangulated hernias always go back on their own
: Stuck bowel can still be alive. Strangulated bowel is dying.

?? Which set of findings in an irreducible hernia suggests strangulation?
+ A thick bowel wall, fluid in the sac, no peristalsis and little wall flow
- A thin wall with normal peristalsis, which slides back
- Bright, compressible fat alone
- A normal fascial line with no defect
: Together with pain and tenderness, these suggest bowel at risk.

?? An irreducible, painful hernia has normal colour flow in the bowel wall. What does this mean?
+ Strangulation is not excluded, so the patient still needs urgent surgical review
- Strangulation is excluded, so the patient can go home
- The scan should be repeated next week
- The hernia must be reduced firmly
: Preserved flow does not rule out strangulation. The clinical picture decides.

?? Why scan the abdomen as well as the lump?
+ Dilated loops upstream suggest obstruction
- To measure the gallbladder wall
- To check the kidneys for stones
- It is never useful
: A stuck loop can obstruct the bowel, which adds to the urgency.

?? The patient is unwell, with a tender, irreducible groin hernia. What should you avoid?
+ Forcing the hernia back
- Giving analgesia
- Calling the surgeons
- Keeping the patient nil by mouth
: Forced reduction can return dead bowel to the abdomen.
