Testicular torsion twists the spermatic cord and cuts off the testis's blood supply. The chance of saving it falls with time. Sudden severe pain, vomiting, a high or horizontal testis and an absent cremasteric reflex need urology now, without waiting for a scan.

When a scan is done, the cord tells you more than the testis. In a 2018 meta-analysis by McDowall and colleagues, the whirlpool sign had a pooled sensitivity of 73% and a specificity of 99%.

## The scan

1. Explain the scan, offer a chaperone and record the offer.
2. Support the scrotum on a rolled towel. Use the linear probe with warm gel.
3. Start on the painless side to set the grey-scale and colour settings.
4. Look at each testis and epididymis in two planes.
5. Compare flow in both testes with the same colour settings, scale set low.
6. Follow the spermatic cord from the top of the testis up towards the groin, in short axis.

## Reading the view

- **Torsion:** a twisted cord, a round mass with vessels spiralling into it, the whirlpool sign. Reduced or absent flow in the testis compared with the other side. An enlarged, uneven testis as it infarcts.
- **Epididymo-orchitis:** an enlarged epididymis with increased flow, often with a reactive hydrocele and a straight cord.

> **The aha** If you suspect torsion, follow the cord, not just the colour box.

> **Pitfall** Never delay urology review for a scan. Partial or intermittent torsion preserves flow, and a testis which has untwisted looks hyperaemic, like epididymo-orchitis. Normal colour Doppler does not exclude torsion.

?? A 15-year-old has sudden severe testicular pain, vomiting and a high-riding testis. What is the next step?
+ Call urology immediately, without waiting for a scan
- Arrange a formal scrotal ultrasound first
- Start antibiotics for epididymo-orchitis
- Give analgesia and review in the morning
: This presentation makes torsion likely. Salvage falls with time, so urology review and exploration must not wait for imaging.

?? What is the whirlpool sign?
+ A twisted spermatic cord, with vessels spiralling into a round mass
- Increased flow in the epididymis
- Swirling echoes in a hydrocele
- A normal spermatic cord in short axis
: The twisted cord forms a round mass with vessels spiralling into it. It is highly specific for torsion, with a pooled specificity of 99%.

?? A boy with sudden testicular pain has normal colour flow in the testis. What do you conclude?
+ Torsion is not excluded
- Torsion is excluded
- He has epididymo-orchitis
- He needs no follow-up
: Partial or intermittent torsion preserves some flow, and a testis which has untwisted becomes hyperaemic. The clinical picture decides the referral.

?? Why start the scan on the painless side?
+ To set the normal grey-scale and colour settings for comparison
- To save time
- Because the painful side cannot be scanned
- To warm the gel
: The normal side shows what normal flow looks like in this patient with these settings. Then scan the painful side with the same settings and compare.

?? Which findings support epididymo-orchitis?
+ An enlarged epididymis with increased flow and a straight cord
- A twisted cord with a whirlpool sign
- Absent flow in an enlarged testis
- A high-riding, horizontal testis
: Inflammation increases blood flow. A straight cord without a twist argues against torsion, but the history still matters.
