Pyomyositis is a bacterial infection of skeletal muscle, usually Staphylococcus aureus. It is commoner in the tropics, but in the UK it affects people with diabetes, immunosuppression, injecting drug use or recent muscle injury, and children. It starts as diffuse swelling and, over one to three weeks, forms an abscess inside the muscle. The skin can look almost normal, so it is easily called a strain, a DVT or [cellulitis](/learn/abscess-ultrasound/).

## The scan

1. Use a high-frequency linear probe for superficial muscles, and a curvilinear probe for the deep thigh or buttock.
2. Find the skin, the fat, the bright fascia and the muscle below it.
3. Scan the tender muscle in long and short axis, through its full depth.
4. Press gently and add colour Doppler.
5. Compare with the same muscle on the other side.

## Reading the view

- **Normal muscle:** dark, with fine bright striations and bright fascial lines.
- **Early infection:** a swollen muscle that has lost its normal striations.
- **Abscess:** a hypoechoic or complex pocket inside the muscle, below the fascia, often with debris, swirl on pressure and flow in its rim.
- **Not this:** cobblestoning in the fat above the fascia is cellulitis, and fluid along the deep fascia raises concern for [necrotising fasciitis](/learn/necrotising-fasciitis-ultrasound/).

> **The aha** If the pain is deep, scan beyond the subcutaneous fat.

> **Pitfall** Early pyomyositis can look almost normal, and deep muscles such as the iliopsoas are hard to see. A haematoma, a tumour or a [DVT](/learn/dvt-ultrasound/) can mimic it. Severe pain, sepsis or rapid progression need urgent escalation, whatever the scan shows.

## Action

Take blood cultures and treat sepsis. MRI, or CT, shows the extent. A collection needs surgical or orthopaedic review for drainage, with antibiotics guided by the cultures.

?? What is pyomyositis?
+ Bacterial infection within skeletal muscle, which can form an abscess
- Infection of the skin only
- A muscle tear
- A clot in a deep vein
: It is usually caused by Staphylococcus aureus.

?? How does normal muscle look?
+ Dark, with fine bright striations and bright fascial lines
- Uniformly bright with no structure
- Black, like fluid
- Bright, with an acoustic shadow
: Loss of the striations is abnormal.

?? Which finding raises concern for pyomyositis?
+ A complex pocket inside the muscle, below the fascia
- Cobblestoning in the fat only
- Muscle with clear, normal striations
- A bright cortex with a shadow
: Cellulitis lies above the fascia; pyomyositis lies within the muscle.

?? In early disease, a near-normal scan means:
+ Infection is not excluded: image further if the picture fits
- The pain is not muscular
- No antibiotics are needed
- The patient can safely go home
: Early pyomyositis can look almost normal.

?? What should you do once pyomyositis is suspected?
+ Blood cultures, MRI or CT for the extent, and surgical review for drainage
- Massage the muscle
- Aspirate blind in the emergency department
- Repeat the scan in a month
: Drainage and antibiotics treat it.
