A pericardial effusion raises the question of tamponade, but the size of the effusion does not answer it. Fluid matters when the pressure it creates stops the right heart filling. That is why the timing of right-sided chamber collapse tells you more than the fact that a wall moves inward.

## The scan

1. Confirm the effusion in more than one view: parasternal long axis, subcostal and apical four-chamber.
2. Record a cine loop, with an ECG trace on the screen if the machine has one.
3. In the apical or subcostal four-chamber view, watch the right atrial free wall. Inward movement which lasts through ventricular systole is the concerning pattern.
4. In the parasternal or subcostal view, watch the right ventricular free wall. Collapse in early diastole, as the ventricle should be filling, is the concerning pattern.
5. Use M-mode through the right ventricular free wall when it helps you time the movement against the mitral valve or the ECG. Do not let a poorly placed cursor replace the cine loop.
6. Finish the picture: the IVC and its change with breathing, left and right ventricular function, and the patient's circulation.

## Reading the view

- **Right atrium:** a brief inward flicker is common and unhelpful. Collapse lasting more than about a third of the cardiac cycle is more specific.
- **Right ventricle:** inward collapse in early diastole is more specific than atrial collapse, and supports tamponade physiology.
- **Context changes the signs:** a dry patient can collapse at lower pericardial pressure, so the signs appear early. Raised right-sided pressures, from pulmonary hypertension or a stiff right ventricle, can hide them.
- **The IVC:** a full IVC which barely changes with breathing is sensitive but not specific. An IVC which collapses well makes tamponade unlikely.

> **The aha** Name the phase, not just the movement: right atrium in systole, right ventricle in early diastole.

> **Pitfall** Tamponade is a clinical diagnosis which the scan supports. No single sign rules it in or out, and in a shocked patient the scan should speed up help, not delay it.

## Action

A shocked patient with a pericardial effusion and supportive chamber collapse needs urgent senior, cardiology and procedural help through your local pathway. Do not wait to perfect the measurements.

?? Which timing best supports right ventricular collapse from tamponade?
+ Early diastole
- Late systole
- Atrial systole only
- Any phase is equally useful
: Pericardial pressure first exceeds right ventricular pressure as the ventricle begins to fill, so the free wall collapses in early diastole.

?? Why is a brief right atrial flicker not enough on its own?
+ Collapse lasting through a good part of systole is what makes the sign more specific
- The right atrium never collapses in tamponade
- Only the left atrium matters
- Timing is irrelevant to the sign
: A momentary inward movement is common. Sustained collapse, more than about a third of the cycle, is more convincing.

?? Which state can make right-sided collapse appear earlier?
+ Hypovolaemia
- Severe pulmonary hypertension
- A high right atrial pressure
- A stiff right ventricle
: Low pressure inside the chambers lets pericardial pressure exceed it sooner. Raised right-sided pressures do the opposite and can hide collapse.

?? Can a full IVC alone diagnose tamponade?
+ No. It is sensitive but not specific, so it must be read with the rest of the scan and the patient
- Yes, in every ventilated patient
- Yes, if the effusion is small
- No, because the IVC is never relevant
: A plethoric IVC has many causes, including ventilation and heart failure. A well-collapsing IVC, on the other hand, makes tamponade unlikely.

?? A hypotensive patient has a pericardial effusion and right ventricular collapse in early diastole. What do you do?
+ Escalate urgently for senior and procedural help while you complete the assessment
- Wait for a formal echocardiogram before telling anyone
- Measure the effusion and discharge if it is under 2 cm
- Ignore the scan because tamponade is a clinical diagnosis
: The scan supports a clinical diagnosis which is time-critical. It should speed up the response.
