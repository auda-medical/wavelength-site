A plethoric IVC that does not collapse with breathing usually points to raised right atrial pressure, from fluid overload, right heart failure, tamponade or a large pulmonary embolism. Occasionally the vein is wide because something is inside it. Thrombus can extend up from the leg and pelvic veins or form around a femoral line or IVC filter. Tumour can grow into the IVC from the kidney or liver. The view you already take for fluid status can show it.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear or phased-array probe in the subxiphoid window, in long axis.
2. Follow the IVC into the right atrium, as in the [IVC pearl](/learn/ivc-ultrasound-fluid-status/), and confirm the hepatic veins draining into it.
3. Before you measure, look inside the lumen. Increase the gain a little to bring out faint echoes.
4. Add colour Doppler with a low scale. Look for flow around or through any material.
5. If you see material, sweep up into the right atrium and down towards the renal veins to find both ends.

## Reading the view

- **Patent plethoric IVC:** wide and still, but black inside with flow throughout. Interpret it with the heart and the clinical picture.
- **Thrombus:** echogenic material within the lumen, with absent or reduced flow around it. Fresh clot can be almost black, so use colour.
- **Clot in the right atrium:** a mobile mass extending into or sitting within the right atrium, a clot in transit.
- **Tumour thrombus:** material extending from a renal vein or the liver; it may contain its own blood flow.

> **The aha** Big does not equal benign. Look inside the vein before you read its size.

> **Pitfall** IVC size alone does not separate thrombus, tamponade, pulmonary embolism and fluid overload. A plethoric IVC is not an instruction to give diuretics or restrict fluids.

## Action

Thrombus in the IVC needs senior review, anticoagulation decisions and formal imaging, usually CT venography or contrast CT. A mobile clot in the right atrium is an emergency: involve senior, cardiology and critical care colleagues at once, because of the risk of pulmonary embolism.

?? A breathless patient has a wide IVC with echogenic material in its lumen. What does this suggest?
+ IVC thrombus or tumour, not just raised pressure
- Fluid overload only
- A normal IVC
- Pneumobilia
: Look inside the vein before reading its size.

?? Why add colour Doppler when the IVC lumen looks black?
+ Fresh clot can be almost anechoic and only shows as a flow defect
- Colour measures IVC pressure
- Colour shows tamponade
- It is never needed
: Fresh thrombus can look black on grey-scale.

?? A mobile mass extends from the IVC into the right atrium. How do you manage it?
+ As an emergency, a clot in transit, with immediate senior help
- As a routine outpatient finding
- With diuretics
- By repeating the scan tomorrow
: A mobile right atrial clot carries a high risk of pulmonary embolism.

?? Which of these can make the IVC wide and still?
+ Tamponade, pulmonary embolism, fluid overload and IVC thrombus
- Only fluid overload
- Only dehydration
- Only pneumothorax
: IVC size alone does not tell you the cause.

?? Where can tumour thrombus in the IVC come from?
+ The kidney or the liver
- The thyroid
- The gallbladder
- The appendix
: Renal cell and liver cancers can grow into the IVC.
