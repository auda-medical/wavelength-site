Torsion is not always complete. When the cord twists less than a full turn, or twists and untwists, venous outflow is blocked first and the arteries may still fill. Colour Doppler can then show flow in a testis that is losing its blood supply. This is why a normal-looking colour scan never clears torsion. See the [testicular torsion pearl](/learn/testicular-torsion-ultrasound/) for the whirlpool sign.

![Wavelength Pearl: partial testicular torsion. Colour flow persists, but the arterial waveform shows high resistance, with lost or reversed diastolic flow, compared with the normal side.](/learn/images/partial-torsion-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Call urology first if torsion is likely on clinical grounds. Scan while they come, not instead.
2. Use a high-frequency linear probe. Start on the painless side to set the grey-scale and colour settings.
3. Compare colour flow in both testes with the same settings.
4. If you can, put a pulsed-wave Doppler gate on an artery inside each testis and compare the waveforms.
5. Follow the spermatic cord up from the testis in short axis, looking for a twist.
6. Note the lie of the testis and its texture compared with the other side.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** symmetrical flow, with a low-resistance arterial waveform that keeps flowing forward in diastole.
- **Partial torsion:** flow may be present but reduced, with a high-resistance waveform: reduced, absent or reversed flow in diastole, compared with the other side.
- **Supporting signs:** a twisted cord (the whirlpool sign), a horizontal lie, and an enlarged or uneven testis.

> **The aha** Flow present is not torsion excluded.

> **Pitfall** Normal colour flow does not exclude partial or intermittent torsion, and a testis that has just untwisted can look hyperaemic, like infection. Waveform measurements are hard in a painful scrotum; do not delay urology to obtain them.

## Action

Sudden testicular pain, especially with vomiting, a high or horizontal testis or an absent cremasteric reflex, goes to urology for exploration whatever the scan shows. Record what the scan found and how it compared with the other side.

?? Why can colour flow persist in partial torsion?
+ The veins are blocked before the arteries
- The testis makes its own blood supply
- Colour Doppler is never accurate
- The cord cannot twist partially
: Arterial inflow can continue while venous outflow fails.

?? Which arterial waveform suggests partial torsion?
+ High resistance, with reduced, absent or reversed diastolic flow
- Low resistance with continuous forward diastolic flow
- No waveform change
- Increased flow on both sides
: Rising resistance shows in diastole.

?? A boy with sudden pain and vomiting has colour flow in both testes. What next?
+ Urgent urology review: flow does not exclude torsion
- Discharge with analgesia
- Diagnose epididymo-orchitis
- Repeat the scan next week
: The clinical picture decides referral.

?? Where else should you look for supporting signs?
+ The spermatic cord, for a twist
- The kidneys
- The inguinal lymph nodes only
- The bladder
: The whirlpool sign in the cord supports torsion.

?? Why compare both testes with the same settings?
+ Reduced flow or a changed waveform shows only against the normal side
- To save time
- Because one testis is always abnormal
- It is not necessary
: Side-to-side comparison is the reference.
