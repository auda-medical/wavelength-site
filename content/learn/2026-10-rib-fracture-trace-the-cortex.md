Chest X-rays miss many rib fractures, especially at the front, the sides and the costal cartilage. Ultrasound has found more fractures than X-ray in several studies, because you scan exactly where it hurts. The number of fractures, the patient's age and the lungs underneath decide the plan.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Ask the patient to point to the most tender spot.
2. Use a high-frequency linear probe along the long axis of the rib, so the cortex is a long bright line with a shadow beneath it.
3. Slide slowly along the rib through the painful area, then turn to short axis over any abnormality.
4. Compare with the rib above and below.
5. Look under the rib, between the ribs, for the pleural line and lung sliding.

## Reading the view

- **Normal rib:** a smooth, continuous bright line with a clean shadow beneath.
- **Fracture:** a step, a gap or a break in the bright line, at the point of tenderness.
- **Supporting signs:** a haematoma over the cortex, and pain as the probe passes over it.
- **Costal cartilage:** darker than bone, with no shadow. A break here is invisible on X-ray but can be seen on ultrasound.
- **Underneath:** absent lung sliding suggests a pneumothorax; fluid above the diaphragm suggests a haemothorax.

> **The aha** Trace the bright line. Find the step.

> **Pitfall** Costal cartilage joins, an oblique probe angle and anisotropy can mimic a break. Scan slowly in two planes, and do not count a step you cannot reproduce.

## Action

Treat the patient, not the crack: analgesia that lets them breathe and cough. Older patients and anyone with several fractures, lung disease or anticoagulation need your local chest injury pathway, often with a regional block and admission. A pneumothorax or haemothorax changes management at once.

?? Where do you start the scan for a suspected rib fracture?
+ At the point of maximal tenderness, along the long axis of the rib
- Over the sternum only
- In the axilla regardless of symptoms
- Over the scapula
: The fracture is usually under the point of tenderness.

?? What does a rib fracture look like on ultrasound?
+ A step, gap or break in the bright cortical line
- A black line with no shadow
- A smooth continuous cortex
- Comet tails from the pleura
: The cortex is normally smooth and continuous.

?? Why can ultrasound find fractures an X-ray misses?
+ It scans exactly where it hurts, including the costal cartilage
- It sees through the bone
- It uses more radiation
- It is always more accurate everywhere
: X-ray misses many anterior, lateral and cartilage fractures.

?? After finding a rib fracture, what else should you look for?
+ Lung sliding and fluid above the diaphragm
- The gallbladder
- Ureteric jets
- The optic nerve
: Pneumothorax and haemothorax matter more than the fracture.

?? Which patients with rib fractures need the most caution?
+ Older patients and those with several fractures, lung disease or anticoagulation
- Young athletes with one fracture
- Anyone with a normal scan
- Patients who can cough freely
: Complications rise with age and number of fractures.
