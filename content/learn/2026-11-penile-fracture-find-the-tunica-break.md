A penile fracture is a rupture of the tunica albuginea, the tough bright capsule around each corpus cavernosum, when an erect penis is bent forcefully, usually during intercourse. The patient describes a crack or a pop, pain, immediate loss of the erection and rapid swelling and bruising, often with the penis deviated away from the tear. The urethra is injured in a minority: blood at the meatus, haematuria or difficulty passing urine raise that question.

## The scan

1. Use a high-frequency linear probe and plenty of gel, with a light touch.
2. Scan each corpus cavernosum along its length and in transverse, starting away from the swelling.
3. Follow the bright line of the tunica albuginea around each corpus.
4. Over the swelling, look for a gap in that line and for haematoma at the defect.
5. Note the site of any tear, so the surgeon can plan the incision.

## Reading the view

- **Intact tunica:** a continuous, thin, bright line around the mid-grey corpus.
- **Fracture:** a discontinuity in the bright line, with dark or mixed haematoma at the gap that may extend into the corpus.
- **Not proof:** a haematoma over an intact tunica can come from a torn dorsal vein or the suspensory ligament, which can mimic a fracture.

> **The aha** Find the capsule. Do not let the scan delay referral.

> **Pitfall** A small tear can be missed, and swelling and pain can make the scan difficult. A classic history with a normal or uncertain scan still goes to urology: MRI or surgical exploration decides.

## Action

Refer urgently to urology. Surgical repair, ideally within 24 hours, gives better erectile function and less curvature than conservative treatment. If the urethra may be injured, avoid a blind urethral catheter and tell the urologist, who will assess it.

?? What is a penile fracture?
+ A tear of the tunica albuginea of a corpus cavernosum
- A fracture of a bone in the penis
- A clot in the dorsal vein only
- A tear of the scrotal skin
: There is no bone. The fracture is a rupture of the tough capsule around the corpus cavernosum.

?? Which history is typical?
+ A snap during intercourse with immediate loss of erection and rapid swelling
- Gradual painless swelling over weeks
- A painful erection lasting six hours
- Fever and dysuria for three days
: The snap and immediate detumescence are the classic features.

?? What does the intact tunica look like on ultrasound?
+ A continuous, thin bright line around the corpus
- A black ring around the corpus
- An irregular dark mass
- It cannot be seen
: A gap in that bright line, with haematoma at the defect, is the sign of fracture.

?? You see a haematoma over the shaft, but the tunica is continuous. What next?
+ Consider a mimic such as a dorsal vein tear, and still refer to urology
- Diagnose a penile fracture with certainty
- Discharge: haematoma is never significant
- Aspirate the haematoma in the emergency department
: Haematoma is not proof of a tunica tear. Urology decides, sometimes with MRI.

?? A patient with a suspected penile fracture has blood at the meatus. What changes?
+ A urethral injury is possible: avoid a blind catheter and tell urology
- Nothing: blood at the meatus is expected
- Insert a large catheter to tamponade the bleeding
- Send him home with antibiotics
: Urethral injury accompanies a minority of fractures and is repaired at the same operation.
