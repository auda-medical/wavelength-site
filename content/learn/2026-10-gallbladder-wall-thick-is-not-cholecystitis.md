A thick gallbladder wall catches the eye and tempts a quick diagnosis of cholecystitis. Heart failure, ascites, low albumin, liver disease and a recent meal all thicken the wall without any inflammation of the gallbladder. The wall tells you something is going on. The pattern around it tells you what.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Find the gallbladder in long axis with the curvilinear probe, below the right costal margin or between the lower ribs.
2. Measure the anterior wall, the one nearest the probe, with the beam at 90° to the wall. The posterior wall blends with bowel behind it, and an oblique cut makes any wall look thicker.
3. Sweep the whole gallbladder in long and short axis, from fundus to neck, looking for stones.
4. Press over the gallbladder with the probe and ask where it hurts most.
5. Look around the gallbladder for a rim of fluid, and check its size.

## Reading the view

- **Thickened wall:** over 3 mm.
- **Supports acute cholecystitis:** gallstones, especially one stuck in the neck, a positive sonographic Murphy's sign (maximal tenderness under the probe over the gallbladder), a distended gallbladder and pericholecystic fluid.
- **Points elsewhere:** a smooth, layered thick wall with no stones and no focal tenderness, in a patient with heart failure, ascites, low albumin, liver disease or a recent meal.

> **The aha** A thick wall is a clue. The pattern makes the diagnosis.

> **Pitfall** Opioids, confusion and recent analgesia blunt the sonographic Murphy's sign. A negative sign in a patient who cannot report pain does not exclude cholecystitis.

## What the scan does not tell you

Acalculous cholecystitis occurs in critically ill patients without stones, and a gangrenous gallbladder loses its tenderness. When the picture does not fit, use bloods, the clinical course and formal imaging.

?? What is the upper limit of normal for gallbladder wall thickness?
+ 3 mm
- 1 mm
- 6 mm
- 10 mm
: A wall over 3 mm counts as thickened. Measure the anterior wall with the beam at 90° to it, because oblique cuts exaggerate thickness.

?? Which wall of the gallbladder do you measure?
+ The anterior wall, nearest the probe
- The posterior wall, furthest from the probe
- The wall at the fundus
- Whichever wall looks thickest
: The anterior wall sits against the liver and has a clean edge. The posterior wall blends with bowel and its gas, which makes it look thicker than it is.

?? A patient with heart failure and ascites has a 6 mm smooth gallbladder wall, no stones and no focal tenderness. What is the best interpretation?
+ Non-specific wall thickening, likely from fluid overload
- Acute calculous cholecystitis
- Gallbladder perforation
- Normal post-prandial gallbladder
: Systemic venous congestion and low oncotic pressure thicken the wall. Without stones, focal tenderness or pericholecystic fluid, the thick wall does not point to cholecystitis.

?? Which combination most strongly supports acute calculous cholecystitis?
+ Gallstones with a positive sonographic Murphy's sign
- A wall of 4 mm alone
- A contracted gallbladder after a meal
- A polyp with no shadow
: Gallstones with focal tenderness under the probe give the strongest bedside pattern. Wall thickening and pericholecystic fluid add weight. A thick wall alone is non-specific.
