Colour Doppler shows the direction and average speed of flow in each small area of the colour box. When the flow is faster than the scale can show, the colour wraps round to the opposite end of the colour map. That is aliasing. It can point to a fast jet, but a scale set too low makes ordinary flow in a normal vessel alias too.

*Scan images in the graphic are fabricated educational illustrations, not patient scans.*

## Set up before you interpret

1. Keep the colour box small, over the vessel you care about.
2. Turn the colour gain up until the lumen fills, then down until colour stops spilling outside the walls.
3. Check the colour scale, also called the pulse repetition frequency (PRF). A very low scale makes normal flow alias.
4. Raise the scale until smooth flow shows without wrap.
5. Improve the angle. A beam at 90 degrees to the vessel shows little or no flow.
6. If aliasing remains at one place, where the vessel narrows, use spectral Doppler and the full examination to describe it.

## Reading the colour

- Red does not mean artery and blue does not mean vein. The colours show direction towards or away from the probe, depending on the colour map chosen.
- A whole normal-sized vessel flickering red and blue at a low scale is usually a settings problem.
- A focal mosaic jet at a narrowing can mean fast, disturbed flow, but colour alone does not grade a stenosis.

> **The aha** Change the machine before you change the diagnosis.

> **Pitfall** Raising the scale too far hides slow flow. Too high and a vein or a slow-flowing vessel can look empty. Set it for the flow you expect.

## Action

Save the optimised colour image and, where it matters, an angle-corrected spectral waveform. Note the scale so the next scanner can reproduce the study.

?? What does red mean on a colour Doppler image?
+ Flow in one direction relative to the probe, depending on the colour map chosen
- Arterial blood
- Oxygenated blood
- Turbulence
: Colour shows direction and speed relative to the probe, not the type of vessel or its oxygen content.

?? A normal vessel flickers red and blue along its whole length. What do you check first?
+ Whether the colour scale is set too low
- Whether every segment is narrowed
- Whether the patient has a fracture
- Whether the B-mode gain is zero
: Wrap along the whole of a normal-sized vessel usually means the scale is too low for the flow.

?? What suggests a true fast jet?
+ Aliasing which stays at one narrowing after sensible adjustment
- Colour outside every vessel wall at maximum gain
- No colour with the beam at 90 degrees
- Smooth colour after raising the scale
: A jet which persists at a visible narrowing deserves a full assessment with spectral Doppler.

?? Why turn the colour gain down when colour spills outside the vessel?
+ Too much gain paints colour beyond the real lumen
- It changes the direction of the blood
- It raises the scale directly
- It measures the pressure in the vessel
: Excess gain makes the colour bloom beyond the walls and overstates flow.

?? Can colour aliasing alone grade a stenosis?
+ No. Use the anatomy, spectral Doppler and the relevant protocol
- Yes, every mosaic is a severe stenosis
- Yes, if the colour is blue
- No, because colour Doppler never shows flow
: Colour helps you find abnormal flow. It does not measure how severe a narrowing is.
