Fasting times guide sedation and anaesthesia, but they do not tell you what is in the stomach. Trauma, pain, opioids, diabetes and pregnancy all slow gastric emptying. A quick scan of the gastric antrum shows the contents directly.

![Wavelength Pearl: gastric POCUS. Classify the antrum as empty, clear fluid or solid.](/learn/images/gastric-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear probe.
2. Place it sagittally in the epigastrium, just to the right of the midline, marker to the head.
3. Find the landmarks: the left lobe of the liver at the top of the screen, and the aorta or IVC deep to the stomach.
4. The gastric antrum lies between them.
5. Scan first with the patient supine, then in the right lateral decubitus position, right side down. Fluid runs into the antrum in this position, so it shows small volumes.

## Reading the view

- **Empty.** A small, collapsed antrum with layered walls, like a target or bull's-eye.
- **Clear fluid.** A distended, round antrum with black, anechoic contents.
- **Solid or thick.** Mixed, bright contents. Fresh solid food often looks like frosted glass, which blurs the back wall.

> **The aha** Empty, fluid or solid. Answer that before you count millilitres.

> **Pitfall** Gastric POCUS informs the aspiration-risk assessment. It is not a stand-alone "safe to sedate" test. Interpret it with the clinical picture and local guidance.

## Taking it further

When the antrum holds clear fluid, the cross-sectional area measured in the right lateral position estimates the volume. Validated formulas exist for adults. Learn qualitative assessment first, because it answers the main question.

?? Where does the gastric antrum lie in a sagittal epigastric view?
+ Between the left lobe of the liver in front and the aorta or IVC behind
- Behind the aorta
- Above the left lobe of the liver
- Next to the right kidney
: In the sagittal epigastric view, the liver sits anterior, at the top of the screen, and the aorta or IVC lies deep. The antrum lies between them. Finding both landmarks confirms the plane.

?? Why scan in the right lateral decubitus position?
+ Fluid moves into the antrum, so small volumes show
- It moves air into the antrum
- It brings the stomach closer to the spine
- It is the only position where the liver is visible
: With the right side down, gravity moves gastric fluid into the antrum, the most dependent part of the stomach in this position. This improves detection of small volumes. Air rises to the fundus, away from the probe's view.

?? A collapsed antrum with layered walls and no contents gives a target appearance. What does this show?
+ An empty stomach
- Clear fluid
- Solid food
- Gastric outlet obstruction
: An empty antrum is small and collapsed, and its muscle and mucosal layers give a bull's-eye pattern. Clear fluid distends the antrum with black contents. Solid food fills it with bright, mixed echoes.

?? The antrum is distended, with bright, mixed contents and a blurred back wall. What does this show?
+ Solid or thick contents
- An empty stomach
- Clear fluid
- A normal fasting stomach
: Food mixed with air gives a frosted-glass appearance which blurs structures behind it. This suggests solid content and a higher aspiration risk. Clear fluid is black and shows the back wall clearly.

?? How should gastric POCUS inform a decision about sedation?
+ As one part of the aspiration-risk assessment, with the clinical picture
- As a stand-alone test which decides whether sedation is safe
- Only when the patient has fasted for six hours
- Only in children
: Gastric POCUS shows what is in the stomach, which fasting times alone cannot. It informs the risk assessment, alongside the urgency of the procedure, the airway and local guidance. It does not replace clinical judgement.
