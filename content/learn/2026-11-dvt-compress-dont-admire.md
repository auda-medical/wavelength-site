A suspected proximal deep vein thrombosis (DVT, a clot in the deep veins of the thigh or behind the knee) is one of the most common reasons to pick up a probe in the emergency department. New scanners often spend their time looking for clot. Fresh clot is often anechoic, black like flowing blood, so you rarely see it. You find it by pressing.

![Wavelength Pearl: DVT scan. Compress the common femoral vein, its bifurcation and the popliteal vein in transverse.](/learn/images/dvt-pearl.webp)

## The scan

1. Lie the patient supine with the head of the bed raised, the hip turned out and the knee slightly bent. Raising the head fills the leg veins and makes them easier to see.
2. Use the linear probe in transverse, across the leg.
3. Start at the groin, at or just below the inguinal ligament. Find the common femoral artery (CFA) and the common femoral vein (CFV), which sits medial to it.
4. Press straight down until the vein walls meet. Release, slide the probe 1 to 2 cm down the leg, and press again.
5. Keep going past the great saphenous vein junction and the CFV bifurcation, where it divides into the femoral and deep femoral veins.
6. Move to the popliteal fossa, with the patient prone or on their side. The popliteal vein lies closest to the probe, above the artery. Compress it down to where it divides in the upper calf.

## The aha

Press until the artery starts to change shape. Then you know you have used enough pressure. If the artery dents and the vein walls meet, the segment is compressible. If the artery dents and the vein stays open, even partly, treat it as clot.

> **The aha** The artery is your pressure gauge. A vein which stays open while the artery deforms is a positive scan.

## Pitfalls

> **Pitfall** Compress in transverse only. In longitudinal, the probe slides off the side of the vein, the vein disappears from the screen, and it looks as though it has collapsed.

- A vein which narrows by half and stops is not compressible. Partial compression means non-occlusive clot.
- Groin lymph nodes look round and dark in one still frame. Sweep up and down: a vessel runs on, a node ends.
- Some patients have two femoral veins. Compress both.
- A negative point-of-care scan covers the segments you scanned, not the calf. Follow your local DVT pathway, which usually combines the Wells score, D-dimer and a repeat or formal scan within a week.

## Test yourself

?? You scan the right groin in transverse. Where does the common femoral vein lie in relation to the common femoral artery?
- Lateral to the artery
+ Medial to the artery
- Directly superficial to the artery
- Directly deep to the artery
: In the groin the vein sits medial to the artery, with the femoral nerve lateral to both. The order lateral to medial is nerve, artery, vein. The vein lies alongside the artery at the groin, not above or below it. The popliteal fossa is different, where the vein lies superficial to the artery.

?? You press over the common femoral vein. The artery deforms. The vein narrows by half but the walls do not meet. What does this show?
- A normal vein, and you need more pressure
+ A non-compressible segment, consistent with DVT
- An artefact, so you repeat the compression in longitudinal
- Venous reflux, so you switch to colour Doppler
: The deformed artery shows you pressed hard enough. A vein which does not close fully under that pressure contains thrombus, even if you see no clot. More pressure only makes sense when the artery has not changed shape. Longitudinal compression gives false reassurance. Colour Doppler adds little to a clear compression result.

?? Why do you compress in transverse rather than longitudinal?
+ In longitudinal the probe slides off the vein, which mimics collapse
- Longitudinal views do not show the vein wall
- Transverse views show clot as bright echoes
- Longitudinal pressure damages the vein valves
: In longitudinal, a small sideways slip takes the vein out of the beam, and the screen shows what looks like a collapsed vein. In transverse you keep the artery and vein on screen together and watch the walls meet. Fresh clot is often black on screen, so its appearance does not decide the plane.
