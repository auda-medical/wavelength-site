A hot, swollen knee needs an answer to two questions: is there fluid, and where is the safest place to aspirate it? The suprapatellar recess answers both. It connects with the knee joint and fills first when fluid builds up.

![Wavelength Pearl: hot knee. Scan the suprapatellar recess in long axis, compress gently to bring fluid into view, and plan the aspiration.](/learn/images/knee-effusion-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Lie the patient supine with the knee flexed about 20 to 30° over a pillow. Slight flexion pushes fluid up into the recess.
2. Place the linear probe in long axis just above the patella, over the quadriceps tendon.
3. Find the bright, fibrillar quadriceps tendon, the top of the patella and the femur deep to them.
4. Look between the tendon and the femur, under the fat pads. This is the suprapatellar recess.
5. Turn to transverse and sweep medially and laterally to find the largest pocket.
6. If the pocket is small, press gently on either side of the patella to squeeze fluid up into the recess.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** the recess is collapsed, a thin dark line between the fat pads.
- **Effusion:** a black or grey space opens deep to the tendon and spreads out under it. It compresses with the probe and moves with pressure.
- **Complex fluid:** debris, strands or a fluid level. Infection, blood and crystals all cause this.
- **Synovial thickening:** grey tissue that does not compress or move with pressure.

> **The aha** Find the pocket. Ultrasound finds fluid, not infection.

> **Pitfall** Ultrasound cannot tell sterile from septic fluid, and a small effusion does not exclude septic arthritis. Use the whole clinical picture, and aspirate when infection is possible.

## Guided aspiration

Use the largest accessible pocket, usually superolateral. Use full aseptic technique, keep the needle in plane, and watch the tip enter the fluid. Send fluid for microscopy, Gram stain, culture and crystals.

?? Where does a knee effusion collect first on ultrasound?
+ The suprapatellar recess, deep to the quadriceps tendon
- The prepatellar bursa, in front of the patella
- The popliteal fossa
- Under the patellar tendon
: The suprapatellar recess connects with the knee joint and lies between the quadriceps tendon and the femur. Fluid in front of the patella is prepatellar bursitis, a different problem.

?? How do you position the knee to scan for an effusion?
+ Flexed about 20 to 30°
- Fully extended and locked
- Flexed to 90°
- Hyperextended
: Slight flexion pushes fluid into the suprapatellar recess. Full flexion tightens the quadriceps and squeezes the recess.

?? A febrile patient has a hot knee and a small effusion on ultrasound. What is the safest next step?
+ Aspirate under ultrasound guidance and send the fluid for culture and crystals
- Exclude septic arthritis because the effusion is small
- Diagnose gout and start colchicine
- Repeat the scan in a week
: Ultrasound cannot tell infected from sterile fluid, and a small effusion does not exclude infection. Aspiration and fluid analysis decide.

?? A small effusion is hard to see. What helps?
+ Gentle pressure on either side of the patella to push fluid into the recess
- Pressing hard with the probe
- Switching to the phased-array probe
- Fully extending the knee
: Compressing around the patella moves fluid up into the recess under the probe. Firm probe pressure pushes the fluid away.
