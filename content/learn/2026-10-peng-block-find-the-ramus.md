The front of the hip capsule receives its nerve supply from articular branches of the femoral nerve, the accessory obturator nerve and the obturator nerve. These branches run between the psoas tendon and the superior pubic ramus. The PENG block, described in 2018, places local anaesthetic in this plane. It treats pain from the joint rather than numbing the whole thigh, so it often leaves the quadriceps working.

## Before you block

1. Record the neurovascular examination and consent.
2. Calculate the maximum safe dose. The block usually uses about 20 mL.
3. Attach monitoring and know where the lipid emulsion is.

## The scan

1. Lie the patient supine.
2. Start with the probe transverse over the anterior inferior iliac spine. A curvilinear probe suits deeper patients.
3. Rotate the probe about 45 degrees, so it lies parallel to the pubic ramus, and slide it until you see the iliopubic eminence, the psoas tendon on top of it, and the femoral artery on the medial side.
4. Insert the needle in plane from lateral to medial, aiming for the plane between the psoas tendon and the pubic ramus.
5. Inject 2 to 3 mL and watch the fluid lift the psoas tendon off the bone. Then inject the rest.

## Reading the view

- **Superior pubic ramus and iliopubic eminence.** A bright, sloping bony line with a shadow below.
- **Psoas tendon.** A bright oval on top of the bone, with iliopsoas muscle around it.
- **Femoral artery.** Round and pulsating, on the medial side.
- **Correct spread.** Fluid between the tendon and the bone, lifting the tendon.

> **The aha** Bone is your backstop. The tip touches the ramus under the tendon, and the fluid lifts the tendon up.

> **Pitfall** Too lateral or too superficial, and the fluid reaches the femoral nerve, giving quadriceps weakness. The PENG block covers the front of the hip. It does not cover the skin of the lateral thigh or the back of the joint.

?? Which nerves does the PENG block target?
+ Articular branches of the femoral, obturator and accessory obturator nerves to the front of the hip
- The sciatic nerve
- The lateral femoral cutaneous nerve only
- The lumbar nerve roots
: The anterior hip capsule receives articular branches from these three nerves. They run between the psoas tendon and the pubic ramus.

?? Where does the needle tip sit?
+ Between the psoas tendon and the superior pubic ramus
- Inside the psoas tendon
- Beside the femoral artery
- Under fascia iliaca, lateral to sartorius
: The plane between the tendon and the bone holds the articular branches. Fluid there lifts the tendon off the bone.

?? What is the main advantage of the PENG block over a femoral nerve block?
+ It tends to spare the quadriceps
- It blocks the whole leg
- It needs no dose calculation
- It also blocks the sciatic nerve
: The PENG block targets the joint branches rather than the main femoral nerve, so quadriceps strength is often preserved.

?? Where do you start the scan?
+ Transverse over the anterior inferior iliac spine, then rotate to lie along the pubic ramus
- Over the posterior superior iliac spine
- Sagittal over the femoral shaft
- Over the greater trochanter
: Starting over the anterior inferior iliac spine and rotating about 45 degrees brings the iliopubic eminence, psoas tendon and femoral artery into one view.

?? What happens if the injection is too lateral and superficial?
+ The femoral nerve is blocked, causing quadriceps weakness
- The block covers the back of the hip
- The bladder fills with fluid
- The block lasts longer with no other effect
: Spread to the femoral nerve weakens the quadriceps and increases the risk of a fall.
