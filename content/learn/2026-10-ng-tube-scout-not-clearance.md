Feeding or flushing through a nasogastric tube that sits in the airway causes serious harm, and in England it is a Never Event. UK guidance confirms position with an aspirate pH of 1 to 5.5, or with a chest X-ray read by a competent person. Ultrasound is not one of the confirmation methods. It is still useful: in the neck it shows whether the tube has gone down the oesophagus, the same anatomy you use when you [check an endotracheal tube](/learn/ultrasound-confirm-intubation/).

![Wavelength Pearl: NG tube. The left neck in transverse with trachea, thyroid, carotid and oesophagus, the tube as a bright echo in the oesophagus, and the stomach window.](/learn/images/ng-tube-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use a high-frequency linear probe in transverse on the left side of the neck, at the level of the thyroid.
2. Find the trachea in the midline: a bright curved line with reverberation below it.
3. Find the oesophagus behind and to the left of the trachea: a round structure with a dark muscular wall and a bright centre. A swallow makes it move.
4. Look for the tube inside it, and turn to long axis along it.
5. Optionally, scan the stomach with a curvilinear probe below the left costal margin.

## Reading the view

- **Tube in the oesophagus:** a bright echo with a shadow inside the oesophagus in transverse, and two bright parallel lines in long axis.
- **Stomach:** a bright line within fluid in the stomach, if gas does not block the view.
- **Not seen:** gas, a collapsed oesophagus or a thin tube can hide it.

> **The aha** A tube seen in the neck tells you the route, not where the tip lies.

> **Pitfall** A tube that cannot be seen is not proof that it is in the airway, and a tube that can be seen is not cleared for use. The tip can still be coiled in the oesophagus or pharynx.

## Action

Follow the local nasogastric tube policy. Do not flush or feed until the aspirate pH is 1 to 5.5, or a chest X-ray read by a competent person confirms the position. Do not use the whoosh test, litmus paper or the patient's comfort to check placement.

?? Where does the cervical oesophagus usually lie on a transverse neck scan?
+ Behind and to the left of the trachea
- In front of the thyroid
- Inside the carotid sheath
- To the right of the right carotid
: Scan the left side of the neck.

?? How does the trachea look on ultrasound?
+ A bright curved line with reverberation artefact below it
- A black fluid-filled circle
- A round target with a bright centre
- A pulsatile anechoic circle
: The trachea holds air, which reflects the beam.

?? How does a nasogastric tube look in the oesophagus in transverse?
+ A bright echo with a shadow inside the oesophagus
- A dark ring around the carotid
- Fluid in the thyroid
- A comet tail on the pleura
: In long axis it shows two bright parallel lines.

?? In UK practice, what confirms nasogastric tube position before first use?
+ Aspirate pH 1 to 5.5, or a correctly interpreted chest X-ray
- Seeing the tube in the neck on ultrasound
- The whoosh test
- The patient not coughing
: Ultrasound is an adjunct, not confirmation.

?? The tube cannot be seen on the neck scan. What does that mean?
+ Nothing on its own: follow the pH or X-ray pathway
- It is in the trachea
- It is in the stomach
- It has fallen out
: A tube that cannot be seen proves nothing either way.
