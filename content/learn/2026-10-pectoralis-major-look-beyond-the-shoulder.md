Pectoralis major tears happen mostly in young men lifting heavy weights, especially in the lowering phase of a bench press. Patients feel a pop, then pain, weakness and bruising over the front of the arm. The tear is often missed as a shoulder sprain. The tendon is superficial, so a linear probe can follow it to the humerus.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Sit the patient up with the arm slightly away from the body and turned out.
2. Use a high-frequency linear probe along the anterior axillary fold, following the muscle towards the arm.
3. Follow the tendon to its insertion on the lateral lip of the bicipital groove of the humerus.
4. Scan in long and short axis, and compare with the other side.
5. Ask the patient to press the hand gently inwards (adduction) while you watch the tendon.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** a bright, fibrillar tendon running onto the humerus, moving as one with contraction.
- **Complete tear:** no tendon at the humerus, a dark gap filled with fluid or haematoma, and a retracted stump further towards the chest.
- **Partial tear:** some continuous fibres with a dark defect, or a tear at the muscle-tendon junction.

> **The aha** An absent tendon at the humerus is the clue.

> **Pitfall** Swelling and haematoma can hide the tendon, and the sternal head can tear while the clavicular head stays intact, so the fold may look partly normal. Compare both sides; MRI is often needed to plan surgery.

## Action

Look for bruising, a thin or absent anterior axillary fold, and weakness of resisted adduction and internal rotation. Suspected complete tears need urgent orthopaedic referral, because repair in the first few weeks gives the best results.

?? Where does the pectoralis major tendon insert?
+ The lateral lip of the bicipital groove of the humerus
- The coracoid process
- The greater tuberosity
- The clavicle
: Follow the tendon to the humerus.

?? Which history suggests a pectoralis major tear?
+ A pop at the front of the shoulder during a heavy bench press
- Gradual shoulder pain over months
- A fall onto an outstretched hand in an older adult
- Pain only at night
: Eccentric loading during bench press is the classic mechanism.

?? What does a complete tear look like on ultrasound?
+ No tendon at the humerus, a gap and a retracted stump
- A continuous bright tendon
- A thickened tendon with normal fibres
- A bright line with a shadow
: The tendon is absent from its insertion.

?? Which movement is weak in a pectoralis major tear?
+ Resisted adduction and internal rotation
- Elbow flexion
- Wrist extension
- Shoulder abduction only
: Pectoralis major adducts and internally rotates the arm.

?? Why is early referral important?
+ Repair in the first few weeks gives the best results
- The tear always heals without treatment
- Imaging is never needed
- It only matters for the shoulder joint
: Delayed repair is harder and less successful.
