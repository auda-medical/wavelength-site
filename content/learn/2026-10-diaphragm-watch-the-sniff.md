The diaphragm does most of the work of breathing. Phrenic nerve injury, an interscalene block, cardiac or thoracic surgery, neuromuscular disease and prolonged ventilation all weaken it. A chest X-ray shows a raised hemidiaphragm but not how it moves. Ultrasound shows movement directly.

![Wavelength Pearl: diaphragm ultrasound. On a sniff, the normal diaphragm moves towards the probe. A paralysed diaphragm moves away from it, or barely moves.](/learn/images/diaphragm-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear or phased-array probe.
2. Right side: place the probe below the right costal margin, between the mid-clavicular and anterior axillary lines, and aim up and back towards the dome. Use the liver as a window.
3. Left side: use the spleen as a window, from a lower intercostal or subcostal position.
4. Find the diaphragm, the bright curved line deep to the liver or spleen.
5. Place the M-mode line through the dome.
6. Record quiet breathing, a sharp sniff, then a deep breath. Repeat on the other side.

## Which way is normal

The probe sits below the diaphragm. On inspiration the diaphragm contracts and moves towards the abdomen, which is towards the probe and up on the screen. The M-mode trace rises with each breath in.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** clear movement towards the probe on inspiration, similar on both sides.
- **Weak:** small movement, less than the other side.
- **Paralysed:** no movement on quiet breathing, and on a sniff the diaphragm moves away from the probe, down on the screen. This is paradoxical movement.

> **The aha** Ask for a sniff and watch the direction.

> **Pitfall** Poor effort, pain and unclear instructions reduce movement. In a ventilated patient, the ventilator pushes the diaphragm passively, so assess during spontaneous breaths. Always compare both sides with the same manoeuvre.

: The probe sits below the diaphragm. On inspiration the diaphragm moves caudally, towards the abdomen and the probe, which is up on the screen.

?? On a sniff, the left hemidiaphragm moves away from the probe. What does this show?
+ Paradoxical movement from left hemidiaphragm paralysis
- Normal movement
- A left pleural effusion
- Poor patient effort
: A paralysed diaphragm cannot contract. The negative pressure of a sniff sucks it upwards into the chest, away from the probe. Poor effort reduces movement but does not reverse it.

?? Which window do you use for the right hemidiaphragm?
+ The liver, from below the right costal margin
- The spleen
- The bladder
- The heart, from the parasternal view
: The liver gives a large acoustic window to the right hemidiaphragm. The left side uses the spleen, which is a smaller window.

?? Why is diaphragm movement during a ventilator breath unreliable?
+ The ventilator pushes the diaphragm passively, so movement does not prove the muscle works
- The ventilator stops all diaphragm movement
- M-mode cannot be used in ventilated patients
- The diaphragm cannot be seen in ventilated patients
: Positive pressure displaces the diaphragm whether or not it contracts. Assess movement during spontaneous breaths, for example during a spontaneous breathing trial.

?? A patient is breathless after an interscalene block on the right. What is the most likely diaphragm finding?
+ Reduced or paradoxical movement of the right hemidiaphragm
- Reduced movement of the left hemidiaphragm
- Increased movement on both sides
- A normal diaphragm on both sides
: Interscalene blocks often spread to the phrenic nerve on the same side, weakening or paralysing that hemidiaphragm until the block wears off.
