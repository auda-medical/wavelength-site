Once you have shown that a lump [is a collection](/learn/abscess-ultrasound/), the next question is whether it is safe to drain it here, and by which route. A minute with the probe gives you the depth, the size and the structures to avoid. It also stops the worst error of all: putting a needle into a vessel.

![Wavelength Pearl: abscess drainage. Calipers from skin to pocket and across it, colour flow in the rim with an artery beside it, and a needle entering the pocket in plane.](/learn/images/abscess-drainage-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use a high-frequency linear probe over the collection, in two planes.
2. Switch on colour Doppler, with the scale set low. Look inside the lump and around it.
3. Measure from the skin to the pocket, and across the pocket.
4. Find any artery, vein or nerve close to your path, and anything deep to the pocket.
5. Choose the shortest route that avoids them. Mark the skin, or watch the needle under real-time guidance.
6. Rescan at the end for a remaining pocket.

## Reading the view

- **Safe target:** a dark or complex pocket with no flow inside it, and sometimes flow in its rim.
- **Path:** the shortest route to the largest part of the pocket, with no vessel in the way.
- **Marked skin:** quick, but the mark moves if the patient or the skin moves after the scan.
- **In plane:** you see the whole needle, and you watch the pocket shrink as you aspirate.

> **The aha** Colour first. Then choose the path.

> **Pitfall** Blood swirls too. Colour Doppler every lump before a needle: a [pseudoaneurysm](/learn/pseudoaneurysm-ultrasound/) can look like an abscess. Be especially careful in the groin, or in anyone who injects drugs.

## Action

Ask for help before draining a lump in the neck, hand, face or groin, and refer a collection close to vessels or nerves. Any flow inside the lump, or a connection to an artery, means no needle. [Suspected necrotising infection](/learn/necrotising-fasciitis-ultrasound/) needs surgeons, not a local drainage. Record the size, the depth, the Doppler findings and what remained at the end.

?? What should you do before any needle goes into a suspected abscess?
+ Colour Doppler inside and around the lump
- Press firmly to rupture it
- Give antibiotics and wait a week
- Inject local anaesthetic into the centre
: Colour Doppler takes seconds and picks up a pseudoaneurysm or a vessel in your path.

?? What two measurements help you plan the drainage?
+ Skin to pocket, and across the pocket
- The patient's height and weight
- The distance to the nearest joint and the nearest bone
- Only the length of the redness on the skin
: These tell you how deep the pocket starts and how big the target is.

?? Which route is best?
+ The shortest route to the pocket that avoids vessels and nerves
- The longest route through healthy muscle
- Any route, as long as the skin is clean
- Directly through the nearby artery
: Short and clear of structures is safest.

?? Why use an in-plane approach under real-time guidance?
+ You see the whole needle as the pocket shrinks
- It needs no sterile technique
- It works without a probe cover or gel
- The needle cannot be seen with any other approach, however it is held
: In plane, the shaft and the tip stay in view, so you know where the needle is throughout.

?? A swirling lump in the groin has turbulent colour flow inside it. What do you do?
+ No needle: treat it as a possible pseudoaneurysm and get urgent help
- Drain it with a wider needle
- Incise it over the most fluctuant part
- Aspirate it and send the fluid for culture
: Flow inside a lump means a vessel until proven otherwise.
