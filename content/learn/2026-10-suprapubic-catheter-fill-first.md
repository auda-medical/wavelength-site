When a urethral catheter cannot be passed, a suprapubic catheter or a suprapubic aspiration drains the bladder. The main serious risk is bowel caught between the abdominal wall and the bladder, especially after previous lower abdominal or pelvic surgery. National guidance recommends ultrasound to confirm a safe window. The same scan in a child confirms enough urine before a suprapubic aspirate.

![Wavelength Pearl: suprapubic catheter. A full bladder gives a broad target and a safe window with no bowel in the path.](/learn/images/suprapubic-catheter-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear probe in the midline just above the pubic symphysis, first in transverse, then in sagittal.
2. Confirm the bladder is full: a large black structure with a wall, rising above the symphysis.
3. Sweep through the whole planned path and look for bowel: loops with gas, a layered wall and movement between the skin and the bladder.
4. In sagittal, measure the depth from skin to the bladder lumen at the planned site, usually about two fingerbreadths above the symphysis.
5. Mark the site and keep the patient in the same position.

## Reading the view

- **Good window:** a full bladder directly under the abdominal wall, with no bowel in the path in either plane.
- **Poor window:** a small bladder deep in the pelvis, with bowel loops lying in front of it.
- **Scars and previous surgery:** adhesions can hold bowel in front of even a full bladder. Look extra carefully.

> **The aha** Fill first, then find the window.

> **Pitfall** A small bladder, or a single view, misleads. If the bladder is not full or bowel cannot be excluded in two planes, do not proceed blind: involve urology or interventional radiology.

## Action

Insert a suprapubic catheter only if you are trained to do so and local policy allows it, usually with urology support. If the bladder is not full, wait for it to fill or fill it through an existing catheter, then scan again. Record the depth and the views you used.

?? Why should the bladder be full before a suprapubic catheter?
+ A distended bladder pushes bowel away and gives a broad target
- A full bladder is easier to puncture accidentally
- It reduces the risk of infection
- It makes no difference
: A full bladder rises above the symphysis and opens a safe window.

?? What must you confirm before inserting a suprapubic catheter?
+ No bowel between the skin and the bladder, in two planes
- The bladder volume only
- Ureteric jets
- Normal kidneys
: Bowel injury is the main serious complication.

?? Which patients have a higher risk of bowel in front of the bladder?
+ Those with previous lower abdominal or pelvic surgery
- Those with a full bladder
- Children only
- Those with kidney stones
: Adhesions can hold bowel in front of the bladder.

?? The bladder is small and bowel lies in front of it. What do you do?
+ Do not proceed: let the bladder fill or involve urology or interventional radiology
- Proceed with a longer needle
- Proceed with a steeper angle
- Proceed without imaging
: Without a safe window, the procedure should not go ahead blind.

?? In which plane do you measure depth from skin to bladder?
+ Sagittal, along the planned path
- Coronal through the kidney
- Transverse through the liver
- It does not need measuring
: The sagittal view shows the depth and the angle of the planned path.
