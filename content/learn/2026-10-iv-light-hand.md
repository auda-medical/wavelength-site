A vein is a thin-walled vessel with low pressure inside it. The probe is a hard object pressed on top. Press even a little too hard and a vein that was easy to see becomes a thin line or disappears. Scanners then conclude the vein is too small, and move on to a worse target.

![Wavelength Pearl: light hand. Use plenty of gel and the lightest pressure that gives a stable image, so the vein stays open.](/learn/images/iv-light-hand-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## What to do

1. Apply generous gel. Gel carries the sound, so you do not need pressure to make contact.
2. Rest the probe on the gel with a light touch.
3. If the vein looks tiny or slit-like, lift off until it reopens and the image steadies. The first fix for a small vein is often to press less.
4. For a very superficial vein, add a thick layer of gel as a stand-off. The vein moves away from the top of the image, where near-field detail is poor, and into clear view.
5. Keep the same light pressure while you slide, and while you puncture.

## Reading the view

- **Too much pressure.** A flat, slit-like vein under the probe, and the artery still round beside it.
- **Just enough pressure.** A round, open, black vein with the skin line intact above it.

> **The aha** Gel buys distance and pressure loses veins. If you cannot see the vein, take the weight off the probe before you look anywhere else.

> **Pitfall** Heavy probe pressure makes small veins vanish and makes the ones you can see look smaller than they are.

## Pressure as a tool

Pressure is useful when you choose it. A vein collapses under gentle pressure and an artery does not. Use that to tell the two apart, then go back to the light touch for the puncture.

?? A small vein disappears when you scan. What is the first thing to try?
+ Lift the probe slightly until the vein reopens and the image steadies
- Press harder to bring the vein closer
- Switch to a lower-frequency probe
- Puncture where the vein was
: Veins collapse easily. Taking pressure off lets the vein refill, so you see its true size.

?? Why use plenty of gel?
+ Gel carries the sound, so you can scan with less pressure
- It makes veins distend
- It replaces the need for a tourniquet
- It improves the needle shaft image
: Good contact comes from gel, not from weight. A light probe on a thick layer of gel keeps the vein open.

?? How does a gel stand-off help with a very superficial vein?
+ It moves the vein away from the poorly seen top of the image and keeps it open
- It makes the vein larger
- It numbs the skin
- It stops the needle bending
: The first few millimetres of an ultrasound image are poorly resolved. A thick gel layer places the vein deeper in the image and takes pressure off it.

?? How do you tell a vein from an artery with the probe?
+ Apply gentle pressure: a vein collapses and an artery does not
- Press as hard as you can: both vessels stay open
- Look at the colour of the lumen on grey scale
- Ask the patient to hold their breath
: A vein collapses with gentle pressure. A pulsating artery remains open. Then return to the light touch before you puncture.
