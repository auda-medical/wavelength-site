A groin lump may be a hernia, a lymph node, a vein, an artery or an abscess, and the treatment for each is very different. Femoral hernias matter most. They are less common than inguinal hernias, are more common in older women, and carry a high risk of strangulation. The inguinal ligament and the femoral vein are the two landmarks that sort them.

![Wavelength Pearl: groin lump. Hernia contents sliding through the inguinal canal, a sac medial to the femoral vein, and a saphena varix filled with colour.](/learn/images/groin-lump-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Use a high-frequency linear probe, with the patient lying flat. Stand them up if the first scan shows nothing.
2. Find the femoral artery and vein in transverse, below the inguinal ligament.
3. Scan along the inguinal ligament and the inguinal canal above it.
4. Scan the lump at rest, and then ask the patient to cough or strain.
5. Add colour Doppler to every groin lump, and press gently to see whether it compresses.

## Reading the view

- **Inguinal hernia:** contents slide through the canal, above the ligament.
- **Femoral hernia:** a sac below the ligament and medial to the femoral vein, which may narrow the vein on straining.
- **Saphena varix:** a round black vessel joining the femoral vein. It compresses and fills with colour.
- **[Pseudoaneurysm](/learn/pseudoaneurysm-ultrasound/):** a pulsatile sac with swirling flow and a neck to an artery.
- **Lymph node:** an oval with a bright centre, which does not change when the patient strains.

> **The aha** Ligament first. Then find the vein.

> **Pitfall** A saphena varix also grows with straining, but it is a vein: it compresses and fills with colour. Colour Doppler every groin lump. A negative scan at rest does not exclude a [small hernia](/learn/abdominal-wall-hernia-ultrasound/).

## Action

A painful, irreducible lump needs [urgent surgical review](/learn/strangulated-hernia-ultrasound/). A femoral hernia needs urgent referral, even when it is not painful, because it often strangulates. A reducible inguinal hernia with no red flags can go through the routine surgical pathway, with advice about pain, vomiting and a lump that will not go back. Never incise a groin lump that has not been scanned.

?? Where does a femoral hernia lie?
+ Below the inguinal ligament, medial to the femoral vein
- Above the inguinal ligament, in the canal
- Lateral to the femoral artery
- Behind the knee
: The ligament and the vein are the two landmarks.

?? Why does finding a femoral hernia change the urgency?
+ Femoral hernias often strangulate
- Femoral hernias never contain bowel
- Femoral hernias always go away by themselves
- Femoral hernias never need surgery
: Even a painless femoral hernia needs urgent referral.

?? A soft groin lump grows when the patient coughs. It compresses easily and fills with blue colour flow. What is it?
+ A saphena varix
- A femoral hernia
- A lymph node
- A lipoma
: It behaves like a hernia on straining, but it is a vein.

?? What should you do before anyone incises a groin lump?
+ Scan it with colour Doppler, looking for a hernia, a vessel or a pseudoaneurysm
- Nothing, because a groin lump is always an abscess
- Give antibiotics and incise
- Press firmly until it goes away
: Bowel, veins and arteries can all sit in a groin lump.

?? The scan at rest shows nothing, but the patient describes a lump that comes and goes. What do you do?
+ Scan again while the patient strains or stands
- Tell them there is no hernia
- Book an emergency operation
- Repeat the same scan at rest
: A small hernia can hide at rest. Make it move.
