Wounds and burns over the back of the thumb, the first web space and the radial side of the back of the hand sit in the territory of the superficial branch of the radial nerve. It is a small, purely sensory nerve. In the forearm it runs beside the radial artery, under brachioradialis. A few millilitres around it, often combined with median and ulnar blocks, completes analgesia for the hand.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## Before you block

1. Record motor and sensory function of the hand before the block.
2. Record consent and calculate the dose. Two to four millilitres is usually enough.

## The scan

1. Rest the forearm with the thumb side up.
2. Place the linear probe transversely on the radial side of the forearm, in its middle third.
3. Find the radial artery with colour Doppler, then look on its radial side for a small, bright, honeycomb oval under brachioradialis.
4. Follow the nerve up and down to confirm it. Near the wrist it leaves the artery, passes under the brachioradialis tendon and becomes superficial, branching in the skin.
5. Insert the needle in plane, keeping clear of the artery and the cephalic vein, and inject small amounts around the nerve.

## Reading the view

- **Radial artery.** Round and pulsating, with colour on Doppler.
- **Superficial radial nerve.** A small honeycomb oval on the radial side of the artery.
- **Brachioradialis.** The muscle and tendon over the nerve.
- **Correct spread.** A small ring of fluid around the nerve.

> **The aha** Use a lot of gel and very little pressure. The nerve is small and superficial, and a heavy hand flattens it and the veins around it.

> **Pitfall** Close to the wrist the nerve has already divided into skin branches and is hard to see. Block in the mid forearm, or use a subcutaneous field block over the radial side of the wrist if you cannot find it.

?? Which area does the superficial radial nerve supply?
+ The back of the thumb, the first web space and the radial side of the back of the hand
- The palm of the hand
- The little finger
- The palmar surface of the index finger
: The superficial branch of the radial nerve supplies the radial side of the back of the hand, the back of the thumb and the first web space.

?? Where does the superficial radial nerve lie in the mid forearm?
+ On the radial side of the radial artery, under brachioradialis
- Between the finger flexors
- Beside the ulnar artery
- Inside the extensor tendon sheaths
: In the mid forearm the nerve runs with the radial artery, under brachioradialis.

?? Why do you use light probe pressure?
+ Heavy pressure flattens the small nerve and nearby veins, distorting the view
- Light pressure makes the artery disappear
- It makes the bone look brighter
- Pressure has no effect on superficial structures
: The nerve and veins are small and superficial. A light touch with plenty of gel keeps their shape.

?? Why can a radial nerve block close to the wrist fail?
+ The nerve has already divided into small skin branches
- The nerve is deeper than the radius at the wrist
- The nerve joins the median nerve
- The radial artery covers the nerve completely
: Near the wrist the nerve becomes superficial and branches. Block in the mid forearm or use a field block.

?? Is the superficial radial nerve motor or sensory?
+ Purely sensory
- Purely motor
- Motor to the thumb muscles
- Motor to the wrist extensors
: The superficial branch carries sensation only. The motor fibres travel in the deep branch, the posterior interosseous nerve.
