The extensor mechanism of the knee runs from the quadriceps tendon, through the patella and the patellar tendon, to the tibial tubercle. A rupture anywhere stops the patient straightening the knee. Swelling can hide the gap from your fingers, but not from the probe.

![Wavelength Pearl: patellar tendon rupture. A continuous fibrillar tendon from patella to tibial tubercle, and a complete rupture with a gap and retracted ends.](/learn/images/patellar-tendon-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Lie the patient supine with the knee slightly bent over a pillow.
2. Place a high-frequency linear probe in long axis below the patella.
3. Follow the tendon from the lower pole of the patella all the way to the tibial tubercle.
4. Turn to transverse at any abnormal spot.
5. If it is comfortable, bend the knee a little. A gap opens; an intact tendon stays continuous.
6. Compare with the other knee.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** a bright, fibrillar, continuous tendon from patella to tubercle.
- **Complete rupture:** the fibres stop, with a dark gap of fluid or haematoma and wavy, retracted ends. The patella may sit higher than on the other side.
- **Partial tear or tendinopathy:** a thickened, darker tendon with some continuous fibres.

> **The aha** Follow the tendon all the way to bone. The gap is the clue, not a blurred patch.

> **Pitfall** Anisotropy makes a normal tendon look dark. Heel-toe the probe until the fibres are bright, and compare with the other side before calling a tear.

## Action

A patient who cannot straight-leg raise, with a gap on ultrasound, needs urgent orthopaedic review: complete ruptures are usually repaired early. X-ray the knee to look for a high patella and a bony avulsion.

?? Where should the scan run to assess the patellar tendon?
+ From the lower pole of the patella to the tibial tubercle
- From the quadriceps muscle to the patella only
- Across the medial joint line
- Behind the knee
: Ruptures happen anywhere along the tendon, including at the bone at either end.

?? What does a complete rupture look like?
+ A dark gap with retracted, wavy tendon ends
- A thin, bright, continuous tendon
- Colour flow throughout the tendon
- A smooth bone cortex
: The fibres stop and fluid or haematoma fills the gap.

?? A dark area appears in the tendon, but the fibres brighten when you tilt the probe. What is it?
+ Anisotropy
- A complete rupture
- An abscess
- A fracture
: Darkness which disappears when you correct the angle is anisotropy.

?? Which clinical finding supports a complete rupture?
+ Being unable to straighten the knee against gravity
- A full range of active extension
- Pain only on kneeling
- Normal walking
: A complete rupture breaks the extensor mechanism.

?? What should happen after a complete rupture is found?
+ Urgent orthopaedic review, with an X-ray of the knee
- Physiotherapy only
- A repeat scan in six months
- Discharge with a knee support only
: Complete ruptures are usually repaired early.
