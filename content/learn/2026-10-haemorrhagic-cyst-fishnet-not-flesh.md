A young woman with sudden pelvic pain and a complex ovarian cyst on scan: is it blood or tissue? Bleeding into a corpus luteum is common and usually settles. Its appearance changes as the blood clots and retracts, and it often mimics a solid mass.

![Wavelength Pearl: haemorrhagic cyst. Look for reticular fishnet echoes and a retracting clot with no internal flow.](/learn/images/haemorrhagic-cyst-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Confirm the pregnancy test result.
2. Scan the uterus and both adnexa with the curvilinear probe, in two planes. Transvaginal scanning by a trained operator gives a clearer view.
3. Find the cyst and sweep through all of it.
4. Look at the internal echoes and any solid-looking part.
5. Add colour Doppler with a low velocity scale over any solid-looking area.
6. Check the pouch of Douglas and the right upper quadrant for free fluid.

## Reading the view

- **Simple cyst:** black, thin-walled, smooth, with no internal echoes.
- **Haemorrhagic cyst:** fine, lacy, net-like internal strands with no flow on colour Doppler.
- **Retracting clot:** a grey mass inside the cyst with a concave or straight edge, no flow inside it, and sometimes moving with the patient.
- **Ruptured cyst:** echogenic free fluid around the ovary or in the pouch of Douglas.

> **The aha** Fishnet echoes and no internal flow usually mean clot, not flesh.

> **Pitfall** A positive pregnancy test, fever, a solid nodule with flow, thick septations or a large amount of free fluid widen the differential. These need gynaecology review and formal imaging.

## What to do next

A typical haemorrhagic cyst in a stable, non-pregnant patient usually needs analgesia and follow-up according to local guidance. Large haemoperitoneum, haemodynamic instability or an atypical appearance need urgent gynaecology review.

?? Which appearance is typical of a haemorrhagic ovarian cyst?
+ Fine, lacy, net-like internal echoes with no internal flow
- A solid nodule with internal blood flow
- A completely anechoic cyst with a thin wall
- A thick-walled mass with hyperaemic septations
: Clotting blood forms fibrin strands that give a fishnet or reticular pattern. Flow inside a solid part suggests tissue, not clot.

?? How do you separate a retracting clot from a solid nodule?
+ A clot has a concave edge and no internal flow on colour Doppler
- A clot always shows strong internal flow
- A clot has a convex, rounded edge
- A clot lies outside the ovary
: A retracting clot pulls away from the wall, leaving a concave or straight margin, and has no blood supply. A solid nodule bulges into the cyst and often shows flow.

?? A patient with a complex adnexal cyst has a positive pregnancy test. What is the priority?
+ Treat as possible ectopic pregnancy and follow the early pregnancy pathway
- Diagnose a haemorrhagic cyst and discharge
- Repeat the scan in six weeks
- Order tumour markers only
: A positive pregnancy test changes the differential. A complex adnexal mass in early pregnancy needs ectopic pregnancy excluded.
