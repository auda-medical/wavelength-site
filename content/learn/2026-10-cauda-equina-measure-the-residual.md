Back pain with new bladder symptoms, saddle numbness or leg weakness raises the possibility of cauda equina syndrome. The clinical signs are often soft, and patients find bladder symptoms hard to describe. A bladder scan after voiding gives one number you can measure, repeat and hand over. It supports the decision for urgent MRI. It does not make it.

![Wavelength Pearl: cauda equina. Scan the bladder before and after the patient voids, and calculate the post-void residual.](/learn/images/cauda-equina-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Ask the patient to pass urine first, in private and as normally as they can. Note the time.
2. Scan within about 10 minutes of voiding. Use the curvilinear probe just above the pubic symphysis, angled down into the pelvis.
3. In transverse, find the widest view of the bladder and measure the width and the front-to-back height.
4. Turn the probe to sagittal and measure the longest length.
5. Volume in millilitres is about width × height × length (in centimetres) × 0.52. A bladder scanner gives the same estimate automatically.
6. If the bladder is very full before voiding, scan it then too. A painless large bladder is itself a warning sign.

## Reading the view

- **Small residual:** the bladder has emptied well. This is reassuring for retention, not for cauda equina syndrome.
- **Large residual:** a bladder that stays full after a real attempt to void. Many UK pathways treat a residual of more than about 200 mL as significant, but no single threshold rules the syndrome in or out.
- **Painless retention:** a large residual without the discomfort you would expect is more worrying than a large residual in pain.
- **Upstream:** with a large bladder, look at both kidneys for hydronephrosis, as in the [urinary retention pearl](/learn/bladder-volume-ultrasound/).

> **The aha** Measure after the patient tries to void. A full bladder before voiding tells you very little.

> **Pitfall** A normal residual does not exclude cauda equina syndrome. Early, incomplete syndrome often has normal bladder function, and that is the stage at which surgery helps most. Retention also has many other causes: opioids, anticholinergic drugs, prostate enlargement, constipation and pain.

## Action

Follow your local suspected cauda equina pathway. If the history or examination suggests the syndrome, arrange urgent MRI whatever the residual. Record the residual volume, the time since voiding and how it was measured, so the next clinician can compare.

?? When should you scan the bladder in suspected cauda equina syndrome?
+ Within about 10 minutes after the patient has tried to pass urine
- Only before the patient voids
- After a catheter has been inserted
- The next morning
: The post-void residual is the useful number. A full bladder before voiding is expected.

?? How do you estimate bladder volume from three diameters in centimetres?
+ Width × height × length × 0.52
- Width × height × length × 2
- Width + height + length
- Length × 0.52 only
: The ellipsoid formula gives an estimate in millilitres. A bladder scanner uses a similar calculation.

?? A patient with back pain and saddle numbness has a post-void residual of 40 mL. What next?
+ Urgent MRI is still needed, because a normal residual does not exclude the syndrome
- Cauda equina syndrome is excluded
- Discharge with analgesia
- Repeat the bladder scan in a week
: Early, incomplete cauda equina syndrome often has normal bladder function. The clinical picture decides imaging.

?? Which finding is most worrying?
+ A large residual which the patient cannot feel
- A full bladder before voiding
- A small residual after voiding
- A bladder which empties completely
: Painless retention suggests loss of bladder sensation, a red flag for cauda equina syndrome.

?? Which of these can also cause a large post-void residual?
+ Opioids, anticholinergic drugs and prostate enlargement
- Only cauda equina syndrome
- Dehydration alone
- A recent meal
: Retention has many causes. Ultrasound measures the bladder, not the cause.
