In early pregnancy, almost every woman has a corpus luteum: a thick-walled cyst inside the ovary, often with a ring of blood flow around it. A tubal ectopic pregnancy can also look like a ring. The key is position. This pearl follows on from the [ectopic pregnancy pearl](/learn/ectopic-pregnancy-ultrasound/), which starts with the uterus.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Explain the scan, offer a chaperone and record the offer. Confirm the pregnancy test first.
2. Look for an intrauterine pregnancy, as in the ectopic pearl.
3. Find each ovary beside the uterus: an oval structure with small follicles.
4. Find the ring and decide whether it is inside the ovary or separate from it.
5. With gentle pressure from the probe, watch whether the ring moves with the ovary or separately from it.
6. Look for free fluid in the pouch of Douglas and around the liver.

## Reading the view

- **Corpus luteum:** inside the ovary, surrounded by ovarian tissue, with a thick wall that is usually less bright than the endometrium. It moves with the ovary.
- **Tubal ectopic:** a ring separate from the ovary, often brighter than the ovary, sometimes containing a yolk sac or fetal pole. It moves separately from the ovary.
- **Free fluid:** echogenic fluid in the pelvis or fluid around the liver raises concern for rupture.

> **The aha** First find the ovary. Then judge the ring.

> **Pitfall** A ring of colour flow appears around both a corpus luteum and an ectopic, so colour does not separate them. A transabdominal bedside scan often cannot see the adnexa well. If you cannot be sure, treat it as a pregnancy of unknown location.

## Action

An unstable patient with a positive test and free fluid needs resuscitation and immediate gynaecology review. Otherwise, a positive test with no definite intrauterine pregnancy goes to the early pregnancy unit for a transvaginal scan and serial hCG, with clear safety-netting.

?? Where does a corpus luteum sit?
+ Inside the ovary, surrounded by ovarian tissue
- In the fallopian tube
- In the uterine cavity
- In the pouch of Douglas
: Position separates it from a tubal ring.

?? A ring separate from the ovary in a woman with a positive test and no intrauterine pregnancy suggests what?
+ A tubal ectopic pregnancy
- A normal corpus luteum
- A simple follicle
- Bowel
: A separate adnexal ring is the concern.

?? Does a ring of colour flow separate a corpus luteum from an ectopic?
+ No, both can show a ring of flow
- Yes, only an ectopic has flow
- Yes, only a corpus luteum has flow
- Colour is never used
: Use position, not colour.

?? How can gentle probe pressure help?
+ An ectopic moves separately from the ovary; a corpus luteum moves with it
- It makes the ring disappear
- It confirms rupture
- It has no use
: The sliding sign separates the two.

?? A woman with a positive test is hypotensive with free fluid in the abdomen. What next?
+ Resuscitate and call gynaecology immediately
- A detailed scan of both ovaries first
- Repeat the hCG in 48 hours
- Discharge with safety-netting
: Rupture is a surgical emergency.
