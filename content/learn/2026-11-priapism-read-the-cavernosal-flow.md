Priapism is an erection lasting more than four hours without sexual stimulation. Nearly all cases are ischaemic (low-flow): the blood in the corpora cannot drain, the corpora become a compartment syndrome, and after a few hours the smooth muscle starts to die. Causes include sickle cell disease, intracavernosal injections and drugs such as antipsychotics, trazodone and cocaine. Non-ischaemic (high-flow) priapism is rare: usually after a blow to the perineum or penis, an arteriolar fistula floods the corpora. It is painless and less rigid, and is not an emergency.

## The scan

1. Use a high-frequency linear probe with colour and spectral Doppler.
2. Scan the shaft in transverse to see both corpora cavernosa, each with a cavernosal artery near its centre, and the corpus spongiosum around the urethra.
3. Look for colour flow in each cavernosal artery, and sample it with spectral Doppler.
4. Scan along the shaft and into the perineum, where the crura lie, looking for a focal blush of turbulent colour.

## Reading the view

- **Ischaemic (low-flow):** rigid corpora with absent or minimal flow in the cavernosal arteries. Later, the corpora become heterogeneous.
- **Non-ischaemic (high-flow):** normal or high-velocity cavernosal flow, often with a focal turbulent blush where a fistula feeds the corpus, typically after trauma.

> **The aha** Low flow is time-critical. Do not wait for a perfect scan.

> **Pitfall** Ultrasound supports the diagnosis; the corporal blood gas makes it. Ischaemic blood is dark, with a low oxygen, a high carbon dioxide and acidosis; non-ischaemic blood looks arterial. Scanning after aspiration or phenylephrine can show returning flow that mimics the high-flow type, so interpret flow in the light of what has been done.

## Action

Ischaemic priapism needs urgent urology: aspiration of the corpora, with irrigation and intracavernosal phenylephrine under monitoring, follows local guidance. In sickle cell disease, involve haematology too, without delaying treatment of the penis. Non-ischaemic priapism is referred to urology; many resolve, and embolisation is an option.

?? A man has a painful, fully rigid erection for six hours. What type of priapism is most likely?
+ Ischaemic (low-flow) priapism, a urological emergency
- Non-ischaemic priapism, which can wait for a clinic
- A normal erection
- Penile fracture
: Painful, fully rigid priapism is ischaemic until proven otherwise, and nearly all priapism is ischaemic.

?? What does colour Doppler show in ischaemic priapism?
+ Absent or minimal flow in the cavernosal arteries
- A turbulent blush of high flow
- Increased flow in both corpora
- Flow in the urethra
: Ischaemic priapism is a compartment syndrome. Little or no blood enters the corpora.

?? Which test confirms the type of priapism?
+ A corporal blood gas
- A urine dipstick
- A full blood count alone
- A plain X-ray of the pelvis
: Ischaemic blood is dark, hypoxic, hypercarbic and acidotic; non-ischaemic blood looks arterial.

?? A painless, partly rigid erection began two days after a fall astride a bar. What might the scan show?
+ Preserved or high cavernosal flow with a focal turbulent blush
- No flow at all
- A break in the tunica albuginea
- Black fluid around the testis
: A straddle injury can create an arteriolar fistula: non-ischaemic, high-flow priapism.

?? Why should the scan never delay aspiration in suspected ischaemic priapism?
+ Corporal smooth muscle dies with time, and the scan does not change the first treatment
- Ultrasound cannot see the penis
- Doppler is harmful to the corpora
- Aspiration is only for the high-flow type
: Every hour of ischaemia increases the risk of fibrosis and permanent erectile dysfunction.
