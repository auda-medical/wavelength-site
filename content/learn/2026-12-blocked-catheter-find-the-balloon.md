A catheter that has stopped draining is a common call in the emergency department and on the wards. Flushing it blind treats only one of the possible causes, and it does nothing for a balloon in the urethra or a patient who is making no urine. Ultrasound shows where the balloon is and how much urine lies around it.

![Wavelength Pearl: blocked catheter. A balloon in an empty bladder, a full bladder around the balloon with clot beside it, and a balloon below the bladder neck.](/learn/images/blocked-catheter-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Check the outside first: a kinked tube, a closed valve, or a bag above the bladder.
2. Use the curvilinear probe just above the pubic bone, in transverse and sagittal.
3. Find the bladder and judge how full it is. [Measure the volume](/learn/bladder-volume-ultrasound/) if it is large.
4. Find the balloon: a round, black circle with a bright wall.
5. Look for bright, irregular material beside it. If you find a full bladder or no urine, go on to look at both kidneys.

## Reading the view

- **Balloon in an empty bladder:** the catheter is in place and draining. Look upstream at the patient's volume state and the [kidneys](/learn/hydronephrosis-ultrasound/).
- **Full bladder around the balloon:** the catheter is blocked, often by clot or debris.
- **Bright material that moves and has no flow:** [clot in the bladder](/learn/bladder-clot-ultrasound/).
- **Full bladder with no balloon inside it:** look below the bladder neck. A balloon there lies in the urethra.

> **The aha** Do not flush blind. Find the balloon.

> **Pitfall** No balloon in the bladder may mean it lies in the urethra. Deflate it and call urology. Do not inflate further. A balloon seen in the bladder does not prove the tip is draining, so the volume in the bladder still decides.

## Action

If the catheter is blocked, flush it or replace it, following local policy. Clot retention needs a three-way catheter and bladder washout, with urology involved if the bleeding continues. If the bladder is dry, assess the patient's volume state, the kidneys and the blood results rather than changing the catheter. A balloon in the urethra needs deflating and urgent urology review.

?? A catheterised patient has stopped draining. What is the first thing the scan should find?
+ The balloon, and how much urine lies around it
- The gallbladder
- The aorta
- The femoral vein
: The position of the balloon and the volume in the bladder separate blocked, misplaced and dry.

?? The scan shows a full bladder around the balloon. What does this mean?
+ The catheter is blocked
- The catheter is working well
- The patient is making no urine
- The balloon lies in the urethra
: Urine is collecting in the bladder and not leaving through the catheter.

?? The balloon sits in an empty bladder and the bag stays dry. Where do you look next?
+ Upstream: the patient's volume state and the kidneys
- Replace the catheter and discharge
- Inflate the balloon further
- Repeat the scan next week
: The catheter has nothing to drain, so the question is why no urine is reaching the bladder.

?? The bladder is full, with no balloon inside it, and a small ring lies below the bladder neck. What do you do?
+ Deflate the balloon and call urology
- Inflate the balloon further
- Flush the catheter hard
- Pull the catheter out with the balloon inflated
: A balloon below the bladder neck may lie in the urethra. Inflating it or pulling on it can cause serious injury.

?? Bright, irregular material lies beside the balloon in a full bladder, in a patient with visible haematuria. What is the usual treatment?
+ A three-way catheter and bladder washout
- Antibiotics alone
- A smaller catheter
- Discharge with a leg bag
: Clot blocks ordinary catheters. A large three-way catheter allows washout and irrigation.
