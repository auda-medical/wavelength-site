Examination of the hip is difficult, especially in a crying child. Ultrasound shows fluid in the joint in minutes and guides aspiration. It does not tell you what the fluid is.

![Wavelength Pearl: hip effusion. Align the probe with the femoral neck, measure the capsule-to-neck distance and compare both hips.](/learn/images/hip-effusion-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Lie the patient supine with the hip in neutral, or slightly flexed and externally rotated.
2. Use the linear probe in children and slim adults, the curvilinear probe in larger patients.
3. Place the probe just below the inguinal ligament, lateral to the femoral vessels, along the long axis of the femoral neck, marker to the head.
4. Find the femoral head, a bright curve, and the femoral neck running from it. The anterior capsule lies over the neck.
5. Measure from the front of the femoral neck to the outer edge of the capsule.
6. Scan the other hip with the same settings.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** the capsule lies close to the neck and follows its concave curve.
- **Effusion:** a dark space lifts the capsule off the neck, and the capsule bulges outwards.
- **In children,** ACEP guidance defines an effusion as a capsule-to-neck distance over 5 mm, or more than 2 mm wider than the other side.

> **The aha** When unsure, scan the other hip.

> **Pitfall** An effusion does not separate transient synovitis from septic arthritis. Fever, refusal to bear weight and raised inflammatory markers raise concern for septic arthritis, which needs urgent orthopaedic review and aspiration.

## Guided aspiration

The anterior recess is the target. Find the femoral vessels with colour Doppler, medial to the joint, and use an in-plane approach from lateral to medial. Follow your local orthopaedic pathway.

?? Which probe orientation do you use to scan the hip for an effusion?
+ Along the long axis of the femoral neck
- Transverse across the inguinal ligament
- Over the greater trochanter
- Posterior to the joint
: Aligning the probe with the femoral neck shows the head, the neck and the anterior capsule over it, where fluid collects in the anterior recess.

?? In a child, which finding defines a hip effusion?
+ A capsule-to-neck distance over 5 mm, or more than 2 mm wider than the other hip
- Any visible fluid in the joint
- A capsule-to-neck distance under 2 mm
- Increased colour flow in the femoral head
: ACEP guidance uses over 5 mm, or a difference of more than 2 mm between sides. Comparing sides is the most useful check.

?? A febrile 6-year-old refuses to walk and has a right hip effusion. What do you do?
+ Arrange urgent orthopaedic review, as septic arthritis must be excluded
- Diagnose transient synovitis and discharge
- Repeat the scan in a week
- Give ibuprofen and review in clinic
: Fever and refusal to bear weight raise concern for septic arthritis. Ultrasound cannot separate it from transient synovitis. The child needs bloods, urgent orthopaedic review and likely aspiration.

?? What does the capsule look like when there is an effusion?
+ Lifted off the femoral neck and bulging outwards
- Closely applied to the neck and concave
- Invisible
- Thinner than on the other side
: Fluid in the anterior recess pushes the capsule away from the neck, turning its normal concave shape into an outward bulge.

?? Where do the femoral vessels lie in relation to the hip joint when you plan an aspiration?
+ Medial to the joint
- Lateral to the joint
- Directly over the femoral neck
- Behind the femoral head
: The femoral vessels lie medial to the anterior hip. Colour Doppler confirms their position, and an in-plane approach from lateral to medial keeps the needle away from them.
