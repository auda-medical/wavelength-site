When a difficult airway is expected and you cannot feel the front of the neck, a minute of ultrasound can find the cricothyroid membrane. In obese patients and distorted necks, palpation often misses it, and ultrasound finds it much more often. The point is preparation: find it while oxygenation is stable, mark the skin, and have the rescue plan ready.

![Wavelength Pearl: cricothyroid membrane. Thyroid cartilage, cricoid cartilage and the membrane between them, in transverse and long axis.](/learn/images/cricothyroid-pearl.webp)

*Scan images in the graphic are fabricated educational illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Position the neck as your airway plan allows. Use a high-frequency linear probe, plenty of gel and a light hand.
2. In transverse, find the thyroid cartilage: a larger, angular, dark structure with a bright line deep to it where air meets the airway lining.
3. Slide down to the cricoid: a smaller, rounded dark ring.
4. Turn the probe into the midline long axis. The membrane is the soft-tissue gap between the bottom of the thyroid cartilage and the top of the cricoid.
5. Mark the centre of the membrane, and the midline, on the skin with the neck in the position you will use for the procedure.
6. Check the mark again if the head or neck moves a lot.

## Use it safely

- It helps most when the landmarks are hard to feel: obesity, neck swelling, previous surgery or radiotherapy.
- Ultrasound does not replace the difficult airway plan, the equipment, a trained assistant or the scalpel technique.
- In a can't intubate, can't oxygenate emergency, do not delay front-of-neck access to fetch or use a scanner.

> **The aha** Ultrasound belongs before the emergency, not between failed oxygenation and rescue.

> **Pitfall** A neat image is not the same as a useful skin mark. Mark the membrane and the midline with the neck positioned as it will be for the procedure.

## Action

If the landmarks are difficult and time allows, find and mark the membrane during airway preparation, and tell the team the mark may need checking if the patient is moved.

?? When is ultrasound localisation of the cricothyroid membrane most useful?
+ Before airway management, when the landmarks are hard to feel
- After oxygenation has failed, before starting rescue
- Only after the skin incision
- In every routine intubation whatever the anatomy
: Its value is in preparation, when a difficult airway is expected and palpation is unreliable.

?? In the midline long-axis view, what lies between the thyroid and cricoid cartilages?
+ The cricothyroid membrane
- The oesophagus
- The carotid artery
- The thyroid isthmus
: The membrane spans the gap between the bottom of the thyroid cartilage and the top of the cricoid.

?? Why use a light hand on the probe?
+ Heavy pressure distorts the soft tissue and moves the skin over the landmark
- The cartilage dissolves under pressure
- It causes colour aliasing
- It is needed only for Doppler
: An accurate skin mark depends on not distorting the neck.

?? The neck is repositioned after marking. What do you do?
+ Check the mark again if time allows
- Nothing, as the mark can never move
- Wipe it off and abandon the airway plan
- Mark the carotid artery instead
: The relationship between the skin and the airway changes with position.

?? Should ultrasound delay front-of-neck access in a can't intubate, can't oxygenate emergency?
+ No
- Yes, until a clear image is saved
- Yes, if a linear probe is in the room
- Only for ten minutes
: The rescue follows the airway algorithm without delay. Ultrasound belongs in the preparation.
