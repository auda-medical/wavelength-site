The appendix testis is a small remnant at the upper pole of the testis. When it twists, it causes pain, swelling and redness which can look as dramatic as testicular torsion. It is most common in boys of about 7 to 14 years, and it settles with rest and analgesia. Testicular torsion does not. The scan helps tell them apart, but only after the clinical picture has been weighed.

*Scan images in the graphic are illustrations, not patient scans.*

## The scan

1. Explain the scan, offer a chaperone and record the offer.
2. Support the scrotum on a rolled towel. Use the linear probe with warm gel.
3. Start on the painless side to set the grey-scale and colour settings.
4. Look at each testis in two planes and compare the flow with the same colour settings.
5. Scan the upper pole slowly, between the top of the testis and the head of the epididymis. This is where the point of tenderness usually is.
6. Follow the spermatic cord up towards the groin in short axis.

## Reading the view

- **Torsion of the appendix testis:** a small round nodule at the upper pole, with no flow inside it and often increased flow around it. The testis itself has normal echoes and normal flow.
- **Reactive changes:** a small hydrocele and a thickened scrotal wall are common. They can make the painful side look worse than the tiny appendage.
- **Testicular torsion:** reduced or absent flow in the testis compared with the other side, and a twisted cord, the whirlpool sign. See the [testicular torsion pearl](/learn/testicular-torsion-ultrasound/).
- **Epididymo-orchitis:** an enlarged epididymis with increased flow and a straight cord.

> **The aha** Normal testicular flow plus a tender avascular nub at the upper pole suggests the appendix, not the testis.

> **Pitfall** Normal testicular flow does not exclude early or intermittent testicular torsion. If the story is sudden and severe, with vomiting or a high-riding testis, call urology, whatever the scan shows.

## Action

Never delay urology review for a scan when testicular torsion is possible. If the clinical picture and the scan agree on a twisted appendix testis, it is usually managed with rest and analgesia, with safety-netting and a clear plan to return if the pain worsens. Follow your local paediatric and urology pathways.

?? Where does a twisted appendix testis usually sit?
+ At the upper pole, between the testis and the head of the epididymis
- At the lower pole of the testis
- Inside the testis
- In the inguinal canal
: The appendix testis is a remnant attached at the upper pole.

?? Which pattern suggests torsion of the appendix testis rather than testicular torsion?
+ Normal testicular flow with a small avascular nodule at the upper pole
- Absent flow in the whole testis
- A whirlpool sign in the spermatic cord
- An enlarged, uneven testis
: The testis is spared. The twisted appendage has no flow, often with hyperaemia around it.

?? A 13-year-old has sudden severe pain, vomiting and a high-riding testis. The scan shows normal testicular flow. What next?
+ Urgent urology review, because normal flow does not exclude torsion
- Discharge with analgesia
- Diagnose appendix testis torsion and send home
- Repeat the scan next week
: Partial or intermittent torsion can preserve flow. The clinical picture decides.

?? Why can the painful side look dramatic in torsion of the appendix testis?
+ A reactive hydrocele and scrotal wall thickening surround a tiny appendage
- The testis infarcts
- The cord is twisted
- The epididymis ruptures
: Secondary inflammation can look worse than the small nodule which causes it.

?? Why start on the painless side?
+ To set the grey-scale and colour settings for a fair comparison
- Because the painful side cannot be scanned
- To save time
- Because the appendix is always on the left
: Comparing both sides with the same settings makes a difference in flow easier to judge.
