title: "Foreign bodies: bright, then read the shadow"
minutes: 12

X-ray shows glass and metal well, but misses wood, plastic and thorns. Ultrasound sees all of them as bright structures, and maps their depth and their relation to tendons and vessels before you cut.

## The scan

1. Use the linear probe with plenty of gel, or a stand-off for fingers.
2. Start at the wound and scan along the likely track, in two planes.
3. Look for a bright structure. Turn the probe until it lies along its length.
4. Note what lies behind it.
5. Mark its position on the skin in two planes, and measure its depth.
6. Note nearby tendons, nerves and vessels. Use colour Doppler for vessels.

## Reading the view

All foreign bodies are brighter than the surrounding tissue. What lies behind them gives a clue to the material:

- **Wood, plastic and thorns:** a bright line with a dark acoustic shadow behind it.
- **Glass and metal:** often reverberation, bright parallel lines or a comet tail behind the object, as well as shadowing. Smooth surfaces give the most reverberation.
- **A dark halo** around the object suggests inflammation, granulation tissue or an abscess. It often appears after a day or more, and makes the object easier to find.

These patterns have been described with surgical correlation in the radiology literature.

> **The aha** Find it, map it, then cut.

> **Pitfall** Gas from the wound, scar tissue, calcification and the edges of bone or sesamoids all mimic a foreign body. Scan in two planes, compare with the other side, and confirm the object is separate from normal anatomy.

## Before removal

- Mark the skin directly over the object, in two planes.
- Measure the depth and the angle.
- Note the nearest tendon, nerve and vessel.
- Consider removal under real-time guidance, with the needle or forceps in plane.
- Deep objects, objects near neurovascular bundles or joints, and those in the hand often need specialist removal.
