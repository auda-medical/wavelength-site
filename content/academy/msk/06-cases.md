Four patients. Read each case, choose your answer, then read the explanation. These cases do not count towards your score.

## Case 1: the splinter

A carpenter has a painful palm two days after a splinter injury. The X-ray is normal. Ultrasound shows a 9 mm bright line 6 mm under the skin, with a clean dark shadow behind it and a dark halo around it. A flexor tendon lies 4 mm deeper.

?? What does this show?
+ A retained wooden foreign body with surrounding inflammation, close to a flexor tendon
- A normal palm
- A glass fragment
- A tendon tear
: Wood is invisible on X-ray but bright on ultrasound, with a clean shadow. The dark halo shows inflammation around it. Its closeness to a flexor tendon in the hand means it needs careful planning or hand surgery review.

## Case 2: the dark Achilles

A 45-year-old felt a snap in his calf playing squash. A long-axis image shows a dark area in the Achilles tendon. When you heel-toe the probe, the fibres brighten and look continuous. The calf squeeze test moves the foot, and the other side looks the same.

?? What is the most likely explanation of the dark area?
+ Anisotropy
- A full-thickness tear
- A partial tear
- Tendinosis
: Darkness which disappears when you correct the angle is anisotropy. The tendon is intact on this scan. Correlate with the examination, and refer if clinical concern for a tear remains.

## Case 3: the shoulder from behind

A 30-year-old fell onto an outstretched arm. From behind, the humeral head lies much deeper than the glenoid rim and does not turn when you gently rotate the arm. After reduction, the head sits level with the glenoid and turns smoothly.

?? What did the first scan show?
+ An anterior dislocation
- A posterior dislocation
- A normal shoulder
- A fracture of the greater tuberosity
: From behind, a head lying deeper than the glenoid has moved forwards, an anterior dislocation. The repeat scan confirms reduction. Ultrasound does not exclude a fracture, so follow your imaging policy.

## Case 4: the limping child

A 5-year-old has refused to walk for a day after a cold. Temperature 38.9°C. The right hip capsule lies 7 mm from the femoral neck and bulges outwards. The left measures 3 mm.

?? What is the next step?
+ Urgent orthopaedic review, as septic arthritis needs to be excluded
- Diagnose transient synovitis and discharge
- Repeat the scan in a week
- Treat with ibuprofen and review in clinic
: The right hip has an effusion: over 5 mm and more than 2 mm wider than the left. Fever and refusal to bear weight raise concern for septic arthritis. Ultrasound cannot tell the two apart, so the child needs bloods, urgent orthopaedic review and likely aspiration.
