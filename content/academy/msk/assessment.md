?? What is anisotropy?
@ 01-technique
+ A tendon or nerve looking dark when the beam is not at 90° to its fibres
- A tear seen in two planes
- Shadowing behind bone
- Reverberation behind metal
: Fibrillar structures reflect the beam back to the probe only when it strikes them at right angles. Tilt the probe and they turn dark. Correcting the angle restores the bright fibres.

?? Why scan the same place on the other side?
@ 01-technique
+ A difference between the sides matters more than any single image
- To double the scan time
- To check the probe works
- To measure the patient's height
: Normal appearances vary between people. The other side, with the same settings, shows what is normal for this patient. Asymmetry is the key sign for hip effusion and many soft tissue findings.

?? Which foreign body is invisible on X-ray but visible on ultrasound?
@ 02-foreign-body
+ Wood
- Metal
- Most glass
- Gravel
: Wood, plastic and thorns are radiolucent. Ultrasound shows them as bright structures with a shadow. Metal, most glass and gravel show well on X-ray.

?? What appears behind a glass or metal foreign body on ultrasound?
@ 02-foreign-body
+ Reverberation, often with shadowing
- Nothing, because they are invisible
- Increased colour flow
- A dark halo only
: Smooth glass and metal surfaces reflect the beam back and forth, giving bright parallel reverberation lines or a comet tail, often with shadowing.

?? What does a dark halo around a foreign body suggest?
@ 02-foreign-body
+ Inflammation, granulation tissue or an abscess around it
- That the object is metal
- That the object has dissolved
- A normal finding immediately after injury
: Tissue reaction around a retained object forms a dark rim, often after a day or more. It makes the object easier to find and suggests infection or inflammation.

?? What do you record before removing a foreign body?
@ 02-foreign-body
+ Its position in two planes, its depth, and the nearest tendon, nerve and vessel
- Only its length
- Only whether it is visible on X-ray
- The colour of the skin
: Mapping the object in two planes, with its depth and relation to structures, guides the incision and shows when specialist removal is safer.

?? A dark area in a tendon brightens when you heel-toe the probe. What is it?
@ 03-tendon
+ Anisotropy
- A partial tear
- A full-thickness tear
- Tendinitis
: A tear stays dark whatever the angle. Darkness which disappears when you correct the angle is anisotropy.

?? Which finding supports a full-thickness tendon tear?
@ 03-tendon
+ Fibres broken across the whole tendon, with ends which do not glide together on movement
- Darkness which changes with probe angle
- Bright parallel fibres in long axis
- A bright speckled oval in short axis
: A full tear separates the tendon ends, often with fluid between them. Gentle movement shows the ends moving independently.

?? From behind, the humeral head lies closer to the probe than the glenoid. What does this show?
@ 04-shoulder
+ A posterior dislocation
- An anterior dislocation
- A normal shoulder
- A rotator cuff tear
: The posterior view looks from behind. A head lying superficial, closer to the probe, has moved backwards.

?? What does shoulder ultrasound not exclude?
@ 04-shoulder
+ A fracture of the greater tuberosity, glenoid rim or humeral neck
- An anterior dislocation
- A posterior dislocation
- A failed reduction
: Ultrasound shows the position of the head. Fractures need X-ray when the mechanism, age or examination raises concern.

?? In a child, which finding defines a hip effusion on ultrasound?
@ 05-hip
+ A capsule-to-neck distance over 5 mm, or more than 2 mm wider than the other side
- Any fluid in the anterior recess
- A capsule-to-neck distance under 2 mm
- Increased colour flow in the femoral head
: ACEP guidance uses over 5 mm, or a difference of more than 2 mm between sides. Comparing sides is the most useful check.

?? A febrile child has a hip effusion. What can ultrasound not tell you?
@ 05-hip
+ Whether the effusion is septic arthritis or transient synovitis
- Whether there is fluid in the hip
- Where the femoral vessels lie
- Which side is affected
: Both conditions cause an effusion. Fever, refusal to bear weight and raised inflammatory markers raise concern for septic arthritis, which needs urgent orthopaedic review and aspiration.

?? Squeezing the calf of a prone patient moves the Achilles tendon above a gap but not below it. What is the diagnosis?
@ 07-tendon-ruptures
+ A complete Achilles tendon rupture
- A normal Achilles tendon
- Anisotropy
- Achilles tendinopathy without a tear
: In a complete rupture, movement from the calf does not pass across the gap. In a normal tendon or tendinopathy, the whole tendon moves as one.

?? Why can a scan which stops at the elbow crease miss a complete distal biceps rupture?
@ 07-tendon-ruptures
+ An intact lacertus fibrosus can hold the torn tendon near its normal position
- The distal biceps always tears at the shoulder
- The tendon is invisible on ultrasound
- Colour Doppler is needed to see the tendon
: The bicipital aponeurosis limits retraction. Only following the tendon to the radial tuberosity shows whether it still inserts on the bone.
