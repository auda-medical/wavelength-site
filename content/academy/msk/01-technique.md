Musculoskeletal ultrasound rewards three habits. They prevent most of the errors new scanners make.

## The probe

- Use the high-frequency linear probe for superficial structures.
- Switch to the curvilinear probe for the hip in adults, a large shoulder, or deep structures.
- Use plenty of gel. A thick layer, or a stand-off, helps for very superficial structures such as fingers.

## Habit one: the angle

Tendons, ligaments and nerves are made of parallel fibres. They look bright only when the beam strikes them at 90°. Tilt the probe a few degrees and a healthy tendon turns dark. This is anisotropy.

- In long axis, rock the probe along the structure, heel then toe, until the fibres brighten.
- In short axis, tilt from side to side.

## Habit two: two planes

A single image misleads. A round dark structure in one plane may be a vessel, a cyst or a tendon. Turn 90° and see whether it runs as a tube, ends, or branches.

## Habit three: the other side

When in doubt, scan the same place on the other side with the same settings. A difference between the sides matters more than any single measurement.

> **The aha** Angle, two planes, the other side. Most musculoskeletal doubts settle with these three.

> **Pitfall** Ultrasound shows soft tissues and the surface of bone. It does not exclude a fracture. Follow your imaging policy and X-ray when the mechanism or examination raises concern.
