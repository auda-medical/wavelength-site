Every block follows the same safety system. The anatomy changes, the system does not.

## Before the needle

1. **Consent.** Explain the benefit, the alternatives and the risks: failure, bleeding, infection, nerve injury, local anaesthetic toxicity and the block-specific risks. Record the discussion.
2. **Weight and dose.** Weigh the patient or estimate the weight. Calculate the maximum safe dose of your local anaesthetic using your trust's guideline or the BNF. Use lean body weight in obesity. Count every dose the patient will receive, including skin infiltration and any other block.
3. **Monitoring and access.** Attach ECG, blood pressure and oxygen saturation monitoring. Secure IV access.
4. **Rescue ready.** Know where the lipid emulsion is, before you start.
5. **Record the neurovascular examination** of the limb before any block.

## Stop Before You Block

Wrong-side blocks still happen, usually after a distraction between the sign-in and the needle. The national Stop Before You Block campaign adds one more check: immediately before the needle touches the skin, stop and confirm the side with the site marking and, where possible, with the patient.

> **The aha** The last check comes after the scan and the skin preparation, at the moment the needle is in your hand.

## During the block

- **Keep the needle tip in view.** Use an in-plane approach where you can, and move the probe, not the needle, when you lose the tip.
- **Hydrodissect.** Inject 0.5 to 1 mL first and watch where it goes. Fluid opening the right plane confirms the tip position.
- **Aspirate and inject in small aliquots**, watching the spread every time.
- **Stop** if the patient reports sharp, shooting pain, if you feel high pressure, if the nerve swells, or if you cannot see the spread. Each suggests the tip is in the wrong place, in a nerve or in a vessel.

## Local anaesthetic systemic toxicity

Toxicity happens when local anaesthetic reaches the circulation in high concentration, from an intravascular injection or from absorption of a large dose. It can appear during the injection or later, as a large-volume plane block is absorbed.

### Recognise it

- **Early:** tingling around the mouth, a metallic taste, ringing in the ears, light-headedness, agitation or confusion.
- **Later:** seizures, a falling level of consciousness, bradycardia, broad-complex arrhythmias, cardiovascular collapse.

Cardiovascular collapse can come with no warning symptoms.

### Manage it

Following the Association of Anaesthetists guideline:

1. Stop injecting and call for help.
2. Manage the airway, give 100% oxygen and ventilate if needed.
3. Treat seizures with a benzodiazepine.
4. If the circulation fails, start advanced life support. Expect a long resuscitation.
5. Give 20% lipid emulsion: a bolus of 1.5 mL/kg over 1 minute, then an infusion at 15 mL/kg/h.
6. If the circulation has not recovered after 5 minutes, give up to two further boluses 5 minutes apart and double the infusion rate to 30 mL/kg/h.
7. Do not exceed a cumulative lipid dose of 12 mL/kg.

> **Pitfall** Do not use lidocaine as an antiarrhythmic in local anaesthetic toxicity. It adds to the toxic dose.

## After the block

- Repeat the observations and the neurovascular examination.
- Observe with monitoring for the time your trust's guideline sets.
- Document the block, side, drug, concentration, volume, total dose, time, operator, complications and the neurovascular examination before and after.
- Give the patient written advice on protecting a numb limb and when the block will wear off.
