?? What does a certificate for this module show?
@ 01-context
+ That you have completed the theory, not that you are competent to perform the blocks
- That you can perform the blocks without supervision
- That your trust has approved you for regional anaesthesia
- That you hold a national accreditation in nerve blocks
: The module teaches the safety system, the anatomy, the needle path and the evidence. Competence comes from supervised practice on patients under your trust's governance.

?? When does the Stop Before You Block check take place?
@ 02-safety
+ Immediately before the needle touches the skin
- At the start of the consent discussion
- When the patient arrives in the department
- After the injection, before documentation
: Wrong-side blocks often follow a distraction between the earlier checks and the needle. The final check of the side happens with the needle in your hand.

?? Which group of symptoms suggests early local anaesthetic systemic toxicity?
@ 02-safety
+ Tingling around the mouth, a metallic taste and ringing in the ears
- Numbness in the territory of the block
- Itching and a rash at the injection site
- Pain at the injection site for a few seconds
: Early toxicity affects the brain: tingling around the mouth, a metallic taste, tinnitus, light-headedness and agitation. Seizures and cardiovascular collapse can follow, sometimes with no warning.

?? What is the initial dose of 20% lipid emulsion in local anaesthetic systemic toxicity?
@ 02-safety
+ A bolus of 1.5 mL/kg over 1 minute, then an infusion of 15 mL/kg/h
- A bolus of 10 mL/kg over 10 minutes
- 50 mL over 30 minutes for every patient
- An infusion of 1 mL/kg/h with no bolus
: The Association of Anaesthetists guideline gives 1.5 mL/kg over 1 minute, then 15 mL/kg/h. Up to two further boluses can follow at 5-minute intervals, with the infusion doubled to 30 mL/kg/h, to a cumulative maximum of 12 mL/kg.

?? During injection the patient reports sharp, shooting pain and you feel high pressure. What do you do?
@ 02-safety
+ Stop injecting and reposition the needle
- Inject faster to complete the block
- Increase the volume
- Continue, as this confirms nerve contact
: Sharp pain and high pressure suggest the tip is in or against a nerve. Injecting into a nerve risks injury. Stop, withdraw slightly and reposition.

?? Where does the greater occipital nerve run at the superior nuchal line?
@ 03-occipital
+ Beside the occipital artery, usually on its medial side
- Inside the vertebral artery sheath
- Lateral to the mastoid process
- Deep to the C2 vertebral body
: At the superior nuchal line, the nerve lies close to the occipital artery, usually medial to it. Colour Doppler finds the artery and guides you to the nerve.

?? Which patient with headache needs investigation rather than an occipital nerve block?
@ 03-occipital
+ A patient whose headache peaked within a minute of onset
- A patient with known migraine and occipital tenderness
- A patient with migraine which persists after metoclopramide
- A patient with a typical migraine aura that has resolved
: A thunderclap onset raises concern for subarachnoid haemorrhage. A block could mask the pain without treating the cause.

?? In a serratus anterior plane block, why do you aim the needle at the rib?
@ 04-serratus
+ The rib acts as a backstop and keeps the needle away from the pleura
- The nerves run inside the rib
- The rib absorbs the local anaesthetic
- It shortens the onset time
: The pleura lies deeper, between the ribs. Keeping the tip on or just above the rib protects against pneumothorax.

?? Why is the total dose the main safety concern in a serratus anterior plane block?
@ 04-serratus
+ Plane blocks need large volumes to spread, which raises the total dose
- The serratus muscle concentrates local anaesthetic
- The block is always intravascular
- The block needs no calculation of dose
: Fascial plane blocks rely on volume. Large volumes bring the total dose close to the maximum, so calculate the safe dose for the patient's weight before you start.

?? Why does a superior trunk block affect the diaphragm less often than an interscalene block?
@ 05-shoulder
+ The injection sits further from the phrenic nerve, which lies close to the C5 root
- The superior trunk has no motor fibres
- The phrenic nerve runs inside the superior trunk
- The block uses a local anaesthetic which spares the diaphragm
: The phrenic nerve runs on anterior scalene close to the C5 root. Moving the injection down to the superior trunk increases the distance, and a meta-analysis found about 90% less complete hemidiaphragmatic paralysis.

?? Which patient should not have a superior trunk block?
@ 05-shoulder
+ A patient with a known paralysed diaphragm on the other side
- A young patient with a first anterior dislocation
- A patient who wants to avoid sedation
- A patient with a proximal humeral fracture
: The block can still affect the phrenic nerve on the injected side. With the other diaphragm already paralysed, the patient could lose both.

?? During a SHAC block, which landmark do you keep medial to the needle?
@ 05-shoulder
+ The coracoid process
- The acromion
- The greater tuberosity
- The clavicle
: The axillary artery, axillary vein and brachial plexus run medial to the coracoid. Staying lateral to it keeps the needle away from them.
