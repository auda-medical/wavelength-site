The Wavelength Pearl [PENG block: find the ramus, then lift the tendon](/learn/peng-block-find-the-ramus/) summarises the technique.

## The idea

The pericapsular nerve group block was described in 2018 for hip fracture. Anatomical studies showed the articular branches of the femoral, obturator and accessory obturator nerves run between the psoas tendon and the superior pubic ramus, near the iliopubic eminence. The PENG block places local anaesthetic in this plane. It targets the joint rather than the thigh, so it often spares the quadriceps.

## On the screen

- **Anterior inferior iliac spine:** the starting point, a bony bump at the top of the image.
- **Iliopubic eminence and superior pubic ramus:** a bright sloping line of bone.
- **Psoas tendon:** a bright oval lying on the bone, with iliopsoas muscle around it.
- **Femoral artery:** round and pulsating, medial.

## Technique

1. Supine. A curvilinear probe suits most adults, a linear probe slim patients.
2. Start transverse over the anterior inferior iliac spine.
3. Rotate about 45 degrees to align with the pubic ramus, and slide until the iliopubic eminence, psoas tendon and femoral artery appear together.
4. In-plane needle from lateral to medial, until the tip touches bone under the psoas tendon.
5. Inject 2 to 3 mL and watch the tendon lift off the bone. Then inject the rest, usually about 20 mL.

## Coverage and limits

- **Covers:** the front of the hip capsule, the main source of pain from a fractured neck of femur on movement.
- **Does not cover:** the skin of the lateral thigh, or the back of the joint. For surgery, it is often combined with a lateral femoral cutaneous nerve block.
- **Evidence:** promising, with small trials and series. Compared with the fascia iliaca block, results are mixed for pain, with less quadriceps weakness.

## Risks

- Quadriceps weakness, if the fluid reaches the femoral nerve. Higher volumes and a superficial tip increase this.
- Vascular puncture, as the femoral artery lies close on the medial side.
- Local anaesthetic toxicity.

> **The aha** Bone is the backstop. Touch the ramus under the tendon, then lift the tendon with fluid.

> **Pitfall** The PENG block is newer, and fewer emergency clinicians are trained in it. Use it within your trust's pathway, and keep the fascia iliaca block as your standard if that is what your department supports.
