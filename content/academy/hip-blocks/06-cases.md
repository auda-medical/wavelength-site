Five patients from the emergency department. Read each case, choose your answer, then read the explanation. These cases do not count towards your score.

## Case 1: the confused faller

An 89-year-old woman with dementia has a fractured neck of femur. She is agitated and cries out when moved. She weighs about 48 kg. She lacks capacity to consent to a block.

?? What is the best approach?
+ A fascia iliaca block in her best interests, with a dose calculated for 48 kg, following the trust's process
- No block, as she cannot consent
- Intramuscular morphine only
- A block with the standard adult volume and concentration, without a dose calculation
: Best interests decisions allow treatment for a patient who lacks capacity. Her weight limits the maximum dose, which may decide the concentration for a 30 to 40 mL volume.

## Case 2: the femoral shaft

A 7-year-old boy has a midshaft femoral fracture after falling from a trampoline. He needs a splint.

?? Which block suits him?
+ A femoral nerve block, with a weight-based dose
- A PENG block
- A digital block
- No block, as children cannot have nerve blocks
: The femoral nerve supplies most of the femur. A femoral nerve block is often the best first analgesic for a femoral shaft fracture in a child.

## Case 3: fluid in the wrong place

During an infra-inguinal fascia iliaca block, the first 3 mL forms a pool around sartorius above the bright fascial line.

?? What do you do?
+ Stop, advance the tip through fascia iliaca and test again before giving the volume
- Inject the full volume, as it will spread down
- Abandon the block
- Inject into the femoral nerve instead
: Fluid above the fascia will not reach the nerves. Reposition under the fascia and confirm the lift before you inject the rest.

## Case 4: the mobile patient

A 70-year-old man with a fractured neck of femur wants to stay as mobile as possible, and the orthogeriatric team is concerned about falls on the ward.

?? Which block is most likely to spare his quadriceps?
+ A PENG block
- A femoral nerve block
- A high-volume infra-inguinal fascia iliaca block
- A 3-in-1 block
: The PENG block targets the articular branches and often spares the quadriceps. Femoral and fascia iliaca blocks commonly weaken it.

## Case 5: tingling after the block

Twenty minutes after a 40 mL fascia iliaca block, the patient becomes confused and complains of a metallic taste. Her blood pressure falls.

?? What do you do first?
+ Call for help, give oxygen, prepare lipid emulsion and treat as local anaesthetic toxicity
- Assume it is delirium from the fracture
- Give more local anaesthetic
- Discharge her to the ward
: Plane blocks absorb slowly, so toxicity can appear after the injection has finished. A metallic taste, confusion and falling blood pressure need immediate treatment.
