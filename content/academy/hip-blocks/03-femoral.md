The Wavelength Pearl [Femoral nerve block: artery first, nerve next, then wrap it](/learn/femoral-nerve-block/) summarises the technique.

## Anatomy

At the inguinal crease, the order from lateral to medial is nerve, artery, vein. The femoral artery and vein lie inside the femoral sheath. The nerve lies outside it, deep to fascia iliaca and on iliopsoas. This matters: local anaesthetic must go under fascia iliaca to reach the nerve.

The femoral nerve supplies the quadriceps, the skin of the front and inner thigh, most of the femur, the front of the knee, branches to the front of the hip capsule, and, through the saphenous nerve, the inner side of the lower leg.

## On the screen

- **Femoral artery:** round, black, pulsating, with flow on Doppler.
- **Femoral vein:** medial, compressible.
- **Fascia iliaca:** a bright line running from lateral to medial, passing deep to the artery.
- **Femoral nerve:** a bright, flattened oval or triangle, lateral to the artery, under the fascia.

## Technique

1. Supine, leg straight.
2. Linear probe transverse, just below the inguinal ligament.
3. Find the artery, then the nerve lateral to it.
4. In-plane needle from lateral to medial.
5. Pass through fascia iliaca. Inject 1 to 2 mL and confirm fluid lifting the fascia around the nerve.
6. Complete the block with 10 to 20 mL, aiming for fluid above and below the nerve.

## When to choose it

- **Femoral shaft fracture**, in adults and children, where it is often the best first analgesic.
- **Knee injury** needing manipulation or immobilisation.
- **Hip fracture**, where it works, though it does not reach the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve.

## Risks

- **Quadriceps weakness.** The patient must not stand on the leg. Warn the ward and record it.
- **Vascular puncture**, reduced by the lateral approach and Doppler.
- **Nerve injury**, reduced by stopping for pain or high pressure.
- **Masking compartment syndrome** in a high-risk injury. Agree a plan for monitoring first.

> **The aha** If the fluid does not lift fascia iliaca, it will not reach the nerve, however close it looks.
