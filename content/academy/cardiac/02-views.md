---
title: "The four views"
minutes: 15
---

Use the phased-array probe and the cardiac preset. On the cardiac preset the marker dot sits on the right of the screen. The steps below follow that convention. Lie the patient flat or slightly left side down for the parasternal and apical views, and flat with the knees bent for the subcostal view.

## Parasternal long axis (PLAX)

1. Place the probe at the left sternal border, third or fourth intercostal space.
2. Point the marker towards the patient's right shoulder.
3. Slide up or down a space, and rock or tilt, until the septum and the posterior wall lie close to horizontal.
4. You see the RV at the top, the LV with the mitral valve, the aortic outflow and aortic valve, and the left atrium behind.
5. Increase the depth until you see the descending thoracic aorta in cross-section behind the left atrium. You need it to place any fluid.

## Parasternal short axis (PSAX)

1. From PLAX, rotate the probe 90° clockwise, so the marker points to the patient's left shoulder.
2. Tilt until the LV looks round, with the papillary muscles at the mid level.
3. The RV wraps around the LV as a crescent. A D-shaped LV, with the septum flattened, suggests RV pressure or volume overload.

## Apical four-chamber (A4C)

1. Find the apex beat, usually in the fifth intercostal space, anterior or mid-axillary line. Roll the patient onto the left side if you can.
2. Point the beam towards the right shoulder, marker towards the patient's left.
3. All four chambers appear, with the ventricles at the top and the atria at the bottom. Compare RV and LV size: the RV is normally smaller than two thirds of the LV.

## Subcostal four-chamber

1. Place the probe just below the xiphoid, slightly to the patient's right, so the liver becomes your window.
2. Hold the probe from above, palm down, marker to the patient's left.
3. Lay the probe almost flat and aim at the left shoulder. Increase the depth. Ask for a deep breath in and hold.
4. The liver fills the near field. The RV lies against it, the LV beyond. The pericardium is the bright line around the heart.

> **The aha** In the subcostal view, flatter is better. Bowel gas means drop your hand, not press harder.

> **Pitfall** One view is never enough. If a finding matters, confirm it in a second view.
