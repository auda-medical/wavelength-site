## The common errors

- **Off-axis views.** A foreshortened apical view makes the LV look round and the RV look large. A tilted PLAX makes EPSS read high.
- **Calling fat an effusion.** Fat sits in front of the RV only. Fluid collects behind the heart first.
- **Calling pleural fluid pericardial.** Find the descending aorta.
- **Measuring the aorta as the IVC.** Follow the vessel into the right atrium.
- **Pressing on the neck.** The IJV collapses under the probe.
- **One view, one answer.** Confirm important findings in a second view.
- **Over-reading a normal scan.** A normal FoCUS does not exclude pulmonary embolism, dissection or an acute coronary syndrome.

## In cardiac arrest

- Scan only during the pulse check. Place the probe in the subcostal position before the pause.
- Record a clip of no more than 10 seconds. Review it after compressions restart.
- Report what you saw: cardiac activity present or absent, effusion present or absent, RV dilated or not. Do not stop resuscitation on ultrasound alone.

## What to document

1. The clinical question.
2. The views obtained, and the views you could not obtain.
3. Each finding as present, absent or not assessed: LV function (hyperdynamic, normal, reduced, severely reduced), EPSS if measured, pericardial effusion and its size, signs of tamponade, RV size, IVC size and change with breathing, collapse point height if assessed.
4. Your conclusion and how it changes management.
5. Whether you saved images, and whether a formal echocardiogram is requested.

## Example entry

> Focused cardiac ultrasound. Question: cause of hypotension. PLAX, PSAX and subcostal views. LV function reduced, EPSS 15 mm. No pericardial effusion. RV not dilated. IVC 2.3 cm, less than 50% change with sniff. Apical view not obtained. Conclusion: findings support a cardiogenic cause. Images saved. Formal echocardiogram requested. Discussed with medical registrar.

## When to escalate

- Pericardial effusion with any sign supporting tamponade.
- Dilated RV in a shocked patient.
- Severely reduced LV function in a patient you are planning to give large volumes of fluid.
- Any finding you are not signed off to act on.

> **The aha** "Not assessed" protects your patient. A confident wrong answer does not.
