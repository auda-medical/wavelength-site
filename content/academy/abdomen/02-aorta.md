---
title: "The aorta: see the whole length"
minutes: 12
---

An abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) kills when it ruptures, and it hides behind presentations which look like renal colic, back pain or diverticulitis. The scan only excludes an aneurysm when you see the whole length.

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear probe, transverse, in the epigastrium.
2. Find the vertebral body, a bright curve with a shadow. The aorta sits just in front of it, slightly to the patient's left. The IVC lies to the right, thin-walled and compressible.
3. Follow the aorta down, past the coeliac axis and the superior mesenteric artery.
4. Continue to the bifurcation into the common iliac arteries, usually around the umbilicus.
5. Measure the largest diameter in transverse, outer wall to outer wall, at a right angle to the vessel.
6. Confirm in long axis.

## Reading the view

- **Normal:** under 3 cm across.
- **Aneurysm:** 3 cm or more. The NHS screening programme uses this threshold.
- **Mural thrombus:** grey material lining the wall. Measuring the black lumen alone underestimates the size, so always measure outer wall to outer wall.

> **The aha** An aneurysm is a diagnosis you make with a tape measure: outer wall to outer wall, at right angles, along the whole length.

> **Pitfall** Ultrasound shows the aneurysm, not whether it has ruptured. Most ruptures bleed into the retroperitoneum, which ultrasound sees poorly. A known or newly found AAA with pain or hypotension is a rupture until proven otherwise.

## Clearing bowel gas

- Steady, firm pressure for several seconds.
- Move the probe to the side and angle back in.
- Ask the patient to bend the knees.
- Use the liver as a window from the right flank in a coronal view.

## Acting on the result

- **AAA with pain or hypotension:** senior and vascular surgical team immediately. Permissive hypotension. Do not delay transfer for CT if the patient is unstable.
- **AAA found incidentally:** refer for vascular follow-up.
- **Incomplete scan:** record it as incomplete. Do not call it negative.
