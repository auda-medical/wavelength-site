title: Final assessment
---

?? Which normal finding, when seen well, excludes the condition being looked for?
@ 01-context
+ A normal aorta seen along its whole length, for abdominal aortic aneurysm
- Not seeing the appendix, for appendicitis
- No hydronephrosis, for a ureteric stone
- Still bowel loops, for small bowel obstruction
: A well-seen aorta under 3 cm from the diaphragm to the bifurcation excludes an aneurysm. Not seeing the appendix is non-diagnostic, no hydronephrosis does not exclude a stone, and late obstruction loses peristalsis.

?? At what diameter is the abdominal aorta aneurysmal?
@ 02-aorta
+ 3 cm or more
- 2 cm or more
- 4 cm or more
- 5.5 cm or more
: An abdominal aorta of 3 cm or more is aneurysmal, the threshold used by the NHS screening programme. 5.5 cm is the usual threshold for considering elective repair, not for diagnosis.

?? How do you measure the abdominal aorta?
@ 02-aorta
+ Outer wall to outer wall, at a right angle to the vessel, at its widest point
- Inner wall to inner wall in long axis
- Across the black lumen only
- At the level of the coeliac axis only
: Measuring the lumen alone ignores mural thrombus and underestimates the size. Measure outer wall to outer wall, at right angles to the vessel, along the whole length.

?? Why does a normal free fluid scan not exclude AAA rupture?
@ 02-aorta
+ Most ruptures bleed into the retroperitoneum, which ultrasound sees poorly
- Blood in the abdomen is always anechoic
- Ruptures only happen above the renal arteries
- Free fluid takes days to appear
: Most ruptured AAAs bleed into the retroperitoneum first. An aneurysm with pain or hypotension is a rupture until proven otherwise, whatever the free fluid scan shows.

?? Which landmark leads you to the gallbladder neck?
@ 03-gallbladder
+ The main lobar fissure, running from the portal vein
- The falciform ligament
- The right kidney
- The inferior vena cava
: The main lobar fissure is a bright line in the liver running from the portal vein to the gallbladder neck. Following it finds the gallbladder even when it is small or contracted.

?? A patient with ascites has a 5 mm gallbladder wall, no stones and no sonographic Murphy's sign. How do you interpret this?
@ 03-gallbladder
+ Wall thickening explained by ascites, not evidence of cholecystitis on its own
- Acute calculous cholecystitis
- Biliary colic
- An impacted stone in the gallbladder neck
: A recent meal, ascites, heart failure, low albumin and hepatitis all thicken the wall. Cholecystitis needs the combination of stones and signs of inflammation in the right clinical context.

?? How do you tell hydronephrosis from parapelvic cysts?
@ 04-kidneys
+ Hydronephrosis forms connected, branching black spaces joining the pelvis. Cysts stay round and separate
- Cysts fill with colour on Doppler
- Hydronephrosis only occurs on the left
- Cysts are always larger than 5 cm
: Follow each black space. A dilated collecting system connects calyces to the pelvis. Parapelvic cysts do not connect. Sinus vessels, not cysts, fill with colour.

?? What did the STONE trial show?
@ 04-kidneys
+ Starting with point-of-care ultrasound rather than CT reduced radiation, with no increase in high-risk missed diagnoses
- Ultrasound measures stone size better than CT
- CT is never needed in renal colic
- Ultrasound missed more serious diagnoses than CT
: In suspected nephrolithiasis, an ultrasound-first approach reduced cumulative radiation exposure without increasing high-risk diagnoses with complications. CT still has a role when the diagnosis is uncertain.

?? A febrile patient has an obstructed left kidney on ultrasound. What is the priority?
@ 04-kidneys
+ Urgent urology review for drainage
- Outpatient urology follow-up
- Oral antibiotics and discharge
- A repeat scan in a week
: An infected, obstructed kidney is a urological emergency. It needs antibiotics, resuscitation and urgent drainage.

?? Which finding suggests mechanical small bowel obstruction rather than ileus?
@ 05-bowel
+ A transition point between dilated and collapsed bowel
- Reduced peristalsis throughout the bowel
- Dilatation of both small and large bowel
- No movement in any loop
: A transition point shows where the blockage lies. Ileus causes generalised dilatation with reduced movement and no transition point.

?? Which group of findings raises concern for strangulated small bowel?
@ 05-bowel
+ A thick bowel wall, free fluid between loops and a still segment
- To-and-fro movement in dilated loops
- A keyboard pattern of thin folds
- Collapsed colon
: Wall thickening, fluid between loops and loss of peristalsis in a segment suggest compromised blood supply. To-and-fro movement and the keyboard pattern support obstruction but not strangulation.

?? Which appendix finding supports appendicitis?
@ 06-appendix-intussusception
+ A blind-ending, non-compressible tube over 6 mm across
- A compressible tube under 6 mm
- A tube with peristalsis
- Not seeing the appendix
: An inflamed appendix is wide, rigid and ends blindly. A compressible tube under 6 mm is normal. Peristalsis belongs to bowel. Not seeing the appendix is non-diagnostic.

?? What did the 2017 meta-analysis of emergency physician appendix ultrasound find?
@ 06-appendix-intussusception
+ High specificity, with lower and more variable sensitivity
- High sensitivity, so a negative scan excludes appendicitis
- Accuracy too low for clinical use
- Accuracy equal to CT in all patients
: A positive scan reliably supports appendicitis. A negative or non-diagnostic scan does not exclude it. Follow local pathways when the scan does not show the appendix.

?? Which two signs together confirm intussusception?
@ 06-appendix-intussusception
+ A target in transverse and a sandwich or pseudokidney in long axis
- A target in transverse and free fluid
- Dilated small bowel and to-and-fro movement
- A blind-ending tube and fat stranding
: The target sign shows bowel inside bowel in cross-section. The sandwich or pseudokidney sign shows the same layers in long axis. Seeing both in the same place confirms the diagnosis.

?? A small, central target in a well child disappears on rescanning. What is it most likely to be?
@ 06-appendix-intussusception
+ A transient small-bowel intussusception
- An ileocolic intussusception
- A lymph node
- An appendicolith
: Transient small-bowel intussusceptions are smaller, central or left-sided, lack a lead point and usually reduce on their own. An ileocolic intussusception is larger, right-sided and persists.
