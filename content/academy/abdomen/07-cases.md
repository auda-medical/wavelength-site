Five patients from the emergency department. Read each case, choose your answer, then read the explanation. These cases do not count towards your score.

## Case 1: the renal colic

A 71-year-old man has sudden left loin pain radiating to the groin, and blood on urine dip. Blood pressure 98/60. Neither kidney shows hydronephrosis. In the epigastrium, the aorta measures 6.2 cm outer wall to outer wall, with mural thrombus.

?? What is the next step?
+ Call the senior and vascular team now, as a symptomatic AAA with possible rupture
- Treat as renal colic with analgesia
- Arrange outpatient vascular follow-up
- Arrange a CT KUB for stones
: A large AAA with pain and borderline blood pressure is a rupture until proven otherwise. Ultrasound sees retroperitoneal bleeding poorly, so a lack of free fluid does not reassure. Renal colic over 50 needs an aortic scan for exactly this reason.

## Case 2: the fatty meal

A 42-year-old woman has right upper quadrant pain and fever. The gallbladder contains several mobile stones, the anterior wall measures 5 mm, there is a thin rim of fluid around it, and pressing the probe over it reproduces her pain.

?? What do these findings support?
+ Acute calculous cholecystitis
- Biliary colic without inflammation
- A normal gallbladder after a meal
- Hepatitis
: Stones with a thick wall, pericholecystic fluid and a positive sonographic Murphy's sign support acute cholecystitis. Check the common bile duct and liver function, and refer to the surgical team.

## Case 3: the vomiting patient

A 66-year-old with previous abdominal surgery has vomiting and distension. You see fluid-filled small bowel loops of 3.4 cm with a keyboard pattern, contents moving back and forth, and collapsed bowel in the right lower quadrant. Between some loops lies free fluid, and one segment has a 5 mm wall and no movement.

?? What do you do?
+ Treat as small bowel obstruction with possible strangulation, and get urgent surgical review
- Treat as ileus and observe
- Discharge with laxatives
- Treat as gastroenteritis
: Dilated loops, to-and-fro movement and a transition point support mechanical obstruction. Free fluid between loops, a thick wall and a still segment raise concern for strangulation. This needs urgent surgical review and CT.

## Case 4: the right iliac fossa

A 19-year-old woman has right iliac fossa pain for 18 hours. With graded compression you see a blind-ending tube arising from the caecum, 9 mm across, which does not compress, with bright fat around it and a bright focus with a shadow inside.

?? What do these findings support?
+ Acute appendicitis with an appendicolith
- A normal appendix
- Terminal ileitis
- A non-diagnostic scan
: A non-compressible, blind-ending tube over 6 mm with surrounding fat change supports appendicitis. The bright focus with a shadow is an appendicolith. Refer to the surgical team. In a woman of this age, also consider the ovaries and pregnancy.

## Case 5: the crying toddler

An 18-month-old has episodes of screaming and drawing up the legs, with pale, quiet spells between them. In the right upper quadrant, under the liver, you see a 3.2 cm target of alternating rings in transverse, and layered bowel inside bowel in long axis.

?? What is the next step?
+ Urgent paediatric surgical and radiology referral for ileocolic intussusception
- Rescan in 24 hours
- Treat as constipation
- Discharge, as transient intussusceptions resolve
: A target in transverse and a sandwich in long axis, over 2.5 cm and in the right upper quadrant, fits ileocolic intussusception. It needs urgent reduction. Transient small-bowel intussusceptions are smaller and central or left-sided.
