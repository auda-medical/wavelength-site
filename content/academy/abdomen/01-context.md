Abdominal pain is one of the commonest emergency presentations. Bedside ultrasound answers focused questions quickly, without radiation, and often while the patient is still in the waiting area. Each scan in this module answers one question.

- **Is there an aortic aneurysm?** The aorta, end to end.
- **Is there gallstone disease or cholecystitis?** The gallbladder.
- **Is the kidney obstructed?** Both kidneys, then the bladder for ureteric jets.
- **Is the small bowel obstructed?** The small bowel, the whole abdomen.
- **Is there appendicitis?** The right lower quadrant.
- **In a child, is there intussusception?** The colon, right side first.

## Rule in, rarely rule out

Most of these scans are better at ruling in than ruling out. A clear positive finding speeds referral. A negative or incomplete scan often leaves the question open. The main exception is a normal aorta seen along its full length, which excludes an aneurysm. A well-seen, normal gallbladder in a fasting patient makes acute cholecystitis unlikely.

> **The aha** Before you start, decide what a positive scan and a negative scan would each change.

## Record what you saw, and what you did not

Every scan in this module has blind spots: bowel gas, body habitus, a retrocaecal appendix, an early obstruction. Write down the views you obtained, the views you could not, and whether the scan was diagnostic.

> **Pitfall** An unstable patient with abdominal pain needs a senior and the right team, not a perfect image. A 30-second look at the aorta and for free fluid is enough to direct care.
