title: "The gallbladder: follow the fissure"
minutes: 12

## Find it every time

1. Lie the patient supine. If bowel gas gets in the way, roll them onto their left side.
2. Place the curvilinear probe below the right costal margin, marker to the head, and ask for a breath in and hold.
3. Find the portal vein, a round black vessel with a bright wall at the liver hilum.
4. Follow the main lobar fissure, a bright line running from the portal vein through the liver, to the gallbladder neck.
5. Sweep from neck to fundus in long axis, then in short axis.
6. If the ribs get in the way, use the intercostal spaces in the anterior axillary line.

## What to look for

- **Stones:** bright, curved echoes with a black shadow behind them, which move when the patient rolls.
- **Wall thickness:** measure the anterior wall. Normal is under 3 mm.
- **Pericholecystic fluid:** a thin black rim around the gallbladder.
- **Sonographic Murphy's sign:** maximal tenderness when you press directly over the gallbladder with the probe.
- **The common bile duct:** in front of the portal vein at the hilum. A dilated duct raises the question of a stone in the duct.

## Putting it together

- **Stones, no other findings:** gallstones. The pain is often biliary colic.
- **Stones with a thick wall, pericholecystic fluid or a positive sonographic Murphy's sign:** supports acute cholecystitis.
- **Stone impacted in the neck, with a distended gallbladder:** supports obstruction.

> **The aha** The fissure is the road to the gallbladder. Find the portal vein, then follow the bright line.

> **Pitfall** A thick wall alone is not cholecystitis. A recent meal, ascites, heart failure, low albumin and hepatitis all thicken the wall. A packed gallbladder shows a bright line with a dense shadow and no lumen: do not mistake it for bowel gas.

## Acting on the result

Fever with jaundice, or signs of sepsis, raises the question of cholangitis and needs urgent senior review. Stones with cholecystitis need surgical referral and formal imaging according to local pathways.
