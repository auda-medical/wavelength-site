title: "The kidneys and ureteric jets"
minutes: 17
---

In suspected renal colic, ultrasound looks for hydronephrosis, checks the bladder for ureteric jets, and rules out alternatives. In the STONE trial, starting with point-of-care ultrasound rather than CT reduced radiation exposure, with no increase in high-risk missed diagnoses.

## The kidneys

1. Use the curvilinear probe with the patient supine.
2. Right kidney: mid to posterior axillary line, through the liver, marker to the head.
3. Left kidney: higher and further back, through the spleen. Put your knuckles on the bed.
4. Sweep each kidney in long and short axis. Compare the sides.

## Grading hydronephrosis

- **Normal:** grey cortex around a bright central renal sinus, with no black spaces.
- **Mild:** the renal pelvis is black, with calyces starting to fill.
- **Moderate:** rounded black calyces joining the pelvis.
- **Severe:** large black spaces with a thin rim of cortex.

## Mimics

- **Parapelvic cysts:** round and separate. They do not connect with each other or the pelvis.
- **Renal sinus vessels:** fill with colour on Doppler.
- **Extrarenal pelvis:** a pelvis outside the kidney, with normal calyces.
- **A full bladder:** causes mild dilatation on both sides. Rescan after voiding.

> **The aha** Hydronephrosis connects. Cysts do not.

## Ureteric jets

1. With a reasonably full bladder, scan transversely above the pubis, angled to the bladder base.
2. Put the colour box over both ureteric orifices, scale set low.
3. Watch for at least two to three minutes.

A jet on one side and none on the painful side, with hydronephrosis on that side, supports obstruction. Weak jets on both sides suggest low urine output or an underfilled bladder.

> **Pitfall** No hydronephrosis does not exclude a stone. Early obstruction and dehydration limit dilatation, and jets are intermittent.

## Pyonephrosis: the infected, obstructed kidney

Hydronephrosis in a patient with fever, rigors or low blood pressure is an obstructed, infected kidney until proven otherwise. Pus behind a stone behaves like an abscess. Antibiotics alone cannot clear it, and the kidney needs decompression by nephrostomy or ureteric stent.

- **Look for** echoes inside the dilated collecting system: floating debris, low-level echoes, or a fluid-debris level with the debris lying dependent. Bright spots with dirty shadowing suggest gas.
- **Check it is real.** Reduce the gain, switch on tissue harmonics and ask the patient to roll a little. Debris shifts and settles. Artefact does not.
- **Do not be reassured** by clear fluid. Thin pus can look anechoic. The decision rests on obstruction plus sepsis.

> **The aha** Not all hydronephrosis is clean fluid. Echoes that move and settle are real.

## Red flags

- **Over 50 with first-time renal colic:** scan the aorta.
- **Fever with an obstructed kidney:** urgent urology review for drainage.
- **A single kidney, or both sides obstructed:** urgent urology review.
