The Wavelength Pearl [Median nerve block: find the fascicles, then build a soft halo](/learn/median-nerve-block/) summarises the technique.

## Anatomy

In the forearm the median nerve runs between flexor digitorum superficialis and flexor digitorum profundus. A few centimetres above the wrist it moves superficial, between the tendons of flexor carpi radialis and palmaris longus, before entering the carpal tunnel.

The **palmar cutaneous branch** leaves the median nerve several centimetres above the wrist crease and runs over the carpal tunnel to supply the skin of the thenar eminence. A block at the wrist crease can miss it. Block in the mid to distal forearm.

## On the screen

The nerve is an oval with a honeycomb texture: dark fascicles within bright connective tissue. Tendons look similar near the wrist, with a bright fibrillar pattern.

The test is to slide the probe up the forearm. Tendons join their muscle bellies and disappear into dark muscle. The nerve keeps its honeycomb look all the way to the elbow. Tendons also show anisotropy: tilt the probe and they turn dark, while the nerve changes less.

## Technique

1. Forearm supported, palm up.
2. Linear probe transverse, a few centimetres above the wrist crease, then slide up until the nerve lies between the muscle bellies.
3. In-plane needle from either side.
4. Inject 0.5 mL and watch where it goes. Fluid spreading around the nerve confirms the tip position.
5. Move the tip above and below the nerve to complete a ring of fluid. Three to five millilitres is usually enough.

> **The aha** A complete ring, or halo, of fluid around the nerve gives a fast, reliable block with a small volume.

## Stop if

- The patient reports sharp, electric pain.
- You feel high resistance.
- The nerve swells rather than the fluid surrounding it.

Each suggests the tip is in the nerve. Withdraw slightly and reposition.

## After the block

Onset takes 10 to 20 minutes. Test sensation in the median territory before you start the procedure. Give the patient advice about protecting a numb hand from burns and injury until sensation returns.
