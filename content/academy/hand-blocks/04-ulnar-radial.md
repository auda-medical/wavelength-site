## The ulnar nerve

In the distal forearm the ulnar nerve runs on the ulnar side of the ulnar artery, covered by flexor carpi ulnaris. Close to the wrist the nerve and artery lie side by side. A few centimetres higher they separate, which gives a safer target.

The **dorsal cutaneous branch** leaves the ulnar nerve about 5 cm above the wrist, winds round the ulna and supplies the ulnar side of the back of the hand. A distal block misses it. For a wound on the back of the ulnar hand, block higher in the forearm or add a subcutaneous field block over the ulnar border of the wrist.

### Technique

1. Forearm supported, palm up.
2. Linear probe transverse on the ulnar side of the forearm. Find the ulnar artery with colour Doppler.
3. Look on the ulnar side of the artery for the honeycomb oval of the nerve.
4. Slide up until the nerve separates from the artery.
5. In-plane needle from the ulnar side, so it meets the nerve before the artery. Three to five millilitres around the nerve.

## The superficial radial nerve

The superficial branch of the radial nerve is purely sensory. In the mid forearm it runs on the radial side of the radial artery, under brachioradialis. About 7 cm above the wrist it passes under the brachioradialis tendon, becomes subcutaneous and divides into branches to the back of the thumb, the first web space and the radial side of the back of the hand.

### Technique

1. Forearm supported, thumb side up. Plenty of gel and a light hand.
2. Linear probe transverse on the radial side, in the middle third of the forearm.
3. Find the radial artery with Doppler and look on its radial side for a small honeycomb oval.
4. Follow it up and down to confirm it.
5. In-plane needle, clear of the artery and the cephalic vein. Two to four millilitres around the nerve.

If you cannot see the nerve near the wrist, a subcutaneous field block across the radial side of the wrist reaches its skin branches.

## Combining blocks

Many wounds sit near the border between two territories. Use the sensory map and the examination to plan. For a large wound of the hand, all three forearm blocks together give complete analgesia distal to the wrist. Add the doses together and keep the total within the maximum for the patient's weight.

> **The aha** Colour Doppler is your friend in both blocks. The artery is easy to find, and the nerve is its neighbour.

> **Pitfall** A small vein can look like a nerve. Doppler shows flow in the vein, and gentle pressure collapses it.
