The Wavelength Pearl [Digital nerve block: block the base, one deposit on each side](/learn/ultrasound-guided-digital-nerve-block/) summarises the technique.

## Anatomy

Each finger has two palmar digital nerves, which run with the digital arteries on each side of the flexor tendon sheath, and two smaller dorsal digital nerves on the back of the finger. In the index, middle and ring fingers, the palmar nerves supply the palmar surface and the back of the finger beyond the middle joint, including the nail bed. The dorsal nerves supply the back of the finger nearer the base. The thumb and little finger receive more of their dorsal supply from the dorsal nerves.

## Landmark or ultrasound

Landmark digital blocks work well and remain standard practice. Ultrasound adds three things: it shows the nerve and artery, it confirms the spread, and it lets you use smaller volumes. It is a good way to practise needle control on a superficial target.

## Technique

1. Hand palm up. Plenty of gel or a gel pad over the base of the finger.
2. High-frequency linear probe transverse over the palmar surface of the proximal phalanx.
3. Identify the bone, the flexor tendon on it, and the digital artery on each side with colour Doppler. The nerve is a tiny bright oval beside the artery.
4. Needle from the side of the finger. Place 1 to 2 mL beside the nerve.
5. Repeat on the other side.
6. For a wound on the back of the base of the finger, add a small subcutaneous injection across the back of the base for the dorsal nerves.

## Adrenaline in the fingers

The old teaching that adrenaline causes finger necrosis has not stood up. A prospective multicentre study of 3,110 hand and finger procedures using lidocaine with adrenaline found no finger loss. Lidocaine with adrenaline gives a longer block and a drier field. Follow your trust's guidance, and avoid it where the circulation to the finger is already poor.

## Pitfalls

- **Volume.** Large volumes around the base of the finger raise the pressure in a closed space. Keep each deposit small.
- **Missed side.** If one side stays painful, give that nerve its own deposit.
- **No examination.** Record sensation on both sides of the finger and tendon function before you block.

> **The aha** Two small deposits beat one big bolus.
