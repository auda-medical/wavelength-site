Hand wounds, burns, foreign bodies and fractures fill a large part of every emergency department shift. Infiltrating local anaesthetic into a wound hurts, distorts the edges and needs large volumes for big wounds. A nerve block in the forearm or at the base of a finger numbs the whole territory with a few millilitres and leaves the wound untouched.

This module covers four blocks:

- **Median nerve block** in the forearm, for the radial palm and the palmar thumb, index and middle fingers.
- **Ulnar nerve block** in the forearm, for the little finger side of the hand.
- **Superficial radial nerve block** in the forearm, for the back of the thumb and the radial back of the hand.
- **Digital nerve block** at the base of a finger.

A small emergency department study showed ultrasound-guided forearm blocks of all three nerves were feasible for hand procedures.

## The sensory map

- **Median nerve:** the radial side of the palm, and the palmar surface of the thumb, index, middle and the radial half of the ring finger, extending onto the back of these fingers beyond the middle joint.
- **Ulnar nerve:** the little finger, the ulnar half of the ring finger and the ulnar side of the palm. Its dorsal branch supplies the ulnar side of the back of the hand.
- **Superficial radial nerve:** the back of the thumb, the first web space and the radial side of the back of the hand.

Overlap between territories is common, so a wound near a border often needs two blocks.

## Examine before you block

A block removes sensation and makes a later examination impossible. Before any block, record:

1. **Sensation** in each nerve territory, including two-point discrimination on both sides of an injured finger.
2. **Tendon function:** flexion at each joint of each finger, tested separately, and extension.
3. **Circulation:** colour and capillary refill of each finger.

A missed digital nerve or flexor tendon injury is a common reason for complaints and claims after hand injuries. Documented findings before the block protect the patient and you.

## What this module is, and is not

This module teaches the theory: the safety system, the anatomy on the screen, the needle path and the evidence. It does not make you competent to perform a block. Hands-on skill comes from supervised practice under your trust's governance.

> **Before your first block** Check your trust's approved list of emergency department blocks, its training requirements and its sign-off process.

## Who should not have a block

- A patient who declines, or who cannot consent and does not need a block in their best interests.
- Infection at the injection site.
- Allergy to local anaesthetic.
- A block which would mask an evolving problem, such as compartment syndrome of the forearm, without a plan to monitor for it.
