## Case 1: the posterior fractures

A 76-year-old man has fractures of the sixth to ninth ribs on the left, close to the spine. He cannot cough.

?? Which block suits him best?
+ An erector spinae plane block on the left
- A serratus anterior plane block in the mid-axillary line
- A rectus sheath block
- A femoral nerve block
: The serratus anterior plane block covers lateral fractures less reliably near the spine. The erector spinae plane block places the injection close to the spine and spreads over several levels.

## Case 2: rib or transverse process

You scan 6 cm from the midline and see a rounded bony line with sliding pleura between the bones.

?? Where are you, and what do you do?
+ Over the ribs: slide towards the midline until you see the flat-topped transverse processes
- Over the transverse processes: insert the needle
- Over the laminae: insert the needle
- Over the spinous processes: move further out
: Rounded bone with pleura between means rib. The transverse processes lie about 3 cm from the midline and have flat tops.

## Case 3: the surgical abdomen

A 50-year-old woman has periumbilical pain and vomiting. A colleague suggests a rectus sheath block for comfort.

?? What do you advise?
+ Investigate the cause of the pain first, as the block treats only abdominal wall pain and could hide the signs
- Perform bilateral blocks and discharge her
- Perform the block below the arcuate line for better spread
- Give the block instead of analgesia and imaging
: Periumbilical pain with vomiting suggests a problem inside the abdomen. A rectus sheath block does not treat visceral pain and could mask examination findings.

## Case 4: the midline wound

A 64-year-old man has a painful infected midline laparotomy wound being packed in the emergency department. He weighs 60 kg.

?? What is the key safety step before bilateral rectus sheath blocks?
+ Calculate the maximum dose for 60 kg and split it between the two sides
- Use the maximum volume on each side regardless of weight
- Inject below the arcuate line
- Skip monitoring, as the blocks are superficial
: Bilateral blocks double the total dose. Calculate the maximum for the patient's weight and split it between the sides.
