?? What does a certificate for this module show?
@ 01-context
+ That you have completed the theory, not that you are competent to perform the blocks
- That you can perform the blocks without supervision
- That your trust has approved you for regional anaesthesia
- That you hold a national accreditation in nerve blocks
: Competence comes from supervised practice under your trust's governance.

?? Why is the dose the main safety concern in plane blocks?
@ 01-context
+ They rely on volume, which raises the total dose, and bilateral blocks double it
- They always enter a blood vessel
- They need no monitoring
- They use adrenaline in every case
: Spread needs volume, and volume brings dose. Calculate the maximum for the patient's weight first.

?? Which chest wall block has the strongest emergency department evidence for lateral rib fractures?
@ 01-context
+ The serratus anterior plane block
- The rectus sheath block
- The femoral nerve block
- The PENG block
: The SABRE trial showed more patients with a meaningful fall in pain and half the opioid use with a serratus anterior plane block.

?? Which group of symptoms suggests early local anaesthetic systemic toxicity?
@ 02-safety
+ Tingling around the mouth, a metallic taste and ringing in the ears
- Numbness in the territory of the block
- Itching at the injection site
- A brief sting during skin infiltration
: Early toxicity affects the brain. Seizures and cardiovascular collapse can follow, sometimes with no warning.

?? Which drug should you avoid as an antiarrhythmic in local anaesthetic toxicity?
@ 02-safety
+ Lidocaine
- Adrenaline in small doses
- Amiodarone
- Lipid emulsion
: Lidocaine is a local anaesthetic and adds to the toxic dose.

?? Where does the needle tip sit in an erector spinae plane block?
@ 03-esp
+ On the transverse process, deep to erector spinae
- Between the ribs, through the pleura
- Inside erector spinae
- On the spinous process
: The plane between erector spinae and the transverse process carries the spread over several levels.

?? How does a transverse process look on a sagittal scan?
@ 03-esp
+ A square, flat-topped bony line about 3 cm from the midline
- A rounded bony line with sliding pleura beneath
- A continuous wavy line in the midline
- A black circle with flow on Doppler
: The flat top is the clue. Rounded bone with pleura between is rib.

?? Why does the extent of an erector spinae plane block vary between patients?
@ 03-esp
+ Spread to the ventral rami and paravertebral space varies
- The transverse process absorbs the drug
- The block never reaches the dorsal rami
- The volume used is always too small
: The block reaches the dorsal rami consistently, but forward spread varies.

?? Where does the needle tip sit in a rectus sheath block?
@ 04-rectus
+ Between rectus abdominis and the posterior rectus sheath
- In the subcutaneous fat
- Inside the peritoneal cavity
- Between external and internal oblique
: The anterior cutaneous branches run in this space before turning forward through the muscle.

?? What lies between rectus abdominis and the peritoneum below the arcuate line?
@ 04-rectus
+ Only thin transversalis fascia
- A thick posterior rectus sheath
- The external oblique muscle
- The inferior vena cava
: The posterior sheath ends at the arcuate line, so the peritoneum lies close beneath the muscle.

?? Which vessels run on the deep surface of rectus abdominis in the lower abdomen?
@ 04-rectus
+ The inferior epigastric vessels
- The femoral vessels
- The deep circumflex iliac vessels
- The ovarian vessels
: Colour Doppler finds the inferior epigastric vessels so you can avoid them.

?? What type of pain does a rectus sheath block not treat?
@ 04-rectus
+ Visceral pain from inside the abdomen
- Pain from a midline skin wound
- Pain from the rectus muscle
- Pain around the umbilicus from the abdominal wall
: The block reaches the nerves of the abdominal wall only.
