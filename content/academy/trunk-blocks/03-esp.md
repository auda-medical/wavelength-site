The Wavelength Pearl [Erector spinae plane block: bone, muscle, then flood the plane](/learn/erector-spinae-plane-block-bone-muscle/) summarises the technique.

## The idea

The erector spinae plane block was described in 2016 for thoracic neuropathic pain. Local anaesthetic placed on the transverse process, deep to erector spinae, spreads up and down over several levels. It reaches the dorsal rami consistently. Spread forward to the ventral rami and the paravertebral space varies between patients, which explains the variable extent of the block.

## On the screen

Scan in a sagittal plane, moving out from the midline:

1. **Spinous processes** in the midline.
2. **Laminae:** a continuous, wavy bony line just lateral.
3. **Transverse processes**, about 3 cm from the midline: square, flat-topped bony lines with dense shadows and gaps between them.
4. **Ribs**, further lateral: rounder bony lines with the bright, sliding pleura visible in the gaps.

Erector spinae lies above the transverse processes, with trapezius and rhomboids above it in the upper chest.

## Technique

1. Sit the patient up, or lie them on the uninjured side.
2. Probe sagittal at the level of the fractures. Find the transverse process and confirm it by sliding out to the ribs and back.
3. In-plane needle from the top or bottom of the probe until it touches the transverse process.
4. Inject 2 to 3 mL and watch the fluid lift erector spinae off the bone and spread along the plane.
5. Inject the rest, typically 20 to 30 mL, checking the total dose.

## Coverage and evidence

- **Coverage:** the back and, variably, the side and front of the chest wall over several levels. The extent varies.
- **Evidence in rib fractures:** observational studies and small trials suggest benefit. The quality is lower than for the serratus anterior plane block, and comparisons between the two are mixed.
- **Other uses:** herpes zoster, thoracic wall pain and, in surgery, spinal and thoracic procedures.

## Safety

The target is bone, far from the pleura and away from major vessels, which makes the block simple to learn and lowers the risk of pneumothorax. It still needs the full safety system: a dose calculation, monitoring and lipid emulsion nearby.

> **The aha** A flat top means transverse process. A rounded top with pleura beneath means rib.

> **Pitfall** A round swelling in the muscle means the tip is too shallow. Touch bone, then inject. Reassess pain after 30 minutes, and plan other analgesia if the block covers less than you hoped.
