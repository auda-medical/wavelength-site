Most of the success in a difficult cannulation comes before the needle touches the skin.

## Fill the vein

Optimise the limb before you scan:

1. **Tourniquet.** Apply it above the site and wait while the veins fill. Release it as soon as the cannula is in.
2. **Dependent arm.** Lower the arm below heart level if the patient tolerates it.
3. **Warmth.** A warm towel or pack helps dilate the veins. Keep it comfortable, never hot.
4. **Hand movements.** Ask for a squeeze and relax of the hand. Avoid repeated pumping if you will send a potassium sample.

Follow your trust's policy on limbs to avoid, such as an arm with a dialysis fistula.

## Use a light hand

A vein holds low pressure, so a heavy probe flattens it. Use plenty of gel and the lightest pressure that gives a stable image. If the vein looks tiny, press less before you do anything else. For a very superficial vein, a thick layer of gel acts as a stand-off and moves the vein away from the poorly seen top of the image.

## Vein or artery?

Do not rely on shape. Scan in short axis and apply gentle pressure. A vein collapses and an artery does not. Colour Doppler can support the answer, but colour shows flow direction relative to the probe and depends on angle and settings. Red does not mean artery and blue does not mean vein. Spectral Doppler gives more: arterial flow is pulsatile, venous flow is phasic or steady.

If a vein will not compress, think of thrombosis, scarring from past cannulation, too little pressure in a deep vein, or an artery.

## Choose the vein

The best vein is not always the biggest. Look for a vein that is:

- **Straight**, with a clear run of several centimetres and no branch, valve or sharp bend. Scan along the vein before you puncture and map the run.
- **Compressible**, so it is a vein and not a clot.
- **Superficial enough** to hold the catheter. A deep vein may hold only the tip of a short catheter, and a catheter with too little inside the vein fails early. A 2020 study found that peripheral catheters lasted longer when more than 2.75 cm of the catheter lay inside the vein. Choose a more superficial vein, or a longer catheter, for a deep target.

In the upper arm, take care over the basilic vein, which runs near the brachial artery and the median nerve.

## Centre the dot

In short axis, slide the probe until the vein sits in the middle of the screen. Set the depth so the vein fills the middle of the image. Rest your scanning hand on the patient. Before you puncture, do the dynamic check: slide a short way along the vein and back, and keep the same vein centred and round. If you cannot, do not puncture yet.

> **The aha** Fill the vein, lighten your hand, then pick the straight run and centre it. All of it happens before the needle.
