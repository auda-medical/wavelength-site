Five patients from the emergency department. Read each case, choose your answer, then read the explanation. These cases do not count towards your score.

## Case 1: the frail arm

An 84-year-old woman with sepsis has had three failed attempts at a cannula. You can see no vein, and the arm is cold.

?? What do you do before you scan?
+ Optimise the limb: apply a tourniquet, lower the arm, add warmth, then scan
- Scan straight away with heavy probe pressure
- Choose the artery as an easier target
- Place a central line without further attempts
: Filling the veins first often turns an unusable arm into one with a good target. Plan the number of attempts with your trust's policy.

## Case 2: the vanishing vein

You find a small vein on a forearm scan. As you press the probe on, it disappears.

?? What do you do first?
+ Lift off until the vein reopens, using more gel and less pressure
- Press harder to bring the vein closer
- Decide that the vein is too small and stop
- Puncture where the vein was
: Veins collapse under a heavy probe. A vein that reopens under a lighter touch is often a good target.

## Case 3: the flash and no thread

You see the vein in short axis, enter it and get a flash. The catheter will not advance.

?? What is your next step?
+ Stop, keep the needle still, rotate to long axis and look for a side-wall tip, tenting or a valve
- Push the catheter harder
- Withdraw and puncture again at the same site
- Flush the cannula to open the vein
: Long axis shows why the catheter fails. Pushing blindly risks dissecting beside the vein.

## Case 4: the guidewire in the neck

You are placing a right internal jugular line in a patient on the intensive care unit. Blood returned freely and the wire passed easily. On the short-axis view you cannot see the wire clearly inside the vein.

?? What do you do?
+ Stop, recheck in both views and do not dilate until the wire is confirmed in the vein
- Dilate, because the wire passed easily
- Dilate gently to test the position
- Advance the wire further to find the vein
: Easy passage and flashback do not exclude artery or extraluminal placement. Dilating the wrong space is the harmful step.

## Case 5: the arterial line in shock

A hypotensive patient needs an arterial line. The radial pulse is barely palpable.

?? What helps you place the line?
+ Scan the forearm, choose a straight superficial segment and enter at a low angle
- Puncture at the wrist crease at the point of the strongest pulse
- Fully extend the wrist to bring the artery up
- Choose the bifurcation, where the artery is largest
: Ultrasound shows the artery when the pulse is weak. A straight segment gives the catheter room to thread.
