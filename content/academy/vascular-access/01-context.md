Vascular access is one of the commonest procedures in emergency care and one of the commonest sources of harm. A failed line delays treatment. A line in the wrong vessel, or a drug through a line outside the vein, hurts the patient. Ultrasound shows the vessel, the needle tip and the neighbours, so you do not have to guess.

## Where ultrasound helps most

For peripheral cannulation in adults, a 2022 Cochrane review found:

- In patients with difficult access, ultrasound guidance may increase first-pass success and reduces the number of attempts.
- In patients with moderately difficult access, it probably increases first-pass success.
- In patients with easy access, first-pass success was lower with ultrasound.
- Ultrasound added roughly one to two minutes to a first-pass procedure.
- The certainty of the evidence ranged from very low to moderate.

The lesson is to choose the patient. Use ultrasound when you cannot see or feel a good vein, and when previous attempts have failed. For central venous access, real-time ultrasound guidance for the internal jugular vein is recommended in the 2019 Society of Hospital Medicine position statement and in international evidence-based recommendations.

## The three jobs of the probe

1. **Find and choose the vessel.** Check its size, depth, path and neighbours.
2. **Guide the needle.** Keep the tip in view from skin to lumen.
3. **Confirm the result.** See the wire or the flush, rather than trust a flash of blood.

## Equipment and hygiene

- Use a high-frequency linear probe.
- Clean the probe between patients according to your trust's policy.
- For a peripheral line, use clean gel and follow local policy on a probe cover.
- For a central line, use sterile gel and a sterile probe cover, as part of full barrier precautions.

## What this module is, and is not

This module teaches the theory and the safety checks. It does not make you competent to place lines. Competence comes from supervised practice under your trust's governance, and central lines need training, sterile technique and local approval.

> **Before you start** Know your trust's policy on who may place peripheral, central and arterial lines, and on supervision, consent and the number of attempts.
