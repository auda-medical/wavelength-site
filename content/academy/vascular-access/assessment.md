?? What does a certificate for this module show?
@ 01-context
+ That you have completed the theory, not that you are competent to place lines
- That you can place central lines without supervision
- That your trust has approved you for vascular access
- That you hold a national accreditation in vascular access
: The module teaches the checks and the technique. Competence comes from supervised practice under your trust's governance.

?? According to the 2022 Cochrane review, in which adults does ultrasound guidance help first-pass cannulation most?
@ 01-context
+ Those with difficult or moderately difficult access
- Those with easily seen veins
- Every adult, by the same amount
- Only those who are obese
: The benefit was in difficult and moderately difficult patients. In easy patients first-pass success was lower with ultrasound.

?? What is the first step when the veins look small and flat?
@ 02-peripheral
+ Optimise the limb with a tourniquet, a dependent arm, warmth and hand movements, then rescan
- Choose the deepest vein
- Press harder with the probe
- Choose the artery
: Simple measures fill the veins. A vein that looked unusable is often a good target afterwards.

?? A small vein disappears as you scan. What do you try first?
@ 02-peripheral
+ Lift the probe until the vein reopens, with plenty of gel
- Press harder
- Switch to a lower frequency
- Puncture where the vein was
: A vein collapses under a heavy probe. Gel carries the sound, so you can scan with a lighter touch.

?? How do you tell a vein from an artery?
@ 02-peripheral
+ Gentle pressure: a vein collapses and a pulsating artery does not
- Red means artery and blue means vein on colour Doppler
- The larger vessel is always the vein
- The deeper vessel is always the artery
: Compression is the key bedside test. Colour depends on the direction of flow relative to the probe and on the settings, so it does not identify the vessel.

?? Which feature makes a vein a poor target even when the puncture succeeds?
@ 02-peripheral
+ A branch, a valve or a sharp bend along the run
- A straight run of several centimetres
- A compressible lumen
- A round shape on short axis
: Branches, valves and bends stop a catheter from threading. Map the run before you puncture.

?? What does it show when you can slide along the vein and keep it centred and round?
@ 02-peripheral
+ The target stays stable, so you can follow the needle path and tip
- The vein is an artery
- The vein is too deep
- You need no gel
: The dynamic check proves you can track the target. If you cannot, do not puncture yet.

?? Why is flashback unreliable for confirming the needle is in the lumen?
@ 03-needle
+ Blood can return when the bevel sits partly in the lumen after the needle has passed through the back wall
- Flashback never occurs in veins
- Flashback occurs only in arteries
- Flashback shows tenting
: A needle through both walls can still return blood. Rely on the image of the tip.

?? What does tenting of the near wall tell you?
@ 03-needle
+ The tip has reached the wall and has not yet entered the lumen
- The needle is through the back wall
- The vein is thrombosed
- The needle is in an artery
: The wall bends under the tip. Advance a little more to enter, then stop.

?? The catheter will not thread although the tip is close to the vein. What do you do?
@ 03-needle
+ Keep the needle still and rotate the probe to long axis
- Push the catheter harder
- Increase probe pressure
- Withdraw the needle and puncture at the same spot
: Long axis shows the tip position, tenting, valves and bends. Pushing blindly can dissect beside the vein.

?? What does fluid collecting in the soft tissue during a flush test mean?
@ 03-needle
+ The cannula is not in the vein, so stop using it
- The cannula is working well
- The vein is distended
- You need to flush faster
: A true intraluminal flush shows a swirl inside the lumen. Fluid in the tissue means infiltration.

?? Why do you view the guidewire in two views before dilation?
@ 04-central
+ Two views reduce the chance of missing a wire in the artery or outside the vein
- Dilation is easier in long axis
- The first view always shows the artery
- It confirms the catheter tip position
: Flashback and easy passage can mislead. See the wire inside the vein, in short and long axis, before you dilate.

?? The wire is not clearly inside the vein lumen. What do you do?
@ 04-central
+ Stop and recheck, and do not dilate until the wire is confirmed in the vein
- Dilate gently
- Advance the wire further
- Place the catheter and check with a chest X-ray
: Dilating an artery or the tissues is the harmful step. Resolve the doubt first.

?? What does a visible wire in the internal jugular vein not tell you?
@ 04-central
+ Where the catheter tip will end up
- That the wire is in the neck
- That the vein is compressible
- Which side you are on
: Confirm tip position and look for complications after insertion, according to your trust's policy.

?? How do you choose the radial artery segment for a line?
@ 05-arterial
+ Scan a few centimetres and choose a straight, superficial stretch away from branches and bends
- Choose the point of the strongest pulse
- Choose a bifurcation, where the artery is largest
- Choose the wrist crease every time
: A straight, superficial stretch gives the catheter room. The strongest pulse is not always the best segment.

?? What do you do if the radial artery puncture succeeds but the catheter will not advance?
@ 05-arterial
+ Rotate to long axis and look at the tip and the wall
- Push the catheter harder
- Extend the wrist fully
- Remove the roll
: Long axis shows the angle, tip position and wall tenting. Pushing blindly can dissect the artery.
