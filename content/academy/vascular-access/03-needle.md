title: "The needle: near wall, back wall, threading and the flush test"
minutes: 16
---

Once the vein is centred, the needle phase has four traps.

## Follow the tip

Keep the tip in view. In short axis, move the probe a little after the needle so the tip stays on the screen. Approach at a low angle so the tip meets the near wall gently.

## Stop at the near wall

When the tip reaches the vein, the near wall tents inward. Slow down. Advance a fraction more until the tip is inside the lumen, then stop. Confirm by image, not by flash, before you advance the catheter.

A flash of blood does not prove the tip is in the lumen. If the needle has gone through the back wall, part of the bevel can still sit in the vein and blood still returns.

If you overshoot:

1. Stop and keep the tip in view.
2. Withdraw slowly until the tip is back inside the lumen.
3. Lower the angle and re-enter.
4. Check the tip before you advance the catheter.

In the antecubital fossa an artery or a nerve often lies beneath the vein. A needle through the back wall can injure it.

## When the catheter will not thread

Short axis is best for finding the vein and entering it. If the catheter will not advance, stop and keep the needle still. Rotate the probe through 90 degrees to long axis. Look for:

- the tip against the side wall,
- wall tenting,
- a valve or a bend ahead.

Flatten your angle, advance the needle a few millimetres so the tip is well inside the lumen, then advance the catheter off the needle under vision. If it still meets resistance, stop. Do not push blindly, because you can dissect beside the vein. Follow your trust's policy on the number of attempts.

## The flush test

After the line is in, hold the probe over the vein just proximal to the catheter tip and flush a small volume of saline.

- **Intraluminal.** A swirl of bright echoes inside the lumen, moving along the vein. The vein may briefly distend.
- **Infiltration.** Fluid collects in the soft tissue, and the vein stays empty. Stop using the cannula.

Confirm first that you are looking at a vein, because a swirl in an artery can look similar. The flush test adds to blood return, ease of flushing, pain and swelling, and does not replace them. The evidence for it in adults is limited, so treat it as a practical bedside check. Before you give an irritant or vesicant drug, follow your trust's policy and confirm placement with extra care.

> **The aha** The image beats the flash. Tent, enter, stop, then flush and watch the vein answer.
