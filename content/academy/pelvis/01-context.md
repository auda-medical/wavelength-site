A woman with pelvic pain in the emergency department has a short list of time-critical diagnoses: ectopic pregnancy, ovarian torsion, a bleeding cyst and pelvic sepsis. Point-of-care ultrasound helps with each, but only when you ask it the right question.

## Start with the pregnancy test

Every patient of reproductive age with pelvic pain needs a pregnancy test before you interpret the scan. A positive test turns every adnexal finding into a possible ectopic pregnancy until proven otherwise.

## What the bedside scan offers

- **Early pregnancy:** confirming a definite intrauterine pregnancy in a stable patient. In a meta-analysis by Stein and colleagues, emergency physician ultrasound, treating anything short of a definite intrauterine pregnancy as positive, missed very few ectopic pregnancies.
- **The unstable patient:** free fluid in the pelvis or in Morison's pouch with a positive pregnancy test, which needs theatre.
- **The adnexa:** a ring, an enlarged ovary, a twisted pedicle, a bleeding cyst or a complex mass, to support an urgent referral.

## What it does not offer

- A bedside scan does not exclude ectopic pregnancy. An empty uterus means a pregnancy of unknown location, not a miscarriage.
- Normal Doppler flow does not exclude ovarian torsion.
- A normal scan does not exclude pelvic inflammatory disease.

> **The aha** In early pregnancy, the bedside scan has one job first: find a definite intrauterine pregnancy. Anything less means the patient follows the early pregnancy pathway.

## Transabdominal and transvaginal

This module teaches transabdominal scanning with the curvilinear probe. Transvaginal scanning gives far better views of an early pregnancy and the ovaries, and needs separate training, local governance and a chaperone. If you cannot complete the scan, say so and refer.

## Consent and dignity

Explain what you will do and why. Offer a chaperone, and record that you offered one. Keep the patient covered except for the area you scan. Remember that a scan in early pregnancy carries emotional weight, so choose your words carefully and do not speculate about viability.
