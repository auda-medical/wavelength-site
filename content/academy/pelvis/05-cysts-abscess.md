---
title: "Bleeding cysts and pelvic sepsis"
minutes: 12
---

Two adnexal problems produce complex masses on scan: a haemorrhagic cyst, which usually settles, and a tubo-ovarian abscess, which is pelvic sepsis. Their appearance, and the patient in front of you, separate them.

## Haemorrhagic cyst

Bleeding into a corpus luteum or follicle cyst is common, especially in the second half of the menstrual cycle. The appearance changes as blood clots and retracts.

- **Fishnet pattern:** fine, lacy, net-like strands inside the cyst, with no flow on colour Doppler.
- **Retracting clot:** a grey mass inside the cyst with a concave or straight edge and no internal flow.
- **Rupture:** echogenic free fluid around the ovary or in the pouch of Douglas.

A solid nodule with internal flow, thick septations or a positive pregnancy test do not fit, and need formal imaging and gynaecology review. A typical haemorrhagic cyst in a stable, non-pregnant patient usually needs analgesia and follow-up according to local guidance. A large haemoperitoneum or instability needs urgent gynaecology review.

> **The aha** Fishnet echoes and no internal flow usually mean clot, not flesh.

## Tubo-ovarian abscess

Pelvic inflammatory disease often shows nothing on scan. When an abscess forms, the picture changes.

- **Complex adnexal mass:** thick-walled and multilocular, with debris, septa or fluid levels. The ovary and tube merge, and you cannot see a separate normal ovary.
- **Hyperaemia:** flow in the wall and septa on colour Doppler.
- **Pyosalpinx:** a dilated, thick-walled tube containing debris.
- **Complex free fluid** in the pouch of Douglas.
- **Tenderness** under the probe.

> **The aha** Complex, thick and tender is the pattern that should worry you.

Treat as pelvic sepsis: blood cultures, intravenous antibiotics according to local guidance, and urgent gynaecology review. Large abscesses or those not settling on antibiotics need drainage. Arrange formal imaging.

> **Pitfall** Ectopic pregnancy, endometrioma, haemorrhagic cyst, appendix abscess and tumour all form complex adnexal masses. Check the pregnancy test, look at the appendix region on the right, and let formal imaging confirm.
