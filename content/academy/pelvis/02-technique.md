---
title: "Technique: the uterus, the adnexa and the pouch of Douglas"
minutes: 10
---

A systematic transabdominal pelvic scan takes a few minutes. Follow the same order every time.

## Set up

- Curvilinear probe, abdominal or obstetric preset.
- Some urine in the bladder helps. The bladder pushes bowel out of the way and acts as a window.
- Patient supine, covered except for the lower abdomen.

## The views

1. **Sagittal midline.** Probe just above the pubic symphysis, marker to the head. Find the bladder, then the uterus behind it. Sweep slowly from one side of the uterus to the other, following the bright endometrial stripe.
2. **Transverse.** Rotate 90°, marker to the patient's right. Sweep from the cervix up to the fundus.
3. **The adnexa.** From transverse, angle towards each side of the uterus. The ovaries usually sit beside the uterus near the iliac vessels, which help you find them. Look for follicles, small black circles, to confirm you have an ovary.
4. **The pouch of Douglas.** In sagittal, look behind the uterus and cervix for free fluid. A little clear fluid is common. Echogenic fluid, or fluid tracking up beside the uterus, suggests blood.
5. **Morison's pouch.** In an unstable patient, scan the right upper quadrant between the liver and the right kidney.

## Using colour Doppler

Colour Doppler shows flow in vessels, the corpus luteum, and inflamed tissue. Set a low velocity scale. Read it last, after the greyscale picture, and remember that flow shows on scan in many normal structures.

> **Pitfall** Bowel loops mimic adnexal masses and fluid. Watch for peristalsis, check the structure in two planes, and confirm the ovary by finding follicles.
