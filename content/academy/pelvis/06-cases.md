## Case 1: the empty uterus

A 28-year-old has a positive pregnancy test, mild left iliac fossa pain and light bleeding. Pulse 84, blood pressure 118/76. The uterus is empty with a thin endometrium. You cannot see the left ovary clearly. There is no free fluid.

?? What is the next step?
+ Refer to the early pregnancy unit as a pregnancy of unknown location, with clear safety-netting advice
- Diagnose complete miscarriage and discharge
- Exclude ectopic pregnancy and discharge
- Diagnose ectopic pregnancy and arrange theatre
: An empty uterus with a positive test is a pregnancy of unknown location. The patient is stable with no free fluid, so she needs formal transvaginal scanning and serial hCG on the early pregnancy pathway, and advice to return immediately with worsening pain, dizziness or shoulder tip pain.

## Case 2: the collapse

A 31-year-old collapsed at home. Pulse 128, blood pressure 82/50. Pregnancy test positive. Ultrasound shows free fluid between the liver and the right kidney and echogenic fluid in the pouch of Douglas.

?? What does this show?
+ A large haemoperitoneum, most likely a ruptured ectopic pregnancy, needing resuscitation and theatre
- A normal early pregnancy
- A ruptured haemorrhagic cyst needing analgesia only
- Pelvic inflammatory disease
: Free fluid reaching Morison's pouch in a shocked patient with a positive test means a large bleed. Resuscitate, activate your major haemorrhage protocol if needed, and involve senior gynaecology for theatre immediately.

## Case 3: the vomiting teenager

A 15-year-old has had sudden severe right pelvic pain for three hours with vomiting. Pregnancy test negative. The right ovary is three times the size of the left, with follicles around the edge. Colour Doppler shows arterial flow in the right ovary.

?? What is the next step?
+ Urgent gynaecology or paediatric surgical review for suspected torsion
- Exclude torsion because arterial flow is present
- Discharge with analgesia and outpatient scan
- Treat for pelvic inflammatory disease
: An enlarged ovary with peripheral follicles and this history strongly suggest torsion. Arterial flow is often preserved. Surgery confirms and treats it, and delay risks the ovary.

## Case 4: the fever

A 26-year-old has lower abdominal pain, fever 38.6°C and vaginal discharge. Pregnancy test negative. Beside the left side of the uterus lies a thick-walled, multilocular mass with debris and hyperaemic walls. It is tender under the probe, and you cannot see a separate left ovary.

?? What is the most likely diagnosis?
+ Tubo-ovarian abscess
- Haemorrhagic cyst
- Simple ovarian cyst
- Ovarian torsion
: A thick-walled, complex, hyperaemic, tender mass with fever fits a tubo-ovarian abscess. Take blood cultures, start intravenous antibiotics according to local guidance, and arrange urgent gynaecology review and formal imaging.
