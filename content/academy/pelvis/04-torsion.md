---
title: "Ovarian torsion: follow the pedicle"
minutes: 10
---

Torsion twists the ovary on its vascular pedicle. Venous outflow blocks first, the ovary swells, and arterial flow fails later. The ovary has a dual blood supply, from the ovarian and uterine arteries, and torsion is often partial or intermittent, so arterial flow is often still present on scan.

## The scan

1. Find the uterus and both ovaries. Find both before you call one abnormal.
2. Compare size and appearance.
3. Find the pedicle between the ovary and the uterus or pelvic side wall. Follow it in short axis and watch for a swirl.
4. Add colour Doppler last.

## Findings

- **Enlarged ovary:** clearly larger than the other side, with oedematous central tissue and follicles pushed to the edge.
- **Whirlpool sign:** a twisted pedicle forming a spiral or target, sometimes with swirling vessels on colour Doppler. This is the most specific sign.
- **Lead point:** a cyst or dermoid, often over 5 cm, which makes the ovary heavy and prone to twist.
- **Free fluid:** a small amount is common.
- **Doppler:** reduced or absent venous flow supports torsion. Present arterial flow does not exclude it.

> **The aha** Follow the pedicle, not just the colour box.

> **Pitfall** Normal Doppler flow, and a normal POCUS scan, do not exclude torsion. Sudden, severe, one-sided pelvic pain with nausea and vomiting needs urgent gynaecology review whatever the scan shows.

## Children and adolescents

Torsion also occurs before puberty, often in a normal ovary. Pain may be vague and central. Involve the paediatric surgical or gynaecology team early. The diagnosis is confirmed at surgery, and the aim is to untwist and preserve the ovary.
