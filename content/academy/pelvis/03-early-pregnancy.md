---
title: "Early pregnancy: find the pregnancy, then look beyond the uterus"
minutes: 14
---

Ectopic pregnancy remains a leading cause of early pregnancy death. Most implant in the fallopian tube. The bedside scan aims to show a definite intrauterine pregnancy, and when it cannot, to look for signs of an ectopic and of bleeding.

## A definite intrauterine pregnancy

All of these:

- A gestational sac within the uterus.
- A yolk sac or a fetal pole inside it.
- The sac surrounded by myometrium on all sides, so it is not in the cornua or cervix.

A sac without a yolk sac or fetal pole is not enough.

## The pseudosac

Bleeding from the endometrium in an ectopic pregnancy collects as fluid in the centre of the cavity. It looks like an early sac. Separate them:

- **Pseudosac:** central, in the cavity line, often irregular, with no yolk sac and no double ring.
- **True early sac:** off-centre, within the endometrium beside the cavity line, round, with a bright rim and later a yolk sac.

## Signs of an ectopic pregnancy

- **Tubal ring:** a thick, bright ring in the adnexa, separate from the ovary, sometimes with a yolk sac or fetal pole.
- **Extra-uterine gestational sac with a yolk sac or fetal pole:** diagnostic.
- **Adnexal mass:** a complex mass separate from the ovary.
- **Echogenic free fluid** in the pouch of Douglas suggests haemoperitoneum.
- **Free fluid in Morison's pouch** with a positive pregnancy test suggests a large bleed. Moore and colleagues found it predicted the need for operative intervention.

> **The aha** Do not stop at the uterus. Find the adnexa, then look behind the uterus and up in the right upper quadrant.

> **Pitfall** A ring of colour flow appears around a normal corpus luteum, which sits inside the ovary. A ring outside the ovary is the concern. Heterotopic pregnancy, an intrauterine and an ectopic pregnancy together, is rare after natural conception but more common after IVF, so a visible intrauterine pregnancy does not exclude an ectopic in these patients.

## Acting on the scan

- **Unstable, positive test, free fluid:** resuscitate, call senior emergency and gynaecology teams, and prepare for theatre.
- **Stable, no definite intrauterine pregnancy:** pregnancy of unknown location. Refer to the early pregnancy assessment unit for transvaginal scanning and serial hCG, with clear safety-netting advice.
- **Stable, definite intrauterine pregnancy:** ectopic pregnancy becomes very unlikely, except after assisted conception. Follow local guidance for pain or bleeding in early pregnancy.

Do not comment on viability from a bedside scan. Leave that to formal assessment.
