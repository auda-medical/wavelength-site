title: Final assessment
---

?? What do you check before interpreting a pelvic scan in a woman of reproductive age with pain?
@ 01-context
+ A pregnancy test
- A full blood count
- The menstrual history only
- A urine culture
: A positive pregnancy test turns any adnexal finding into a possible ectopic pregnancy until proven otherwise. Check it first.

?? What does an empty uterus with a positive pregnancy test mean on a bedside scan?
@ 01-context
+ A pregnancy of unknown location, needing the early pregnancy pathway
- A complete miscarriage
- Ectopic pregnancy is excluded
- A normal pregnancy too early to see
: The bedside scan cannot separate a very early intrauterine pregnancy, a miscarriage and an ectopic pregnancy. The patient needs formal transvaginal scanning and serial hCG.

?? How do you confirm you are looking at an ovary?
@ 02-technique
+ Find follicles, small black circles within it
- Find colour flow
- Find a bright shadow behind it
- Measure it over 5 cm
: Follicles identify the ovary. Bowel loops mimic the ovary but show peristalsis and lack follicles.

?? Which finding confirms an intrauterine pregnancy on POCUS?
@ 03-early-pregnancy
+ A gestational sac in the uterus containing a yolk sac or fetal pole
- Any fluid collection in the endometrial cavity
- A thick endometrium
- Colour flow around the endometrium
: A sac alone may be a pseudosac. A yolk sac or fetal pole within a sac surrounded by myometrium confirms an intrauterine pregnancy.

?? Where does a pseudosac sit?
@ 03-early-pregnancy
+ In the centre of the endometrial cavity, with no yolk sac
- Off-centre within the endometrium, with a yolk sac
- In the adnexa
- In the pouch of Douglas
: A pseudosac is fluid or blood in the cavity itself. A true early sac implants off-centre in the endometrium.

?? A shocked patient with a positive pregnancy test has free fluid in Morison's pouch. What does this indicate?
@ 03-early-pregnancy
+ A large haemoperitoneum needing resuscitation and theatre
- A normal finding in pregnancy
- Ovarian hyperstimulation, needing outpatient review
- Urinary retention
: Free fluid reaching the right upper quadrant in early pregnancy suggests a large bleed, most often a ruptured ectopic pregnancy.

?? Which patients can have an intrauterine and an ectopic pregnancy at the same time more often than others?
@ 03-early-pregnancy
+ Patients after assisted conception such as IVF
- Patients over 40
- Patients with a previous caesarean section only
- Patients with a negative pregnancy test
: Heterotopic pregnancy is rare after natural conception but more common after assisted conception, so a visible intrauterine pregnancy does not exclude an ectopic in these patients.

?? Which sign is most specific for ovarian torsion?
@ 04-torsion
+ A twisted vascular pedicle, the whirlpool sign
- Free fluid in the pouch of Douglas
- Increased arterial flow
- A simple cyst under 3 cm
: The whirlpool sign shows the twist itself. Free fluid and small cysts are common and non-specific.

?? Why does arterial flow often persist in ovarian torsion?
@ 04-torsion
+ The ovary has a dual blood supply and torsion is often partial or intermittent
- Torsion only affects lymphatic drainage
- Doppler cannot detect ovarian arteries
- The ovary grows new vessels within hours
: Venous outflow blocks first, and the ovarian and uterine arteries both supply the ovary. Present arterial flow does not exclude torsion.

?? Which appearance favours a haemorrhagic cyst over a solid lesion?
@ 05-cysts-abscess
+ Lacy fishnet echoes and a clot with a concave edge and no internal flow
- A solid nodule with internal flow
- Thick septations with strong flow
- A completely anechoic thin-walled cyst
: Clotting blood forms fibrin strands and a retracting clot with no blood supply. Flow within a solid part suggests tissue.

?? Which pattern suggests a tubo-ovarian abscess?
@ 05-cysts-abscess
+ A thick-walled, multilocular, tender adnexal mass with debris and hyperaemia, with fever
- An enlarged ovary with peripheral follicles and a twisted pedicle
- A thin-walled anechoic cyst
- A central fluid collection in the uterus
: Thick walls, septa, debris, hyperaemia and tenderness in a febrile patient point to pelvic sepsis.

?? A febrile patient with pelvic pain has a normal bedside pelvic scan. What do you conclude?
@ 05-cysts-abscess
+ Pelvic inflammatory disease is not excluded, so treat on clinical grounds
- Pelvic inflammatory disease is excluded
- The patient needs no antibiotics
- A tubo-ovarian abscess is confirmed
: Pelvic inflammatory disease often shows nothing on ultrasound. Treat according to the clinical picture and local guidance.
