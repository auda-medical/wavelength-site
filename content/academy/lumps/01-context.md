Red, hot, swollen skin is one of the commonest presentations in the emergency department. The treatment depends on what lies beneath:

- **Cellulitis** needs antibiotics.
- **An abscess** needs drainage.
- **A haematoma** usually needs nothing but time.
- **A pseudoaneurysm** needs a vascular or interventional radiology opinion, and must never be incised.

Examination often cannot tell them apart. Fluctuance is unreliable, especially in deep, small or indurated collections. Ultrasound can.

## The evidence

A 2020 meta-analysis found point-of-care ultrasound diagnosed abscess with a sensitivity of 94.6% and a specificity of 85.4%. Examination alone misses collections, especially deep or small ones, and ultrasound shows when there is nothing to drain.

## Who is at risk of a vascular lump

- After femoral arterial puncture: angiography, arterial lines, arterial blood gases.
- People who inject drugs, especially into the groin.
- After trauma near a major artery.
- Patients on anticoagulants or antiplatelets.

> **The aha** In the groin, every hot lump is a pseudoaneurysm until colour Doppler says otherwise.

## What this module covers

You will learn the scan, the cellulitis and abscess patterns, the vascular lumps, and how to drain an abscess safely under ultrasound. The module ends with cases.

> **Pitfall** A lump which pulsates, has a bruit, or sits over a major artery is never needled or incised before a colour Doppler scan.
