## Before you drain

1. Confirm a collection which swirls or is clearly liquid.
2. Switch on colour Doppler. No flow inside the collection, and no artery or vein in the path you plan to take.
3. Check the depth and the structures beneath. Avoid nerves, vessels and joint spaces.
4. Mark the skin over the largest, most superficial part of the collection, in two planes.
5. Measure the depth from skin to the centre of the collection.

Then choose the shortest route that avoids what you have found, and decide how you will use it. A skin mark is quick, but it moves if the patient or the skin moves after the scan. Real-time guidance shows you the needle. The Wavelength Pearl [Abscess drainage: plan the needle path](/learn/abscess-drainage-ultrasound/) shows each step.

## Needle aspiration or incision

Small collections, and those in cosmetically sensitive areas such as the face or breast, are often aspirated under ultrasound guidance. Larger collections usually need incision and drainage. Follow your local guidance and senior advice.

## Under real-time guidance

- Use an in-plane approach so you see the whole needle.
- Watch the collection shrink as you aspirate.
- Rescan at the end for a remaining pocket.

## Two collections that need a different plan

**Breast.** Bright, swollen tissue with no pocket is mastitis. A dark or complex pocket with no internal flow suggests an abscess. Give antibiotics, encourage the patient to keep feeding or expressing, and refer to the breast team, who usually offer needle aspiration under ultrasound, repeated if needed. A red breast that does not settle, or a solid area, needs the breast clinic, because inflammatory cancer can look like infection. See the Wavelength Pearl [Breast abscess: aspirate before you cut](/learn/breast-abscess-ultrasound/).

**Perianal.** A small lump on the skin can be the top of a deep collection. With a covered linear probe and a light touch, note the depth and the relation to the sphincter. A collection tracking deep needs imaging and surgery, and ultrasound can miss deep and supralevator collections. Bright gas with dirty shadowing raises concern for Fournier gangrene. See the Wavelength Pearl [Perianal abscess: judge the depth](/learn/perianal-abscess-ultrasound/).

## When not to drain in the emergency department

- Any flow inside the lump, or a connection to an artery.
- A deep collection near a major vessel or nerve.
- The hand, the perianal region beyond simple superficial abscesses, the neck near the airway, or the breast in some services. Follow local pathways.
- Suspected necrotising infection.

## What to document

1. The clinical question.
2. The probe and the area scanned, with the comparison side if used.
3. Findings: cellulitis pattern, collection present or absent, size in three dimensions, depth, swirl, colour Doppler flow inside and around, any connection to a vessel.
4. Your conclusion and plan.
5. Whether images were saved.

> **The aha** Colour Doppler before every needle. It takes ten seconds.

> **Pitfall** A collection which is not drainable today can become drainable in 24 to 48 hours. Arrange a repeat scan for phlegmon which does not settle.
