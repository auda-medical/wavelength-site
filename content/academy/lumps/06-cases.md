Five patients from the emergency department. Read each case, choose your answer, then read the explanation. These cases do not count towards your score.

## Case 1: the red leg

A 60-year-old man has a red, warm, swollen shin. Ultrasound shows thickened, bright subcutaneous fat with dark strands between the lobules. There is no discrete collection. Gentle pressure shows no movement.

?? What is the best plan?
+ Treat as cellulitis with antibiotics, with no drainage
- Incise and drain the area of maximum redness
- Needle aspirate the cobblestoned area
- Arrange urgent vascular review
: A cobblestone pattern without a collection fits cellulitis. There is nothing to drain. If the area does not settle, rescan in 24 to 48 hours to look for a developing abscess.

## Case 2: the grey lump

A 30-year-old woman has a tender lump in the axilla. On a still image it looks grey and solid, 2.5 cm across, with a bright rim. When you press gently with the probe and release, the contents swirl. Colour Doppler shows flow around the rim and none inside.

?? What is it?
+ An abscess
- A lymph node
- A solid tumour
- A haematoma with active bleeding
: Swirl under gentle pressure proves the contents are liquid. Flow around the rim and none inside fits an abscess. A lymph node has a bright fatty centre with flow entering at the hilum. Active bleeding shows flow inside.

## Case 3: after angiography

A 72-year-old man has a painful groin swelling the day after coronary angiography. Colour Doppler shows red and blue swirling flow inside a round 3 cm sac. A thin channel connects it to the common femoral artery. Pulsed-wave Doppler in the channel shows flow above the baseline in systole and below it in diastole.

?? What is the next step?
+ Refer to the vascular or interventional radiology team as a femoral pseudoaneurysm
- Aspirate the sac to exclude infection
- Incise and drain as a haematoma
- Discharge with advice
: A sac with swirling flow, a neck to the artery and a to-and-fro waveform is a pseudoaneurysm. Formal duplex defines the treatment, such as thrombin injection. Needling or incising it risks major bleeding.

## Case 4: the injecting drug user

A 34-year-old man who injects into the groin has a hot, tender, pulsatile swelling. A colleague has the scalpel ready. On B-mode you see a mixed collection. Colour Doppler shows a pulsing jet of flow from the artery into part of the collection.

?? What do you do?
+ Stop the procedure and refer urgently as a probable infected pseudoaneurysm
- Proceed with incision, as the collection looks like an abscess
- Aspirate with a fine needle to confirm pus
- Start antibiotics and drain tomorrow
: Flow from the artery into the collection shows a vascular connection. In this group, abscess and pseudoaneurysm often coexist, and infected pseudoaneurysms rupture. Incision risks catastrophic bleeding. Refer urgently to the vascular team.

## Case 5: the bruise after a fall

An 80-year-old woman on apixaban has a large, firm swelling on her thigh after a fall. Ultrasound shows a 6 cm layered collection in the subcutaneous tissue. Colour Doppler with the scale set low shows no flow inside and no connection to any vessel.

?? What is it most likely to be?
+ A haematoma
- A pseudoaneurysm
- An abscess
- An arteriovenous fistula
: A layered collection with no internal flow and no connection to a vessel, after trauma on an anticoagulant, is a haematoma. A pseudoaneurysm and a fistula both show flow. Monitor the size, check the haemoglobin, and review the skin for pressure damage.
