## Probe and settings

- Use the high-frequency linear probe with a superficial or soft tissue preset.
- Set the depth so the collection sits in the middle of the screen, with normal tissue visible below it.
- Set the focus at the level of the collection.
- Use plenty of gel and light pressure on a tender area. A stand-off pad or a thick layer of gel helps for very superficial lumps.

## The sequence

1. **Start over normal skin** next to the swelling, so you know what normal fat and fascia look like in this patient.
2. **Move over the swelling** and scan it in two planes, at right angles.
3. **Measure** the collection in three dimensions and its depth from the skin.
4. **Press gently and release.** Watch the contents.
5. **Colour Doppler** over and around the collection, with the scale lowered to pick up slow flow.
6. **Pulsed-wave Doppler** in any channel or vessel connecting to the lump.
7. **Compare** with the same area on the other side if you are unsure.

## Normal layers

From the surface down:

- **Skin:** a thin bright line.
- **Subcutaneous fat:** grey, with thin bright lines of fibrous tissue running through it.
- **Fascia:** a bright line over the muscle.
- **Muscle:** striated, with fibres in long axis.

> **The aha** Scan normal tissue first. You cannot call fat abnormal until you know what normal fat looks like in this patient.

> **Pitfall** Heavy pressure squashes a small collection out of view and hurts. Let the probe float on the gel.
