title: "The lump that comes and goes: abdominal wall hernia"
minutes: 10

A lump in the groin, at the umbilicus or by a scar may be a hernia. Incising one by mistake injures bowel. A scan with movement shows the fascial defect, what passes through it, and whether it goes back.

## The scan

1. Use the linear probe, or the curvilinear probe in a larger patient.
2. Place the probe over the lump or the most tender point, across the abdominal wall layers.
3. Identify skin, fat, the anterior rectus sheath or aponeurosis, and the muscle. Follow the bright fascial line looking for a break.
4. Ask the patient to cough or bear down, and watch the defect.
5. If you still see nothing, stand the patient up and repeat.
6. Press gently with the probe and watch whether the contents slide back.

## Findings

- **Defect:** a break in the bright fascial line, with tissue passing through it.
- **Fat:** bright and compressible.
- **Bowel:** a layered wall with fluid or gas inside, sometimes moving with peristalsis.
- **Reducible hernia:** contents slide back through the defect at rest or with gentle pressure.
- **Incarcerated hernia:** contents stay in the sac.
- **Concern for strangulation:** a non-reducible sac with thick-walled bowel, fluid in the sac, no peristalsis, or no flow in the bowel wall on colour Doppler, together with pain and tenderness.

> **The aha** If the lump hides at rest, make it move.

> **Pitfall** A negative scan in a supine patient at rest does not exclude a small hernia. A lipoma, a lymph node, a haematoma or an abscess also form lumps, and an abscess sometimes sits next to a hernia. Look for a fascial defect and movement before you call a lump a hernia, and before anyone drains it.

## What to do with the finding

- **Painful, non-reducible hernia, or signs of bowel obstruction:** urgent surgical review.
- **Reducible hernia with no red flags:** the routine surgical pathway, with safety-netting advice about pain, vomiting and a lump that will not go back.
- **Uncertain scan with a convincing story:** formal imaging or surgical review.

## Case

A 70-year-old has a tender lump above an old midline scar and has vomited twice. At rest, a sac containing bowel sits above the rectus sheath. It does not reduce with gentle pressure. The bowel wall is thick, there is fluid in the sac, and colour Doppler shows no flow in the bowel wall.

?? What do you do?
+ Arrange urgent surgical review for a likely strangulated incisional hernia
- Drain the fluid in the sac under ultrasound guidance
- Reduce it firmly and discharge
- Book a routine outpatient scan
