---
title: "The full bladder: measure, then look upstream"
minutes: 10
---

A bedside bladder scan confirms retention in minutes, avoids unnecessary catheters, and checks that a catheter is working.

## Estimating the volume

1. Curvilinear probe just above the pubic symphysis, transverse, angled into the pelvis.
2. In the widest transverse view, measure the width, side to side, and the depth, front to back.
3. Rotate to sagittal and measure the length, head to foot.
4. Volume ≈ length × width × depth × 0.52.

The formula treats the bladder as an ellipsoid and gives an estimate. Record the number and compare it with the volume drained.

## Look upstream

Scan both kidneys. Bilateral hydronephrosis with a large bladder suggests longstanding or high-pressure obstruction. Check the creatinine, and expect a diuresis after catheterisation.

## The catheterised patient

A full bladder around a catheter means the catheter is blocked or misplaced. Find the balloon. A balloon inflated in the urethra needs urgent attention. Clot in the bladder needs a three-way catheter and washout.

> **The aha** Measure the bladder. Then check the kidneys.

> **Pitfall** Ascites, pelvic cysts, a full uterus and fluid-filled bowel mimic the bladder. Confirm it in two planes, behind the pubic symphysis, with a visible wall. Automated bladder scanners make the same mistake with ascites.
