## Case 1: the swollen patient

A 78-year-old with heart failure and ascites has vague abdominal discomfort. The gallbladder wall measures 7 mm and looks layered. There are no stones, and pressing over the gallbladder causes no more pain than elsewhere.

?? What is the best interpretation?
+ Non-specific wall thickening from fluid overload, with no evidence of cholecystitis on this scan
- Acute calculous cholecystitis
- Gallbladder perforation
- Normal gallbladder
: Heart failure, ascites and low albumin thicken the wall. Without stones, focal tenderness or pericholecystic fluid, the thick wall does not point to cholecystitis.

## Case 2: the jaundice

A 52-year-old has right upper quadrant pain, jaundice and a fever of 38.9°C. The gallbladder contains stones. The common bile duct measures 12 mm with no flow on colour Doppler.

?? What does this suggest, and what do you do?
+ A dilated duct with likely ascending cholangitis, needing antibiotics, urgent specialist review and drainage
- Normal duct for age, discharge with analgesia
- Acute cholecystitis only, outpatient scan
- Hepatitis, no further imaging needed
: A 12 mm duct is dilated. Fever, jaundice and pain suggest cholangitis from an obstructing stone, which needs antibiotics and urgent drainage.

## Case 3: the confused man

An 84-year-old man is confused with lower abdominal discomfort. The bladder measures 14 × 11 × 10 cm. Both kidneys show moderate hydronephrosis. Creatinine is raised.

?? What is the estimated bladder volume, and what do you expect after catheterisation?
+ About 800 mL, with a possible post-obstructive diuresis needing fluid and electrolyte monitoring
- About 150 mL, so no catheter is needed
- About 1540 mL, with no further monitoring
- About 400 mL, with oliguria expected
: 14 × 11 × 10 × 0.52 is about 800 mL. Chronic retention with hydronephrosis and renal impairment risks a post-obstructive diuresis, so monitor urine output, fluids and electrolytes.

## Case 4: the clot

A 70-year-old has visible haematuria and cannot pass urine. The bladder is full of echogenic material that moves and changes shape when you roll him, with no internal flow.

?? What is the next step?
+ A three-way catheter and bladder washout, with urology referral for the haematuria
- Discharge with antibiotics
- Diagnose bladder tumour from the scan
- Standard catheter and no follow-up
: Mobile, avascular material in a patient with haematuria and retention is clot. A three-way catheter allows washout. Visible haematuria still needs urology assessment.
