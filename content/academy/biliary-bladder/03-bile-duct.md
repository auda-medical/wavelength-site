title: "The common bile duct: find the parallel channel"
minutes: 10

The common bile duct runs from the porta hepatis down behind the duodenum to the pancreatic head. At the porta, it lies anterior to the portal vein, beside the hepatic artery.

## Finding the duct

1. Follow the gallbladder neck to the porta hepatis, or find the portal vein entering the liver.
2. In cross-section, the portal vein and the two tubes in front of it form a face with two ears: the bile duct on the patient's right, the hepatic artery on the left.
3. Rotate so the duct runs across the screen, anterior and parallel to the portal vein.
4. Add colour Doppler with a low velocity scale. The vessels fill. The duct stays black.
5. Measure from inner wall to inner wall at the widest point.

## Normal values

- Most references treat up to 6 mm as normal in an adult with a gallbladder, and some use 7 mm.
- The duct widens with age. A common rule allows about 1 mm more for each decade over 60.
- After cholecystectomy, up to 10 mm can be normal.

## Interpreting a dilated duct

A dilated duct with gallstones, jaundice, deranged liver tests or pancreatitis suggests obstruction, most often a stone. Arrange further imaging and specialist review. Cholangitis, with fever, jaundice and pain, needs urgent antibiotics and drainage.

> **The aha** One parallel tube has no flow. That is your duct.

> **Pitfall** The distal duct hides behind the duodenum, so a bedside scan rarely shows a stone there. A normal-calibre duct does not exclude choledocholithiasis, especially early in obstruction.
