?? Which statement about a normal-calibre bile duct on a bedside scan is correct?
@ 01-context
+ It does not exclude a stone in the distal duct
- It excludes choledocholithiasis
- It excludes cholangitis
- It means the jaundice is pre-hepatic
: The distal duct hides behind the duodenum, and early obstruction may not dilate the duct.

?? What is the upper limit of normal for gallbladder wall thickness?
@ 02-gallbladder-wall
+ 3 mm
- 1 mm
- 6 mm
- 10 mm
: A wall over 3 mm counts as thickened. Measure the anterior wall with the beam at 90° to it.

?? Why measure the anterior gallbladder wall?
@ 02-gallbladder-wall
+ It lies against the liver and has a clean edge, while the posterior wall blends with bowel
- It is always thicker
- It is closer to the cystic duct
- Guidelines forbid measuring the fundus
: The posterior wall merges with bowel and gas behind it, which exaggerates its thickness.

?? Which combination gives the strongest bedside evidence of acute calculous cholecystitis?
@ 02-gallbladder-wall
+ Gallstones with a positive sonographic Murphy's sign
- Wall thickening alone
- A contracted gallbladder after a meal
- A polyp with no shadow
: Stones with focal tenderness under the probe are the strongest pair. Wall thickening and pericholecystic fluid add weight.

?? How do you tell the common bile duct from the hepatic artery?
@ 03-bile-duct
+ Colour Doppler: the artery fills with flow and the duct does not
- The duct always lies behind the portal vein
- The duct is always the larger tube
- The artery shows posterior shadowing
: Both run anterior to the portal vein. Only colour Doppler reliably separates them.

?? A 45-year-old with a gallbladder has a common bile duct of 11 mm. How do you classify it?
@ 03-bile-duct
+ Dilated
- Normal for age
- Normal after a meal
- Too small to measure
: Most references use about 6 mm as the upper limit in a 45-year-old with a gallbladder. 11 mm is dilated.

?? How much wider may the common bile duct be after cholecystectomy and still be normal?
@ 03-bile-duct
+ Up to about 10 mm
- Up to about 3 mm
- Up to about 20 mm
- It must stay under 4 mm
: The duct often widens after cholecystectomy, and up to 10 mm can be normal. Interpret it with symptoms and liver tests.

?? Which measurements do you need to estimate bladder volume?
@ 04-retention
+ Width and depth in transverse, and length in sagittal
- Length in sagittal only
- Width in transverse only
- Wall thickness in both planes
: The ellipsoid formula multiplies three diameters by 0.52.

?? A bladder measures 10 × 9 × 8 cm. What is the estimated volume?
@ 04-retention
+ About 375 mL
- About 720 mL
- About 150 mL
- About 1000 mL
: 10 × 9 × 8 = 720, and 720 × 0.52 is about 375 mL.

?? A catheterised patient has a full bladder on scan. What do you check?
@ 04-retention
+ The balloon position and whether the catheter is blocked
- The ureteric jets
- The gallbladder
- The bladder wall thickness only
: A full bladder around a catheter means the catheter is blocked or not in the bladder.

?? Which findings favour clot over a bladder wall lesion?
@ 05-bladder-contents
+ It moves with position and has no internal flow
- It is fixed to the wall with internal vessels
- It casts a dense shadow
- It lies at the bladder dome
: Clot is unattached and avascular. A fixed mass with vessels suggests a wall lesion.

?? An avascular echogenic mass in the bladder does not move. The patient has visible haematuria. What do you do?
@ 05-bladder-contents
+ Treat it as indeterminate and refer to urology
- Diagnose clot and discharge
- Diagnose a stone
- Repeat the scan in a year
: Organised clot moves little and some tumours show little flow. Visible haematuria with a fixed mass needs urology assessment.
