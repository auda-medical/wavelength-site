Finding the gallbladder and the bladder is a core skill. This module goes one step further, to four questions that change management: is this thick gallbladder wall cholecystitis, is the bile duct dilated, is the bladder truly full and has it affected the kidneys, and is that echogenic material in the bladder clot or a lesion?

## What the bedside scan offers

- **Gallbladder:** the combination of stones, sonographic Murphy's sign, wall thickening and pericholecystic fluid.
- **Bile duct:** a measured duct, separated from vessels with colour Doppler.
- **Bladder:** a volume estimate and a check of both kidneys.
- **Bladder contents:** movement and colour flow to separate clot from an attached lesion.

## What it does not offer

- A normal duct does not exclude a stone in the distal bile duct.
- No flow in a bladder mass does not prove it is clot.
- A scan does not replace bloods, the clinical picture or formal imaging when the question remains open.

> **The aha** Every finding in this module is a clue. The pattern makes the diagnosis.

## Before you start

Complete the abdominal module first, or be confident finding the gallbladder with the main lobar fissure and the kidneys in long axis.
