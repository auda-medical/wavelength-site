title: "Something in the bladder: move it, then add colour"

Echogenic material in the bladder is usually clot, debris or a catheter, but sometimes a tumour. Two checks separate most of them.

## The scan

1. Scan the bladder in transverse and sagittal. Some urine in the bladder helps.
2. Find the material and where it sits.
3. Reposition the patient, roll them or tilt the bed, and scan again.
4. Add colour Doppler over the material.

## Findings

- **Clot:** dependent, moves or changes shape with position, no internal flow. Flow at the edge from the bladder wall is common.
- **Debris:** fine, layering echoes that swirl with movement.
- **Wall lesion:** fixed to the wall, does not move, often with internal vessels.
- **Stone:** bright, with a shadow, and rolls to the dependent wall.

> **The aha** Move it. Colour it. Then decide the next step.

> **Pitfall** Organised clot moves little, and a tumour bleeds and forms clot around itself. No flow does not prove clot. Uncertain findings need urology assessment.

## Acting on it

Visible haematuria needs urology input according to your local pathway, including the suspected cancer referral criteria. Clot retention needs a three-way catheter and bladder washout.
