title: "The gallbladder wall: read the pattern"
minutes: 10

A gallbladder wall over 3 mm is thickened. Many conditions cause it, and only some involve the gallbladder.

## Measuring the wall

1. Find the gallbladder in long axis.
2. Measure the anterior wall, nearest the probe, where it lies against the liver.
3. Keep the beam at 90° to the wall. An oblique cut exaggerates thickness.
4. Do not measure the posterior wall. Bowel gas behind it blurs its edge.

## Causes of a thick wall

- **Gallbladder disease:** acute cholecystitis, chronic cholecystitis, adenomyomatosis, tumour.
- **Systemic causes:** heart failure, ascites, hypoalbuminaemia, liver disease, renal failure, hepatitis, pancreatitis.
- **Physiological:** a contracted gallbladder after a meal.

## The pattern of acute calculous cholecystitis

- Gallstones, especially one impacted in the neck.
- A positive sonographic Murphy's sign: maximal tenderness when you press the probe directly over the gallbladder.
- Wall thickening over 3 mm, often layered or striated.
- Pericholecystic fluid.
- A distended gallbladder.

Gallstones with a positive sonographic Murphy's sign give the strongest bedside combination.

> **The aha** A thick wall is a clue. The pattern makes the diagnosis.

> **Pitfall** Opioids, confusion and a gangrenous gallbladder all remove tenderness. Critically ill patients develop acalculous cholecystitis without stones. When the picture does not fit, use bloods, the clinical course and formal imaging.
