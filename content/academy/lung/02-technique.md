## Probe and settings

- **Linear probe** for the pleural line and sliding, especially in suspected pneumothorax.
- **Curvilinear or phased-array probe** for B-lines, consolidation and effusion at the bases.
- Use a lung preset if you have one, or switch off harmonic imaging and image smoothing, which remove the artefacts you need.
- Set the focus at the pleural line.

## Find the bat first

1. Place the probe head to foot, across two ribs, with the marker to the head.
2. Find two ribs, each with a black shadow beneath it.
3. Between them, a bright line sits slightly deeper than the top of the ribs. This is the pleural line.
4. The ribs form the wings and the pleura the body of a bat.

If you lie the probe along a rib, you see one bright curved line with black below it. That is the rib surface. A rib never slides, so this is how new scanners call absent sliding by mistake.

> **The aha** No two rib shadows, no pleural line you trust. Find the bat, then judge sliding.

## The zones

Divide each side of the chest into areas and scan each one:

- **Anterior:** between the sternum and the anterior axillary line, upper and lower. Free air collects here in a supine patient.
- **Lateral:** between the anterior and posterior axillary lines, upper and lower.
- **Posterolateral (PLAPS point):** posterior axillary line, just above the diaphragm, probe pushed back towards the bed. Fluid and consolidation collect here.

An eight-zone scan, four on each side, is a common approach for interstitial syndrome. For a suspected pneumothorax, start at the highest point of the chest, usually the second to fourth intercostal spaces in the mid-clavicular line, then work outwards.

## M-mode

Place the M-mode line across the pleural line.

- **Seashore sign:** flat lines above the pleura, grainy sand below. The lung is moving.
- **Barcode sign:** flat lines all the way down. No movement at this spot.

> **Pitfall** Moving the probe, or the patient talking, makes a moving trace. Hold the probe still against the chest for M-mode.
