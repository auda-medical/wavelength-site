---
title: "Pleural effusion: find the spine above the diaphragm"
minutes: 12
---

A supine chest X-ray misses a moderate effusion, and it does not separate fluid from collapsed lung. Ultrasound answers both.

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear probe, or the phased array between narrow ribs.
2. Place it in the posterior axillary line, low on the chest, coronal plane, marker to the head. In a supine patient push the probe back as far as the bed allows.
3. Find the diaphragm first: the bright curved line above the liver on the right, or the spleen on the left.
4. Look above the diaphragm.

## Reading the view

- **Normal.** Above the diaphragm, aerated lung moves with breathing, the curtain sign. The spine stops at the diaphragm, because air blocks the beam.
- **Effusion.** A black space above the diaphragm, with the spine seen continuing upwards under the fluid. This is the spine sign.
- **Collapsed lung.** A wedge of tissue-like lung floats or flaps in the fluid.
- **Consolidation.** The spine sign appears here too, because airless lung carries the beam. Look for the black fluid.

> **The aha** Air hides the spine. If you see the spine above the diaphragm, something other than air fills the base. Black means fluid.

## Simple or complex

- **Anechoic:** often a transudate, but exudates can look like this too.
- **Echoes, strands, septations or swirling:** an exudate, such as a parapneumonic effusion, empyema or haemothorax.
- **Thickened, nodular pleura:** raises the question of malignancy.

## Pericardial or pleural

On the left, compare with the parasternal long-axis view. Pleural fluid sits behind the descending aorta. Pericardial fluid runs in front of it.

## Procedures

The British Thoracic Society guideline advises ultrasound guidance for pleural procedures. Mark the site with the patient in the position you will use, measure the depth to fluid, and check for intercostal vessels with colour Doppler. Avoid the area close to the diaphragm, liver and spleen.

> **Pitfall** The spine sign on its own does not separate effusion from consolidation. Confirm black fluid, and scan both sides.
