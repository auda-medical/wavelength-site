Air stops ultrasound. Healthy lung is full of air, so you never see the lung itself. You see the pleural line and the artefacts it makes. Lung ultrasound works because disease changes those artefacts in predictable ways.

## What normal lung looks like

- **The pleural line.** A bright horizontal line, where the chest wall meets the lung, sitting just deeper than the ribs.
- **Lung sliding.** Shimmer along the pleural line as the lung moves with breathing.
- **A-lines.** Bright horizontal lines repeating at equal distances below the pleural line. They are reverberation artefacts: the beam bouncing between the pleura and the probe. A-lines mean air sits below the pleura.

## What disease does

- **Air between the layers (pneumothorax):** sliding stops. A-lines remain, because air is still below the line.
- **Fluid in the lung tissue (interstitial syndrome):** vertical B-lines replace A-lines.
- **Airless lung (consolidation):** the lung looks solid, like liver, with bright air bronchograms.
- **Fluid in the pleural space (effusion):** a black space above the diaphragm, with the spine seen through it.

> **The aha** In healthy lung you only see artefacts. In disease, the artefacts change or the real tissue appears.

## Why it matters

In breathless patients, lung ultrasound is more sensitive than a supine chest X-ray for pneumothorax, pulmonary oedema, consolidation and pleural effusion. The BLUE protocol showed a short sequence of lung signs, combined with a leg vein scan, identifies the main causes of acute respiratory failure in most patients. The 2012 international recommendations support its use at the bedside in trained hands.

## Limits

- Lung ultrasound sees only what touches the pleura. A central pneumonia or tumour is invisible.
- Subcutaneous emphysema, dressings and large body habitus block the view.
- Signs need interpreting in context. B-lines have many causes, and absent sliding has many causes.

> **Pitfall** A normal lung scan in one zone says nothing about the next. Scan every zone the question needs, on both sides.
