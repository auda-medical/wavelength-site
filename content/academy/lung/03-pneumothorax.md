---
title: "Pneumothorax: absent sliding is not enough"
minutes: 15
---

You have found the bat, watched the pleural line, and seen no sliding. Many clinicians stop here and call a pneumothorax. Absent sliding tells you something is wrong. It does not tell you what.

## Four signs, two questions

Each sign answers one question: are the two layers of pleura touching under the probe?

- **Lung sliding.** The layers touch.
- **B-lines.** Vertical lines starting at the lung surface. The lung is against the chest wall, so the layers touch.
- **Lung pulse.** A subtle flicker of the pleural line in time with the heartbeat, with no sliding. The heart is moving lung which lies against the chest wall. The layers touch.
- **Lung point.** The place where absent sliding meets sliding, moving in and out of view with breathing. The edge of the collapsed lung. This confirms pneumothorax.

> **The aha** One B-line or a lung pulse at a spot means no pneumothorax under the probe at that spot. Absent sliding alone means keep scanning.

## Causes of absent sliding without pneumothorax

- Apnoea or breath-holding.
- Right main bronchus intubation: the left lung does not ventilate, shows no sliding, but shows a lung pulse.
- Pleural adhesions after infection, surgery or pleurodesis.
- Severe consolidation, contusion, or large bullae.
- Severe acute respiratory distress, with very stiff lungs.

## Finding the lung point

1. Start anteriorly where sliding is absent.
2. Slide the probe laterally, one intercostal space at a time, keeping it across two ribs.
3. Watch for the place where sliding appears with each breath, then disappears.
4. With M-mode on the lung point, the trace switches between seashore and barcode with breathing.

The further lateral the lung point, the larger the pneumothorax. A lung point in the posterior axillary line suggests a large pneumothorax.

## Acting on the findings

- **Sliding, B-lines or lung pulse present:** no pneumothorax under the probe.
- **Absent sliding, no B-lines, lung point found:** pneumothorax confirmed.
- **Absent sliding, no B-lines, no lung point:** pneumothorax likely, but not proven. A very large pneumothorax has no lung point because the lung has collapsed away from the chest wall. Use the clinical picture.

> **Pitfall** A tension pneumothorax is a clinical diagnosis. In a peri-arrest patient, do not delay decompression to search for a lung point.
