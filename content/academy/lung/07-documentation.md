## A pattern approach for the breathless patient

Scan both sides, anterior, lateral and posterolateral. Then combine with the heart and the leg veins.

- **Sliding present, A-lines everywhere:** a dry lung surface. Think asthma, COPD, pulmonary embolism or a cause outside the lung. Scan the leg veins and the heart.
- **Absent sliding anteriorly, A-lines, lung point:** pneumothorax.
- **Bilateral, evenly spread B-lines with a smooth pleural line:** cardiogenic pulmonary oedema likely. Look at the LV and IVC.
- **Patchy B-lines, irregular pleura, small consolidations:** ARDS or pneumonitis.
- **Focal consolidation or focal B-lines:** pneumonia.
- **Effusion at the bases:** look at the heart and the abdomen for the cause.

## What to document

1. The clinical question.
2. The probe and the zones scanned, left and right.
3. For each zone: sliding present or absent, A-lines or B-lines (number or confluent), consolidation, effusion.
4. Lung point present or absent, if pneumothorax was the question.
5. Effusion: side, approximate depth, simple or complex.
6. Your conclusion and how it changes management.
7. Whether images were saved.

## Example entry

> Lung ultrasound, linear and curvilinear probes. Question: pneumothorax after central line. Right anterior zones: absent sliding, A-lines, no B-lines, lung point in the right mid-axillary line. Left anterior zones: sliding present, A-lines. Conclusion: right pneumothorax. Clips saved. Discussed with registrar, chest X-ray requested and chest drain planned.

> **The aha** Write the finding for each zone. A single word, "normal", hides which zones you looked at.

> **Pitfall** Ultrasound does not measure pneumothorax size reliably. Use the clinical picture and the chest X-ray for management decisions.
