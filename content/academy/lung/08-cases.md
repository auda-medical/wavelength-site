Five patients from the emergency department. Read each case, choose your answer, then read the explanation. These cases do not count towards your score.

## Case 1: the tall young man

A 22-year-old man has sudden right-sided chest pain and mild breathlessness. Oxygen saturation 95%. With the linear probe across two ribs in the right second intercostal space, you see no sliding and A-lines. Moving laterally, in the anterior axillary line, sliding appears and disappears with each breath at one spot.

?? What does this show?
+ A right pneumothorax, confirmed by the lung point
- Normal lung
- Pleural adhesions
- A right pleural effusion
: Absent sliding with A-lines suggests air between the pleural layers. The spot where sliding appears and disappears with breathing is the lung point, the edge of the collapsed lung. This confirms a pneumothorax.

## Case 2: the ventilated patient

A patient was intubated 10 minutes ago. Oxygen saturation is falling. Left anterior chest: no sliding, but the pleural line flickers in time with the heartbeat. Right anterior chest: normal sliding.

?? What is the most likely cause?
+ Right main bronchus intubation
- Left tension pneumothorax
- Left pleural effusion
- Bilateral pneumothorax
: A lung pulse shows the left lung is against the chest wall, so there is no pneumothorax under the probe. The left lung is not ventilating, which fits a tube in the right main bronchus. Check the tube depth and pull it back.

## Case 3: the wet lungs

An 80-year-old woman is breathless and cannot lie flat. Both sides show four or more B-lines in every anterior and lateral zone, with a thin, smooth pleural line and normal sliding. The LV contracts poorly. The IVC is 2.4 cm.

?? What is the most likely diagnosis?
+ Cardiogenic pulmonary oedema
- Bilateral pneumonia
- Pulmonary fibrosis
- Bilateral pneumothorax
: Evenly spread bilateral B-lines with a smooth pleural line, poor LV function and a full IVC point to cardiogenic pulmonary oedema. Pneumonia gives focal or patchy findings. Fibrosis gives a thick, irregular pleural line. B-lines exclude pneumothorax under the probe.

## Case 4: the febrile cough

A 55-year-old man has fever and a productive cough. At the right base you see tissue-like lung with bright dots which move outwards with each breath in. There is a small black space above the diaphragm.

?? What does this show?
+ Right lower lobe pneumonia with a small effusion
- Right lower lobe collapse from a mucus plug
- Pulmonary oedema
- A right haemothorax
: Consolidation with dynamic air bronchograms, which move with breathing, strongly supports pneumonia. The black space above the diaphragm is a small parapneumonic effusion. Look for septations, and sample it if it is large or complex.

## Case 5: the white-out

A 70-year-old woman has a white-out of the left hemithorax on chest X-ray. At the left base you see a large black space above the diaphragm, with the spine seen through it. A wedge of tissue-like lung flaps in the fluid. The fluid contains no echoes.

?? What causes the white-out?
+ A large pleural effusion with compressive atelectasis
- Left lower lobe consolidation alone
- Left pneumonectomy
- Left pneumothorax
: A large anechoic space above the diaphragm with the spine sign is an effusion. The flapping wedge is compressed, airless lung. Ultrasound separates fluid from collapse in a way the X-ray cannot. Plan an ultrasound-guided pleural procedure.
