title: Final assessment
---

?? What are A-lines?
@ 01-context
+ Reverberation artefacts showing air below the pleural line
- Fluid in the interlobular septa
- The ribs seen in cross-section
- The surface of consolidated lung
: A-lines are bright horizontal lines repeating at equal distances below the pleural line. The beam bounces between the pleura and the probe because air lies below the pleura. They appear in normal lung and in pneumothorax.

?? Which finding cannot be seen on lung ultrasound?
@ 01-context
+ A central pneumonia which does not reach the pleura
- A small anterior pneumothorax
- A pleural effusion at the base
- Bilateral B-lines
: Lung ultrasound sees only changes which reach the pleura. Aerated lung blocks the beam, so a central consolidation stays hidden. A normal scan does not exclude a central pneumonia.

?? You see one bright curved line with a black shadow filling the screen below it. What is the problem?
@ 02-technique
+ The probe lies along a rib, so you are watching bone
- The patient has a pneumothorax
- The patient has a large effusion
- The depth is set too shallow
: A probe lying along a rib shows the rib surface and its shadow. A rib never slides. Turn the probe head to foot across two ribs and find the pleural line between the rib shadows, the bat sign.

?? What does the barcode sign on M-mode show?
@ 02-technique
+ No movement of the pleural line at that spot
- Normal lung sliding
- Fluid in the pleural space
- B-lines
: In the barcode, or stratosphere, sign, flat lines run all the way down because nothing moves below the pleural line. Moving lung produces the grainy seashore pattern. Barcode on its own does not prove pneumothorax.

?? Sliding is absent at a spot, but you see two B-lines there. What do you conclude?
@ 03-pneumothorax
+ There is no pneumothorax under the probe at that spot
- There is a pneumothorax under the probe
- The patient has pulmonary oedema
- The scan is inconclusive
: B-lines start at the lung surface. If you see them, the lung is touching the chest wall, so there is no air between the pleural layers at that spot. Absent sliding here has another cause, such as adhesions.

?? Which sign confirms a pneumothorax?
@ 03-pneumothorax
+ The lung point
- Absent lung sliding
- A-lines
- The barcode sign
: The lung point is where sliding and no sliding meet as the edge of the collapsed lung moves with breathing. Absent sliding, A-lines and the barcode sign all occur in other conditions, such as apnoea or adhesions.

?? A ventilated patient has absent sliding on the left with a lung pulse. What is the most likely cause?
@ 03-pneumothorax
+ Right main bronchus intubation
- Left pneumothorax
- Left pleural effusion
- Pulmonary oedema
: A lung pulse shows the lung is against the chest wall, which excludes pneumothorax under the probe. A non-ventilating left lung with a beating heart beneath it fits a tube in the right main bronchus.

?? Which feature defines a B-line?
@ 04-b-lines
+ It starts at the pleural line and runs to the bottom of the screen without fading
- It runs parallel to the pleural line
- It fades after 2 cm
- It stays still while the lung slides
: A B-line starts at the pleural line, reaches the bottom of the screen without fading, erases A-lines and moves with sliding. Short vertical lines which fade are comet tails and are normal.

?? Which pattern points most towards cardiogenic pulmonary oedema?
@ 04-b-lines
+ Evenly spread bilateral B-lines with a thin, smooth pleural line
- Patchy B-lines with a thickened, irregular pleural line
- B-lines in one zone only
- A-lines everywhere with sliding
: Cardiogenic oedema gives diffuse, even B-lines on both sides, with a normal pleural line. ARDS and pneumonitis give a patchy pattern with an irregular pleura. Focal B-lines suggest pneumonia or contusion.

?? Which finding most strongly supports pneumonia in an area of consolidation?
@ 05-consolidation
+ Air bronchograms which move outwards with each breath in
- Air bronchograms which stay still
- A black space above the diaphragm
- A-lines over the area
: Dynamic air bronchograms show air still flowing through an open airway inside consolidated lung. This strongly supports pneumonia. Static air bronchograms occur in resorptive atelectasis and in some pneumonias.

?? What does the shred sign describe?
@ 05-consolidation
+ The ragged deep edge where consolidated lung meets aerated lung
- Septations in a complex effusion
- Movement of the lung edge at a lung point
- Vertical lines from the pleura
: Consolidation which does not fill a whole lobe has an irregular, shredded deep border where it meets aerated lung. This helps separate consolidation from liver or spleen, which have smooth edges.

?? In a normal scan of the right lung base, why does the spine stop at the diaphragm?
@ 06-effusion
+ Air-filled lung above the diaphragm blocks the beam
- The diaphragm reflects all of the beam
- The liver absorbs the beam
- The spine ends at the diaphragm
: Air scatters ultrasound, so aerated lung hides everything behind it. When fluid or consolidation replaces the air, the beam reaches the spine above the diaphragm, giving the spine sign.

?? A febrile patient has a pleural effusion with septations. What does this suggest?
@ 06-effusion
+ An exudate, such as a complex parapneumonic effusion or empyema
- A simple transudate
- Pulmonary oedema
- A normal finding
: Septations, strands and swirling echoes suggest an exudate. In a febrile patient this raises the question of a complex parapneumonic effusion or empyema, which needs sampling and specialist input.

?? What does the British Thoracic Society advise for pleural procedures?
@ 06-effusion
+ Ultrasound guidance, with the site marked in the position used for the procedure
- Landmark technique at the eighth intercostal space
- Ultrasound only for small effusions
- Ultrasound only after a failed landmark attempt
: The BTS guideline advises ultrasound guidance for pleural procedures. Marking the site in the procedure position, measuring the depth to fluid and checking for intercostal vessels with colour Doppler reduce complications.

?? Why record findings zone by zone, rather than "lungs normal"?
@ 07-documentation
+ It shows which areas were scanned and lets the next clinician compare
- It is faster to write
- It replaces the need for a chest X-ray
- It measures the size of a pneumothorax
: Lung findings vary from zone to zone. A zone-by-zone record shows what you looked at, what you did not, and gives a baseline for repeat scans. Ultrasound does not measure pneumothorax size reliably.
