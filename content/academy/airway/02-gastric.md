---
title: "The stomach: empty, fluid or solid"
minutes: 12
---

Fasting times guide sedation and anaesthesia, but they do not tell you what is in the stomach. Trauma, pain, opioids, diabetes, pregnancy and bowel obstruction all slow gastric emptying. A scan of the gastric antrum shows the contents directly.

## The scan

1. Use the curvilinear probe.
2. Place it sagittally in the epigastrium, just to the right of the midline, marker to the head.
3. Find the landmarks: the left lobe of the liver near the probe, and the aorta or IVC deep to the stomach.
4. The gastric antrum lies between them.
5. Scan supine, then in the right lateral decubitus position, right side down. Fluid runs into the antrum in this position, so small volumes show.

## Reading the view

- **Empty.** A small, collapsed antrum with layered walls, like a target or bull's-eye, in both positions.
- **Clear fluid.** A round, distended antrum with black contents. Small volumes in the right lateral position only are often normal secretions.
- **Thick fluid or solid.** Mixed, bright contents. Fresh solid food often looks like frosted glass, which hides the back wall.

Van de Putte and Perlas review this qualitative grading, and the evidence that antral cross-sectional area in the right lateral position tracks gastric volume. Validated formulas estimate the volume of clear fluid in adults. Learn qualitative grading first, because it answers the main question.

> **The aha** Empty, fluid or solid. Answer that before you count millilitres.

> **Pitfall** Gastric ultrasound informs the aspiration-risk assessment. Use it with the history, the urgency of the procedure and local guidance. A full stomach changes how you sedate or intubate, not whether a life-saving procedure goes ahead.

## Acting on the result

- **Empty:** supports proceeding as planned.
- **Solid or large fluid volume:** treat as a full stomach. Discuss timing, airway protection and technique with a senior.
