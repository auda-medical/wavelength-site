?? Which statement about airway ultrasound is correct?
@ 01-context
+ It adds to waveform capnography for tube confirmation but does not replace it
- It replaces capnography for tube confirmation
- It shows the depth of the tube tip
- It is a stand-alone test of whether sedation is safe
: Waveform capnography remains the standard for tube confirmation. Neck ultrasound shows trachea or oesophagus, not tube depth. Gastric ultrasound informs the aspiration-risk assessment alongside the history and guidance.

?? Where do you place the probe to see the gastric antrum?
@ 02-gastric
+ Sagittally in the epigastrium, just right of the midline, between the left lobe of the liver and the aorta or IVC
- Transversely over the left upper quadrant
- In the right iliac fossa
- Over the suprasternal notch
: The antrum lies in the epigastrium, just right of the midline, between the left lobe of the liver anteriorly and the great vessels posteriorly. These landmarks keep the view reproducible.

?? Why scan the antrum in the right lateral decubitus position?
@ 02-gastric
+ Fluid runs into the antrum, so small volumes show
- It moves the liver out of the way
- It makes solid food look like fluid
- It empties the stomach
: Gravity carries fluid into the dependent antrum when the patient lies right side down. Small volumes only seen in this position are often normal secretions.

?? A collapsed antrum with layered walls, like a target, is seen in both positions. What does this show?
@ 02-gastric
+ An empty stomach
- Clear fluid
- Solid food
- Gastric outlet obstruction
: The empty antrum is flat with layered walls, often described as a target or bull's-eye. Fluid distends it with black contents. Solid food gives bright, mixed contents.

?? What does the double tract sign show?
@ 03-tube
+ Oesophageal intubation
- Tracheal intubation
- Main bronchus intubation
- A normal neck before intubation
: Air in an intubated oesophagus creates a second air-mucosa interface beside the trachea. Two air shadows side by side mean the tube is in the wrong place.

?? Where does the oesophagus usually lie at the suprasternal notch?
@ 03-tube
+ Behind and to the left of the trachea
- Directly in front of the trachea
- To the right of the right carotid artery
- Inside the thyroid
: The oesophagus usually sits posterolateral to the trachea on the left. Find it before intubation to know where a second tract would appear.

?? Why does neck ultrasound help during cardiac arrest?
@ 03-tube
+ Low pulmonary blood flow gives a low or flat capnography trace even with the tube in the trachea
- Capnography does not work during chest compressions
- Ultrasound shows tube depth during arrest
- Ultrasound replaces the need to ventilate
: In arrest, little carbon dioxide reaches the lungs, so the trace is low even with a tracheal tube. Ultrasound gives a direct look at tube position. A trace usually appears with effective compressions.

?? After intubation, the left chest shows no sliding but a lung pulse. What does this mean?
@ 04-lungs
+ The left lung is against the chest wall but not ventilated
- There is a left pneumothorax
- The tube is in the oesophagus
- The left lung is ventilating normally
: A lung pulse shows the lung touches the chest wall, which excludes pneumothorax under the probe. Absent sliding means it is not moving with ventilation, as in a right main bronchus intubation.

?? A ventilated patient becomes hypotensive with rising airway pressures and falling saturations. What do you do?
@ 04-lungs
+ Treat as tension pneumothorax and decompress without waiting for imaging
- Search for a lung point before acting
- Arrange a chest X-ray first
- Increase the tidal volume
: Tension pneumothorax in a ventilated patient is a clinical diagnosis. Absent sliding supports it, but decompression must not wait for a lung point or an X-ray.

?? In a subcostal view of the right hemidiaphragm, which way does a normal diaphragm move on inspiration?
@ 05-diaphragm
+ Towards the probe, up on the screen
- Away from the probe, down on the screen
- It stays still
- Sideways across the screen
: The probe sits below the diaphragm. On inspiration the diaphragm contracts and moves caudally, towards the probe, which is up on the screen. The M-mode trace rises with each breath in.

?? What is paradoxical diaphragm movement?
@ 05-diaphragm
+ Movement away from the probe on a sniff, as negative chest pressure pulls a paralysed diaphragm upwards
- Movement towards the probe on a sniff
- Larger movement on the left than the right
- Movement only during expiration
: A paralysed hemidiaphragm cannot contract. A sharp sniff lowers the pressure in the chest and sucks it upwards, away from the probe. This confirms paralysis.

?? Why is diaphragm movement during a ventilator breath unreliable?
@ 05-diaphragm
+ The ventilator pushes the diaphragm down passively, so movement does not prove the muscle is working
- The ventilator stops the diaphragm moving
- M-mode does not work in ventilated patients
- The liver blocks the view in ventilated patients
: Positive pressure displaces the diaphragm whether or not it contracts. Assess movement during spontaneous breaths to judge muscle function.
