Unrecognised oesophageal intubation is a preventable cause of death. The 2022 consensus guideline from the Project for Universal Management of Airways sets the standard: sustained waveform capnography, and if there is no trace, the tube is in the wrong place until proven otherwise.

Capnography has a weakness. In cardiac arrest, low pulmonary blood flow gives a low or flat trace even when the tube is in the trachea. Neck ultrasound shows the tube's position directly.

## Anatomy

In a transverse view just above the suprasternal notch:

- **The trachea** sits in the midline as a bright curved line, the air-mucosa interface, with reverberation shadows behind it.
- **The oesophagus** usually sits behind and to the left of the trachea, as a round, layered structure. It is collapsed when empty.

## The scan

1. Use the linear probe, transverse, just above the suprasternal notch.
2. Find the trachea and the oesophagus before intubation.
3. Hold the probe still while the tube passes. Watch the trachea and the oesophagus.
4. Confirm with waveform capnography.

## Reading the view

- **Tracheal intubation.** One air-mucosa interface in the midline. A brief flutter in the trachea as the tube passes. The oesophagus stays collapsed.
- **Oesophageal intubation.** A second bright curved line with reverberation appears where the oesophagus was. Two air shadows side by side: the double tract sign.

Chou and colleagues described this tracheal rapid ultrasound examination during emergency intubation and found it accurate in experienced hands.

> **The aha** One tract good. Two tracts stop.

> **Pitfall** Scanning off the midline, or pressing hard, distorts the view. Neck ultrasound shows whether the tube is in the trachea, not how deep it sits. Ultrasound adds to capnography. It does not replace it.

## The nasogastric tube

The same window shows a nasogastric tube in the oesophagus: a bright echo with a shadow in transverse, two bright parallel lines in long axis. That helps when insertion is difficult. It is a scout, not clearance: in UK practice, position is confirmed by an aspirate pH of 1 to 5.5 or a chest X-ray read by a competent person before anything goes down the tube. A tube that cannot be seen proves nothing, and one that can be seen may still be coiled.
