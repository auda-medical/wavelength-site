Five patients from the emergency department. Read each case, choose your answer, then read the explanation. These cases do not count towards your score.

## Case 1: the negative scan

A 54-year-old man has a painful, swollen right calf after a long flight. His Wells score is 2. You start interim anticoagulation while the D-dimer is processed. Your compression scan shows the common femoral vein, saphenofemoral junction, bifurcation and popliteal vein all compress fully. The D-dimer comes back positive.

?? What is the best next step?
- Continue anticoagulation for three months
+ Stop interim anticoagulation and arrange a repeat proximal scan in 6 to 8 days
- Discharge with no follow-up
- Request a CT pulmonary angiogram
: The proximal veins are clear, but the positive D-dimer leaves a calf clot possible. NICE advises stopping interim anticoagulation and repeating the proximal scan 6 to 8 days later, to catch a clot which has spread up into the popliteal vein. Treating without a diagnosis exposes him to bleeding risk. Discharge without follow-up misses this group. He has no symptoms of pulmonary embolism.

## Case 2: the whole leg

A 70-year-old woman, three weeks after a hip replacement, has a swollen left leg from thigh to ankle. In the groin, the common femoral vein is larger than the artery. You press until the artery dents. The vein does not change shape, and you see faint grey echoes inside it.

?? What does this show?
+ Acute proximal DVT
- A normal vein which needs more pressure
- Chronic DVT
- A groin lymph node
: The artery dented, so the pressure was enough. A distended vein which does not compress, with faint echoes in the lumen, is acute thrombus. More pressure is only needed when the artery has not changed shape. Chronic clot shrinks the vein and brightens. A lymph node does not sit in the line of the vein beside the artery.

## Case 3: behind the knee

A 45-year-old man with knee arthritis has a painful swelling behind the knee and in the calf. The popliteal vein compresses fully from the crease to the trifurcation. On the medial side of the fossa you see an anechoic, comma-shaped structure with a neck towards the joint. It does not compress and does not run up and down the leg.

?? What is the most likely diagnosis?
+ A popliteal (Baker's) cyst, with no DVT in the segments scanned
- Popliteal vein thrombosis
- A popliteal artery aneurysm
- A duplicated popliteal vein containing clot
: The popliteal vein compressed, so there is no clot in it. The medial, comma-shaped, anechoic structure with a neck towards the joint is a popliteal cyst. A ruptured cyst explains the calf pain. A popliteal aneurysm is a dilated, pulsatile artery with colour flow. A duplicated vein runs alongside the artery as a tube.

## Case 4: the groin node

A 62-year-old woman has a swollen right leg. In the groin you see a round, dark structure beside the vessels which does not compress. Sweeping up and down, it ends after 2 cm and has a bright centre. The common femoral vein beside the artery compresses fully, all the way through the bifurcation.

?? What is the round structure?
+ A lymph node
- A thrombosed common femoral vein
- A thrombosed great saphenous vein
- A femoral artery pseudoaneurysm
: A structure which ends after a short distance and has a bright fatty centre is a lymph node. A vein runs on as a tube. The common femoral vein compressed fully, so it contains no clot. The great saphenous vein joins the common femoral vein from the medial side and runs down the leg. A pseudoaneurysm shows swirling colour flow.

## Case 5: the flat signal

A 38-year-old woman has swelling of her whole left leg. The compression scan is normal from the groin to the trifurcation. Spectral Doppler in the left common femoral vein shows a flat, continuous signal with no change on breathing. On the right, the signal varies with each breath.

?? What is the best next step?
+ Arrange formal imaging of the pelvic veins
- Discharge, as the compression scan is normal
- Repeat the compression scan in 6 to 8 days
- Treat as cellulitis
: Loss of respiratory variation in the common femoral vein, with whole-leg swelling, suggests a blockage higher up, in the iliac veins or the IVC. The compression scan does not reach these. Arrange formal imaging, such as CT or MR venography, under your local pathway. Discharge misses a proximal clot. The 6 to 8 day repeat scan covers calf clot, not the pelvis.
