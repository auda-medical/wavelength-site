A patient arrives with a swollen, painful leg. The question is narrow: is there a clot in the proximal deep veins, from the groin to the back of the knee? Proximal deep vein thrombosis (DVT) carries most of the risk of pulmonary embolism. Clot confined to the calf carries less.

Point-of-care compression ultrasound answers that question at the bedside, in minutes, without waiting for a formal scan. It does not replace clinical judgement or your local pathway. It gives the pathway its answer sooner.

## The NICE pathway

NICE guideline NG158 sets the pathway in England. It starts with the two-level DVT Wells score.

- **DVT likely: Wells 2 or more.** Offer a proximal leg vein ultrasound scan within 4 hours. If the scan is not available within 4 hours, take a D-dimer, start interim therapeutic anticoagulation, and scan within 24 hours.
- **DVT unlikely: Wells 1 or less.** Offer a D-dimer. If it is positive, offer a proximal leg vein scan within 4 hours, or interim anticoagulation and a scan within 24 hours. If it is negative, think about other diagnoses.

After the scan:

- **Scan positive.** Diagnose DVT and treat.
- **Scan negative, D-dimer positive.** Stop interim anticoagulation and repeat the proximal scan 6 to 8 days later. This catches a calf clot which has spread upwards.
- **Scan negative, D-dimer negative.** Stop interim anticoagulation and think about other diagnoses.

> **The aha** The 6 to 8 day repeat scan exists because a proximal scan does not look at the calf. A negative point-of-care scan answers one question, not all of them.

## The two-level Wells score

One point for each:

- Active cancer, treated within 6 months or palliative
- Paralysis, paresis or recent plaster cast of a leg
- Bedridden for 3 days or more, or major surgery within 12 weeks
- Localised tenderness along the deep veins
- Entire leg swollen
- Calf swelling over 3 cm compared with the other leg, measured 10 cm below the tibial tuberosity
- Pitting oedema confined to the symptomatic leg
- Collateral superficial veins, not varicose
- Previous DVT

Subtract 2 points if an alternative diagnosis is at least as likely as DVT.

## How accurate is it?

A 2024 systematic review of emergency physician DVT scanning pooled 15 studies and 2,511 scans. Sensitivity was 90% and specificity 95% against formal imaging. Two findings matter for your practice:

- Experienced scanners were more accurate than trainees.
- Three-point compression was more sensitive than two-point compression.

Accuracy depends on the operator. Your department's governance decides when a point-of-care result stands alone and when a formal scan confirms it. Until you are signed off, scan under supervision and confirm your result.

## Who this scan suits

- Patients with suspected proximal DVT, as part of the NICE pathway.
- Out of hours, when a formal scan means interim anticoagulation and a return visit.
- Patients with a contraindication to anticoagulation, where waiting carries risk.

It does not suit patients where the question is the calf, the pelvis or the IVC. Those need formal imaging.
