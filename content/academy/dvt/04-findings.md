## Normal

In every segment, the vein walls meet under pressure and the lumen disappears. The artery stays round or dents slightly. When you release, the vein springs back.

## Abnormal: non-compressible vein

A vein which does not collapse under enough pressure to dent the artery contains thrombus. This is a positive scan.

- **Complete non-compression.** The vein stays the same size under pressure. Occlusive clot.
- **Partial compression.** The vein narrows but the walls do not meet. Non-occlusive clot. This is still a positive scan.

> **The aha** "Mostly compressible" is not compressible. If the walls do not meet, call it.

## What the clot looks like

- **Acute clot** is often anechoic or faintly grey, so it looks like blood. The vein is often distended, larger than the artery beside it. You may see a free-floating tail moving in the lumen.
- **Older clot** is brighter. The vein is smaller, the wall is thick and irregular, and the vein partly compresses as it recanalises.

Telling acute from chronic clot is a job for formal imaging. A patient with a previous DVT in the same leg needs a formal scan, because old clot makes a point-of-care scan hard to read.

Once you have seen a non-compressible segment, you have your answer. Do not keep pressing on a visible free-floating clot.

## Indeterminate

Some scans do not answer the question:

- You cannot see the vein, because of obesity, oedema or a dressing.
- You cannot press hard enough, because of pain.
- The anatomy is unclear.

Record the scan as indeterminate and arrange a formal scan. An indeterminate scan is not a negative scan.
