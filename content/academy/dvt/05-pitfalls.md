## Lymph nodes

Groin lymph nodes look like round, dark structures in a single frame, and they do not compress. Sweep up and down. A vein runs on as a tube. A node ends after a centimetre or two, and often has a bright centre, the fatty hilum.

## Popliteal (Baker's) cyst

A fluid-filled cyst on the medial side of the popliteal fossa, between the medial head of gastrocnemius and the semimembranosus tendon. It is anechoic, often comma-shaped, with a neck towards the joint. It does not run up and down the leg like a vein. A ruptured cyst causes calf pain and swelling which mimics DVT. A cyst and a DVT also occur together, so always compress the popliteal vein.

## Duplicated veins

Two femoral or popliteal veins beside one artery. If you compress one and miss the other, you miss the clot. Compress both.

## Femoral or deep femoral?

Below the bifurcation, the deep femoral vein dives laterally and deep. Follow the vein running with the femoral artery down the thigh.

## Not enough pressure

The most common cause of a false positive. If the artery has not changed shape, you have not pressed hard enough.

## Compressing in longitudinal

The most common cause of a false negative. The vein slides out of the beam and looks collapsed. Compress in transverse.

## Superficial vein thrombosis

Clot in the great saphenous vein is superficial. Clot close to the saphenofemoral junction is close to the deep system and needs urgent formal assessment. Follow your local pathway.

## What the scan does not see

- **Calf veins.** A proximal scan does not exclude a calf DVT. This is why the NICE pathway repeats the scan at 6 to 8 days when the D-dimer is positive.
- **Iliac veins and the IVC.** Swelling of the whole leg with a normal groin compression scan, or a flat CFV Doppler signal, points to a clot higher up. Arrange formal imaging of the pelvic veins.

## Other causes of a swollen leg

Cellulitis gives a cobblestone pattern of fluid in the fat. A calf muscle tear or haematoma gives a collection within the muscle. Heart failure and low albumin give swelling in both legs. Point-of-care ultrasound helps you find these, but it does not replace clinical assessment.
