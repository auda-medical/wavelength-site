?? A patient has a two-level DVT Wells score of 2. Within what time does NICE advise a proximal leg vein ultrasound scan?
@ 01-context
- 1 hour
+ 4 hours
- 24 hours
- 72 hours
: For a Wells score of 2 or more, NICE NG158 advises a proximal leg vein scan within 4 hours. If the scan is not available in 4 hours, take a D-dimer, start interim therapeutic anticoagulation and scan within 24 hours.

?? A patient with a Wells score of 0 has a negative D-dimer. What is the best next step?
@ 01-context
+ Think about other diagnoses, with no scan needed
- Proximal leg vein scan within 4 hours
- Interim anticoagulation and a scan within 24 hours
- Repeat the D-dimer in 6 to 8 days
: With DVT unlikely and a negative D-dimer, NICE advises thinking about other diagnoses. A scan is offered when the D-dimer is positive. Interim anticoagulation applies when a scan or D-dimer result is delayed.

?? Which item scores minus 2 on the two-level DVT Wells score?
@ 01-context
- Previous DVT
+ An alternative diagnosis at least as likely as DVT
- Age over 65
- Varicose veins
: An alternative diagnosis at least as likely as DVT subtracts 2 points. Previous DVT adds 1 point. Age and varicose veins are not part of the score. Collateral superficial veins score, but only when they are not varicose.

?? A 2024 systematic review of emergency physician DVT scanning found which result?
@ 01-context
+ Three-point compression was more sensitive than two-point compression
- Trainees were as accurate as experienced scanners
- Sensitivity was below 60%
- Doppler was required for accurate diagnosis
: The review pooled 15 studies with 2,511 scans: sensitivity 90%, specificity 95%. Three-point compression was more sensitive than two-point, and experienced scanners were more accurate than trainees. Compression, not Doppler, made the diagnosis.

?? In the groin, which vessel joins the common femoral vein from the medial side?
@ 02-anatomy
+ The great saphenous vein
- The deep femoral vein
- The femoral vein
- The external iliac vein
: The great saphenous vein joins the common femoral vein medially at the saphenofemoral junction. The femoral and deep femoral veins are the branches of the common femoral vein below the junction. The external iliac vein continues the common femoral vein above the inguinal ligament.

?? A formal report describes clot in the "superficial femoral vein". How do you interpret this?
@ 02-anatomy
+ A deep vein thrombosis, as the femoral vein is a deep vein
- A superficial vein thrombosis, needing no anticoagulation
- A clot in the great saphenous vein
- An artefact of the old naming system with no clinical meaning
: "Superficial femoral vein" is the old name for the femoral vein, which is a deep vein. Clot in it is a DVT and needs treatment. The great saphenous vein is the main superficial vein of the leg.

?? You scan the popliteal fossa from behind, in transverse. Which deep vessel lies closest to the probe?
@ 02-anatomy
+ The popliteal vein
- The popliteal artery
- The deep femoral vein
- The anterior tibial vein
: From behind, the popliteal vein lies between the skin and the popliteal artery, so it is the first deep vessel on the screen. The deep femoral vein runs in the thigh. The anterior tibial vein lies at the front of the leg, below the knee.

?? Why raise the head of the bed before the scan?
@ 03-technique
+ To fill the leg veins so they are easier to see and compress
- To reduce the risk of pulmonary embolism during compression
- To bring the popliteal vein closer to the skin
- To make the artery easier to compress
: Raising the head of the bed by 10° to 30° lets gravity fill the leg veins. A full vein is easier to find, and its collapse under pressure is easier to see. It does not change embolic risk or the depth of the popliteal vein.

?? You press over the common femoral vein. The artery has not changed shape and the vein is still open. What is the best next step?
@ 03-technique
+ Press harder until the artery starts to deform
- Record the vein as non-compressible
- Switch to longitudinal and compress again
- Use colour Doppler to confirm clot
: If the artery has not deformed, the pressure is not yet enough to judge the vein. Press harder. Only a vein which stays open while the artery dents is non-compressible. Compression in longitudinal gives false reassurance. Colour Doppler does not replace compression.

?? Which plane do you compress in?
@ 03-technique
+ Transverse
- Longitudinal
- Either, as long as the vein stays on screen
- Oblique at 45°
: Compress in transverse. In longitudinal, a small sideways slip takes the vein out of the beam and mimics collapse. In transverse, you keep both vessels on screen and watch the vein walls meet.

?? Spectral Doppler in the common femoral vein shows a flat signal with no variation on breathing. What does this suggest?
@ 03-technique
+ A blockage higher up, in the iliac veins or IVC
- A normal finding
- A calf vein thrombosis
- A popliteal cyst
: Normal flow in the common femoral vein varies with breathing. A flat, continuous signal suggests obstruction between the groin and the heart, in the iliac veins or the IVC. The compression scan does not reach these, so arrange formal imaging.

?? Under pressure that dents the artery, the common femoral vein narrows by half but the walls do not meet. How do you report this segment?
@ 04-findings
+ Non-compressible, consistent with non-occlusive thrombus
- Compressible, as the vein narrowed
- Indeterminate, needing a repeat scan in 6 to 8 days
- Normal for a patient with leg swelling
: A vein which does not close completely under enough pressure contains thrombus, even if it narrows. Partial compression means non-occlusive clot. Report it as a positive scan.

?? You cannot see the vein clearly in a patient with severe leg oedema. How do you record the scan?
@ 04-findings
+ Indeterminate, with a formal scan arranged
- Negative, as no clot was seen
- Positive, as the vein did not compress
- Not needed, as oedema explains the swelling
: When the scan cannot answer the question, record it as indeterminate and arrange a formal scan. An indeterminate scan is not a negative scan. Calling it positive or negative without a clear view risks harm.

?? In the groin you see a round, dark structure which does not compress. It ends after 2 cm and has a bright centre. What is it?
@ 05-pitfalls
+ A lymph node
- A common femoral vein thrombus
- A saphenofemoral junction thrombus
- A femoral artery aneurysm
: A structure which ends after a short distance and has a bright fatty hilum is a lymph node. A vein runs on as a tube. Sweep up and down to tell them apart. An aneurysm is a dilated artery with pulsation and colour flow.

?? A compression scan of the left leg is normal from the groin to the trifurcation. Which statement is correct?
@ 06-documentation
+ The scan does not assess the calf veins, iliac veins or IVC
- The scan excludes DVT in the left leg
- The scan excludes pulmonary embolism
- The D-dimer result no longer matters
: A proximal compression scan answers whether there is clot in the segments scanned. It does not assess the calf veins, the iliac veins or the IVC, and it does not exclude pulmonary embolism. With a positive D-dimer, NICE advises a repeat proximal scan in 6 to 8 days.
