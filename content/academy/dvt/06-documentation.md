## What to record

A good record says what you scanned, what you found, what you did not scan, and who scanned it.

- Indication and Wells score
- Side, probe and technique
- Each segment and whether it compressed fully
- What you did not assess
- Your impression, in one line
- Images or clips stored
- Your name, grade and point-of-care ultrasound status: supervised, or signed off for independent practice

## Example

> **Example record** Point-of-care compression ultrasound, left leg, two-region technique, linear probe. Indication: suspected DVT, Wells 3. Common femoral vein above, at and below the saphenofemoral junction, and the femoral vein bifurcation: fully compressible. Popliteal vein from the popliteal crease to the trifurcation: fully compressible. Calf veins, iliac veins and IVC not assessed. Impression: no proximal DVT in the segments scanned. Clips stored. Scanned under supervision, confirmed by a signed-off supervisor.

## Store your images

Save a clip of each region with and without compression. Stored images let a supervisor review your scan, support your portfolio, and protect you if the result is questioned later. Never store patient-identifiable images on a personal device.

## Act on the result

- **Positive.** Treat as DVT under your local pathway.
- **Negative, Wells likely.** Check the D-dimer. If it is positive, stop interim anticoagulation and book a repeat proximal scan in 6 to 8 days.
- **Negative, Wells unlikely, with a positive D-dimer.** The negative scan answers the question at this point. Think about other diagnoses.
- **Indeterminate.** Arrange a formal scan.

## Safety-net

Tell the patient what the scan did and did not look at. Give clear advice to return with new chest pain, breathlessness, coughing blood, or swelling which spreads or worsens.

## Your scope

Point-of-care ultrasound answers a focused question. Work within your department's governance, record your status on every scan, and keep a logbook. Supervised scans build towards sign-off for independent practice.
