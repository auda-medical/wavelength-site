Four patients. Read each case, choose your answer, then read the explanation. These cases do not count towards your score.

## Case 1: the curtain

A 58-year-old has seen a dark curtain coming down over the upper half of his left visual field since this morning. His central vision is normal. Ocular ultrasound shows a thick, bright, folded membrane tethered to the optic disc, which moves little when he looks from side to side.

?? What is the next step?
+ Urgent same-day referral to ophthalmology by phone, for a macula-sparing retinal detachment
- Routine outpatient ophthalmology referral
- Discharge, as central vision is normal
- Repeat the scan in a week
: A thick membrane tethered to the disc with little movement is a retinal detachment. A detachment which spares the macula needs urgent surgery to protect central vision.

## Case 2: the floaters

A 64-year-old has new floaters and flashes in the right eye. Ultrasound with high gain shows a thin, faint membrane which swings freely and keeps moving after the eye stops. It does not attach to the optic disc.

?? What do you do?
+ Arrange same-day ophthalmology assessment for a dilated examination
- Discharge, as this is a benign age-related change
- Treat with antibiotic eye drops
- Repeat the scan in a month
: A free, mobile membrane is a posterior vitreous detachment. It can tear the retina as it separates, and ultrasound misses small tears. New flashes and floaters need a same-day dilated examination.

## Case 3: the teenager

A 14-year-old woke at 5 am with sudden severe right testicular pain and has vomited twice. The right testis lies high and horizontal. The cremasteric reflex is absent.

?? What is the next step?
+ Call urology immediately for scrotal exploration, without waiting for a scan
- Arrange a formal scrotal ultrasound first
- Start antibiotics for epididymo-orchitis
- Give analgesia and review in the morning
: Sudden severe pain, vomiting, a high horizontal testis and an absent cremasteric reflex make torsion likely. The chance of salvage falls with time. Imaging must not delay exploration.

## Case 4: the hot epididymis

A 32-year-old has two days of gradually worsening left scrotal pain and dysuria. The left epididymis is enlarged with much more colour flow than the right. The cord follows a straight course with no twist, and the testis shows normal flow.

?? What do these findings support?
+ Epididymitis, with torsion clinically and sonographically unlikely
- Testicular torsion
- Torsion of a testicular appendage
- A normal scan
: Gradual onset, urinary symptoms and an enlarged, hyperaemic epididymis with a straight cord fit epididymitis. Remember a testis which has untwisted also looks hyperaemic, so the history matters. If in doubt, involve urology.
