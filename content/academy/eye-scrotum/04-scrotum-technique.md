## Set up

1. Explain the scan, offer a chaperone and record the offer.
2. Lie the patient supine. Support the scrotum on a rolled towel between the thighs, and cover the penis.
3. Use the high-frequency linear probe with warm gel.

## The scan

1. **Start on the painless side.** It sets the grey-scale and colour settings for a normal testis and reassures the patient.
2. **Testis in long and short axis.** Look at its size, shape and texture. Normal testis is evenly grey and finely speckled.
3. **Epididymis.** The head sits at the upper pole. Compare its size and flow with the other side.
4. **Colour Doppler.** Set the scale low and the gain to just below noise. Compare flow inside the two testes side by side, in one image if you can.
5. **The spermatic cord.** Follow it from the top of the testis up towards the inguinal canal, in short axis. A twist often lies just above the testis.
6. **The other side.** Repeat for the painful side with the same settings.

> **The aha** Same settings, both sides, side by side. A difference matters more than any single image.

> **Pitfall** Slow intratesticular flow, especially in young boys, is hard to show on colour Doppler. Absent colour in a normal testis can be a settings problem, and present colour does not exclude partial torsion.
