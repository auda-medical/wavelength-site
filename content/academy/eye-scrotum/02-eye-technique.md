## Before you start

- **Suspected open globe:** do not scan. Penetrating trauma, an irregular pupil, a collapsed anterior chamber or extruded contents mean pressure risks further damage. Shield the eye and refer urgently.
- **Select the ophthalmic or ocular preset.** The lens and retina are sensitive to heating and mechanical effects. Regulatory limits for the eye are far stricter than for the rest of the body, with a mechanical index of 0.23 or less. The ophthalmic preset keeps the output within them. Keep the scan short.

## The scan

1. Lie the patient back with the eye closed.
2. Cover the closed eyelid with a thick layer of gel. A clear adhesive dressing under the gel keeps gel out of the eye.
3. Rest the side of your hand on the patient's nose or forehead. The probe floats on the gel with no pressure on the globe.
4. Scan in transverse, then sagittal, sweeping through the whole globe.
5. Increase the gain to see faint membranes and echoes in the vitreous.
6. Ask the patient to look left, then right, then straight ahead, while you watch. Then stop and watch what keeps moving. These are the after-movements.

## Normal anatomy

- **Anterior chamber and lens:** a thin bright curve at the front, with the lens behind it as a bright outline.
- **Vitreous:** black.
- **Retina and choroid:** a thin, bright, smooth line lining the back of the globe.
- **Optic nerve:** a dark band running back from the globe.

> **The aha** Float the probe. If the eyelid dents, you are pressing.

> **Pitfall** Low gain hides a posterior vitreous detachment and a mild haemorrhage. Turn the gain up before you call the vitreous clear.
