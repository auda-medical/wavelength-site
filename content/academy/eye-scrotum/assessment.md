?? A patient has a penetrating injury to the eye and an irregular pupil. What do you do?
@ 02-eye-technique
+ Do not scan. Shield the eye and refer urgently
- Scan with firm pressure to see the retina
- Scan with the abdominal preset
- Scan only the other eye
: An irregular pupil after penetrating trauma suggests an open globe. Pressure risks extruding the contents. Protect the eye and refer to ophthalmology urgently.

?? Why use the ophthalmic preset for ocular ultrasound?
@ 02-eye-technique
+ It keeps the acoustic output within the stricter limits set for the eye
- It improves the depth of penetration
- It switches on colour Doppler
- It makes the scan quicker
: The lens and retina are sensitive to heating and mechanical effects. The limits for the eye, including a mechanical index of 0.23 or less, are far stricter than for other organs.

?? How do you hold the probe during ocular ultrasound?
@ 02-eye-technique
+ Resting your hand on the nose or forehead so the probe floats on thick gel without pressing
- Pressing firmly to get good contact
- Holding it with the fingertips only, above the face
- With the eye open to see the lens
: Anchoring your hand on the face controls the probe, and a thick layer of gel gives contact without pressure on the globe. If the eyelid dents, you are pressing.

?? Which features describe a retinal detachment?
@ 03-eye-findings
+ A thick, bright membrane tethered to the optic disc, which moves little
- A thin membrane which moves freely and is not attached to the disc
- Grey swirling echoes throughout the vitreous
- A black vitreous with a smooth retina
: The retina stays anchored at the optic disc, so a detachment is tethered there. It is thicker and stiffer than a posterior vitreous detachment and moves little with eye movement.

?? What did the Lahham study show about posterior vitreous detachment?
@ 03-eye-findings
+ Ultrasound had a low sensitivity of 42.5% but a high specificity of 96.0%
- Ultrasound had a sensitivity of 96.9% for posterior vitreous detachment
- Ultrasound cannot see the vitreous
- Posterior vitreous detachment never causes symptoms
: Emergency physician ultrasound detected retinal detachment well but missed many posterior vitreous detachments. A normal scan does not exclude them, so new floaters need ophthalmology review.

?? What is the snow globe appearance on ocular ultrasound?
@ 03-eye-findings
+ Vitreous haemorrhage, with low-level echoes swirling after eye movement
- Retinal detachment
- A normal vitreous
- Lens dislocation
: Blood in the vitreous gives fine grey echoes which swirl after the eye moves. Turn the gain up to see them, and look carefully for a detachment hidden behind the blood.

?? Which side do you scan first in a patient with a painful testis?
@ 04-scrotum-technique
+ The painless side, to set the normal grey-scale and colour settings
- The painful side, to save time
- Only the painful side
- It does not matter
: The normal side gives you settings and a reference for flow. Then scan the painful side with the same settings, and compare the two side by side.

?? Why set the colour Doppler scale low for the testis?
@ 04-scrotum-technique
+ Intratesticular flow is slow and a high scale misses it
- A low scale reduces the scan time
- A low scale shows the epididymis better in grey-scale
- A low scale prevents aliasing in the cord
: Blood flow inside the testis is slow, especially in young boys. A high scale shows no colour even in a normal testis. Set the scale low and the gain just below noise.

?? What does the whirlpool sign show?
@ 05-torsion
+ A twisted spermatic cord, with vessels spiralling into a round mass
- Increased flow in the epididymis
- A reactive hydrocele
- A normal spermatic cord
: The twisted cord forms a round mass with vessels spiralling into it. In the 2018 meta-analysis it had a specificity of 99%, so a whirlpool strongly supports torsion.

?? A boy has sudden testicular pain and normal colour flow in the testis. What do you conclude?
@ 05-torsion
+ Torsion is not excluded, because partial or intermittent torsion preserves flow
- Torsion is excluded
- He has epididymo-orchitis
- He needs no follow-up
: Partial torsion preserves some arterial flow, and a testis which has untwisted becomes hyperaemic. Normal flow does not exclude torsion. The clinical picture decides referral.

?? Which scan findings support epididymo-orchitis?
@ 05-torsion
+ An enlarged epididymis with increased colour flow and a straight cord
- A twisted cord with a whirlpool sign
- Absent flow in an enlarged testis
- A small upper-pole structure with no flow
: Inflammation increases blood flow, so the epididymis looks large and hyperaemic. A straight cord without a twist argues against torsion. A small avascular upper-pole structure suggests a torsed appendage.

?? When should a scrotal scan delay urology referral?
@ 05-torsion
+ Never, when torsion is clinically suspected
- When the scan shows normal flow
- When the patient is over 18
- When the pain started more than 6 hours ago
: Torsion is a clinical diagnosis, and salvage falls with time. Refer on clinical grounds. A bedside scan can be done while urology attends, not instead.
